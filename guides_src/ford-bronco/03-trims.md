Bronco trims read like a list of places to go, but they're really a menu of hardware. Some are about comfort, some about trail equipment and some about looks. Equipment also moved between trims and packages over the years, so trust the window sticker or the build sheet, not the badge on the fender. Buy the equipment you'll use, not the photograph you hope to take.

## The trims

| Trim | Years | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Base | 2021–2023, 2025–2026 | The no-frills Bronco, steel wheels, 2.3-liter and manual standard. A blank canvas for accessories |
| Big Bend | 2021–2026 | Often the smarter everyday starting point, with alloy wheels and more comfort equipment |
| Black Diamond | 2021–2024, then a Big Bend package | Steel bumpers, skid plates and a washable interior for trail use |
| Outer Banks | 2021–2026 | Comfort and convenience first. Tire and package choices change its ride a lot |
| Badlands | 2021–2026 | The serious trail Bronco, with a front stabilizer bar that disconnects for more wheel travel when crawling |
| Wildtrak | 2021–2024 | The desert runner before the Raptor, with the V6 and the Sasquatch Package |
| First Edition | 2021 | Limited to 3,500, with Badlands hardware and the Sasquatch Package |
| Everglades | 2022 and 2024 | A 2.3-liter with a factory WARN winch and a snorkel for deep water. Ford pulled it for 2023 |
| Heritage Edition | 2023–2026 | 1960s-style grille, wheels and paint, with the Sasquatch Package standard on the 2024. A Heritage Limited Edition, limited to 1,966, was offered for 2023 and 2024 |
| Stroppe Edition | 2025–2026 | Honors Bill Stroppe's Baja racers. 2.7-liter V6. 4-door only for 2026 |
| Raptor | 2022–2026 | The fastest, widest Bronco. See the Raptor chapter |

## The Sasquatch Package

Sasquatch means 35-inch tires on beadlock-capable wheels, a suspension lift and different axle gearing, and it was standard on some trims, like the Wildtrak and First Edition, and optional on others. It raises ground clearance to 11.6 inches and makes the Bronco look the part. It also raises the step-in height, replacement-tire costs and road noise, and it costs fuel economy: Sasquatch Broncos are rated at 17 or 18 mpg combined. For 2021, it couldn't be combined with the manual gearbox.

## How to choose

For mostly paved driving, a Big Bend or Outer Banks on standard tires is the easier truck to live with. For slow, technical trails, the Badlands and its stabilizer-bar disconnect are worth every compromise. For the look, a Heritage Edition carries the 1960s face. And whichever trim you choose, a Sasquatch truck has more capability but is less pleasant on the freeway.
