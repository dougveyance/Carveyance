Replacing the 4Runner was always going to be awkward, because people loved the old one precisely for refusing to change. Toyota had to improve nearly everything without upsetting anyone, on a truck with the fan base of a folk hero and the aerodynamics of a filing cabinet.

For 2025 the 4Runner moved to TNGA-F, the truck platform it shares with the Land Cruiser and the Lexus GX. It's still body-on-frame, it's still built at Toyota's Tahara plant in Japan, and the rear window still rolls down. Under the hood, the V6 gave way to a turbocharged 2.4-liter 4-cylinder, and the 5-speed automatic to an 8-speed. The 2026 is the second model year of the same design.

## What the turbo changes

On paper the horsepower barely moves: 278 against the V6's 270. Torque is the real story. The turbo makes 317 lb-ft from 1,700 rpm, where the V6 needed 4,400 rpm to reach its 278. That low-down shove, with 3 extra gears to keep it working, makes the new truck feel far more willing pulling away, merging and climbing. Car and Driver timed a turbo Limited at 7.5 seconds to 60 mph, and Motor Trend got an SR5 there in 7.3.

[[chart:torque]]

What it doesn't do is sound like the old one. A 4-cylinder working hard will never have the easygoing hum of a big V6 loafing along, and some owners will miss it.

Fuel economy improves without becoming impressive. The EPA rates the turbo 4x4 at 21 mpg combined (19 city, 25 highway) and the two-wheel drive at 22. Against the old 4x4's 17, that's about 168 fewer gallons a year at 15,000 miles. Better, but still truck numbers.

## Is the interior finally good?

Mostly, yes. Toyota Safety Sense 3.0 is standard across the range. Higher grades get a 14-inch touchscreen above a 12.3-inch digital gauge cluster, with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, and Toyota kept real knobs for the volume and the climate, which deserves a thank-you card.

[[photo:ch2-cabin]]

What a bigger screen can't change is the truck underneath. It's tall, it moves around over broken pavement, and the ride is busier than any crossover's.

A third row is available, but only with the gas engine, because the hybrid's battery takes up the space. Treat it as seating for children and short trips. If you carry 6 or 7 people every day, drive a Highlander, Grand Highlander or Sequoia first.

## 9 grades, briefly

Toyota offers 9: SR5, TRD Sport, TRD Sport Premium, TRD Off-Road, TRD Off-Road Premium, Limited, Platinum, TRD Pro and Trailhunter. The [trim chapter](../trims/) sorts them out. The short version: the SR5 and TRD Off-Road are the sweet spots, the hybrid is standard on the 3 most expensive grades, and a Trailhunter that never leaves pavement is a very efficient way to turn money into roof rack.

Most versions tow up to 2,722 kg (6,000 lb), a 454 kg (1,000 lb) gain over the old truck, though some off-road grades are rated a little lower. Check the rating for the exact truck before you hitch anything heavy.

## Buying one used

These trucks are young, and nobody has a long-term record for the new engines yet. Toyota's factory warranty covers the early years, so find out exactly how much is left on the truck in front of you. Ask for the service records and any software updates. Then run the VIN: the 2025 4Runner is part of a 2025 Toyota and Lexus recall for instrument-panel software that can leave the gauge display blank (25V-595), and newer campaigns may have been added since.

## The verdict

The sixth-generation 4Runner is the better all-round tool: stronger where it counts, more efficient, safer and finally modern inside. It will make a rough trail feel ordinary. It will never make an expressway feel like a luxury sedan. If that's the deal you wanted, Toyota updated its most stubborn SUV without sanding off the stubbornness.

::: curiosity The Window That Almost Didn't Make It
[[photo:ch2-curio]]
Every 4Runner since the first one in 1984 has had a rear window that rolls down into the tailgate. (The original also had a fiberglass top over the cargo area that came off.) For 2025, Toyota seriously considered dropping the window because it adds weight and complexity. The American side of the team pushed back, telling the engineers in Japan that "the roll down window is the soul of the 4Runner." So they went through the truck part by part, trimming weight elsewhere to keep it. Dog owners, surfers and anyone who has slid lumber through the back can relax.
:::
