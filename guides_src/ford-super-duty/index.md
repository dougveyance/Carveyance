The Super Duty is the F-Series that does the lifting. Since Ford split its heavy trucks onto their own platform in 1999, the F-250 and F-350 have become the default tool of ranchers, fleets and anyone whose trailer outweighs their house, and the numbers have escalated to freight class: the 2026 High Output diesel makes 500 hp and 1,200 lb-ft, and a properly equipped F-450 tows 18,144 kg (40,000 lb) by gooseneck.

The buying question is almost never trim; it is engine and year. This guide covers the current 2023–2026 trucks, the 2017–2022 aluminum generation that dominates the used market, the gas-or-diesel decision that determines your next decade of running costs, and a checklist with teeth — because the wrong used diesel is the most expensive mistake in trucks.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder, new:** a 2026 F-250 XLT or Lariat with the 7.3 gas V8 — unless you tow heavy weekly, in which case the standard 475-hp diesel.
- **Best used value:** a 2020–2022 F-250 with the 10-speed, either the 7.3 Godzilla or the 1,050-lb-ft diesel, with documented service.
- **Budget workhorse:** a 2011–2016 6.7 Power Stroke with receipts, or a 6.2 gas truck for lighter duty and cheaper everything.
- **The one to research first:** any 2003–2007 6.0 Power Stroke. Bulletproofed with paperwork, it's a deal; stock and undocumented, it's a project.
- **Think twice if** your trailer is under 3,629 kg (8,000 lb). A properly equipped F-150 tows that for less money, fuel and ride harshness.

Heavy-duty pickups are exempt from EPA fuel-economy ratings, so no window-sticker mpg exists for any of them; owner-reported figures put the diesels in the mid-to-high teens empty and the gas V8s a few mpg below.
