A Land Cruiser can be exactly right for a long, rough trip and expensive overkill for the school run. Its reputation for lasting forever makes buyers skip the arithmetic they'd do on any other truck. Don't. Match the truck to your life, then let the records and an inspection decide.

## First, know which one it is

The 2017–2021 Land Cruiser is the 200 Series, with a V8 and up to 8 seats. There were no new Land Cruisers sold in the US for 2022 or 2023. The 2024 and newer Land Cruiser is the 250 Series, a 5-seat hybrid. Listings and comparison sites sometimes blur the line, so confirm the generation, the seating and the grade from the VIN and the window sticker.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2017–2019 | 200 Series. 5.7-liter V8, 8-speed, TSS-P | Passenger airbag sensor recall. Fuel pump recall (2018–2019) |
| 2020–2021 | Heritage Edition added. 2-row only for 2020 | No factory Apple CarPlay or Android Auto, like every 200 Series |
| 2022–2023 | Not sold in the US | A listing for a 2022 or 2023 Land Cruiser is a mistake, so check the VIN |
| 2024 | 250 Series. Hybrid, 5 seats, First Edition | Fuel tube, instrument display and load-label recalls |
| 2025 | 2 grades, First Edition dropped | Fuel tube, instrument display and load-label recalls |
| 2026 | No changes | Fuel tube recall |

## The test drive

Drive it cold and fully warm. Listen for rattles, whines, rough running and driveline vibration, and feel for slow or clunky shifts. Brake hard from highway speed and remember how heavy these trucks are. Test the climate control, every camera, the 4WD modes, low range and the locking differentials where it's safe to try. If you'll tow, have the hitch and wiring checked and confirm the truck's ratings on its door label.

## Underneath is where the truth is

Have a shop that knows Toyota 4x4s put it on a lift. Look for rust on the frame rails, body mounts, suspension brackets, brake lines and skid plates, and for fresh undercoating hiding it. Check the differentials, driveshafts, steering parts and bushings. Trail scratches aren't a problem. Bent crossmembers, bad welds and hidden rust are. Ask what was modified, by whom and whether there are receipts.

## 200 Series checks, 2017–2021

Ask for oil, coolant, transmission and differential service records. Look for leaks from the engine and cooling system, and for any fluid around the KDSS cylinders and lines. Confirm the seating: a 2020 Heritage Edition has only 2 rows. Budget for tires, brakes and fuel, not just the purchase price. A cheap, neglected 200 Series can turn into an expensive restoration.

## 250 Series checks, 2024–2026

Ask for the full Toyota service history. It should start quietly in Ready mode with no hybrid or engine warnings. Look for leaks around the turbo and cooling system, check the 12-volt battery and look underneath for trail damage. Its long-term record is still being written. Toyota covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 150,000 miles and other hybrid parts for 8 years or 100,000 miles, and the battery coverage transfers to the next owner, so confirm the first-use date.

## Recalls and campaigns: check by VIN

- **2008–2019:** Passenger seat belt sensor wire that can break and switch off the passenger-side airbags (18V-887).
- **2018–2019:** Low-pressure fuel pump that can fail and stall the engine (20V-012 and 20V-682).
- **2024–2027:** Fuel tube connection near the tank that can come apart and leak fuel (26V-673). Toyota planned to notify owners by mid-December 2026.
- **2024–2025:** Part of the 12.3-inch instrument display can go blank at startup, hiding warnings (26V-341).
- **2024–2025 with accessories fitted before sale:** A load-capacity label that may list the wrong accessory weight (24V-548 and 26V-179). The fix is a new label.
- **Every year:** New recalls can appear at any time. Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask a Toyota dealer to check for open service campaigns too. Ask for proof that each repair was done.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and with 2 generations and a lot of modified trucks, an average tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same generation, year, grade and mileage, with the same title history, and separate stock trucks from modified ones. Keep dealer asking prices apart from what trucks actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you, comparing a listing with similar ones nearby. On a 200 Series, the underside and the service records can be worth more than a year of age. On a 250 Series, check the recall status and what's left of the warranty.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the truck past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Also worth a look

- **Lexus LX 570 (2008–2021):** the 200 Series' luxury twin, with the same platform and a 383-hp version of the V8.
- **Lexus GX 550 (2024 on):** built on the same TNGA-F frame as the 250 Series, with a 349-hp twin-turbo V6 and a third row on most versions.
- **Lexus LX 600 (2022 on):** the US version of the 300 Series Land Cruiser that Toyota didn't bring here.
- **Toyota 4Runner:** if you want Toyota off-road ability for less money.

## Our shortlist

- **Most space and towing:** a documented, unmodified 2019–2021 200 Series.
- **Best value:** a 2025–2026 Land Cruiser 1958 with its recalls done.
- **Best for trails:** a 2024–2026 Land Cruiser grade, for its disconnecting anti-roll bar.
- **For collectors:** a 2024 First Edition or a 2020–2021 Heritage Edition, inspected like any other.
