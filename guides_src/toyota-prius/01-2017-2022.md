The fourth-generation Prius arrived for 2016, so the 2017–2022 cars are its settled years. It's the Prius most people picture: the sharp nose, the tall tail, the split rear window and fuel economy that ordinary cars still can't touch. It's also the most sensible way into a used Prius, because it's plentiful, proven and simple to own.

## How it drives

Every one uses a 1.8-liter 4-cylinder and a pair of electric motor-generators, for 121 hp combined, through a continuously variable transmission. It isn't quick. Car and Driver ran a 2017 Three Touring from 0 to 60 mph in 10.5 seconds, and you plan your freeway merges accordingly. What it does well is everything below that: it pulls away silently on electric power, glides at low speeds and rides more comfortably than the third-generation car it replaced.

[[chart:mpg]]

The fuel economy is the point. The EPA rates most front-drive versions at 52 mpg combined (54 city, 50 highway). The 2017–2018 Two Eco, and the L Eco that replaced it, is rated at 56 combined (58 city, 53 highway), the best of this generation. Car and Driver lists about 25 cubic feet of cargo space behind the rear seat of a 2017 Three Touring, so it carries more than a typical compact sedan.

## The years and grades

For 2017, Toyota made its Toyota Safety Sense P suite standard on every Prius: automatic emergency braking with pedestrian detection, lane departure alert with steering assist, adaptive cruise control and automatic high beams. The grades were numbered, from the Two and Two Eco up to the Four Touring, and the Two started at $24,685 ($33,900 in 2026 dollars).

The 2019 refresh brought a calmer nose and new taillights, renamed the grades L Eco, LE, XLE and Limited, and added AWD-e on the LE and XLE. Apple CarPlay and Amazon Alexa became standard for 2020. For 2021, Android Auto arrived on every grade except the Limited, the safety suite moved up to Toyota Safety Sense 2.0, and Toyota sold a 2020 Edition, limited to 2,020 cars. For 2022, the last year of this generation, a rear seat reminder became standard and Toyota added a blacked-out Nightshade Edition.

## AWD-e

The AWD-e adds a small electric motor that drives the rear wheels from a standstill up to 6 mph, then again when needed up to 43 mph. Above that, it's a front-drive Prius. It isn't an off-road system, but it helps a lot when you pull away on snow or climb a slick driveway. AWD-e cars use a nickel-metal hydride battery, which Toyota chose for its cold-weather performance, and the EPA rates them at 50 mpg combined. In 2019, the LE AWD-e cost $26,380 ($34,700 in 2026 dollars).

## What to check before you buy

The hybrid battery warranty is the biggest difference between years. Toyota covers the battery on 2020 and later hybrids for 10 years or 150,000 miles from the date the car was first sold; 2017–2019 cars had 8 years or 100,000 miles in most states, so many are now out of coverage. Have a Toyota shop scan the hybrid system, and check the engine wire harness recall on 2017–2018 cars and the brake booster pump recall on 2019 cars. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A documented 2020–2022 LE or XLE is the sweet spot of the whole Prius family: CarPlay, the longer battery warranty and 52 mpg, for less than the newer car. A 2017–2019 car is cheaper still and just as frugal, but budget for an aging battery and pay accordingly. Choose an AWD-e if you see snow every winter. If you want more power and a car that looks nothing like this one, read [the 2023–2026 chapter](../2023-2026/).

::: curiosity "To Go Before"
Toyota says the Prius name is Latin for "to go before." In 2011, it also asked the American public to pick a plural, and "Prii" won the vote, though Latin scholars point out that the word doesn't work that way.
