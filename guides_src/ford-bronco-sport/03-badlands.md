A used Bronco Sport Big Bend and a Badlands share a silhouette, not a driving experience. If steep or loose surfaces are a regular part of your life, the Badlands is the one to look at. Other Bronco Sports are capable on maintained dirt roads and in snow on the right tires, but that doesn't make them interchangeable with it.

## What's different underneath

The Badlands has the 2.0-liter turbo 4-cylinder, rated at 245 hp for 2021–2024 and 250 hp from 2025, with the 8-speed automatic. It also has Ford's Advanced 4x4 with a twin-clutch rear drive unit, which can lock the rear wheels together like a locking differential and send torque to either side. The other trims use a simpler system shared with the Escape. The Badlands adds more off-road drive modes and, from 2025, could be ordered with a Sasquatch Package for still more hardware.

## What it does better

The bigger engine makes highway passing and steep grades feel relaxed: Car and Driver ran a 2021 Badlands to 60 mph in 5.9 seconds, 2.3 seconds quicker than the 1.5-liter Outer Banks it tested. It tows more, 1,225 kg (2,700 lb) for 2025–2026 against 998 kg (2,200 lb) for a 1.5-liter. It also sits a little higher, with 8.1 inches of ground clearance on 2026 models against 7.8.

## What it costs

You pay for it in fuel and tires. The EPA rates the Badlands at 23 mpg combined against 27 for a 1.5-liter, and its all-terrain tires cost more to replace. The 2021 Badlands started at $32,660 before delivery, or $40,500 in 2026 dollars, and the 2026 Badlands at $37,265.

## The special editions

The 2021 First Edition had the Badlands' mechanicals with more equipment and its own graphics. The 2023 Heritage Limited Edition was also built on the Badlands, with nearly every option and 1960s-inspired styling, and limited to 1,966. The regular Heritage Edition, by contrast, is based on the Big Bend and has the 1.5-liter. Check which one you're looking at.

## Used Badlands: look where the ads don't

Off-road use isn't a defect, but damage is expensive. Check the lower radiator area, the front underbody, the engine protection, the suspension joints, the wheels, the tires and the alignment. Look for dirt and moisture in electrical connectors and in the carpets if it's been through water. Make sure the rear drive unit and the drive modes work as the owner's manual describes. Package names can sound alike while the hardware differs, so compare the original build sheet, not the badges.
