The Transit replaced the Econoline in 2015 with a boron-steel unibody built in Kansas City, and the switch bought real capability: up to 600 lb more payload than a comparable E-Series, a load floor you can stand on under the 110.1-inch high roof, and as much as 536 cubic feet of cargo in the long-wheelbase extended body. The series badges work like pickup names — 150, 250, 350 and 350HD climb in springs and axle, from 8,670 up to 11,000 lb GVWR — and because every version but the passenger wagon sits above the EPA's 8,500-lb line, cargo vans carry no window-sticker fuel rating at all. Figure on mid-teens in mixed work; the rated wagons say 16–17 mpg combined.

Engines sort cleanly by era. From 2015 to 2019 the base was an honest 275-hp 3.7 V6 (260 lb-ft, and convertible to CNG or propane), with 2 options: the 310-hp, 400-lb-ft 3.5 EcoBoost and a 185-hp, 350-lb-ft 3.2-liter Power Stroke inline-5 diesel. The 2020 refresh retired both the 3.7 and the diesel, put a port-and-direct-injected 3.5 V6 (275 hp, 262 lb-ft) in the base slot, swapped the 6-speed automatic for the F-150's 10-speed, and brought back something the E-Series never had — optional all-wheel drive. It also added power sliding doors, modern driver aids and, eventually, the Transit Trail, a 310-hp AWD-only trim with a Quaife limited-slip diff aimed squarely at the camper-conversion crowd.

## Getting the combination right

Buy the body for the job and the engine for the load. A low-roof 150 drives most like a car and fits most parking garages; a high-roof 250 is the delivery and conversion default; a 350 or 350HD is for heavy upfits, big passenger counts and serious towing. The EcoBoost is the one that never feels burdened — 7,500 lb of tow rating at launch and effortless mountain merges — while the base V6s are slower (figure high-8s to 60 unloaded, against the mid-7s of an EcoBoost) but simpler and cheaper to keep. The 15-seat wagon deserves a special word: it is the rare American 15-passenger van with modern stability control and crash structure, and lightly-used shuttle examples are the used market's quiet bargain.

If a used van's spec is wrong for you — wrong roof, wrong length, no AWD where you need it — walk away, however clean it is. The configuration is permanent; the next right van is always coming, because Ford sells about 150,000 a year.
