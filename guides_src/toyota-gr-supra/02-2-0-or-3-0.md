For 2021, Toyota slipped a second Supra in underneath the first: a 2.0-liter turbocharged 4-cylinder, also from BMW, with 255 hp and 295 lb-ft. It was the first 4-cylinder Supra, it started at $42,990 against $50,990 for the 3.0 (about $53,500 and $63,400 in 2026 money), and it lasted 4 model years. Toyota dropped it after 2024, explaining that once the 3.0 could be had with a manual, buyers had drifted toward the 6-cylinder.

That makes the 2.0 a small, slightly forgotten corner of the used market. Here's how it compares, and when it's worth seeking out.

## What's different

| | 2021–2024 2.0 | 2021–2026 3.0 |
|---|---|---|
| Engine | 2.0-liter turbo 4-cylinder | 3.0-liter turbo inline-6 |
| Power | 255 hp | 382 hp |
| Torque | 295 lb-ft | 368 lb-ft |
| Transmission | 8-speed automatic only | 8-speed automatic; 6-speed manual from 2023 |
| 0–60 mph (Toyota) | 5.0 sec | 3.9 sec automatic |
| Curb weight | 1,443 kg (3,181 lb) | 1,542 kg (3,400 lb) |
| Wheels | 18-inch | 19-inch |
| Front brakes | Single-piston calipers, smaller rotors | 4-piston calipers |
| Seats | Manually adjusted | 14-way power |
| EPA combined (2024) | 27 mpg | 26 mpg automatic, 21 manual |

## What the 2.0 does well

Weight. The 2.0 is about 100 kg (219 lb) lighter than the 3.0, and Toyota spread the savings through the whole car, from the engine and differential to the suspension, seats and stereo, so the near 50:50 balance stayed. On a tight road, it feels a little lighter on its feet.

[[chart:weight]]

It's still quick. Toyota's 5.0-second 0–60 time made it the second-quickest Toyota on sale when it arrived, behind only the 3.0 Supra. It has the same electronically limited 155-mph top speed. Its 18-inch tires and smaller brakes cost less to replace, and it uses a little less fuel.

## What it gives up

The obvious things are 127 hp and the sound and effortless midrange of the straight-six, which suit the Supra's character best. The less obvious ones matter more to some buyers. The 2.0 was never offered with the manual. It has smaller brakes and less standard equipment. And if you're planning track days, the 3.0's bigger brakes count for more than its extra power.

## The money question

[[chart:mpg]]

Fuel isn't the reason to choose the 2.0. At the EPA's 2024 ratings and 12,000 miles a year, it uses about 444 gallons, against about 462 for a 3.0 automatic. At $5 a gallon of premium, that's a difference of about $85 a year. The 3.0 manual, at 21 mpg, uses about 571 gallons, roughly $635 a year more than the 2.0. These are examples, not forecasts.

The real savings, if there are any, are in the purchase price, the insurance quote and the cost of tires and brakes. So compare listings of both engines at the same year and mileage, and get insurance quotes on each. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) helps with the first part. If the 2.0 isn't clearly cheaper, the 3.0 is the better car to own, and probably the easier one to sell later.

## Who should buy which

- **Choose the 2.0** if you want the lightest, least expensive Supra to buy and run, you're happy with an automatic, and the price reflects what it is.
- **Choose a 2021–2022 3.0 automatic** for the full engine at the best value.
- **Choose a 2023–2026 3.0 manual** if shifting is the point.
- **Look twice at a 2020 3.0.** It has 47 fewer hp than a 2021 and the recalls that hit the earliest cars, so it should cost noticeably less. The checklist chapter has the details.
