The Corolla Cross is what happens when Toyota takes its most sensible car and gives it more ground clearance, a taller seat and a hatch. The result isn't exciting, but it is useful, efficient and easy to own, which is exactly why people buy it.

## What it is

The Corolla Cross shares its platform with the Corolla and sits between the smaller C-HR and the bigger RAV4. Corolla Crosses for the US are built by Mazda Toyota Manufacturing, a joint venture plant in Huntsville, Alabama, that opened with the capacity to build up to 150,000 Corolla Crosses a year.

There are 2 powertrains. The gas model uses a 169-hp 2.0-liter with a CVT that has a physical launch gear for quicker starts, and you can choose front-wheel drive or all-wheel drive. The all-wheel-drive system can send up to 50% of the power to the rear wheels. From 2023, there's also a hybrid with 196 hp and all-wheel drive as standard, using a separate electric motor at the rear.

## How it drives

The gas Corolla Cross is slow. Car and Driver needed 9.2 seconds to reach 60 mph, and reviewers have complained that the engine is noisy when you work it hard. Around town, at a gentle pace, it's calm and easy to drive.

The hybrid changes the character. Car and Driver got a 2023 Hybrid XSE to 60 mph in 7.3 seconds, and Consumer Reports found the hybrid more powerful and more refined than the gas model, as well as far more efficient. If you're choosing between them, drive both.

## Space and practicality

Up front, there's plenty of room. The back seat is the weak spot: rear legroom is 32.0 inches, which is tight for tall adults. Cargo space behind the rear seat is 24.0 cubic feet in front-wheel-drive gas models and 21.5 in all-wheel-drive gas models and hybrids, because the rear drive hardware takes up some of the floor. With the rear seat folded, a hybrid holds 61.8 cubic feet.

Every version can tow up to 680 kg (1,500 lb), enough for a small utility trailer, and has about 8 inches of ground clearance, which helps on gravel roads and in snow.

## Fuel economy

The EPA rates the 2022–2026 gas model at 32 mpg combined with front-wheel drive and 30 with all-wheel drive. The hybrid is rated at 42. Toyota's estimates for 2027 drop the gas models by 1 mpg and keep the hybrid at 42.

## Who it suits

The Corolla Cross makes the most sense for singles, couples and small families who want a higher seat, a hatch and the option of all-wheel drive in a car that's cheap to run. If you often carry adults in the back or need a big cargo area, the RAV4 is worth the extra money. If you don't need the height, a Corolla Hybrid sedan is more efficient still.

## The verdict

The Corolla Cross is a good small SUV with one big decision attached. Buy the hybrid if you can. The gas model is fine for gentle drivers on a budget, but the hybrid is the version that feels finished.

::: curiosity Built on the Apollo Line
The Corolla Cross is built in a plant Toyota shares with Mazda. Mazda Toyota Manufacturing in Huntsville has 2 side-by-side assembly lines: the Corolla Cross is built on one, the Mazda CX-50 on the other. They're named Apollo and Discovery, a tribute to Huntsville's role in the US space program, and the plant can build 300,000 vehicles a year, split evenly between the 2 brands.
:::
