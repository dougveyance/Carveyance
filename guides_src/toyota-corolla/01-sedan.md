Nobody buys a Corolla sedan to be noticed. People buy it because it starts, stops, sips fuel and asks for very little in return. In this decade, though, the Corolla also got better in ways you can feel, and the year you choose makes a real difference.

## 2017–2019: the old guard

The 2017 Corolla was a refresh of the 11th generation, timed for the car's 50th anniversary. The big news wasn't the new grille. It was safety: every 2017 Corolla came with Toyota Safety Sense P, which bundled automatic emergency braking with pedestrian detection, lane departure alert with steering assist, automatic high beams and radar cruise control. A backup camera became standard too. At the time, that was unusual equipment for a cheap compact car.

Toyota also renamed the grades to match the Camry: L, LE, LE Eco, SE, XLE and XSE. A 50th Anniversary Special Edition, based on the SE, was sold for 2017 only. Most versions use a continuously variable transmission (CVT), and the SE could still be had with a 6-speed manual.

The engine was the weak point. The 1.8-liter made 132 hp, or 140 hp in the efficiency-tuned LE Eco, and reviewers called the car underpowered next to its rivals. The EPA rated the L, LE and XLE at 32 mpg combined and the LE Eco at 34. The 2017 L started at $18,500 before destination, about $25,400 in 2026 money. The sedan carried on largely unchanged through 2019.

## 2020: a different car

The 12th-generation sedan arrived for 2020, a year after the hatchback, on Toyota's TNGA platform. It's lower, wider at the wheels and much better to drive. Toyota Safety Sense 2.0 and Apple CarPlay were standard, and the sedan started at $19,500, about $25,400 in 2026 money.

There were 2 gas engines. The L, LE and XLE used a 139-hp 1.8-liter, rated at 33 mpg combined (32 for the XLE). The SE and XSE got a new 169-hp 2.0-liter, rated at 34 mpg with the CVT. The SE could be ordered with a 6-speed manual from 2020 to 2022. A Corolla Hybrid joined the lineup too, and it gets its own chapter.

## 2023: the update that matters

For 2023, Toyota dropped the 1.8-liter and the L grade, so every gas Corolla sedan now has the 2.0-liter. Toyota says that makes the LE about 2 seconds quicker to 60 mph than before, and it's also more efficient: 35 mpg combined, against 33 for the old 1.8. Every 2023 Corolla also got Toyota Safety Sense 3.0, a new 8-inch touchscreen and updated styling. The XLE became hybrid-only, and the manual transmission was dropped.

The 2024–2026 cars are largely the same. For 2026, the EPA rates the LE at 35 mpg combined and the SE and XSE at 34. The current car continues for 2027, joined by a 60th Anniversary Special Edition based on the SE Hybrid and limited to 2,500 cars in the US. Toyota has already shown a concept that previews the next generation.

## How it drives

The 1.8-liter does the job but gets loud when you ask for more. The 2.0-liter is the one to have, quieter and more willing on highway ramps. Either way, the 12th-generation car rides more quietly and steers more precisely than the old one. Rear-seat room is fine for adults on shorter trips, and the trunk is ordinary for the class.

## What to check

- **Fuel pump (2018–2020):** Certain cars were included in Toyota's low-pressure fuel pump recall. A failing pump can stall the engine (20V-012, expanded as 20V-682).
- **Driver airbag connection (2023):** About 48,300 2023 Corollas built between February and July 2023 needed the spiral cable in the steering column inspected, because the driver's airbag might not deploy (23V-480). Some were later called back because the first inspection may have been done incorrectly (25V-040).
- **Steering shaft (2023–2024):** About 8,000 Corollas and Corolla Hybrids built between mid-July and early September 2023 needed a new steering intermediate shaft, which may have been made with cracks (24V-878).

Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls). The used checklist chapter has the rest.

## Which one to buy

- **2023–2026 LE:** the best all-round choice. The 2.0-liter, the newer safety tech and 35 mpg.
- **2020–2022 SE:** the 2.0-liter before it was standard, and the last chance at a manual sedan.
- **2020–2022 LE:** a fine, cheaper car if the price reflects the slower 1.8-liter.
- **2017–2019:** only if it's clearly cheaper and well kept. Standard safety tech helps, but the engine and cabin are a generation behind.

## The verdict

The Corolla sedan is at its best from 2023 on, when Toyota finally gave every gas car the engine it deserved. Earlier 12th-generation cars are still good buys if you price them honestly. And the 11th-generation car remains what it always was: dependable, frugal and slow.

::: curiosity The Corolla That Went to the Police
Corollas for the US have been built in Mississippi, Canada and Japan. When Toyota's Blue Springs, Mississippi, plant started building the 12th-generation Corolla in March 2019, the plant president presented the town with a souvenir: the first 11th-generation Corolla the plant had built, on September 7, 2013. The mayor said it would go to work supporting the Blue Springs Police Department.
:::
