The earlier Insights looked like hybrids: the first was a 2-seat teardrop with covered rear wheels, and the second a 5-door hatchback with a sloping tail. The 2019 Insight went the other way. It went on sale on June 29, 2018, as a conventional-looking 4-door sedan, built on the platform of the Civic of the same years, from $22,830 ($30,000 in 2026 dollars).

## How the hybrid works

[[chart:mpg]]

Honda calls it a 2-motor hybrid. A 1.5-liter Atkinson-cycle 4-cylinder and an electric drive motor together make 151 hp, and the motor makes 197 lb-ft of torque. There's no conventional gearbox. Most of the time the electric motor drives the front wheels, with the engine either switched off or turning a second motor that works as a generator. In certain conditions, a clutch connects the engine directly to the wheels. The battery sits in a compact pack under the rear seat, so the trunk stays the full 15.1 cubic feet on the LX and EX.

Steering-wheel paddles set how hard the car slows down when you lift off, using the motor to recharge the battery. The EPA rates the LX and EX at 55 mpg in the city, 49 on the highway and 52 combined, and the heavier Touring at 48 combined.

## Speed

[[chart:accel]]

It's the quickest Insight by a long way. Car and Driver ran a 2019 Touring to 60 mph in 7.6 seconds, against 10.3 for a 2010 Insight and 10.6 for the original.

## The trims

Every Insight has LED headlights, push-button start and Honda Sensing, with adaptive cruise control, automatic emergency braking and lane keeping assist.

- **LX:** the base car, 2019 to 2021, with a 1-piece folding rear seat.
- **EX:** adds an 8-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, and a 60/40 split folding rear seat.
- **Touring:** adds leather, an 8-way power driver's seat, navigation and dual-zone climate control. It's rated at 48 mpg instead of 52.

## Year by year

- **2019:** launch year. LX $22,830 ($30,000 in 2026 dollars), EX $24,060 ($31,600) and Touring $28,090 ($36,900).
- **2020:** small price rises, with the LX at $22,930 ($29,800).
- **2021:** carryover.
- **2022:** the LX was dropped, so the EX became the base car, at $25,210 at launch ($29,000). Production ended in June 2022, and Honda's Greensburg plant moved to building the CR-V, the CR-V Hybrid and the Civic hatchback.

Honda sold about 78,000 in the US, so used ones aren't as common as Civics or Priuses from the same years. The engine, the hybrid system and the battery pack were all built in Ohio.

## Which one to buy

The EX is the one to find. It has the best EPA rating, 52 mpg, plus phone mirroring and the split rear seat, and it costs less than a Touring. A Touring is worth it if you want leather and a power seat and you'll accept 4 mpg less. An LX makes sense if it's priced clearly below an EX and you don't need CarPlay.

The year matters less than the history: the recalls in chapter 2, especially the 2020–2021 power-supply recall, and the condition of the hybrid system.
