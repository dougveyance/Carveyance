Most Corolla Cross buying guides spend a lot of time on grades and colors. They shouldn't. The choice that changes the car is gas or hybrid, and for most people it isn't close.

## Power and speed

The hybrid makes 196 hp against the gas model's 169, and it uses electric torque to get moving, so the difference feels bigger than 27 hp.

[[chart:power]]

Car and Driver's testing puts numbers to it: 9.2 seconds to 60 mph for a gas Corolla Cross and 7.3 seconds for a Hybrid XSE. That's the difference between a crossover that struggles to merge and one that simply gets on with it.

[[chart:zero60]]

## The cost of driving

The EPA rates the gas model at 32 mpg combined with front-wheel drive and 30 with all-wheel drive. The hybrid, which always has all-wheel drive, is rated at 42.

[[chart:fuelcost]]

At 12,000 miles a year and $4 a gallon, that's about $1,500 a year for a gas FWD model, $1,600 for gas AWD and $1,143 for the hybrid. Against a gas AWD model, the hybrid saves about $457 a year.

Now compare the purchase price. When the hybrid launched for 2023, the base Hybrid S cost about $3,610 more than a gas L with all-wheel drive. At $457 a year, fuel savings alone would take nearly 8 years to cover that. That sounds like a poor deal, until you remember what else you get: a quicker car, a sportier suspension and better standard equipment on the hybrid grades. On the used market, compare the actual asking prices. The gap is often smaller or larger than it was new.

## Space

[[chart:cargo]]

The hybrid's rear motor takes a bite out of the cargo floor, so it has 21.5 cubic feet behind the rear seat, the same as an all-wheel-drive gas model. A front-wheel-drive gas model has the most room, at 24.0. Passenger space is the same in every version, and every version is rated to tow 680 kg (1,500 lb).

## Choose the gas model if

You want the cheapest Corolla Cross, you drive gently and mostly on local roads, and you don't need all-wheel drive. A front-wheel-drive LE gives you the most cargo space and a lower price. Just be honest with yourself about highway merging and passing.

## Choose the hybrid if

You drive a lot, spend time on highways, want all-wheel drive or simply want the better car. It's quicker, far more efficient and, according to Consumer Reports, more refined. Its battery is covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles.

## Our pick

For most buyers, the hybrid. It turns the Corolla Cross from a slow, sensible box into a pleasant, efficient small SUV. The gas model is still a reliable, affordable choice for drivers who never ask much of it.

::: curiosity The Hybrid That Stops Shorter
Hybrids are usually heavier than their gas twins, and Consumer Reports often finds they need more distance to stop. Not this one. In its testing, the Corolla Cross Hybrid stopped shorter from 60 mph than the gas model on both dry and wet pavement.
:::
