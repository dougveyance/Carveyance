Discontinued does not mean orphaned. Ford has to honor the 8-year, 100,000-mile high-voltage battery warranty on every Lightning, parts share heavily with the regular F-150, and the company has said it will support the fleet for years, with corrosion coverage on the last trucks running to 2033. The risks of a used Lightning are specific and checkable, so check them.

## The battery, first and always

- **State of health:** have the seller pull the battery report through the FordPass app or a dealer scan. A healthy pack after 3–4 years typically shows single-digit capacity loss; anything past the low teens is a negotiation.
- **Charging history:** ask how it was charged. A life of home Level 2 charging is the gentle case; a fleet truck that lived on DC fast chargers aged faster. The truck's lifetime energy stats help tell the story.
- **A full-charge test:** charge to 100 percent and compare the indicated range with the EPA figure for that battery and trim. One number, most of the answer.

## Recalls and campaigns, by VIN

- The **February 2023 battery fire recall** that paused production: confirm the battery module work or inspection is closed.
- Later campaigns covered software, seat belts and charging components; run the VIN at the dealer or NHTSA and insist on all-clear, in writing.
- Confirm the **NACS adapter** and, on extended-range trucks, the **80-amp Charge Station Pro** are included. Both are real money to replace.

## The ordinary truck stuff

- Tires: 3 tons and 775 lb-ft eat them; check wear and the date codes, and budget for a set on most used examples.
- Suspension and brakes: regen means brakes often look new; surface rust on lightly used rotors is normal and polishes off in a drive.
- The bed and frame: same checks as any F-150, and the frunk seal and drains deserve a look nothing else on the lot needs.
- Software: confirm the truck takes over-the-air updates and the FordPass account transfers cleanly to you before you drive away.

Price the truck against what the battery report says, not what the window sticker said in 2023. The first owner paid for the hype; you're buying the hardware.
