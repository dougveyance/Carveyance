For years the industry said nobody wanted a coupe. Then Honda brought back the Prelude and asked a harder question: what if the reborn sports coupe used the same hybrid as a sensible Civic? The old Prelude was never a muscle car. It was Honda's proof that a front-drive car could be sophisticated and fun, and the new one keeps that spirit. It's a handsome, efficient, surprisingly practical coupe with a very good chassis, and a few compromises that are worth understanding before you buy.

## The hardware

The powertrain comes straight from the Civic Hybrid: a 2.0-liter Atkinson-cycle 4-cylinder and 2 electric motors, for 200 hp and 232 lb-ft, driving the front wheels. There's no conventional transmission. The EPA rates it at 44 mpg combined (46 city, 41 highway), and it runs on regular fuel.

The chassis is where Honda spent the money. The Prelude borrows the Civic Type R's dual-axis front struts, which keep the steering from tugging under power, its adaptive dampers, retuned for this car, its wider tracks and its Brembo 4-piston front calipers, here painted Prelude Blue, on 13.8-inch rotors. It rides on 19-inch wheels and weighs 3,261 pounds, with 63 percent of that on the front wheels.

## How it drives

[[chart:zero60]]

It's quick enough, not fast. Car and Driver ran one from 0 to 60 mph in 6.5 seconds, and through the quarter mile in 15.3 seconds at 90 mph, a little behind the Civic Sport Touring Hybrid it tested at 6.2 seconds. What the numbers miss is how well it goes around corners: the steering is precise, the body stays flat and composed, and the brakes are far stronger than 200 hp needs. In its softer modes it's quiet and comfortable enough to cross a state in.

S+ Shift is the Prelude's party trick. Press the button and the car simulates 8 gears, with paddle shifts, rev-matched downshifts and enhanced engine sound. It makes the car more engaging on a back road, though it also makes it slower: in S+ Shift, Car and Driver's Prelude needed 7.3 seconds to reach 60 mph. Some drivers love it, others find it artificial. Try it before you decide.

## The cabin

There's one trim, and it's well equipped: a 10.2-inch digital instrument cluster, a 9-inch touchscreen with Google built-in, wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, wireless charging and an 8-speaker Bose system. The front seats, unique to the Prelude, are supportive and comfortable. The rear seats suit children or short trips, but they fold, and the liftback hatch opens onto 15.1 cubic feet of cargo space, more than double that of a Toyota GR86 or Subaru BRZ.

## The verdict

The Prelude is a better car than its spec sheet suggests, and a more expensive one than its powertrain suggests. If you want a stylish, comfortable coupe that's genuinely rewarding on a good road and returns 44 mpg, it's a delight. If you want the most speed or the most car for the money, [read the comparison](../prelude-or-civic/) first.

::: curiosity Slower Than 1997
In S+ Shift mode, Car and Driver's 2026 Prelude took 7.3 seconds to reach 60 mph. That's slower than the 1997 Prelude SH, with its 5-speed manual, which the magazine ran in 6.9 seconds.
:::
