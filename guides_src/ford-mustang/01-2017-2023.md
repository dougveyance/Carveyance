The sixth-generation Mustang went on sale for 2015, and the 2017–2023 cars in this chapter are its mature years. It was the first Mustang with independent rear suspension as standard, which makes it calmer over bumps and more composed in fast corners than the solid-axle cars before it. It came as a fastback or a convertible, with a 2.3-liter EcoBoost turbo 4-cylinder, a 5.0-liter V8 in the GT and, for 2017 only in this window, a 3.7-liter V6.

The year that matters most is 2018. Ford dropped the V6, raised the GT's 5.0-liter from 435 to 460 hp, gave the EcoBoost more torque, 350 lb-ft instead of 320, and replaced the 6-speed automatic with a 10-speed. A 2017 GT and a 2018 GT feel like different cars when you put your foot down.

## Engines and transmissions

| Engine | Years | Power | Torque | Transmissions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.7-liter V6 | 2017 | 300 hp | 280 lb-ft | 6-speed manual or 6-speed automatic |
| 2.3-liter EcoBoost | 2017 | 310 hp | 320 lb-ft | 6-speed manual or 6-speed automatic |
| 2.3-liter EcoBoost | 2018–2023 | 310 hp | 350 lb-ft | 6-speed manual or 10-speed automatic |
| 2.3-liter EcoBoost, High Performance Package | 2020–2023 | 330 hp | 350 lb-ft | 6-speed manual or 10-speed automatic |
| 5.0-liter V8, GT | 2017 | 435 hp | 400 lb-ft | 6-speed manual or 6-speed automatic |
| 5.0-liter V8, GT | 2018–2023 | 460 hp | 420 lb-ft | 6-speed manual or 10-speed automatic |

Outputs are from the cars Car and Driver tested, as listed by 0-60specs. The Bullitt, the Mach 1 and the 2 Shelbys have their own chapters.

## How quick they are

[[chart:accel]]

The 10-speed automatic is the quicker choice in a straight line. Car and Driver ran a 2018 GT Premium with the Performance Pack and the automatic to 60 mph in 3.8 seconds, against 4.3 seconds for the same car with the manual. The EcoBoost is no slouch either: a 2018 EcoBoost with the 6-speed manual took 5.1 seconds. The manual is the one to have if you want to be involved, but it costs you time on paper.

## Fuel economy

[[chart:mpg]]

The 2.3-liter EcoBoost is the one to buy if fuel cost matters. The EPA rates a 2018 EcoBoost automatic at 25 mpg combined, against 19 for a GT with the 10-speed and 18 with the manual. The V6 that disappeared after 2017 was rated at 21 mpg and is slower than the EcoBoost, so it makes sense only at a lower price.

## Year by year

| Model year | What changed |
|---|---|
| 2017 | Last year of the 3.7-liter V6 and of the 435-hp GT. 6-speed automatic |
| 2018 | Update: new face, V6 dropped, GT up to 460 hp, EcoBoost to 350 lb-ft, 10-speed automatic. 12.4-inch digital instrument cluster available, and MagneRide dampers as part of the Performance Package. Performance Pack Level 2 on the GT |
| 2019 | Bullitt, 480 hp, manual only (2019–2020) |
| 2020 | EcoBoost High Performance Package, 330 hp. Shelby GT500 arrives; last year of the Shelby GT350 |
| 2021 | Mach 1 replaces the Bullitt, 480 hp |
| 2022 | Last year of the Shelby GT500 |
| 2023 | Last year of the generation and of the Mach 1 |

## Who should own one

Buy a 2018–2023 GT if you want the V8 at a used price; it's the strongest value in this guide. Buy an EcoBoost if most of your miles are commuting, and look for the High Performance Package on a 2020–2023 car if you want the 4-cylinder to feel special. Avoid paying a premium for a 2017 over a 2018 unless you specifically want the 6-speed automatic's simplicity, or the car is unusually well kept.
