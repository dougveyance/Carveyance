The CR-V was America's best-selling SUV when the 2018 model arrived, and it earns its popularity with a roomy rear seat, a big, low cargo area and easy manners rather than drama. Between 2017 and 2026, Honda sold it in 2 generations. The fifth generation, through 2022, used a turbocharged 1.5-liter in most versions and added the first US CR-V Hybrid for 2020. The sixth generation, from 2023, made the hybrid the volume model, offered it with front-wheel drive, and spawned the CR-V e:FCEV, a plug-in hydrogen fuel cell version leased only in California.

For a used buyer, the choices are the generation, gas or hybrid, and front- or all-wheel drive. This guide has 5 chapters: one for each generation, one for the hybrid across both, one for the e:FCEV, and the checklist to use before you buy.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2023 or later CR-V Hybrid, with 204 hp and an EPA rating of up to 40 mpg combined with front-wheel drive.
- **Best value:** a 2020–2022 CR-V EX with the 1.5-liter turbo. Honda Sensing is standard, and it has 39.2 cubic feet of cargo space behind the rear seat.
- **Best for snow on a budget:** a 2020–2022 CR-V Hybrid. All-wheel drive is standard, and it's rated at 38 mpg.
- **Most cargo space:** a 2023 or later gas CR-V EX or EX-L, with 76.5 cubic feet with the rear seat folded.
- **Only if you live near a station:** the 2025–2026 CR-V e:FCEV, with 29 miles of electric range and 270 miles in all.
- **Think twice if** you want a spare tire. The CR-V Hybrids don't have one.
