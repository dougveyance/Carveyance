Toyota and Subaru developed this car together, and Subaru builds it, alongside its BRZ twin. In America, the first generation was sold as the Scion FR-S until Scion closed, then as the Toyota 86 for 2017 through 2020. There was no 2021 car. The second generation arrived for 2022 as the GR86, with a bigger 2.4-liter engine.

Both are light, rear-drive, 4-seat coupes with a manual or an automatic, and both are built for people who like to drive. That's the appeal, and it's the main thing to check on a used one. This guide has 2 chapters: the review, and the checklist to use before you buy.

## Quick picks

- **Best value:** a stock, cared-for 2017–2020 86 manual.
- **Best all-rounder:** a GR86 manual with a clean history. The bigger engine fixes the old car's soft midrange.
- **For commuting:** an automatic of either generation. It's less engaging, but easier in traffic and better on fuel.
- **Think twice if** you need a quiet cabin, a smooth ride or a usable back seat. A Mazda MX-5 is the open-top alternative, and a used GR Supra is the faster one.

## At a glance

| Engine | 2.0-liter flat-4 | 2.4-liter flat-4 |
| Power | 205 hp manual, 200 hp automatic | 228 hp |
| Torque | 156 lb-ft at 6,400 rpm (manual) | 184 lb-ft at 3,700 rpm |
| Transmission | 6-speed manual or 6-speed automatic | 6-speed manual or 6-speed automatic |

| EPA combined | 24 mpg manual, 27 automatic | 22 mpg manual, 25 automatic (2022) |
