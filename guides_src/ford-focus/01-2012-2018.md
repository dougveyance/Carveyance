The third-generation Focus was One Ford's masterpiece — a single global compact, built in Wayne, Michigan, good enough to be the world's best-selling car in 2012. The steering and chassis embarrassed the Corolla, the Titanium hatch played premium, and the range ran from a 123-hp 1.0 EcoBoost 3-cylinder to the Focus Electric, Ford's first modern EV. Then the gearbox happened.

## The PowerShift rule

The volume automatic was the PowerShift, a dry-clutch dual-clutch unit that shuddered, hesitated and slipped its way into thousands of lemon-law claims. Ford cycled through at least 6 clutch-pack and control-module revisions and endless software updates without ever issuing a recall. The cars never got dangerous — just infuriating — and the fix pattern means a well-documented late car with a replaced clutch pack can serve fine. But the clean answer costs nothing: **manual-gearbox cars were never affected.** Buy 3 pedals and the entire saga belongs to someone else.

## Engines and the oddballs

The 160-hp 2.0 GDI is the right default — honest pace, 31 mpg combined, and sweet with the 5-speed. The 1.0 EcoBoost (2015-on) adds 1 mpg and a charming 3-cylinder thrum but needs its turbo oil history. The **Focus Electric** is the quiet bargain of early EVs: 76 miles of range at launch, 115 with the 33.5-kWh battery from 2017, 107 MPGe, and used prices in the single-digit thousands for a city runabout. The sedan is cheaper; the hatch is the one worth owning. US production ended May 4, 2018 when Ford quit sedans, and the Chinese-built Focus Active crossover meant to follow died over tariffs — so this is the last American Focus there will ever be.
