A Pilot is usually a family's main car, and many have towed, done school runs and covered long road trips. That's no reason to avoid one, but the records, the recalls and the condition of the cabin matter more than the badge on the tailgate. Bring the people who'll sit in the third row, and fold every seat yourself.

## First, know which one it is

Decode the VIN to confirm the year, the trim and whether it has all-wheel drive. On a 2016–2020 Pilot, the trim tells you the gearbox: the LX, EX and EX-L have the 6-speed, the Touring and Elite the 9-speed. On a 2023 or later Pilot, count the seats and check whether the second-row middle seat is in the car or stowed under the cargo floor.

## The test drive

Start the engine from cold and listen for knocking or rattles, then again once it's warm. A knock that rises with engine speed on a 2016 or 2018–2019 Pilot needs a dealer's look under the connecting rod bearing recall. Drive in stop-and-go traffic and at highway speed: shifts should be smooth, with no flare, clunk or shudder, and the 6-speed shouldn't judder as it settles into a cruise. Reverse and park slowly, and make a few tight turns on an all-wheel-drive car, listening for groaning. Check the steering for heavy or sticky spots on a 2023–2025 car.

## Engine and drivetrain checks

The V6 has a timing belt, due every 7 years or 105,000 miles, usually with the water pump. Ask for proof it was done, or price it in. Look for records of transmission fluid changes and, on all-wheel-drive Pilots, rear differential fluid. On a Pilot that has towed, check the hitch, the wiring and the brakes. Look for oil leaks around the engine.

## Cabin and body checks

Fold, slide and recline every seat in all 3 rows, and check the latches. Test the climate controls at the rear vents, the child-seat anchors, the USB ports, the cameras and the power tailgate. Look under the cargo floor for water and missing tools, and check the hood latch closes firmly. On a car from a salt-belt state, have a mechanic look at the rear subframe.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2016–2019:** 21V932. The hood latch striker can separate, letting the hood open while driving.
- **2016 and 2018–2019 (some engines):** 23V751. A connecting rod bearing may wear and seize, damaging the engine. The fix is an inspection, and a repair or a new engine if needed.
- **2016–2022, sold in 22 salt-belt states and DC:** 26V365. The rear subframe can corrode at the suspension mounts. Honda installs a reinforcement kit; owner letters went out in November 2026.
- **2016–2021:** 23V858. The fuel pump may fail, by VIN.
- **2018:** 18V221, the power front seats. **2019–2020:** 19V784, front frame upper members that may not have been welded properly.
- **2019–2022:** 20V439, 20V440 and 23V431, rearview camera and software faults.
- **2017–2022:** 26V332, and 24V064 for 2020–2022. The passenger seat weight sensor can fail, so the air bag may not work correctly.
- **2021–2022:** 23V458. A brake master cylinder fastener may not be secured.
- **2023–2024:** 23V735 (a loose ball bearing in some steering racks) and 24V859 (seat fasteners).
- **2023–2025:** 24V900 (fuel filler neck), 25V031 (fuel injection software), 25V391 (brake pedal pivot pin) and 26V721 (power steering software).
- **2025:** 25V056, a third-row seat belt routed incorrectly. **2026:** 26V424, rear subframe bolts not tightened properly.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title, flood damage or a musty smell you can't explain. Walk away from an engine knock the seller can't explain, a transmission that slips or bangs into gear, an all-wheel-drive system that binds in tight turns, and any seller who won't allow an independent inspection.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and an average that mixes a 2017 LX with a 2023 Elite tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same generation, trim, drive and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Toyota Highlander or Kia Telluride of the same age, and take the cost of a due timing belt, tires and brakes off the price.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Pilot past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for the transmission and the all-wheel-drive system, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2023 or later EX-L or Touring with all-wheel drive and its recalls done.
- **Best value:** a 2019–2022 EX-L with records.
- **Most flexible seating:** a 2023 or later Touring or Elite with the removable middle seat.
- **Best for dirt roads:** a 2023 or later TrailSport.
