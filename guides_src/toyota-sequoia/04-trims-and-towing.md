Toyota sells the Sequoia with a long menu of names, and each one points it at a different job. None of them changes the basics: it's big, it seats 7 or 8 and it tows. Decide what the Sequoia has to do before you choose a badge, because the most expensive grades add weight, complexity and repair costs more than capability.

## The grades

| Grade | Years | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| SR5 | 2017–2026 | The value pick. 8 seats in the second generation |
| TRD Sport | 2018–2022 | Street styling, Bilstein shocks and thicker anti-roll bars |
| Limited | 2017–2026 | Comfort. Leather from 2026 on the third generation |
| Nightshade Edition | 2021–2022 | A Limited with black wheels and trim |
| Platinum | 2017–2026 | Luxury, with captain's chairs |
| TRD Pro | 2020–2026 | Off-road: Fox shocks, a front skid plate and 4x4 only |
| Capstone | 2023–2026 | The flagship, with 22-inch wheels and its own leather |
| 1794 Edition | 2025–2026 | Luxury in a western style, with American walnut trim |

## SR5 and Limited: start here

On a used Sequoia, the SR5 and Limited are where most of the value is. They have the same engine, the same frame and, on the third generation, the same towing hardware as the expensive grades, without paying for features that cost a lot to fix when they're out of warranty. On the 2023–2026 model, a Limited adds most of the comfort a family will use day to day.

## TRD Pro: for people who go off-road

The TRD Pro is more than a badge. The 2020–2022 version has Fox internal bypass shocks, a front skid plate and 4x4 as standard. The 2023–2026 version keeps Fox internal bypass shocks and has a selectable locking rear differential, Multi-Terrain Select, Crawl Control, a 1/4-inch aluminum front skid plate and black 18-inch wheels. It's a real trail truck, and its off-road tires and shocks give up a little quiet on the freeway. Look underneath for scrapes and dents before you buy one, and pay for a TRD Pro only if you'll use it.

## Platinum, 1794 Edition and Capstone: comfort first

The top grades are about the cabin. On the 2023–2026 model, the Platinum adds captain's chairs, a JBL sound system, a panoramic roof and a head-up display, and from 2025 massaging front seats. The 1794 Edition has similar equipment in a western-themed cabin with American walnut trim. The Capstone adds 22-inch wheels, power running boards, acoustic glass in the front doors and its own leather, semi-aniline through 2025 and a textured leather from 2026. On a used one, test every seat function, screen, camera and power running board, because they're expensive to repair.

## Towing: the headline is only the start

[[chart:tow]]

The 2017–2022 Sequoia is rated at up to 3,357 kg (7,400 lb). The 2023–2026 is rated at up to 4,318 kg (9,520 lb), but only as a 2-wheel-drive SR5. At launch, Toyota's ratings ran down to 4,073 kg (8,980 lb) for a 4x4 Capstone, and the TRD Pro was rated at 4,091 kg (9,020 lb). Every extra feature, and the 4x4 system itself, takes something away.

Payload matters as much as the trailer number. Before you buy for towing:

- Read the door-jamb label for the vehicle's payload, and the owner's manual for its exact tow rating.
- Remember that the trailer's tongue weight, usually 10% to 15% of the trailer's weight, comes out of the payload, along with every passenger and everything in the back. With a full third row, that adds up fast.
- Test the trailer brake controller, the wiring and, on 2023–2026 models, the Tow Tech features such as Trailer Backup Guide, standard on the TRD Pro, Platinum, 1794 Edition and Capstone from 2025.
- Check the hitch receiver for wear, and ask what the previous owner towed.

A weight-distributing hitch improves stability. It doesn't raise the vehicle's ratings.

## When the old one wins

The 2017–2022 Sequoia gives up 961 kg (2,120 lb) of towing, but it's cheaper to buy and carries much more inside. If your trailer is light and your cargo area is full, a well-kept 2020–2022 Limited can be the better tool than a new TRD Pro. See [the space chapter](../space/).

## Our picks

- **Family hauler:** a 2020–2022 SR5 or Limited, or a 2024–2026 Limited.
- **Towing near the limit:** a 2023–2026 SR5 or Limited with 2-wheel drive, checked against the door label.
- **Comfort:** a 2023–2026 Platinum, which has most of what the 1794 Edition and Capstone offer.
- **Off-road:** a TRD Pro, if you'll use it.

::: curiosity Named for the Biggest Tree
Toyota named the Sequoia after California's giant sequoias, the largest trees on Earth by volume. The biggest of all, the General Sherman Tree in Sequoia National Park, holds 52,508 cubic feet of wood, according to the National Park Service. That's about 437 times the old Sequoia's 120.1 cu ft of cargo space. By coincidence, 437 is also the new Sequoia's horsepower.
:::
