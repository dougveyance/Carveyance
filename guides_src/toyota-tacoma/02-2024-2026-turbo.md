The 2024 Tacoma was a complete redesign, not a facelift. It moved to TNGA-F, the boxed ladder frame Toyota shares with the Tundra, Sequoia and Land Cruiser, and it swapped both old engines for a turbocharged 2.4-liter 4-cylinder. Most versions also traded the leaf springs for a coil-spring rear suspension. The result is a Tacoma that pulls harder, rides better and finally has a cabin from this decade.

It's still a truck. Big all-terrain tires and off-road shocks don't make it a crossover, and the Double Cab's back seat is still on the small side. But for most people, this is the better Tacoma to live with every day.

## The engine and gearbox

The turbo comes in 3 tunes. The base SR makes 228 hp and 243 lb-ft. Every other gas Tacoma with the 8-speed automatic makes 278 hp and 317 lb-ft, the same power as the old V6 with 52 more lb-ft, and that torque arrives at far lower revs. A 6-speed manual is still offered on some 4x4 grades, rated at 270 hp and 310 lb-ft.

[[chart:torque]]

It shows on the road. Car and Driver ran a 2024 TRD Off-Road to 60 mph in 7.0 seconds, about 0.6 second quicker than the V6 version it tested, even though the new truck weighed 2,175 kg (4,794 lb). Edmunds measured a TRD Sport at 7.6 seconds and a lighter TRD PreRunner at 7.1.

The EPA rates the turbo at up to 23 mpg combined as a 4x2, 21 as an SR or SR5 4x4 and 20 as a TRD Sport or TRD Off-Road 4x4. The manual 4x4 is rated at 20. That's a modest gain over the old V6, and in real driving, speed, tire size and load matter more than the badge.

## Cabs, beds and suspension

There are 2 cabs. The Double Cab has 4 doors and a 5- or 6-foot bed. The XtraCab, which replaced the old Access Cab, has 2 doors, 2 seats and a 6-foot bed, and it's offered only on the SR, SR5 and TRD PreRunner. There's no back seat at all, just storage space behind the front seats.

Pay attention to the suspension. The SR, SR5 XtraCab and TRD PreRunner keep leaf springs, while most other versions use the new multi-link coil setup, and the Limited adds adaptive dampers. A coil-spring truck rides noticeably better with an empty bed, so check what's under the one you're looking at.

Properly equipped, the turbo tows up to 2,948 kg (6,500 lb), on an SR5 or TRD PreRunner XtraCab. Most Double Cabs are rated at 2,903 kg (6,400 lb), and the SR at 1,588 kg (3,500 lb). Maximum payload is about 775 kg (1,709 lb), on a TRD Off-Road.

## Living with it

Toyota Safety Sense 3.0 is standard, with automatic emergency braking and adaptive cruise control. Lower grades get an 8-inch touchscreen and higher ones a 14-inch screen, with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto on both. The changes since launch are small. For 2025, the TRD Pro and Trailhunter got red front tow hooks, and a fourth auxiliary switch became available. For 2026, the TRD grades wear a black Toyota badge, 18-inch TRD wheels with all-terrain tires are a new option and the SR XtraCab gets a standard tow hitch. Prices start at $32,145, before delivery.

## What to check before you buy

This generation is too new to have a long-term reliability record, so a used one needs its campaign history checked more carefully than an old V6 would. Run the VIN for these:

- **Rear brake hoses (25V-058):** mud and dirt packed inside the rear wheels can damage the hoses and cause a fluid leak. The recall started with about 106,000 4x4s and was expanded to roughly 222,000 2024–2025 trucks with 16-inch brakes.
- **TRD Off-Road shocks:** in August 2026, Toyota recalled about 48,000 2024–2025 TRD Off-Roads because corrosion can let the Bilstein shocks' external reservoirs break off. Owner letters were due by early October 2026.
- **Front driveshafts (25V-656):** about 6,000 2025 4x4s, gas and hybrid, have front CV joints that can deform and break.
- **Instrument panel software (25V-595):** certain 2024–2025 trucks may start without the speedometer and some warning lights showing.

Then test the truck. Let it start cold, drive it on rough pavement and at freeway speed, and make sure the 8-speed shifts cleanly. Check that 4-high and 4-low engage, and look underneath for trail damage. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

The best buy is an SR5 or TRD Off-Road with the 278-hp automatic, ideally a coil-spring Double Cab with every recall done. The TRD PreRunner is the lightest and quickest, but its leaf springs make it a working truck first. Skip the base SR unless you want a fleet-spec truck at a low price, because its 228 hp and 1,588-kg (3,500-lb) tow rating are a big step down.

Buy this Tacoma if you want the torque, ride and technology the old one never had. If you want even more torque, the hybrid is in [the next chapter](../hybrid/).
