For 2020 the Explorer moved to the rear-drive CD6 platform it shares with the Lincoln Aviator — the first rear-drive-based Explorer since the body-on-frame era ended in 2010. The payoff is balance: better weight distribution, a real performance flagship, and towing up to 2,404 kg (5,300 lb) with the tow package. Every version runs the 10-speed automatic, and the Police Interceptor Utility variant remains what you see in your mirror in all 50 states.

## The engines, and the one that left

The **2.3 EcoBoost**, 300 hp, is the volume engine and the correct default: mid-6-second 0–60 in instrumented tests, 23 mpg combined with all-wheel drive, 24 with rear drive. The **3.0 EcoBoost V6** comes 2 ways — 365 hp in the 2020–21 Platinum, 400 hp in the ST. The **3.3 hybrid**, 318 hp and 25 mpg combined, was quietly dropped from the retail lineup after 2024, which does used shoppers a favor: a capable, efficient 3-row the market has already stopped talking about. There is no diesel and no plug-in in the US lineup.

## 2020–2024 vs the 2025 refresh

The 2024-and-earlier trucks carry the launch design and the wide trim ladder — XLT, Limited, Timberline, King Ranch, Platinum, ST. The 2025 refresh brought a new face, a new dash with a 13.2-inch screen, and a ruthless lineup cut to Active, ST-Line, Platinum and ST; 2026 adds the off-road Tremor and a price-leader Active 100A package at $38,465, with the ST topping out at $56,905 with all-wheel drive. BlueCruise hands-free highway driving is optional from ST-Line up and standard on ST and Platinum. Mechanically, 2020 through 2026 is one continuous story, so buy on condition and equipment, not model year.

## On a test drive

Early 2020–2021 builds had teething issues worth checking by VIN: a rear-axle-bolt recall, camera-software campaigns and some 10-speed harshness complaints, most addressed under warranty or recall since. Listen for driveline clunk on throttle lift, confirm the big screen and cameras behave, and on any AWD truck check the tires for even wear — a set of 4 on a 20-inch Explorer is real money.
