The third-generation Highlander arrived for 2014, and for 2017 Toyota gave it a thorough refresh that it billed as "the year of more." More power, more safety equipment and more choice, all in a package that already did the family-hauling job well. These last 3 years of the generation are the ones to shop, and they're still sensible family SUVs today.

## What changed for 2017

The big news was under the hood. A new 3.5-liter V6 with both direct and port fuel injection made 295 hp and 263 lb-ft of torque, up from 270 hp, and it was paired with a new 8-speed automatic in place of the old 6-speed. Most V6 grades also gained automatic engine stop-start to save fuel. The base LE with front-wheel drive kept a 2.7-liter 4-cylinder with 185 hp and a 6-speed automatic.

The Highlander Hybrid also got the new V6, combined with electric motors for 306 hp, and it kept all-wheel drive as standard. It added cheaper LE and XLE grades below the Limited and Limited Platinum, which made the hybrid a far easier sell.

Every 2017 Highlander got Toyota Safety Sense P as standard: a pre-collision system with pedestrian detection, lane departure alert, automatic high beams and radar cruise control. The Limited Platinum added a Bird's Eye View camera. The gas Highlander came as an LE, LE Plus, XLE, Limited and Limited Platinum, plus a new SE with a sport-tuned suspension and 19-inch wheels, all with front-wheel or all-wheel drive. Prices started at $30,630 for a 4-cylinder LE, about $42,100 in 2026 dollars.

The EPA rated the front-drive V6 at 23 mpg combined and the all-wheel-drive V6 at 22, a 2-mpg gain over the 2016 model. The 4-cylinder, despite having 110 fewer horsepower, was rated at 22, so it saves nothing at the pump. The hybrid was rated at 29 mpg combined as an LE and 28 in the other grades.

Car and Driver ran a 2017 V6 XLE with all-wheel drive from 0 to 60 mph in 7.0 seconds and stopped it from 70 mph in 181 feet. Its test car weighed 2,068 kg (4,560 lb). A Hybrid Limited it tested weighed 2,219 kg (4,891 lb), stopped from 70 mph in 183 feet and averaged 23 mpg in Car and Driver's mixed driving.

## How it drives

The 2017–2019 Highlander is quiet, soft and easy to drive. The V6 is the heart of it: smooth, responsive and strong enough to make light work of a full load, and the 8-speed shifts smoothly. The 4-cylinder, by contrast, is overmatched in a vehicle this heavy and isn't worth seeking out. The hybrid is quick for its size, since its electric motors add to the V6's pull, and it's the quietest of the 3 around town.

Inside, the cabin is roomy in the first 2 rows, and the third row is best kept for children. Behind it there's about 14 cu ft of cargo space. Toyota added 4 more USB ports for 2017. There's no Apple CarPlay or Android Auto, though; both arrived with the 2020 model.

## What to check

These Highlanders are now 7 to 10 years old, so maintenance history and condition matter more than any model-year quirk.

- **Hybrid oil cooler pipe (campaign 19TC04):** About 30,000 2017–2019 Highlander Hybrids built from late October 2016 to late January 2019 can develop a small engine oil leak from the oil cooler pipe because of a manufacturing issue. Left alone, it can lead to abnormal engine noise, a check engine light and a low oil warning. Dealers replace the pipe free of charge. In California, a car that hasn't had the campaign done can't be re-registered.
- **The V6:** Listen for ticking at a cold start and look for oil seeping from the engine. Check that the stop-start system restarts the engine smoothly.
- **The hybrid:** Have a shop that knows Toyota hybrids scan the system for stored codes and check the battery's health. Make sure the battery cooling intake isn't blocked.
- **Everything else:** Look for rust underneath if the car spent winters on salted roads, uneven tire wear and signs of water in the cargo area.

Run the VIN on [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask the seller for proof that recall and campaign work was done.

## Which one to buy

- **V6 LE Plus or XLE:** the best balance of equipment and price. Skip the 4-cylinder LE.
- **SE:** the sportiest-looking and firmest-riding version.
- **Limited or Limited Platinum:** the most comfortable, with heated and ventilated front seats.
- **Hybrid LE or XLE:** the efficient choice, with all-wheel drive and more power than the V6.

## The verdict

A 2017–2019 Highlander V6 or Hybrid is a dependable, comfortable family SUV that does nothing badly. You give up the newer car's phone integration and some cargo space, but you keep a superb V6. Find one with records, and make sure the oil cooler campaign is done on any hybrid.
