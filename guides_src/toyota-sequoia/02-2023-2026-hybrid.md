For 2023, the Sequoia moved to Toyota's new TNGA-F frame, shared with the Tundra and the Land Cruiser, and production moved from Princeton, Indiana, to Toyota's truck plant in San Antonio, Texas. Every one has the same powertrain: the i-FORCE MAX, a twin-turbo V6 with an electric motor between the engine and a 10-speed automatic. It's the most powerful Sequoia ever, and by a wide margin the most efficient.

It's also less practical for a certain kind of family. That trade is covered in [the space chapter](../space/).

## How it drives

The hybrid makes 437 hp and 583 lb-ft, 182 lb-ft more than the old V8, and it makes its peak torque at just 2,400 rpm. It feels muscular from a standstill and effortless with a trailer behind. Car and Driver timed a 2023 Capstone to 60 mph in 5.6 seconds, and Hagerty lists one at 2,805 kg (6,185 lb).

[[chart:torque]]

The new Sequoia rides on a solid rear axle with coil springs, with load-leveling rear air suspension and adaptive dampers available. It's well controlled for something this big, but it's still a large, heavy body-on-frame SUV, and tight parking garages will remind you. Properly equipped, it tows up to 4,318 kg (9,520 lb), about 29% more than the old one.

The EPA rates it at 22 mpg combined with 2-wheel drive and 20 mpg with 4x4, including the TRD Pro. That's 6 to 7 mpg better than the V8. It isn't a plug-in, and it won't feel like a Prius at the pump, but it cuts a typical fuel bill by about 30%.

## Living with it

The cabin is a big step up. An 8-inch touchscreen is standard and a 14-inch screen is available, the SR5 already has a 12.3-inch digital instrument display, and Toyota Safety Sense 2.5 is standard. The third row slides 6 inches, so you can trade legroom for cargo space, and an adjustable cargo shelf helps organize the back.

Changes since launch are small but worth knowing. For 2025, a 1794 Edition with American walnut trim joined the range, massaging front seats arrived as standard on the 1794 Edition, Platinum and Capstone, and a wireless trailer camera became available. For 2026, power-folding third-row seats became standard on every grade, the Limited moved to leather, the Capstone got a new interior and a rear seat reminder was added. The 2023 Sequoia started at $58,300, about $64,300 in 2026 dollars. The 2026 starts at $64,025, before delivery, so in real terms the price has barely moved.

## What to check before you buy

This generation is too new for a long-term reliability record, so the campaign history matters. The Sequoia isn't part of the Tundra's engine debris recalls, which cover the gas-only Tundra, Lexus LX and GX. It does share several other Tundra recalls. Run the VIN for these:

- **2023:** A spare tire chain that may have been poorly welded, on about 1,400 vehicles (23V-468).
- **2023–2024:** Transmission software that can let the vehicle creep forward in Neutral (24V-125), and a rear tow hitch cover that can come loose, on about 43,000 Sequoias (24V-640).
- **2024:** Passenger-side tires that may have inner sidewall damage (24V-767).
- **2023–2025:** Rearview camera software that may not show an image (25V-657).

Then test it. Make sure it starts quietly in Ready mode with no hybrid warnings, check that the 10-speed shifts smoothly and test every drive mode. Slide and fold the third row, check the cargo shelf and test every camera. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

The best buy is a 2024–2026 SR5 or Limited with its recalls done. It has nearly all the capability of the expensive grades for much less. A 2023 is fine if the price reflects its first-year status and the hitch cover and spare tire work is done. Pay for a TRD Pro if you'll use it off-road, and for a Platinum, 1794 Edition or Capstone if the cabin matters most.

Buy this Sequoia if you tow, want modern safety equipment and lower fuel bills, and don't need a giant flat cargo floor. If you do, read [the space chapter](../space/) before you decide.
