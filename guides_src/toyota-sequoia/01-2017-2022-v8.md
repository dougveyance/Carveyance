The second-generation Sequoia went on sale for 2008 and stayed until 2022, so by the years in this chapter it was already a veteran. It's what happens when a company decides that children, dogs, camping gear and a cooler should all travel together without negotiation. It's huge, its V8 is smooth and relaxed, and its cargo hold is one of the biggest of any SUV. It is not subtle, and it's not cheap to run.

## How it drives

Every Sequoia in these years uses Toyota's 5.7-liter V8, with 381 hp and 401 lb-ft, and a 6-speed automatic. The power builds smoothly, without waiting for a turbo, and the truck feels unhurried at highway speed and on long grades. MotorWeek timed a 2020 TRD Pro to 60 mph in 7.0 seconds, and that truck weighs about 2,715 kg (5,985 lb).

[[chart:power]]

Unlike most body-on-frame SUVs, this Sequoia has independent suspension at all 4 corners. That's why its ride is calmer than you'd expect and its floor is low enough for a big cargo area. It still feels like a large truck, with slow steering and plenty of body roll on winding roads. Properly equipped, it tows up to 3,357 kg (7,400 lb), and the 4x4 versions add a low range for loose ground.

## Living with it

The cabin seats 8, or 7 with second-row captain's chairs, and the third row has 35.3 inches of legroom, more than the new Sequoia offers even with its seat slid back. Fold both rear rows and there's 120.1 cu ft of space behind the front seats, about 38% more than the 2023 and later Sequoia. That alone keeps the old one in demand.

The best years are the last ones. The 2018 update brought new styling, LED headlights, blind spot monitoring and standard Toyota Safety Sense P, with automatic emergency braking and adaptive cruise control. For 2020, every Sequoia gained Apple CarPlay, Android Auto and Amazon Alexa, along with push-button start, and the off-road TRD Pro arrived. A Nightshade Edition with black trim joined for 2021.

Fuel is the catch. The EPA rates a 4x4 at 14 mpg combined (13 city, 17 highway) and a 2-wheel-drive version at 15. At 15,000 miles a year and $4 a gallon, 14 mpg is about $4,286 of fuel.

## What to check before you buy

Get it on a lift. Look for rust on the frame, brake lines and exhaust, and check the differential and transfer case seals on 4x4s. With 4 independent corners, there are more bushings and ball joints to wear, so listen for clunks over bumps and look for uneven tire wear. Check for coolant leaks, and start the engine cold to listen for ticking or exhaust leaks. Test every power seat, the third row and the liftgate.

Then run the VIN. Certain 2018–2020 Sequoias are part of Toyota's fuel pump recall (20V-012 and 20V-682), because the low-pressure pump can fail and stall the engine. Toyota also recalled certain 2008–2022 Sequoias because the power steering gear can leak and suddenly lose its assist (21V-920). Ask for proof the repairs were done. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

The sweet spot is a documented 2020–2022 SR5 or Limited: the phone connection and safety equipment on the proven V8, with all that space. A 2018–2019 makes sense when the price reflects the missing phone connection. A 2017 also lacks the safety equipment, so it should be cheaper still. Pay TRD Pro money only if you'll use its Fox shocks off-road.

Buy this Sequoia if you fill the cargo area and the third row at the same time and can live with the fuel bills. If you tow heavy or want the newer technology, read [the 2023–2026 chapter](../2023-2026-hybrid/).

::: curiosity The Window That Goes Down
The second-generation Sequoia's liftgate has its own power rear window, with auto up and down and jam protection, so you can drop the glass without opening the whole tailgate. It's handy for sliding in long items like lumber or fishing rods, or for a dog who wants to look backward. The 4Runner is famous for the same trick.
:::
