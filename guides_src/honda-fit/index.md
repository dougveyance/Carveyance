The Fit is a 5-door subcompact hatchback, sold as the Jazz in most other countries. The last one sold in America, the third generation, went on sale in April 2014 as a 2015 model and was the first US Fit built in North America, at Honda's plant in Celaya, Mexico. Every one has a 1.5-liter 4-cylinder with up to 130 hp, front-wheel drive, and a 6-speed manual or a CVT automatic.

What sets it apart is the space. Honda put the fuel tank under the front seats, so the rear seat cushions flip up and the seat backs fold almost flat. That's Honda's Magic Seat, and it gives a car about 160 inches long 16.6 cubic feet of cargo space behind the rear seat and 52.7 cubic feet with it folded.

In July 2020, Honda said the 2020 would be the last Fit for North America, and the HR-V, built in the same Mexican plant, became its cheapest model. A newer Fit is still sold in other markets, but not here, so every Fit on sale in the US is now a used one.

This guide has 3 chapters: the 2017–2020 Fit, the choice between the manual and the CVT, and the checklist to use before you buy.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2018–2020 EX with the CVT, for standard Honda Sensing, Apple CarPlay and Android Auto.
- **Best to drive:** a 2018 or 2019 Sport or EX with the 6-speed manual. The 2018–2019 EX is the only manual Fit with Honda Sensing.
- **Most economical:** an LX with the CVT, rated at 36 mpg combined.
- **Cheapest way in:** a 2017 LX with service records.
- **Think twice if** you need all-wheel drive, which no Fit had, or a lot of power. A Civic or HR-V of the same age may suit you better.
