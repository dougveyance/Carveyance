The Avalon was Toyota's flagship sedan in the US for nearly 3 decades: bigger and softer than a Camry, built in Kentucky and aimed at buyers who wanted luxury-car comfort without a luxury badge. The last 6 years cover 2 generations. The 2017–2018 cars are the end of the fourth generation, with a 268-hp V6 or a 200-hp hybrid. The 2019 redesign brought a sharper body, a 301-hp V6 with an 8-speed automatic and a 215-hp hybrid rated at up to 44 mpg. Toyota ended production in August 2022 and replaced it with the Crown.

For a used buyer, the choices are the generation and the powertrain, and both make a big difference. This guide has 4 chapters: one for each generation, one on choosing between the V6 and the hybrid, and the checklist to use before you buy.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2019–2022 Avalon Hybrid XLE, rated at 44 mpg combined.
- **Best V6:** a 2019–2022 XLE or Limited, with 301 hp and Apple CarPlay.
- **Most luxury:** a 2019–2022 Limited, or the Touring if you want the adaptive suspension.
- **Cheapest way in:** a documented 2017–2018 XLE, with Toyota Safety Sense P as standard.
- **Think twice if** you need snow traction. All-wheel drive was offered for 2021 only, with a 205-hp 4-cylinder.

## At a glance

|  | 2017–2018 (fourth generation) | 2019–2022 (fifth generation) |
|---|---|---|
| V6 | 3.5-liter, 268 hp, 6-speed automatic | 3.5-liter, 301 hp, 8-speed automatic |
| Hybrid | 2.5-liter, 200 hp, CVT | 2.5-liter, 215 hp, CVT |
| All-wheel drive | Not offered | 2021 only: 2.5-liter 4-cylinder, 205 hp |
| EPA combined | V6 24 mpg, hybrid 40 | V6 25 to 26 mpg, hybrid 43 to 44, AWD 28 |
| Safety suite | Toyota Safety Sense P, standard | TSS-P; TSS 2.5 for 2022 |
| Apple CarPlay / Android Auto | Neither | CarPlay from 2019, Android Auto from 2021 |
| Trunk | 16.0 cu ft | 16.1 cu ft |
| Base price new | XLE $33,300 for 2017 ($45,700 in 2026 dollars) | XLE $35,650 for 2019 ($46,900 in 2026 dollars) |
