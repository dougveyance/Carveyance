A Ranger's first decision isn't the color or the trim. It's whether you need the 2019–2023 SuperCab's 6-foot bed, the newer truck's cabin, the stronger 2.7-liter V6 or the specialized Raptor. Write down the heaviest loaded trailer you'll pull and how many people will regularly ride in the back. If the numbers don't work, moving up to an F-150 may cost less than modifying the wrong truck. On the other hand, a Ranger is easier to live with in parking garages and tight driveways than a full-size pickup.

## Start underneath

Put the truck on a lift before you fall for its appearance package. Look for kinked frame rails, odd welds, fresh coatings hiding corrosion, crushed crossmembers and impact damage near the transfer case. Inspect the front suspension mounts, the steering, the axle seals, the brake lines and the rear differential. Rust isn't the same everywhere, so inspect the actual truck rather than judging by the state it's registered in. On a 4x4, all 4 tires should be the same size with similar wear.

## The road test

Start it cold and listen for rattles or a persistent misfire. Check for a smooth idle, then test gentle upshifts, hard acceleration, rolling downshifts and low-speed maneuvering once the transmission is hot. Judge the 10-speed by what this truck does, not by internet anecdotes either way. Brake firmly several times from road speed, check that the steering returns to center, and feel for vibration at highway speed. Test the heat and air conditioning, the mirrors, the windows and the cameras.

## 4x4 and towing audit

On a safe, low-traction surface, engage 4-wheel drive as the owner's manual describes, and don't lock a part-time system into 4-wheel drive on dry pavement. Check any rear locker and the drive modes. Photograph the payload label on the door jamb and match it to Ford's towing guide for that year and drive. Inspect the trailer wiring, the hitch and the connectors for corrosion, light faults or improvised splices. A truck that has towed a lot deserves a careful look at its fluids, brakes and cooling system.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2019:** 19V071, a shifter that can leave Park without the key or the brake. 19V366, shifter cable bracket fasteners that can loosen. 19V726 and 20V137, a blower motor that can short.
- **2019–2021 SuperCab:** 21V811, and 19V518 for 2019, a front passenger seat belt that may be assembled incorrectly. 21V765, rear seats whose fixed head restraints may not meet the standard on 2020–2021 SuperCabs.
- **2020:** 20V197, a shift cable clip that may let the transmission be in a different gear from the one selected.
- **2024–2026:** 25V541, side curtain air bags that can tear on the B-pillar during deployment; dealers install protective shields. 26V104, a trailer module that can lose communication, cutting a trailer's brake and turn-signal lights. 26V268, front seat height-adjust bolts.
- **2025–2026:** 26V343, an engine that may fail on a small number of trucks; dealers replace the long block.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and with Ford, and ask for proof that each repair was done. A recall for a model year doesn't mean every truck is affected, and an empty list online is never proof a VIN is clear.

## Documents to ask for

Ask for the maintenance history with dates and mileage, the original window sticker, all the keys, tire invoices, receipts for towing equipment and records of software updates and warranty repairs. If it has a lift or oversized tires, ask who installed them and whether the alignment was checked. Have a shop scan for trouble codes before anyone clears them. An independent pre-purchase inspection is especially worthwhile on a truck with off-road hardware or an uncertain towing history.

## Walk away if

The truck has a branded title, flood damage, a bent or patched frame, a transmission that slips or bangs into gear, a 4x4 system that won't engage, or towing wiring nobody can explain. Walk away from any seller who won't allow an independent inspection.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and an average that mixes a 2019 SuperCab XL with a 2026 Raptor tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same generation, cab, engine, drive and trim, with the same mileage and title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what trucks actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Toyota Tacoma, a Chevrolet Colorado or a Nissan Frontier of the same age.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Ranger past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for the turbocharged engines, the 10-speed and the 4-wheel-drive system, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best value:** a clean 2019–2023 XLT with its recalls done and verified maintenance.
- **Best for long loads:** a 2019–2023 SuperCab with the 6-foot bed.
- **Best all-rounder:** a 2024 or later XLT or Lariat with the 2.7-liter V6.
- **Fastest off-road:** a Ranger Raptor with a clean underside. Not for heavy towing.
