For 2021, Honda gave the Ridgeline a bigger, blockier grille, a new front bumper, a slightly wider track and twin exhaust outlets, mainly to make it look less like a Pilot and more like the pickups it competes with. Underneath, it's the same unibody truck, with the same 280-hp V6 and the 9-speed automatic that arrived for 2020. The big change for buyers is that every Ridgeline from 2021 has all-wheel drive.

## The drivetrain

[[chart:accel]]

Honda's i-VTM4 torque-vectoring all-wheel drive is standard, and the EPA rates the truck at 21 mpg combined, or 20 for the TrailSport on its all-terrain tires. Car and Driver ran a 2021 Sport with the HPD package to 60 mph in 6.2 seconds and a 2024 TrailSport in 6 flat. Towing stays at 2,268 kg (5,000 lb), and the bed, In-Bed Trunk and Dual-Action Tailgate carry over.

## The trims and packages

For 2021, the range was Sport, RTL, RTL-E and Black Edition, from $36,490 for the Sport ($45,300 in 2026 dollars) to $43,920 for the Black Edition ($54,500). A physical volume knob was added to the dashboard. The HPD package, developed with Honda's US racing company and installed after the truck leaves the line, added a different grille, black fender flares, bronze-colored wheels and bed graphics for $2,800 ($3,500 in 2026 dollars); it's cosmetic.

For 2024, every Ridgeline got a 7-inch digital instrument display and a 9-inch touchscreen with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, and the range became Sport, RTL, TrailSport and Black Edition. The TrailSport, at $44,980 ($48,200 in 2026 dollars), adds off-road suspension tuning, General Grabber A/T Sport all-terrain tires and steel underbody protection for the oil pan. It's more capable on a trail than the other trims, but the unibody layout still limits the Ridgeline on difficult terrain.

## The last of the line

In July 2026, Honda confirmed a third-generation Ridgeline to arrive within 2 years, still built in Alabama, and said production of the current truck would pause for the changeover. That makes the 2026 the last of this generation. Parts and service shouldn't be a worry: the current truck shares most of its parts with the Pilot, and the new one will be built alongside the Pilot, Passport and Odyssey.

## What to check before you buy

The rearview camera's tailgate wiring can fatigue and break on 2020–2024 trucks (24V321). A brake master cylinder fastener may not be secured on 2020–2023 trucks (23V458), the heated mirror pads can overheat on 2020–2021 trucks (23V174), and some 2023 trucks have a steering gearbox ball joint that may loosen (24V184). 2021–2023 trucks sold in salt-belt states are part of the rear subframe corrosion recall (26V365). The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A 2024 or later RTL is the best Ridgeline to buy: standard all-wheel drive, the bigger screen and wireless phone mirroring, and leather, without paying for the TrailSport's tires if you don't need them. A 2021–2023 Sport or RTL is the value pick. Buy a TrailSport for dirt roads and campsites, and skip the HPD package unless you like the look.
