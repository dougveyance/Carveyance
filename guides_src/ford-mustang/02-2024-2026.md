The seventh-generation Mustang went into production at Flat Rock, Michigan, in May 2023 as a 2024 model. Under the new skin it keeps the basic layout of the car it replaced: front engine, rear-wheel drive, independent rear suspension and a choice of fastback or convertible. What changed most is the cabin, which is now built around a digital instrument cluster and a 13.2-inch center screen, and the power, which went up on every engine.

The EcoBoost now makes 315 hp and comes only with the 10-speed automatic. The GT's 5.0-liter V8 makes 480 hp, or 486 with the active-valve exhaust, and you can still have it with a 6-speed manual. Above it, the Dark Horse makes 500 hp and comes with a Tremec 6-speed manual or the 10-speed automatic.

The Dark Horse SC and the GTD are covered in the special-editions chapter.

## How quick they are

[[chart:accel24]]

Car and Driver ran a 2024 GT Premium with the Performance Pack and the manual to 60 mph in 4.2 seconds, and a 2026 GT Premium with the automatic in 3.7. The Dark Horse took 4.1 seconds with the manual and the Handling Package. The automatic is quicker off the line, but every GT and Dark Horse still offers the choice.

## Fuel economy

[[chart:mpg26]]

The EcoBoost's 26 mpg combined is the best of any gasoline Mustang the EPA has rated. A GT automatic is rated at 19 mpg, a manual at 18, and the Dark Horse at 17 with either gearbox.

## Which one to buy

For most buyers, a GT is the Mustang to have: it's the one people picture, and the manual is still available. The EcoBoost makes sense if you want the shape and the cabin without the V8's fuel and insurance bills. The Dark Horse is for drivers who will use its extra grip and cooling on a track; on the road, a GT with the Performance Pack is most of the experience for less money. A 2024 is fine if its recall history is complete; several early recalls, including the rear suspension knuckles and the clutch line, applied only to 2024 cars.
