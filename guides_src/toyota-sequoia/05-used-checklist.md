A used Sequoia can be a very satisfying buy, but it's big, heavy and expensive to look after. A single set of tires, a neglected cooling system or a hidden towing mishap can wipe out the savings of a good price. Buy the condition and the records, not the badge.

## Start with what it has to carry

Take your car seats, your stroller and your tallest passenger to the test drive. Measure the third row and the cargo area with the seats where you'll really use them. The 2017–2022 Sequoia has far more room with the seats folded. The 2023–2026 has a sliding third row and a cargo shelf but a higher, smaller load space. If you tow, find your trailer's real loaded weight, not its brochure dry weight, and compare it with the door-jamb label of the vehicle you're looking at.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 | Second generation. 5.7-liter V8, 6-speed | No standard TSS-P, no Apple CarPlay or Android Auto. Power steering gear recall |
| 2018–2019 | New styling, standard TSS-P, TRD Sport | No Apple CarPlay or Android Auto. Fuel pump and power steering gear recalls |
| 2020–2022 | Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, TRD Pro. Nightshade from 2021 | Fuel pump recall (2020). Power steering gear recall |
| 2023 | All-new third generation. Hybrid, 10-speed | Spare tire chain, hitch cover, transmission and camera software recalls |
| 2024 | Small updates | Hitch cover, transmission software, tire and camera software recalls |
| 2025 | 1794 Edition, massaging seats | Camera software and load-label recalls |
| 2026 | Power-folding third row on every grade | Too new for long-term data |

## The V8 inspection, 2017–2022

Start underneath. Look for rust on the frame, brake lines and exhaust, oil or fluid leaks at the differentials and transfer case, and worn bushings, ball joints and shocks. With independent suspension at all 4 corners, there's more to wear, so listen for clunks and check the tires for uneven wear. Check the coolant level with the engine cold, look for seepage, then start it and listen for ticking or exhaust leaks.

On the road, the 6-speed should shift smoothly cold and warm. Brake firmly from highway speed and feel for pulsing. On a 4x4, make sure 4-high and 4-low engage where it's safe to test them. Test every power seat, the third row, the liftgate and its power window.

## The hybrid inspection, 2023–2026

Ask for the Toyota dealer service history and a full diagnostic scan. It should start quietly in Ready mode with no hybrid or engine warnings. Look for coolant or oil leaks around the turbochargers, and for signs of crash or flood damage near the battery. The 10-speed should shift smoothly, and every drive mode should work. Slide and fold the third row, check the cargo shelf for damage and test every camera, including the trailer camera if it has one.

On models with rear air suspension, check that the rear sits level and rises and lowers as it should. Toyota covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 150,000 miles from first use, and the other hybrid components for 8 years or 100,000 miles. Confirm the vehicle's first-use date so you know what's left.

## Recalls and campaigns: check by VIN

- **2008–2022:** Power steering gear that can leak and suddenly lose its assist (21V-920).
- **2018–2020:** Low-pressure fuel pump that can fail and stall the engine (20V-012 and 20V-682).
- **2023:** Spare tire chain that may have been poorly welded (23V-468).
- **2023–2024:** Transmission software that can let the vehicle creep forward in Neutral (24V-125), and a rear tow hitch cover that can come loose (24V-640).
- **2024:** Passenger-side tires that may have inner sidewall damage (24V-767).
- **2023–2025:** Rearview camera software that may not show an image (25V-657).
- **2023–2025 with accessories fitted before sale:** A load-capacity label that may list the wrong accessory weight (24V-548 and 26V-179). The fix is a new label.
- **Every year:** New recalls can appear at any time. Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask a Toyota dealer to check for open service campaigns too. Ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Walk away if

The seller won't allow an independent inspection, can't explain a warning light or has gaps in the title history. Walk away too if you find flood damage, heavy rust underneath, homemade trailer wiring, mileage that doesn't add up or signs of structural repairs nobody mentioned. There are plenty of Sequoias for sale, so there's no reason to take a chance on a seller who won't answer questions.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and a national average that mixes a 2017 SR5 with a 2025 Capstone tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, grade, drive and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what vehicles actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you, comparing a listing with similar ones nearby. If a listing says TRD Pro, Capstone or 1794 Edition, confirm it with the window sticker or the build data, then take the cost of any tires, overdue service or repairs off the price.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Sequoia past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Most space:** a documented 2020–2022 SR5 or Limited.
- **Best all-rounder:** a 2024–2026 SR5 or Limited with its recalls done.
- **Best for towing:** a 2023–2026 SR5 or Limited with 2-wheel drive, checked against the door label.
- **Off-road:** a TRD Pro from either generation, inspected underneath.
- **Budget pick:** a 2018–2019 SR5 with records.
