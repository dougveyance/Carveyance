For 2023, Toyota stopped designing the Prius around a wind tunnel and started designing it to be wanted. The result is low and sleek, with a steeply raked windshield and a roof that slopes all the way to the tail, and for the first time it's a car people buy partly for its looks. It's also the first Prius that isn't slow.

## How it drives

A 2.0-liter 4-cylinder replaces the old 1.8, and with its motor-generators the system makes 194 hp with front-wheel drive or 196 hp with all-wheel drive, up from 121. Car and Driver ran a front-drive Limited from 0 to 60 mph in 7.1 seconds, against 10.5 seconds for the previous generation, and Toyota claims 7.0 seconds for the AWD car. Merging onto a fast freeway is no longer something you plan for.

[[chart:power]]

The fuel economy survived. The EPA rates the front-drive LE at 57 mpg combined, the best of any Prius in this guide. The XLE and Limited ride on 19-inch wheels and are rated at 52; the LE with all-wheel drive is rated at 54, and the XLE and Limited with AWD at 49. The all-wheel-drive system works the way it did on the AWD-e, with an electric motor for the rear wheels and no driveshaft.

## Living with it

The price of the shape is space. Edmunds found the rear door openings smaller and the back seat short of headroom for anyone near 6 feet tall, and the steep rear window and thick rear pillars cut the view behind. CarMax lists 23.8 cubic feet of cargo space for the 2023, against 27.4 for the 2022. If you regularly carry adults in the back, sit there before you buy.

Every one has Toyota Safety Sense 3.0 and Toyota's newer multimedia system, with an 8-inch touchscreen, or 12.3 inches on the Limited. Some drivers find that the steering wheel hides part of the small instrument display, so check your seating position on the test drive.

## The years and grades

The fifth generation went on sale in January 2023 in LE, XLE and Limited grades. The LE started at $27,450 ($30,300 in 2026 dollars), the LE with AWD at $28,850, the XLE at $30,895 and the Limited at $34,465. The 2024 was a carryover, with the LE at $27,650. For 2025, Toyota added a Nightshade grade based on the XLE, with black 19-inch wheels and an exclusive paint color called Karashi, and the plug-in version changed its name from Prius Prime to Prius Plug-in Hybrid. For 2026, the LE started at $28,550.

[[chart:price]]

Adjusted for inflation, the 2023 LE cost less new than a 2017 Two, despite 73 more horsepower and a newer safety suite.

## What to check before you buy

Ask whether the rear door switch recall has been done. Water can short the switch, so an unlocked rear door may open unexpectedly. Toyota first replaced the switches under 24V-274, then expanded the recall as 26V-049 with a new remedy for 2023–2026 cars, including ones that were already repaired. Check the rear camera software recall (25V-744) too. The battery is covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles from the date the car was first sold, so most are still well within it. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

This is the best regular Prius Toyota has built: quicker, better to drive and nicer to look at, with no penalty at the pump. The LE is the one to buy unless you want the Limited's bigger screen and features, because it has the best fuel economy of any Prius. Choose AWD if you see snow, and test the back seat if anyone tall will ride there. If you want the fuel economy and a back seat with more room, a 2020–2022 car in [the previous chapter](../2017-2022/) still makes sense.
