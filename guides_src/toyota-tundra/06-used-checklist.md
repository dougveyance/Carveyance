Stories of Tundras running forever are part of why they're so popular used. They aren't a warranty. A truck that towed heavy without the right maintenance, spent winters on salted roads or was badly repaired after a crash can be expensive whatever its reputation. Start with what you need the truck to do, then let the records and an inspection decide.

## Pick the job first

For a simple, proven truck, look at a 2020–2021 5.7-liter V8. For the best daily driver and the most towing, look at a 2022–2026 gas V6, either a 2025–2026 or a 2022–2024 with its engine campaigns documented. For the most torque, look at an i-FORCE MAX hybrid. For the lowest price, a 2017–2019 5.7 still does the job. If you rarely tow or haul, ask whether you need a full-size truck at all.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2017–2019 | Second generation. 310-hp 4.6 or 381-hp 5.7 V8, 6-speed | No Apple CarPlay or Android Auto. Fuel pump recall (2018–2019). Power steering gear recall |
| 2020–2021 | 5.7 V8 only, Apple CarPlay and Android Auto | Fuel pump recall (2020). Power steering gear recall |
| 2022–2023 | All-new third generation. Twin-turbo V6 or hybrid, 10-speed | Engine debris recalls (gas). Transmission, reverse light and camera recalls |
| 2024 | Small updates | Engine debris recalls (gas), including 26V-320. Camera, tire and reverse light recalls |
| 2025 | Power tailgate, TRD Rally package | Camera and reverse light recalls |
| 2026 | IsoDynamic seats on the TRD Pro, bigger fuel tank | Too new for long-term data |

## Body, frame and history

Run a vehicle history report, but don't treat a clean one as proof. Look for uneven panel gaps, mismatched paint and fresh undercoating that could hide rust. On a lift, check the frame, brake lines, crossmembers and differential housings, and look for damage from poorly fitted recovery gear or lift kits. Check the bed and tailgate for heavy damage and the hitch for wear, and ask whether the truck was used commercially or for regular towing.

## Engine, transmission and 4WD

Start the truck cold. On a 5.7, listen for ticking or exhaust leaks. On a 2022–2024 gas V6, listen carefully for knocking at idle and under load, and walk away from any rough running the seller can't explain. On a hybrid, make sure it starts quietly in Ready mode with no warning lights. On any truck, check that the automatic shifts smoothly in stop-and-go traffic, that the brakes don't pulse and that 4-high and 4-low engage where it's safe to test them.

Ask for service records, especially oil changes and, on trucks that tow, transmission fluid and differential oil. Aftermarket tunes, intakes and exhausts can cause trouble with the warranty. Ask what's been changed.

## Recalls and campaigns: check by VIN

- **2007–2021:** Power steering gear that may have been built incorrectly and can leak.
- **2018–2020:** Low-pressure fuel pump that can fail and stall the engine (20V-012 and 20V-682).
- **2022:** Rear axle flange nuts that may need tightening (22V-445).
- **2022–2024 gas:** Machining debris that can damage the engine's main bearings (24V-381, 25V-767 and 26V-320). See [the engine recall chapter](../engine-recall/).
- **2022–2024:** Transmission software that can let the truck creep forward in Neutral (24V-125).
- **2022–2025:** Reverse lights that can fail when moisture gets in (25V-322), and rearview camera software that may not show an image (25V-657).
- **2024:** Passenger-side tires that may have inner sidewall damage (24V-767).
- **2024–2025 with the Panoramic View Monitor:** Parking assist software that may not show the rearview camera image (26V-038).
- **2023–2026 with accessories fitted before sale:** A load-capacity label that may list the wrong accessory weight (24V-548 and 26V-179). The fix is a new label.
- **Every year:** New recalls can appear at any time. Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask a Toyota dealer to check for open service campaigns too. Ask for proof that each repair was done.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and a national average that mixes $40,000 work trucks with $80,000 Capstones tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, grade, engine, cab, bed, drive and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what trucks actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you, comparing a listing with similar ones nearby. On a 2022–2024 gas truck, the engine recall status should be in the price.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the truck past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2025–2026 SR5 or Limited with the gas V6.
- **Best value:** a 2022–2024 SR5 or Limited with its engine campaigns completed and documented.
- **Simplest:** a documented 2020–2021 5.7-liter V8.
- **Strongest:** a 2022–2026 Limited or Platinum with the i-FORCE MAX.
- **Budget pick:** a 2017–2019 5.7-liter SR5 with records.
