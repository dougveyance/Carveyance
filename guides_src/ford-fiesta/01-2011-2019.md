The Mk6 Fiesta arrived for 2011 from Cuautitlán, Mexico with the best chassis in the subcompact class and a cabin that didn't apologize for the price. The 120-hp 1.6 Duratec did the work, hatch or sedan; the 2014 refresh added the trapezoidal face and the 1.0 EcoBoost SFE, a 123-hp turbo 3-cylinder rated 35 mpg combined. Half a million sold before Ford's 2018 sedan exit ended it — the 2019 cars were the sell-down, and US Fiesta production stopped in Mexico that August.

## The transmission rule

The PowerShift dual-clutch automatic is the whole story of buying one. Shudder, slip and delayed shifts drew class actions covering roughly 1.5 million Fiestas and Focuses, repeated clutch-pack and module revisions, warranty extensions in 2014 and 2019, and a settlement of at least $30 million — about $39 million in 2026 dollars. Ford never recalled it. A documented late car with a replaced clutch pack can serve; an undocumented shudderer is someone else's problem. Or skip the entire saga for free: **the 5-speed manual cars were never affected**, and they're the better drive anyway.

## On a test drive

Beyond the PowerShift test — gentle stop-and-go, feeling for shudder — these are simple cars. Check for completed recalls by VIN (door latches on several years), rust on northern cars, and on the 1.0 EcoBoost, oil-change receipts: the little turbo forgives nothing. Prices run from $3,000 beaters to $8,000 gems, and with this many sold, patience always finds the manual, 1-owner, documented car worth having.
