A used Crown or Crown Signia is a nearly new car, and many still have factory warranty left. That makes the job simpler than with an older car, but not automatic: confirm the recalls, check the condition carefully, and make sure the price reflects the grade and options you're actually getting.

## First, know which one it is

Confirm the model: a Crown sedan or a Crown Signia SUV. Then the grade, because on the Crown it decides the powertrain: the XLE, Limited and Nightshade have the 236-hp hybrid, and only the Platinum has the 340-hp Hybrid MAX. Look for the Hybrid MAX badge, or check the window sticker or a VIN decoder.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | Crown returns: XLE, Limited, Platinum Hybrid MAX | Blank instrument display (25V-595, over-the-air fix). Load label on some cars (24V-548) |
| 2024 | Crown carryover | Blank instrument display (25V-595, over-the-air fix). Rear camera software (25V-744). Load label on some cars (24V-548) |
| 2025 | Crown: Nightshade, more standard equipment. First Crown Signia | Crown and Signia: blank instrument display (25V-595), rear camera software (25V-744). Crown: load label on some cars (24V-548). Signia: label (26V-179) |
| 2026 | Carryover | Crown: rear camera software (25V-744) |

## The test drive

Start the car several times from cold and make sure the 12.3-inch instrument display comes on each time with no warning lights, because a blank display is the subject of a recall. Drive in town and on the freeway, and brake firmly from highway speed. Test the cameras in reverse, the touchscreen, the phone connection and every driver aid. On a Platinum, try each drive mode and feel the 6-speed shift under load; on a 2025 or later Platinum, check the head-up display. On any Crown with 21-inch wheels, look closely at the tires and wheels for curb damage, and price replacement tires.

## Hybrid checks

Have a Toyota shop scan the hybrid system for stored codes. The engine will start and stop by itself, which is normal; warning lights are not. The hybrid components are covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles from the date the car was first sold, and the basic warranty for 3 years or 36,000 miles, so check the in-service date and the mileage and ask a dealer what coverage remains.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2023–2025 Crown and 2025 Crown Signia:** 25V-595. A software error can leave the 12.3-inch instrument display blank at startup, so speed and warning lights aren't shown. The fix is a software update, which 2023–2024 Crowns and the Signia can receive over the air.
- **2024–2026 Crown and 2025 Crown Signia:** 25V-744. The rear camera image can freeze or go blank in reverse. The fix is a software update.
- **2023–2025 Crown:** 24V-548. On some cars sold through Gulf States Toyota, the load capacity label may show the wrong figures, and new labels are mailed out.
- **2025 Crown Signia:** 26V-179. A label on some cars may need replacing.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title, signs of flood damage, or hybrid warning lights the seller can't explain. Walk away from any seller who won't allow an inspection or can't show that the recalls were done.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and an average that mixes a 2023 XLE with a 2025 Platinum tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same model, year, grade and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Lexus ES or a Camry for the sedan, and a Lexus RX or a Highlander Hybrid for the Signia, with similar miles, and take the cost of tires and any damage off the price.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Crown past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for the hybrid system and the electronics, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2025 or later Crown XLE or Limited, with heated and ventilated seats as standard.
- **Quickest:** a Crown Platinum with the Hybrid MAX.
- **Most practical:** a Crown Signia XLE.
- **Best value:** a 2023–2024 Crown XLE with its recalls done.
