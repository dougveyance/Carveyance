For 2018 the Expedition got the F-150 treatment: an aluminum-alloy body over a boron-steel frame, a 10-speed automatic, and nothing but EcoBoost V6s. The weight saved went into capability — a 2018 XLT 4x4 runs about 2,551 kg (5,623 lb), less than the old 5.4 trucks — and the Max replaced the EL as the long body, about a foot longer. Prices started around $55,000 for a Max XLT, roughly $73,700 in 2026 dollars, which is why a clean used one now looks like the deal of the full-size segment.

## The power ladder

The 2018–21 trucks make 375 hp, or 400 in the Platinum — and the Platinum is no badge job: Car and Driver ran one to 60 in 5.7 seconds in 2018. The 2022 refresh bumped the standard engine to 380 hp and added 2 ways to get 440: the Timberline, on all-terrain tires with skid plates, which still ran 5.1 to 60 in Car and Driver's hands, and the Limited's Stealth Performance package, which did 4.9 — the quickest Expedition tested to that point. Even the family-spec 2022 Max ran 6.1 in MotorWeek's test. Fuel economy holds between 17 and 19 mpg combined regardless, which is the price of 2.5 tons and 3 rows.

## Which years, which trims

Mechanically 2018–2024 is one continuous truck, so buy on condition, options and the 2022 split: pre-refresh trucks are cheaper and plainer; 2022-on brings the new dash with the available 15.5-inch screen, the Timberline, and the Stealth. The Max costs little more used and tows and carries meaningfully more — if you ever load the 3rd row and the cargo bay at once, find one. A 2020 recall covering certain Kentucky Truck Plant builds is worth a VIN check, as are the 10-speed software campaigns; both chapters of that story are routine dealer work when documented. The known mechanical caveat of the era — cam phaser rattle on 2018–21 EcoBoosts — is in the checklist chapter, and it is the single most important cold-start listen on these trucks.
