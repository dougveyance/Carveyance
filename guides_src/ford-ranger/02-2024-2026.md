The redesigned Ranger arrived for 2024 with a wider stance and a much more modern cabin, but its real achievement was choice. Ford kept the torquey 2.3-liter turbo 4-cylinder, added an available 2.7-liter twin-turbo V6 and finally brought a factory Ranger Raptor to American showrooms. That makes 3 different buying decisions under 1 name. On a showroom floor they look like trim levels. On the road, the trail and the towing scale, they behave more like 3 ideas of what a midsize truck should do.

## 3 engines, 3 philosophies

The 2.3-liter is still rated at 270 hp and 310 lb-ft. The 2.7-liter V6 makes 315 hp and 400 lb-ft, and the Raptor's 3.0-liter V6 makes 405 hp and 430 lb-ft. All use a 10-speed automatic. Don't dismiss the 4-cylinder as an economy option: it has plenty of everyday torque, and Car and Driver ran a 2.3-liter Lariat 4x4 to 60 mph in 6.2 seconds. The V6 suits the buyer who wants effortless passing and loaded acceleration without the Raptor's price; Car and Driver timed a 2.7-liter XLT FX4 at 5.6 seconds.

The EPA rates a 2024 2.3-liter at 22 mpg combined with either drive and the 2.7-liter 4x4 at 20. The 2026 2.3-liter 4x4 is rated at 21.

## What the redesign dropped

In America, the 2024 Ranger comes only as a 4-door SuperCrew with a bed just under 5 feet, 4 feet 11.6 inches. The SuperCab and its 6-foot bed are gone, and at launch there was no Tremor. If you carry long material, a 2019–2023 SuperCab may suit you better. The new truck's bed is wider, though, and holds 4-foot-wide loads between the wheel arches.

## Cabin, screens and details

[[photo:ch2-cabin]]

The redesign brought an 8-inch or 12.4-inch digital instrument cluster, a 10.1-inch or 12-inch center touchscreen with SYNC 4A, an available 360-degree camera and an available 400-watt power system with outlets in the cab and the bed. Which features a given truck has depends on the trim and packages, so check the window sticker or the build sheet. Screen size shouldn't outrank condition: test the touchscreen, phone pairing, cameras, drive modes and trailer controls with the truck parked. Check tonneau covers, bed rails, tailgate cables and aftermarket racks for damage at their mounting points.

## Towing is arithmetic, not a slogan

Ford's 2026 towing guide rates properly equipped 2.3-liter and 2.7-liter Rangers at 3,402 kg (7,500 lb) and the Raptor at 2,499 kg (5,510 lb). Even then, the payload label on the door jamb sets the practical limit once passengers, a hitch and luggage are aboard. A trailer with 10 to 15% of its weight on the tongue can use up hundreds of pounds of payload before anyone climbs in. Check the gross combined weight rating, the brake controller and the tow package rather than quoting a maximum from a different configuration.

## What it cost

The 2024 Ranger started at $32,565 before delivery for an XL, or $34,900 in 2026 dollars, $10,000 more than the 2019 XL in raw dollars and about $2,900 more after inflation. A Lariat started at $43,525 and the Raptor at $55,365.

## The model-year decision

| Model year | What changed |
|---|---|
| 2024 | Redesign. XL, XLT, Lariat and Raptor, SuperCrew only. 2.7-liter V6 available |
| 2025 | New colors and package changes. The Lariat 4x4 gets a mechanical shifter in place of the electronic one. Black Appearance Package |
| 2026 | Steel wheels standard on the XL, FX4 package available on the XL, power moonroof optional on the XLT and Lariat |
| 2027 | Reported by Ford Authority: the 3.0-liter V6 replaces the 2.7 on the XLT and Lariat |

A 2024 offers the new cabin and chassis, but it's the first year of a new design. A 2025 or 2026 may have different packages and software. None of them should be declared more reliable without evidence: compare the actual options by VIN and window sticker. Judge any modifications for workmanship, because lifts change suspension geometry, wheel offsets change bearing loads and badly fitted accessories can block cameras and sensors. The right truck is the one that can still do its original job without excuses.
