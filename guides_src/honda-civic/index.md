The Civic is the car most Americans picture when they think of Honda, and between 2017 and 2026 it came in more forms than ever. The tenth generation, sold here through 2021, offered a sedan, a coupe and a hatchback built in England, plus the first Civic Type R sold new in the US. The eleventh generation, from 2022, dropped the coupe, moved hatchback production to Indiana and, for 2025, added a 200-hp hybrid rated at up to 49 mpg. The Si and the Type R run alongside both generations, with a year off each.

For a used buyer, the choices are the generation, the body, the engine and, for the Si and Type R, how the car has been driven. This guide has 6 chapters: one for each generation, one each for the Hybrid, the Si and the Type R, and the checklist to use before you buy. We've combined our separate sedan, coupe and hatchback reviews into the generation chapters, because those bodies share their engines, cabins and recalls.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2025 or later Civic Hybrid, with 200 hp and an EPA rating of up to 49 mpg combined.
- **Best value:** a 2019–2021 Civic sedan with the 2.0-liter engine. Honda Sensing is standard, and it's rated at 33 mpg.
- **Most practical:** a Civic hatchback, either generation, for its big rear opening and folding seat.
- **Best to drive on a budget:** a 2017–2020 or 2022 and later Civic Si, with a 6-speed manual and a limited-slip differential.
- **The real performance car:** a 2017–2021 or 2023 and later Type R. Car and Driver ran both from 0 to 60 mph in 4.9 seconds.
- **Think twice if** you want an automatic Si or Type R, or all-wheel drive. Neither exists.
