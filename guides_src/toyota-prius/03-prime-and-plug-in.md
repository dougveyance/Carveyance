The plug-in Prius gives you 2 cars in one. On a full charge it drives on electricity alone, and when the battery runs down it carries on as a regular Prius, with no range anxiety and no need to plan your trip around chargers. Toyota called it the Prius Prime from 2017 to 2024 and renamed it the Prius Plug-in Hybrid for 2025. There are 2 very different generations, and the newer one is much better.

## 2017–2022: the first Prime

The first Prime used the regular Prius's 121-hp powertrain with an 8.8 kWh lithium-ion battery, good for an EPA-rated 25 miles on electricity and 133 MPGe. Once the battery is depleted, it's rated at 54 mpg combined, a little better than the regular car. It looked different from the regular Prius, with its own nose and tail, and the Premium and Advanced grades had a tablet-like 11.6-inch touchscreen. It wasn't quick: Car and Driver tested all 3 grades in hybrid mode and none reached 60 mph in less than 10 seconds.

The quirk to know is the seating. From 2017 to 2019, the Prime had a console between 2 rear seats, so it carried only 4 people. Toyota added a fifth seat for 2020, along with Apple CarPlay, Amazon Alexa and rear USB ports, and the grades were renamed LE, XLE and Limited. The Plus started at $27,100 for 2017 ($37,200 in 2026 dollars), and the 2022 Prime started at $28,770.

## 2023–2026: the Prime gets serious

The second generation shares the new Prius's body and adds a lot more power: a 150-hp 2.0-liter engine and a 161-hp motor-generator combine for 220 hp, and Toyota claims 0 to 60 mph in 6.6 seconds. By Toyota's figures, that makes it the quickest Prius yet. The battery grew to 13.6 kWh, and the electric range nearly doubled.

[[chart:evrange]]

The SE, on 17-inch wheels, is rated at 44 miles of electric range and 52 mpg in hybrid mode. The XSE and XSE Premium, on 19-inch wheels, are rated at 39 miles and 48 mpg. All are front-wheel drive; there's no all-wheel-drive plug-in Prius. It charges through a standard J1772 plug at up to 3.5 kW, so a 240-volt charger fills it in about 4 hours, and a household outlet takes about 11. It went on sale in May 2023 at $32,350 for the SE ($35,700 in 2026 dollars), $35,600 for the XSE and $39,170 for the XSE Premium.

For 2025, the Prime became the Prius Plug-in Hybrid, with a PHEV badge on the tail but no mechanical changes. A Nightshade Edition, based on the XSE, joined the lineup for 2026, when the SE started at $33,775.

## Does a plug-in pay?

It depends on where you charge and how far you drive. If you can charge at home and most of your days are shorter than the battery's range, you'll buy very little gas. Toyota says a 2023 or later car can cover most daily commutes on electricity. If you can't charge where you park, a plug-in is just a heavier, more expensive Prius, and the regular car makes more sense. Before you buy, check the price of electricity where you live against the price of gas, and remember that the electric range drops in cold weather.

## What to check before you buy

Ask for the charging cable that came with the car, and test both charging and the electric range on the test drive. The rest of the checks match the regular Prius of the same years: the 2019–2020 Primes are in the brake booster pump recall, the 2022 cars in the hybrid system software recall, and the 2023–2026 cars in the rear door switch recall. The battery warranty is 10 years or 150,000 miles for 2020 and later cars. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

If you can charge at home, a 2023 or later Prime SE or Plug-in Hybrid SE is the most satisfying Prius of all: quick, frugal and able to run errands on electricity alone. A first-generation Prime is a cheaper way to try plug-in driving, but its 25-mile range is limited, and avoid a 2017–2019 car if you need 5 seats.

::: curiosity Back to the Old Name
Before the Prime, Toyota sold a Prius Plug-in Hybrid from 2012 to 2015. When it renamed the Prime for 2025, it went back to almost the same name, saying it wanted buyers to tell the Prius powertrains apart more easily.
:::
