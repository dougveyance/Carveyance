The Mach-E went on sale for the 2021 model year with a choice of 2 batteries, standard range or extended range, and 2 motor layouts, a single motor driving the rear wheels or a motor on each axle for all-wheel drive. On top of those sat the trims: Select, Premium, the range-focused California Route 1 and the performance GT. The combinations matter far more than the trim names, because they decide the range, the power and the price.

The 2021 standard-range battery had 68 kWh usable and the extended-range battery 88 kWh. From 2023, some standard-range rear-drive cars switched to a lithium-iron-phosphate (LFP) battery, which the EPA lists separately. A 2021 Select started at $42,895 before delivery, or $53,200 in 2026 dollars, and a GT at $59,900, or $74,300.

## How far they go

[[chart:range21]]

The EPA ranges spread widely. A 2021 extended-range car with rear-wheel drive was rated at 300 miles and the California Route 1, which pairs that battery with rear-wheel drive and efficient wheels and tires, at 305. A standard-range all-wheel-drive car was rated at just 211. By 2024, small efficiency gains had lifted the extended-range rear-drive car to 320 miles and the standard-range all-wheel-drive car to 230.

## How quick they are

[[chart:accel21]]

Car and Driver ran a 2021 Premium with the extended-range battery and all-wheel drive, rated at 346 hp, to 60 mph in 4.9 seconds. The GT is quicker still; it has its own chapter.

## Year by year

| Model year | What changed |
|---|---|
| 2021 | Launch. Select, Premium, California Route 1, GT and the limited First Edition. GT Performance Edition |
| 2022 | Carryover. California Route 1 offered with all-wheel drive |
| 2023 | Some standard-range rear-drive cars move to an LFP battery |
| 2024 | Rally added. The EPA no longer lists a GT Performance Edition |

## What to know about the early cars

The most important history item on a 2021 or 2022 car is the high-voltage battery junction box. Recall 22V412 updated the software on 48,924 cars because the battery's main contactors could overheat, and recall 23V687, Ford's 23S56, replaced the junction box on 34,762 extended-range cars. If a car hasn't had both done, a dealer should check it before you buy. The checklist chapter has the full list.

## Which one to buy

For most buyers, an extended-range car is the one to have. It's the best combination of range and pace. Rear-wheel drive gives the most range; all-wheel drive gives more power and winter traction. A standard-range car makes sense if you drive short distances and charge at home, and its lower price reflects that.
