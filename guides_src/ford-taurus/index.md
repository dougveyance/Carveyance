No American car of the past 40 years carried a bigger arc than the Taurus. The 1986 jelly bean rewrote sedan design and won Motor Trend's Car of the Year; the 1992 car took the best-seller crown from Honda and held it 5 straight years; the oval 1996 redesign handed that crown to the Camry; the 2000s turned it into a rental fleet queen; and the 2010 revival made it a 365-hp flagship and a police car before the last one left Chicago on March 1, 2019. Roughly 8 million sold in 33 years.

For today's buyer the Taurus is 3 different propositions: dirt-cheap honest transportation (1992–2007), a surprisingly good used flagship (2010–2019), and a genuine enthusiast collectible — the SHO, whose Yamaha-built engines get their own chapter.

## Quick picks

- **Best used buy:** a 2013–2019 with the 288-hp 3.5 or the 25-mpg 2.0 EcoBoost — enormous, safe, comfortable, and cheaper than the equivalent Japanese sedan every time.
- **The legend:** a 1989–1991 SHO with the 5-speed. 220 Yamaha horsepower, 6.6 to 60 in Car and Driver's test, and values climbing from nothing.
- **The modern muscle one:** a 2013–2019 SHO — 365 hp, AWD, 5.2 to 60, the quickest Taurus ever, still priced like a used family car.
- **The oddball flex:** a 1996–1999 SHO, the only V8 Taurus — Yamaha heads, Cosworth block, genuine rarity.
- **Cheap wheels, no shame:** any clean 2000s Duratec car. $2,000–$4,000 buys decades of proven engineering.
