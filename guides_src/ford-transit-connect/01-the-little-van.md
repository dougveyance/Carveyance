The van itself was simple: a tall, narrow Turkish-built box on a front-drive platform, 136 hp through a 4-speed automatic, 12 seconds to 60 in MotorWeek's wagon test, and $21,475 to start — about $33,100 in 2026 dollars, or $4,500 less than a base Econoline. What it offered was packaging nobody else sold: 135 cubic feet of upright cargo behind 2 seats, a load floor low enough for a hand truck, 59 inches of interior height, and a 39-foot turning circle that made the big vans look like barges. Florists, locksmiths and cable installers understood immediately, and the industry named it 2010 North American Truck of the Year.

The import route made it a legend. The 1963 "chicken tax" puts a 25% tariff on imported trucks but only 2.5% on passenger vehicles — so every US-bound Transit Connect left Kocaeli with rear seats and windows, cleared customs as a passenger car, and had the seats stripped and the glass swapped for steel panels at a facility by the Port of Baltimore. The conversion cost a few hundred dollars per van against thousands in avoided duty. Customs cried foul; in 2019 a federal appeals court sided with the government, ruling the vans had been cargo vehicles all along. There was even an electric version — the Azure Dynamics Transit Connect Electric of 2011–12, 56 EPA miles and $57,400, a decade early and priced like it.

## Buying one now

These are now 13-to-17-year-old work tools, and most led hard lives. The 2.0 Duratec and 4F27E automatic are genuinely durable — both served years in the Focus — so the checklist is condition, not catastrophe: rust along the rockers, doors and rear arches in salt states, sliding-door rollers, sagging rear doors, and whatever the previous trade bolted through the floor. Interiors are vinyl and hose-out simple. Parts are cheap, mechanics are unintimidated, and $4,000–$7,000 buys a sound one almost anywhere.

The case for a first generation over a second is character and price; the case against is the 4-speed's buzzy 75-mph cruise. If your routes are local, it doesn't matter — and an unrusted original with the seats long gone to a Baltimore shredder is a better story than anything else at the money.
