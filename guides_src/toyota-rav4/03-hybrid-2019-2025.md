If one version of the fifth-generation RAV4 deserves its reputation, it's the hybrid. It has more power than the gas model, all-wheel drive on every car and fuel economy that a compact SUV this size had no business reaching a few years earlier. In Consumer Guide's testing, a 2019 Hybrid Limited returned 40.3 mpg, against 25.5 mpg for a gas RAV4 driven the same way.

## What you get

The RAV4 Hybrid combines a 2.5-liter 4-cylinder with Toyota's hybrid system for 219 hp. There's no conventional transmission: an electronically controlled continuously variable transmission blends engine and motor power. A separate electric motor drives the rear wheels when needed, so every 2019–2025 RAV4 Hybrid sold in the US has all-wheel drive, with no driveshaft running to the back.

The hybrid arrived in March 2019, a few months after the gas model, starting at $27,700, about $36,500 in 2026 dollars. It launched as an LE, XLE, XSE and Limited. Toyota later added an XLE Premium and an SE, and for 2023 the Woodland Edition, with a TRD-tuned suspension, bronze-colored wheels on all-terrain tires and roof rack cross bars. The 2023 multimedia update, with its 8-inch or 10.5-inch screen, applies to the hybrid too.

## The numbers

The EPA rated the RAV4 Hybrid at 40 mpg combined at launch, with 41 mpg in the city. Later ratings settled at 39 mpg combined for most grades, and the Woodland Edition, with its all-terrain tires, is rated at 37. Consumer Guide timed a 2019 Hybrid Limited from 0 to 60 mph in 7.5 seconds, which is quicker than its tests of the gas model.

Over 12,000 miles a year at $4 a gallon, the difference against the gas RAV4 is about $370, as chart 3 in the previous chapter shows. The bigger gain comes in town and in traffic, where the hybrid can creep along on electricity and recover energy when it slows down.

## How it drives

The hybrid is the smoother, quieter RAV4. It pulls away on electric power and starts its engine without fuss. Under full throttle, the engine drones at high revs, as most Toyota hybrids do, but in ordinary driving it's relaxed and responsive.

The all-wheel-drive system is designed for slippery roads, not serious off-roading. It does the job in rain and snow, and the Woodland Edition's tires and suspension make it more comfortable on dirt roads and trailheads.

## What to check

The hybrid is a durable system, but there's 1 known issue worth asking about on every AWD hybrid, along with the usual recalls.

- **Rear motor cable corrosion (program 22TE09 and campaign 25TD01):** On RAV4 Hybrids, the high-voltage cable connection at the rear motor generator can corrode, especially where roads are salted. Toyota's own documents say it can cause AM radio static or leave the car unable to start. In December 2022, Toyota set up customer support program 22TE09 to cover the repair beyond the 3-year, 36,000-mile basic warranty, starting with 437,140 2019–2022 RAV4 Hybrids. In 2025, it added limited service campaign 25TD01, which covers about 659,210 2019–2024 RAV4 Hybrids and installs a protector seal on the connector after an inspection. Owners who have the seal fitted get coverage on the cable for 10 years with no mileage limit. Ask whether the inspection and seal were done.
- **Front passenger airbag sensor (23V-865):** About 76,800 2020–2021 RAV4 Hybrids were part of a December 2023 recall for a seat sensor that can short-circuit and stop the passenger airbag from deploying. Dealers inspect and replace the sensor as needed.
- **Rearview camera software (25V-744):** About 93,000 2023–2025 RAV4 Hybrids with the Panoramic View Monitor were part of an October 2025 recall. The rear camera image can freeze or go blank in reverse. The fix is a free software update.
- **Hybrid battery warranty:** From the 2020 model year, Toyota covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 150,000 miles. A 2019 RAV4 Hybrid is covered by the older 8-year, 100,000-mile hybrid warranty, so most have already run out. Have a hybrid specialist check the battery's health on a 2019 car.

Run the VIN on [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls), and ask a Toyota dealer to check the VIN for open campaigns as well, since a service campaign doesn't always show up as a recall.

## Which one to buy

- **Hybrid LE or XLE:** the best value of any used RAV4.
- **Hybrid XLE Premium:** a nicer cabin without the Limited's price.
- **Hybrid XSE or SE:** the sportier look and, on the XSE, the firmer suspension.
- **Woodland Edition (2023–2025):** the one for gravel roads and trailheads, at a small cost in mpg.
- **2020 or newer:** worth finding over a 2019 for the 10-year, 150,000-mile battery warranty.

## The verdict

The 2019–2025 RAV4 Hybrid is the easiest recommendation in this guide. It's quicker, thriftier and better to drive than the gas model, and every one has all-wheel drive. Find one with the cable campaign done and a clean recall history, and you'll have a hard time doing better for the money.

::: curiosity The Sporty One Was the Hybrid
When the fifth-generation RAV4 launched, the sportiest grade, the XSE, came only as a hybrid. It had glossy black trim, a sport-tuned suspension and blue-and-black seats, and there was no gas version. For a company once known for sensible, slow hybrids, it was a quiet change of attitude.
:::
