Buying a used 4Runner is a little like buying a tent from someone who swears it was only used a few weekends. The number of weekends hardly matters. What happened on them does.

The 4Runner's reputation is real, and sellers know it. It's in the price before anyone has looked at the brakes, the frame or the transfer case. Use this list to make sure you're paying for the truck and not just the badge.

## Pick the job first

For a daily driver that sees the occasional dirt road, start with a clean, stock SR5 Premium or Limited. For real trail work, look for a documented TRD Off-Road. For a stronger, more modern powertrain, try a 2025–2026 turbo, and for the most torque or the hybrid-only grades, the i-FORCE MAX. If the third row is the main reason you're shopping, the 4Runner may be the wrong Toyota.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2017–2019 | Fifth generation. 4.0-liter V6, 5-speed automatic, no Apple CarPlay or Android Auto | Fuel pump recall on certain 2018–2019 trucks. Age and mileage wear |
| 2020–2024 | Same V6 and gearbox, with an 8-inch screen, CarPlay, Android Auto and Toyota Safety Sense P standard | Frame and underbody rust in salt states. Modifications |
| 2025 | Sixth generation. 2.4-liter turbo or i-FORCE MAX hybrid, 8-speed automatic, new platform | First-year truck. Instrument-panel software recall (25V-595) |
| 2026 | Second year of the new design | Too young for long-term reliability data |

## Frame, rust and underbody

Pay for an independent inspection on a lift. Look for structural rust, crushed or kinked frame sections, damaged mounting points, leaks from the differentials and transfer case, torn boots, bent suspension arms and corroded brake lines. Be suspicious of fresh undercoating sprayed over metal you can't see. Trucks from states that salt their roads need the most attention, and a glossy photo of a freshly washed truck tells you nothing about the chassis.

## Four-wheel drive and hardware

Confirm the drivetrain from the VIN and the window sticker, not the seller's description. Where it's safe, test four-high and four-low. Never lock a part-time 4x4 into four-wheel drive on dry pavement. If the truck has a locking rear differential, make sure it engages and the light on the dash confirms it. A seller saying "all the buttons work" isn't a test.

While you're at the back, roll the rear window all the way down and back up. It's the 4Runner's signature feature, and if it's going to need a repair, you want the seller paying for it.

## Modifications and towing

Big tires and roof racks aren't proof of careful ownership. Inspect the lift installation, tire rub, wheel bearings, brakes, suspension bushings, added wiring, the hitch mounting and any trailer brake controller. Ask whether the axle gearing was changed. Ask what it towed, and how often. A headline rating of 2,722 kg (6,000 lb) on the newer trucks, or 2,268 kg (5,000 lb) on the older ones, doesn't apply to every combination of options and cargo, so read the payload and towing labels on the truck itself.

## Engine and electronics

On 2017–2024 V6 trucks, look for coolant or oil leaks, a tired battery, aging hoses and smooth, prompt shifts. On 2025–2026 turbo and hybrid trucks, ask about oil change intervals, software updates and any warning lights in the service history. Test every electronic feature, including the cameras, the screen, the driver assistance systems and that rear window.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2018–2019:** certain trucks were part of Toyota's fuel pump recall (20V-012, later expanded as 20V-682). A failing pump can stall the engine.
- **2025:** the 4Runner is among the models in Toyota's 2025 instrument-panel software recall (25V-595).
- **Every year:** newer recalls can appear at any time. Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done. Recalls and service bulletins aren't the same thing, so ask about both.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly and hide the differences that matter. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, grade, drivetrain, mileage and title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what trucks actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you, comparing a listing with similar ones nearby. Expect low-mileage TRD Pros to carry a premium, and don't assume the premium is justified by the truck's condition. A pre-purchase inspection is worth more than any seller's speech about Toyota reliability.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the truck past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Older truck, best value:** a stock, documented V6 SR5 or SR5 Premium, ideally 2020 or newer.
- **Trail bargain:** a TRD Off-Road with working hardware and proof of careful maintenance.
- **Newer all-rounder:** a turbo SR5 or TRD Off-Road with clean update and recall records.
- **Specialist:** a hybrid TRD Pro, Trailhunter or Platinum, bought for what it can do rather than how the badge looks in a parking lot.
