For a few years, Prius was a family of cars rather than one car. Beside the regular hatchback, Toyota sold the subcompact Prius c and the wagon-shaped Prius v, both launched for 2012. Neither had a successor with the same name. The Prius v ended after 2017 and the Prius c after 2019, so the used cars in this guide are the last of each.

## Prius c: the small one

The Prius c is a subcompact hatchback, about 16 inches shorter than the regular Prius, with a 1.5-liter hybrid system rated at 99 hp. The EPA rates it at 46 mpg combined (48 city, 43 highway), and it has 17.1 cubic feet of cargo space behind the rear seat. At 1,148 kg (2,530 lb), it's light and easy to park, which makes it a good city car. On the freeway it feels its age, and by 2019 Autoblog's reviewers found it outclassed by newer cars, including some of Toyota's own.

For 2017, Toyota Safety Sense C became standard on every grade, with pre-collision braking, lane departure alert and automatic high beams. The 2017 grades ran from One to Four, at $20,150 to $24,965. For 2019, the last year, Toyota cut them to L and LE, priced at $21,530 ($28,300 in 2026 dollars) and $22,955, and in the US it was replaced by the Corolla Hybrid.

## Prius v: the big one

The Prius v is the opposite: a tall wagon with the most space of any Prius. Its rear seat slides and reclines, so you can trade legroom for cargo room. With the seat in place there are 34.3 to 40.2 cubic feet behind it, and with it folded there are 67.3 cubic feet, more than many small SUVs. The front passenger seat folds flat too, so it will swallow long items. Its 1.8-liter hybrid system makes 134 hp, and the EPA rates it at 41 mpg combined (43 city, 39 highway), well below the regular Prius but excellent for a car this roomy. Kelley Blue Book lists 0 to 60 mph in 10.9 seconds.

The 2017 was the final year in the US, sold in Two, Three, Four and Five grades from $26,675 ($36,600 in 2026 dollars) to $30,935. It predates Toyota's adoption of Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, so budget for an aftermarket head unit if you want them.

## What to check before you buy

Both are at least 7 years old, so their hybrid batteries are likely out of the 8-year, 100,000-mile warranty that covered most states. Have a Toyota shop scan the hybrid system and check the battery's health, and pay accordingly. On the Prius v, confirm that recall 20V-369 has been done: excessive voltage in the inverter can make the hybrid system shut down and the car stall while driving, and the fix is a software update, plus an inverter repair on cars that have already shown the fault. Some 2017 Prius c cars sold through Gulf States Toyota were recalled for a spare tire inflated to the wrong pressure (17V-295). The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

Choose the Prius c if you want the cheapest, easiest-to-park Prius for a mostly urban life. A 2019 L or LE is the newest you can find. Choose the Prius v if you need space and can live with older technology. For most other people, a 2017–2022 regular Prius in [the first chapter](../2017-2022/) is the better buy, with better fuel economy and a newer battery warranty on the later cars.

::: curiosity The Aqua
In Japan, the Prius c was sold as the Toyota Aqua, and production of this generation continued into 2021, 2 years after its US sales ended.
:::
