The Escape got there first. When it arrived in 2000 as a joint Ford-Mazda project, the compact crossover barely existed as a category; by 2017 the Escape was selling 308,296 a year and every brand on earth had copied the recipe. Then Ford's own Bronco Sport and Maverick ate into it from both sides, and in August 2025 Ford announced the end: US production stopped in December 2025, and the Escape leaves after the 2026 model year — a roughly 5.15-million-sale American run.

For a buyer, the ending is the opportunity. The 2026 trucks are the last new ones and start at $30,350. The 2020–2026 generation includes the quickest Escape ever tested (5.7 seconds to 60) and the most efficient (a 101-MPGe plug-in). The 2013–2019 cars are everywhere and cheap. And the hybrid story — which began in 2005 when the Escape became the first hybrid SUV on the US market — gets its own chapter, because it is the best reason to buy one today.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2023–2026 Escape Hybrid — 192 hp, 39–40 mpg combined, the refreshed cabin, and the drivetrain with the 20-year taxi-proven pedigree.
- **The last new one:** a 2026 ST-Line Elite or Platinum, $36,215–$37,210. Buy it because you want the final year, with the hybrid if possible.
- **Best used value:** a 2017–2019 SE with the 1.5 EcoBoost — post-refresh cabin and Sync 3 at bottom-dollar prices.
- **The quick one:** a 2020+ with the 250-hp 2.0 EcoBoost — 5.7 to 60 in Car and Driver's test, quicker than plenty of hot hatches of its day.
- **Think twice if** you're cross-shopping new: a RAV4 or CR-V will still exist in 2027, with the resale story that implies. The Escape's discount is real, and so is the reason for it.
