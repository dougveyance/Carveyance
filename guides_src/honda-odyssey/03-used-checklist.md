An Odyssey has usually carried children, car seats, dogs and sports gear, with the sliding doors opening and closing thousands of times. None of that is a reason to avoid one, but it means the doors, the seats and the records deserve more attention than the engine's sound on a short drive. Plan to spend 20 minutes on the doors and seats alone.

## First, know which one it is

Decode the VIN to confirm the year and trim. A 2017 is the last of the old van, with the 6-speed automatic. On a 2018 or 2019, the LX, EX and EX-L have the 9-speed and the Touring and Elite the 10-speed; from 2020, every Odyssey has the 10-speed. Magic Slide second-row seats came on the EX and above.

## The doors and seats

Open and close both power sliding doors at least 5 times each, from the key fob, the inside buttons and the outside handles, and watch that each one latches fully without reopening. On a van from a cold state, ask whether the handle cable recall was done. Then remove and refit the second-row center seat if it has one, slide the Magic Slide seats through every position, and fold the third row into the floor and back. On a 2017, check that the second-row floor strikers have Honda's bracket.

## The test drive

Start from cold and listen for a knock, especially on a 2018–2019. Drive in stop-and-go traffic: shifts should be smooth, without hesitation or a thump into first. Check that the rearview camera comes on every time you select reverse, and that the screen doesn't freeze. Try every rear climate control and the rear entertainment system, if the van has one.

## Service checks

The V6 has a timing belt, due every 7 years or 105,000 miles, usually with the water pump. Ask for proof it was done, or take its cost off the price. Look for records of transmission fluid changes, check for oil leaks, and look at the tires and brakes, which work hard on a heavy van.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2011–2017:** 17V725. A second-row outboard seat that isn't latched properly can tip forward under braking. Dealers fit a bracket.
- **2017–2023:** 23V858. The fuel pump may fail, by VIN.
- **2018–2019:** 18V795. The power sliding door rear latches may stick and not latch. 23V751: on some engines, a connecting rod bearing may wear and seize. 19V299: a weak battery or loose terminal can cause a transmission control fault.
- **2018–2020:** 20V437. Water in the sliding door handle cables can freeze, so the doors may not latch. Also 23V158, front seat belt buckles, and 20V066, a third-row power outlet wire that can be pinched.
- **2018–2022:** 26V227. A software error can make the side and side curtain air bags deploy unexpectedly. The fix is new software or a new control unit.
- **2018–2023:** 23V431 and others. Faults can stop the rearview camera image from displaying; 20V438 and 26V423 cover water getting into the camera.
- **2018–2026:** 26V332, and 24V064 for 2020–2022. The front passenger seat weight sensor can fail, so the passenger air bag may not work correctly.
- **2022:** 21V432, deformed second-row seat rail locks, and 21V768, tires with a possible pinhole on some vans.
- **2023–2024:** 24V114. The steering gearbox may not have been tightened properly.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title, flood damage or a musty smell you can't explain. Walk away from sliding doors that don't latch reliably, an air bag warning light, an engine knock or a transmission that slips, and from any seller who won't allow an independent inspection.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and an average that mixes a 2017 LX with a 2025 Elite tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same generation, trim and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Toyota Sienna or Kia Carnival of the same age, and take the cost of a due timing belt, tires and brakes off the price.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2021 or later EX-L.
- **Best value:** a 2018–2020 EX or EX-L with the door and air bag recalls done.
- **Best for long trips:** a Touring or Elite.
- **Cheapest way in:** a 2017 EX or SE with the timing belt done.
