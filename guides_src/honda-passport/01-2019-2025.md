Honda unveiled the new Passport at the Los Angeles Auto Show in November 2018, and it went on sale on February 4, 2019. It's built in Lincoln, Alabama, on the same light-truck platform as the Pilot, Ridgeline and Odyssey, but it's shorter than the Pilot and has 2 rows of seats instead of 3. That leaves a cargo area of 41.2 cubic feet behind the rear seat and 77.9 cubic feet with it folded, enough for bikes, a dog crate or a week of camping gear.

## The drivetrain

[[chart:mpg]]

Every Passport has the 3.5-liter V6, rated at 280 hp and 262 lb-ft, and a 9-speed automatic worked by push buttons on the console. Front-wheel drive was standard at first, and Honda's i-VTM4 torque-vectoring all-wheel drive, with Snow, Sand and Mud modes, was a $1,900 option ($2,500 in 2026 dollars) and standard on the Elite. There's no low-range transfer case. Ground clearance is 7.5 inches with front-wheel drive and 8.1 with all-wheel drive, and towing is rated at 1,588 kg (3,500 lb) with front-wheel drive and 2,268 kg (5,000 lb) with all-wheel drive.

It's quick for its size. Car and Driver ran a 2019 Elite to 60 mph in 5.8 seconds and an EX-L in 6.1. Fuel economy is ordinary for a V6 SUV: the EPA rates it at 22 mpg combined with front-wheel drive and 21 with all-wheel drive.

## The trims, year by year

At launch, the range was Sport, EX-L, Touring and Elite, from $31,990 for a front-drive Sport ($42,100 in 2026 dollars) to $43,680 for the Elite ($57,400). The EX-L and above had the 8-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, and for 2021 every Passport got it.

The 2022 restyle brought a new nose and the first TrailSport, with its own bumpers, a slightly wider track and more aggressive all-season tires, and it cut the range to EX-L, TrailSport and Elite. The TrailSport cost $42,470 ($48,800 in 2026 dollars). For 2023, all-wheel drive became standard on every Passport. For 2024, the TrailSport got an off-road suspension tune and General Grabber all-terrain tires on 18-inch wheels, the first all-terrain tires on a Passport, every version got a bigger center console with a new armrest, and a Black Edition replaced the Elite. Car and Driver ran a 2022 TrailSport to 60 mph in 6.0 seconds and a 2024 TrailSport in 5.9. The last of this generation was built in March 2025.

## What to check before you buy

The 2019 has the most recalls: front frame members that may not have been fully welded (19V784), a hood latch striker that can let the hood open while driving (21V932) and overcured Continental tires on some 2019–2020 cars (21V165). Fuel pumps were recalled on 2019–2021 cars (21V215 and 23V858), the rearview camera connector on 2019–2023 cars (23V431), and a brake master cylinder fastener on 2021–2023 cars (23V458). 2019–2023 cars sold in salt-belt states are part of the rear subframe corrosion recall (26V365). The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A 2023–2025 EX-L is the best of this generation to buy, with standard all-wheel drive and the bigger screen. A 2024–2025 TrailSport is worth the extra if you drive dirt roads, for its tires and suspension. A 2019–2021 Sport or EX-L with front-wheel drive is the cheapest way in, but check its recalls carefully.
