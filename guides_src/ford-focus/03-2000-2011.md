The original Focus was a genuine event: European chassis engineering, New Edge styling and steering feel that had no business on a car this cheap — about $25,400 in 2026 dollars for a car that listed near $13,000. America answered with 286,166 sales in calendar 2000, a record no later Focus year touched. The ZX3 3-door was the charmer, the ZX5 and wagon the practical ones, and the recall-heavy early build quality the asterisk the fundamentals kept outrunning.

## Which early cars still matter

Skip the 110-hp SPI base engine unless the car is nearly free; the 130-hp **Zetec** is the one that drives like the reviews said, and the 2005–07 **Duratec** cars added refinement as the styling went soft. The 2008–2011 "second generation" was a reskin of the same platform — sedan and a short-lived coupe only, 140 hp, no hatch — notable mainly for debuting Sync and for riding the 2008 gas spike to 195,823 sales. As transportation, the later the better; as a car to enjoy, it's Zetec hatches or nothing.

## Buying at the bottom of the market

Every one of these is condition-first now. Rust decides everything — rear arches, subframes, brake lines — and a clean southern ZX3 is worth 3 rusty northern ones. The Zetec and Duratec engines run forever on basic care; the automatics are ordinary 4-speeds that want fluid evidence. Budget nothing for depreciation, because there is none left: these are $2,000–$5,000 cars that either serve faithfully or donate parts. The exception is anything wearing an SVT badge, which belongs in the previous chapter and increasingly in collections.
