A used Mirai can look like the bargain of the decade. They lose value quickly, because the number of people who can live with one is small. The car itself is rarely the problem. The fuel, the service network and the resale market are. Work through those first, then look at the car.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2017–2020 | First generation. Front-drive, 151 hp, 4 seats, 312-mile EPA range | A fuel cell software update (campaign H0E) on 2016–2017 cars |
| 2021–2022 | Second generation. Rear-drive, 182 hp, 5 seats, up to 402 miles | Stability-control software recall (22V-239); a check-engine light from the fuel cell air-pressure regulator, fixed under the fuel cell warranty |
| 2023–2024 | Same car, XLE and Limited | Camera recall on 2023 cars (24V-124) |
| 2025–2026 | XLE only; the Limited was dropped for 2025 | Too new for long-term data |

## Map the real fueling network

Visit the stations on your usual routes at different times, and check that they're actually dispensing, what they charge and how you pay. A station on an old map isn't a plan. Use the [Hydrogen Fuel Cell Partnership's station map](https://h2fcp.org/stationmap) for live status, and don't commit until you have a working backup station. The supply breakdown in early 2026, when only 9 stations were open statewide at the worst point, showed what happens to drivers who only had one.

In California, also confirm which dealers still service the Mirai near you. Not every Toyota store does.

## The fuel card

New Mirais come with a fuel card. For the 2026 car it's $15,000 of hydrogen, good for 6 years for buyers or 3 years for lessees, whichever runs out first. Don't assume a private seller's card comes with the car, or that it has money left on it.

Toyota has also put $15,000 cards on Toyota Certified Used Mirais. An April 2025 offer covered certified 2018–2024 cars with 85,000 miles or less, for 3 years or $15,000. Offers like this come and go, so ask the dealer, and get the amount, the time limit and the stations it works at in writing before you sign.

## The car's legal and financial history

Confirm the title status, whether it was leased or a fleet car, and the terms of any fuel card. Get the warranty booklet for that VIN and model year. Toyota's fuel cell system warranty runs 8 years or 100,000 miles from the day the car first went into service, so work out how much is left.

## Mechanical and safety inspection

Have a Toyota technician who knows the Mirai check the hydrogen storage and fuel cell systems for stored codes and warnings, along with the 12-volt and high-voltage batteries, tires, brakes and suspension. Ask for the service and campaign history. Don't attempt hydrogen-system repairs yourself, and don't let a shop that isn't trained for it try.

## The test drive must include a filling station

Don't rely on the owner's word that fueling is easy. Check that the dispenser, pressure rating and payment method you plan to use are working, and try a real fill if you can, making sure the station has enough hydrogen to finish it. Keep notes on how reliable your stations are over a few weeks. If your routine depends on 1 nearby dispenser, you don't have a backup plan, and that should outweigh every flattering photo in the listing.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2016–2017:** campaign H0E, a fuel cell software update. In some driving conditions, the voltage from the fuel cell's boost converter could exceed its limit and shut the system down. In the US, Toyota ran it as a customer satisfaction campaign, not a formal recall, so ask for proof it was done.
- **2021–2022:** 22V-239, a stability-control software update shared with several Toyota and Lexus hybrids.
- **2023:** 24V-124. The panoramic camera may not show the required image when reversing. About 2,100 cars.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done. Recalls and service bulletins aren't the same thing, so ask about both.

## Watch the economic assumptions

Hydrogen prices and fuel card offers vary by station and by how the car was bought. Work out your own numbers with your real yearly mileage, then do it again without any fuel card. As an example, many owners report about 60 miles per kilogram in mixed driving (the EPA rating works out to about 72). At 12,000 miles a year, that's about 200 kg of hydrogen: about $5,700 at April 2026's $28.46 national average, or $7,200 at $36. Add the time lost to detours, and the days a station outage could leave the car parked. A used Mirai may be cheap to buy, but cheap to buy isn't cheap to run.

## Understand what resale could look like

The pool of buyers with dependable hydrogen access is far smaller than the pool for gas hybrids. A Mirai that works perfectly for your household may be hard to sell quickly, and harder still in another state. That isn't a reason to dismiss the technology, but it's a reason not to count on normal trade-in values. The Mirai makes the most sense when the buyer understands both the car and the fueling network before spending a dollar.

## Ownership math

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly and hide the differences that matter. Compare listings for the same year, grade, mileage and title history, and check whether each one includes a fuel card. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the car past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for the fuel cell system, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best bet:** a Toyota Certified Used 2021–2024 XLE with a fuel card confirmed in writing and plenty of fuel cell warranty left.
- **Cheapest way in:** a 2017–2020 car with documented service, if you have 2 reliable stations nearby.
- **Walk away from:** any Mirai you'd have to refuel at a single station.
