The Crown Signia is the Crown family's practical member. Toyota revealed it in late 2023, put it on sale in the summer of 2024 as a 2025 model, and dropped the Venza to make room for it. It's a 2-row, 5-seat SUV, but a long and fairly low one, closer to a tall wagon than a boxy crossover, and like the Crown sedan it comes only as a hybrid with all-wheel drive.

## How it drives

The Signia uses a 2.5-liter 4-cylinder with an electric motor at each axle, for 240 hp, through a CVT. The EPA rates it at 38 mpg combined (39 city, 37 highway), excellent for a vehicle of this size, and it runs on regular fuel. There's no Hybrid MAX version in the US, so the Signia is tuned for smooth, efficient progress rather than speed. It's rated to tow up to 1,225 kg (2,700 lb), enough for a small trailer, but less than many all-wheel-drive rivals.

## Living with it

Space is the reason to choose it over the sedan. The XLE has 25.8 cubic feet behind the rear seat and 68.8 with the seat folded, against the Crown sedan's 15.2-cubic-foot trunk.

There are 2 grades. The Limited adds a fixed panoramic glass roof with a power shade, and a hands-free driving system for low-speed traffic is available. The XLE started at $43,590 ($45,500 in 2026 dollars) and the Limited at $47,990, before delivery.

## What to check before you buy

Every Signia is still young, but run the VIN for 3 recalls on 2025 models. The 12.3-inch instrument display may be blank at startup, hiding warning lights (25V-595); the Signia can take the fix over the air. The rear camera image can freeze or go blank in reverse (25V-744). And a label on some cars may need replacing (26V-179). The hybrid components are covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles from the date the car was first sold, so every Signia is still within the time limit. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

For most people who like the Crown's style but need room for luggage, dogs or sports gear, the Signia is the better buy than the sedan. The XLE has the same powertrain and nearly all the equipment that matters, so it's the value pick; the Limited's glass roof is the main reason to pay more. If you don't need the space and want the efficiency or the speed of the sedan, go back to [the Crown chapter](../crown-sedan/).

::: curiosity One of 4 Crowns
In Japan, the current Crown comes in 4 body styles: Crossover, Sport, Sedan and Estate. The US gets 2 of them. The car sold here as the Crown is the Crown Crossover, and the Crown Signia is the Crown Estate, which Japan also offers as a plug-in hybrid.
:::
