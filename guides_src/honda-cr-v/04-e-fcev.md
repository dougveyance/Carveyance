Somewhere in California, there's a CR-V that charges in your garage overnight and fills up with hydrogen when the battery runs low. The CR-V e:FCEV is Honda's attempt to fix the biggest problem with fuel cell cars: needing a hydrogen station for every mile. It's the first production plug-in fuel cell vehicle sold in America, and the first car to use the fuel cell module Honda developed with General Motors.

## How it works

The e:FCEV is an electric car with 2 energy sources. A 17.7 kWh battery, charged from a plug, gives it 29 miles of EPA-rated range on its own. A 92.2 kW fuel cell turns hydrogen, stored at 10,000 psi, into electricity for longer trips, for 270 miles in all. Either way, a 174-hp electric motor drives the front wheels, with 229 lb-ft of torque. It's heavier than other CR-Vs, at 4,460 pounds, and Honda retuned the suspension for it, but it drives like a calm, quiet electric CR-V. A plug-in power adapter turns its charging port into a 110-volt outlet that can run tools, lights or camping gear at up to 1,500 watts.

## Leasing one

Honda doesn't sell the e:FCEV. It leases it in California only, through a small group of dealers near hydrogen stations, for $459 a month ($479 in 2026 dollars) over 3 years and 36,000 miles, with up to $15,000 of hydrogen fuel included ($15,700). Honda lists it at $50,000 ($52,200), but there's no purchase option on the lease. It's built at Honda's Performance Manufacturing Center in Marysville, Ohio, with a fuel cell module from Fuel Cell System Manufacturing, Honda's joint venture with GM in Brownstown, Michigan.

The big question isn't the car. It's whether the hydrogen stations near you will be open and working when you need them. California's network is small and has shrunk: Shell closed its stations in early 2024. Check the stations on your daily route and your longest regular trip, and how often they've been out of service, before you sign.

## What to check before you lease

Ask the dealer to confirm that every recall is done. The most serious is 25V858: on 388 2025 cars, coolant can leak into the fuel cell stack case and cause an internal short, cutting drive power, and the fix is a new fuel cell stack assembly. The e:FCEV is also in the steering gearbox recall (24V744) and the tire repair kit recall (26V366). Ask where the nearest dealer certified to service the fuel cell system is, and what happens if the car can't be fueled.

## The verdict

If you live and work near reliable hydrogen stations in California, and most of your driving is short enough to do on the battery, the e:FCEV is a genuinely clever, comfortable CR-V that can cost very little to fuel during its lease. Everyone else should choose [the CR-V Hybrid](../hybrid/).

::: curiosity "Made in Ohio, Driven in California"
The e:FCEV is the only fuel cell passenger car built in America. It's assembled in Marysville, Ohio, and its fuel cell comes from Michigan, but you can only lease it in California.
:::
