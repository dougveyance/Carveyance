A Super Duty odometer tells you less than any other number in trucks. These machines idle for hours, tow at max weight and get serviced by fleets or not at all, so the checklist is mostly forensic. Bring patience and ask for every receipt.

## Know your engine before you go

- **7.3 Power Stroke (1999–2003):** the legend, and mostly deserved. Check for oil-pan rust, cam-position-sensor spares in the glovebox (owners know), and injector o-ring leaks. High miles are fine; neglect is not.
- **6.0 Power Stroke (2003–2007):** the minefield. Head studs, EGR cooler, oil cooler — "bulletproofed" with invoices is a buyable truck, stock without records is a gamble priced accordingly. Walk away from any 6.0 whose seller can't discuss this history fluently.
- **6.4 Power Stroke (2008–2010):** thirsty, idle-averse, emissions hardware aging. Buy on condition and price, not promises.
- **6.7 Power Stroke (2011 on):** the good era. Confirm fuel-filter intervals and clean fuel sources for the CP4 pump, check for emissions-system fault history, and treat any "deleted" truck as a legal and warranty problem you're buying on purpose.
- **Gas V8s (6.2, 6.8, 7.3):** low drama. Plugs, oil history and exhaust-manifold ticks on cold start are the whole conversation.

## The front end, at speed

Solid front axles that tow heavy wear ball joints, unit bearings and track-bar bushings, and the end state is "death wobble" — a violent shimmy triggered by a bump at highway speed. Test drive at 65–70 mph over imperfect pavement; any shimmy, wander or steering-wheel slop means a front-end rebuild quote before any offer. Check tires for choppy wear (an alignment tell) and look underneath for fresh undercoating hiding tired parts.

## The forensic file

- **Hours vs miles:** many clusters show engine hours. 3,000 hours on a 60,000-mile truck means a life spent idling — price it like the 150,000-mile truck it mechanically is.
- **Towing history, in writing:** what trailer, how heavy, how far. Gooseneck pucks and worn receivers don't lie.
- **Recalls by VIN** at the dealer or NHTSA, closed and documented.
- **Frame and underbody** in salt states: the aluminum cab era (2017 on) protects the body, never the frame.
- **DEF and emissions records** on 2011-on diesels; a truck that lived on short trips regens often and wears accordingly.

The right Super Duty is out there wearing honest wear and a folder of receipts. The wrong one costs more than any truck on this site. Buy the folder.
