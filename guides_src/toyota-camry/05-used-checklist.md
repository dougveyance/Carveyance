The Camry's great strength as a used car is also its trap. Everyone knows it's reliable, so sellers price that in, and buyers stop asking questions. But a Camry can be neglected, crashed, flooded or driven 60,000 miles a year like any other car. Reliability is a reason to buy one, not a reason to skip the inspection.

## Pick the job first

For cheap, easy commuting, start with a 2018–2024 LE or SE 4-cylinder. If you drive a lot, especially in traffic, look at a 2018–2024 Hybrid LE, or a 2025–2026 LE if the budget stretches. If you want the Camry that's fun to drive, find a V6 XSE or TRD while they're still around. If you face snow every winter, look at a 2020–2024 AWD 4-cylinder or any 2025–2026 with AWD.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 | Last year of the seventh generation. 2.5-liter, 6-speed automatic, with V6 and hybrid versions | Age and mileage wear. Older cabin tech and older hybrid system |
| 2018–2020 | Eighth generation. New 2.5-liter and V6, 8-speed automatic, 52-mpg Hybrid LE | No CarPlay on 2018s unless a dealer added it. Brake vacuum pump recall (2018–2019) and fuel pump recall (2018–2020) |
| 2021–2024 | Refreshed eighth generation with a new screen and Toyota Safety Sense 2.5+ | Ordinary wear. V6 vacuum pump noise on some cars |
| 2025 | Ninth generation, hybrid only, AWD available on every grade | 2 instrument cluster software recalls and the inverter bolt recall |
| 2026 | Second year, adds the Nightshade Edition | Instrument cluster and inverter bolt recalls. Too new for long-term data |

## Body and history

Run a vehicle history report, but don't treat a clean one as proof. Look for uneven panel gaps, paint that doesn't quite match, overspray on rubber seals and replacement headlights on one side only. Check under the carpet in the trunk for water, rust or a dirt line, which can point to flood damage. If the car came from a state that salts its roads, look at the subframes, brake lines and rocker panels.

Plenty of Camrys spend their lives in fleet, rental or rideshare service. That isn't automatically a problem, since those cars often get regular maintenance, but it means hard miles. Look at the wear on the driver's seat, steering wheel and rear seats, and compare it with the odometer.

## Engine, transmission and hybrid system

Start the car cold if you can. On a 4-cylinder or V6, listen for ticking or knocking, watch for smoke and check that the 8-speed automatic shifts smoothly and promptly, without flares or harsh engagement. On a V6, listen at idle for the vacuum pump knock covered by Toyota service bulletin T-SB-0115-22.

On a hybrid, make sure the car starts in Ready mode quietly and that the gas engine starts and stops smoothly as you drive. There should be no warning lights. Ask a shop that knows hybrids to scan the system for stored codes, and check that the battery cooling intake isn't blocked. The hybrid chapter has more on battery warranties and what to check.

On every car, look for oil and coolant leaks, read the tire wear for signs of alignment trouble and listen for knocks over bumps. A worn tire on one edge can mean a bent part from a pothole or a curb.

## Electronics and safety tech

Test everything with a screen or a button. Pair your phone and try Apple CarPlay or Android Auto. The 2018 Camry launched without CarPlay, though Toyota later offered dealers a way to add it. CarPlay was built in from 2019, and Android Auto arrived for 2020. Check the backup camera, the radar cruise control, the lane departure warning and the blind spot monitor, if the car has one. On a 2025–2026, make sure the instrument cluster comes on every time you start the car, since that's the subject of 2 recalls.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2018–2019:** Certain cars were recalled because a part inside the brake vacuum pump could break and cause a sudden loss of brake assist (21V-890).
- **2018–2020:** Certain cars were included in Toyota's low-pressure fuel pump recall. A failing pump can stall the engine (20V-012, later expanded as 20V-682).
- **2025:** Certain cars with the 12.3-inch digital cluster may not show the speed and some warning lights at startup (25V-595).
- **2025–2026:** About 508,000 Camrys with the 7-inch cluster may start with a blank display and no turn signals (August 2026 recall). Certain hybrids built in late 2025 need an inverter bolt inspection (25V-869).
- **Every year:** New recalls can appear at any time. Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done. Recalls and service bulletins aren't the same thing, so ask about both.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly and hide the differences that matter. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, grade, engine, mileage and title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you, comparing a listing with similar ones nearby. Expect hybrids and V6 TRDs to carry a premium, and don't assume the premium is justified by the car's condition. A pre-purchase inspection is worth more than any seller's speech about Toyota reliability.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the car past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best value:** a documented 2021–2023 LE or SE 4-cylinder.
- **Lowest running costs:** a 2020–2024 Hybrid LE, with the 10-year battery warranty.
- **Snow-belt commuter:** a 2020–2024 AWD 4-cylinder, or a 2025–2026 LE AWD.
- **Enthusiast's pick:** a well-kept XSE V6 or TRD with full records.
- **Newest:** a 2025–2026 LE or XLE with every recall done.
