The 2007 Edge was Ford admitting the Explorer had become too much truck for most driveways. Built in Oakville on car underpinnings, it paired a 265-hp 3.5 Duratec with a 6-speed and a chrome-barred face that photographed like confidence, from $25,995 — about $42,200 in 2026 dollars. America bought 130,125 in the first full year and never dropped below 88,000 through the recession, which tells you the shape, size and ride height were precisely what the market had been waiting for.

The 2011 facelift brought 285 hp, capacitive MyFord Touch controls (the era's great patience trainer), and the range's 2 characters: the Sport, with the Mustang's 305-hp 3.7 and available 22-inch chrome wheels, and the 240-hp 2.0 EcoBoost, an early turbo-4 bet that returned 24 EPA combined when the V6 managed 22. Period tests put the Sport at 7.5 seconds to 60 — brisk for 2 tons of 2011 family furniture.

## Buying one now

These are now 12-to-19-year-old crossovers priced in the $4,000–$12,000 band, and the inspection is mostly mechanical bookkeeping. The known sore spot on early 3.5s is the internal water pump — driven off the timing chain, expensive when it lets go, so coolant-service records matter more than miles. Check PTU (transfer unit) fluid service on AWD cars, listen for wheel-bearing drone, confirm the panoramic roof drains flow, and on 2011-plus cars make sure every MyFord Touch screen actually responds — module replacements outlive the market's patience. The 2.0 EcoBoost wants oil-change proof like every turbo.

Buy the facelift if the budget allows: more power, better economy and the sorted 6-speed. A clean 285-hp SEL with records is the honest pick; a tidy Sport on the standard 20s (skip the cracked-prone 22s) is the one with a pulse.
