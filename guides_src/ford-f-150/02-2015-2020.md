In 2015, Ford did the thing rivals ran attack ads about: it built the body of America's best-selling vehicle from aluminum. The weight savings ran as high as 340 kg (750 lb), the frame stayed high-strength steel, and every number a truck buyer cares about improved. A decade on, the payoff for a used shopper is simple and unglamorous: these are half-ton trucks whose cabs and beds do not rust. In the salt states, that single fact should steer your search.

## The engines, honestly ranked

The 3.5 EcoBoost is the one the generation is remembered for, 365 hp through 2016 and 375 after, 450 in the 2019–20 Limited, and with the 10-speed that arrived for 2017, Car and Driver hit 60 mph in 5.7 seconds. It is also the strongest tower, rated to 5,897 kg (13,000 lb) properly equipped. The 2.7 EcoBoost is the sleeper: 325 hp, nearly as quick in the real world, EPA-rated 19 combined, and cheaper on the used lot. The 5.0 V8, 385 then 395 hp, trades a second to 60, Car and Driver ran 5.9, for natural aspiration and the nicest noise. The base 3.5 and later 3.3 V6 are honest fleet engines; buy one for a price, not for pleasure. The 3.0 Power Stroke diesel of 2018–20 is rated up to 22 combined with 4x4 but is rare and costlier to service; it makes sense only for owners who tow long distances constantly.

## Years and the 2018 line

The 2018 refresh is the pivot of the generation, more power nearly everywhere, the 10-speed spreading across the lineup, standard automatic emergency braking on most trims, and the diesel option. A 2018–2020 truck is the default recommendation. The 2015–2017 trucks are fine buys at the right price; prefer a 2017 for the 10-speed behind the 3.5. Trim-wise, the market pays premiums for Lariat and up; a clean XLT with the 302A package carries most of what matters for thousands less.

## What a used one should prove

Ask for towing history, not just mileage: an EcoBoost that spent its life at max tow wears differently than a commuter. Confirm the recalls are closed, there were several for seat belts, brake master cylinders on 2015–17 EcoBoost trucks, and tailgate latches, and scan for cam-phaser rattle on 2017-on 3.5s, covered in the checklist. One aluminum-specific note: body repairs cost more and demand a shop certified for the material, so walk away from crash damage repaired on the cheap. A paint-depth check and a look at the bed floor from below tell you most of what the seller won't.

The EPA rates the best of the generation, the 2.7 and 3.5 EcoBoost 4x4s, at 19 mpg combined, with the 5.0 at 16 and the Raptor, covered next chapter, at 16.
