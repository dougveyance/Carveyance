The Focus arrived in 2000 as the best-driving small car America could buy, sold a record 286,166 copies that first year, and spent 18 years proving that Ford could build a world-class compact — right up until Ford decided it didn't want to build compacts at all. The last US car left Michigan Assembly on May 4, 2018, which turned the whole nameplate into a used-market story with one giant catch and 3 glorious exceptions.

The catch is the PowerShift dual-clutch automatic in 2012–2018 cars, whose shudder generated thousands of lemon-law claims. The exceptions are the fast ones: the 170-hp SVT Focus of 2002–04, the 252-hp ST of 2013–18, and the 350-hp, all-wheel-drive Focus RS of 2016–18 — the first RS Ford ever let America buy, and a car that has already stopped depreciating.

## Quick picks

- **The rule before everything:** on 2012–2018 cars, buy the manual. Stick-shift cars dodge the PowerShift saga entirely.
- **Best all-rounder:** a 2015–2018 Titanium hatch with the manual — the class-benchmark chassis, grown-up cabin, low-teens money.
- **The driver's bargain:** a Focus ST. 252 hp, manual only, hot-hatch royalty still priced like a commuter.
- **The keeper:** a Focus RS, if the budget reaches. 4.6 to 60, Drift Mode, and values that have found their floor.
- **The cheap classic:** an SVT Focus — America's first real hot hatch of the modern era, now rare, cheap and quietly collectible.
- **Think twice if** the car you're looking at is a 2012–2016 automatic with no PowerShift paper trail. Walk, or price in a clutch job.
