The Grand Highlander isn't simply a stretched Highlander. Toyota designed it as its own vehicle, at its Calty design studios, and builds it at its plant in Princeton, Indiana, alongside its luxury sibling, the Lexus TX. The goal was a 3-row SUV that works for families of adults, with room for their luggage too, and on the numbers, it gets there.

## What makes it grand

The Grand Highlander is 201.4 inches long on a 116.1-inch wheelbase, 3.9 inches longer between the wheels than the Highlander. Overall, it's 6.5 inches longer, 2.3 inches wider and 2 inches taller. Most of that extra size goes to the third row, which has 5.5 inches more legroom than the Highlander's, and to the cargo area. With every seat up, there's 20.6 cu ft of space behind the third row, enough, Toyota says, for 7 carry-on suitcases.

Toyota also packed it with the small things families notice. There are 13 cupholders and 7 USB-C ports, including ports for the third row, and 3-zone automatic climate control is standard. Every Grand Highlander comes with Toyota Safety Sense 3.0.

## The numbers

[[chart:cargo]]

Chart 1 shows the difference in cargo space. The Grand Highlander gives you about 30% more room behind the third row than the Highlander, and about 13 cu ft more with all the rear seats folded. For a family that fills every seat, that's the difference between packing the roof box and leaving it at home.

## How it drives

For its size, the Grand Highlander drives well. It's quiet and comfortable on the highway, and its long wheelbase gives it a settled ride. Car and Driver measured 0.80 g of grip on the skidpad in both a gas XLE and a Hybrid MAX, a respectable result for a vehicle this big, though it noted that the gas model's all-season tires limit what the chassis can do. The real difference between versions comes from the powertrain, which is why each one has its own chapter.

## Grand Highlander or Highlander?

If the third row is mostly for occasional use and you want a smaller, easier-to-park SUV, the Highlander still makes sense. If you regularly carry 6 or 7 people, or adults in the third row, or you need luggage space with every seat filled, the Grand Highlander is the better choice. Toyota's pricing pushes in the same direction. For 2026, the Highlander dropped its cheaper grades and made all-wheel drive standard, and its starting price climbed above the Grand Highlander's. Toyota is also turning the 2027 Highlander into an electric SUV, which leaves the Grand Highlander as its gas and hybrid 3-row crossover.

## The verdict

The Grand Highlander does what the Highlander couldn't: it carries a full load of grown-ups and their bags in comfort. Choose your powertrain carefully, and check that the recalls covered in the checklist are done, and it's one of the most practical family vehicles Toyota sells.

::: curiosity The Lexus Twin
The Grand Highlander has a luxury twin, the Lexus TX, and the 2 are built in the same Indiana plant from many of the same parts. That cuts both ways. When Toyota found a fault with the driver's curtain airbag in 2024, it stopped sales of both, and the 2026 recalls for rear axle carriers and steering knuckles covered both too.
:::
