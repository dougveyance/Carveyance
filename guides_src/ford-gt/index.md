The GT is the rarest thing in this library: a Ford that trades like a Ferrari. The 2005–06 car was the company's 100th-birthday gift to itself — the GT40 shape reborn over a 550-hp supercharged V8 and a stick shift, $139,995 new and never once cheaper since. The 2017–22 car ran the logic in reverse: design the race car, win Le Mans in 2016 on the 50th anniversary of the 1966 sweep, then hand-build 1,350 road versions at Multimatic and make buyers apply for the privilege of paying $450,000.

Both are blue-chip collectibles now, which makes this guide less about rust and more about provenance, mileage math and which editions lead the market. The short version: you can't really buy one wrong, but you can absolutely buy one expensively.

## Quick picks

- **The one to drive:** a 2005–06 GT with miles already on it. Docile, stout, serviced with Ford parts — the rare supercar that rewards actually driving.
- **The blue chip:** a 2006 Heritage — 346 built in Gulf colors, the most wanted first-generation car at every auction.
- **The modern masterpiece:** any 2017–22 GT. Only 1,350 exist, and the Le Mans provenance is baked into every one.
- **The trophy tier:** Liquid Carbon (exposed weave, $750,000 new), the Heritage liveries, or the track-only Mk II and Mk IV — 45 and 67 built.
- **The honest warning:** flipped-contract cars and sub-100-mile garage queens trade on paper, not driving. Know which collector you are before bidding.
