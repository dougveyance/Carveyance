The 2021 redesign kept the aluminum body and changed the question a truck answers. Every F-150 before it carried things; this one also powers things. The PowerBoost hybrid pairs the 3.5 EcoBoost with an electric motor inside the 10-speed for 430 hp and 570 lb-ft, and feeds Pro Power Onboard, up to 7.2 kilowatts of real outlets in the bed. Contractors run saws off it. Tailgaters run televisions. During the Texas grid failure of February 2021, owners ran their houses for days. No rival had an answer then, and none has a better one now.

## Pick the engine first

Trims are taste; the engine decides what the truck is. The 2.7 EcoBoost, 325 hp, is the quiet bargain, rated at 20–21 mpg combined with 4-wheel drive and strong enough that most owners never miss more. The 3.5 EcoBoost, 400 hp and 500 lb-ft, is the towing engine and the Tremor's standard fit; Edmunds ran a Tremor to 60 in 5.8 seconds. The 5.0 V8, 400 hp, is the sentimental pick and the last naturally aspirated V8 in any half-ton; MotorTrend's test ran 6.2 seconds and the EPA says 19 mpg. The PowerBoost does everything at once, 5.3 to 60 in MotorTrend's hands and 24 mpg combined, and is the one this guide would buy. The 3.0 Power Stroke diesel left after 2021; the all-electric Lightning, covered in its own guide, was dropped after 2025.

## What changed when

The 2021–2023 trucks and the 2024–2026 trucks are one generation with two faces. The 2024 refresh brought new grilles and lights, the 12-inch screen on every trim, a 5G modem, and the clever Pro Access tailgate, which swings 100 degrees with a hitch in place. For 2026 the lineup runs XL ($40,085), STX, XLT, Lariat, Tremor, King Ranch, Platinum, Raptor and Raptor R, with the hybrid spread across more trims than ever and upper trims newly available with rear-wheel drive for less money.

Properly equipped, a 2026 F-150 tows up to 6,123 kg (13,500 lb), the hybrid up to 5,126 kg (11,300 lb). As always, the number on the window sticker never replaces the payload label on the door jamb, the tongue weight, or the specific tow package the truck actually has. Verify, don't assume.

## On a test drive

The 10-speed should shift cleanly once warm; a consistent shudder or clunk, especially 2021–2022, deserves a scan for the transmission recalls and software updates, including the large 2026 park-system recall. On a PowerBoost, watch the gas-to-electric handoff at parking speeds, where early software was clumsiest, and test every Pro Power outlet with a real load. On any EcoBoost, listen cold: a brief rattle at startup is the known timing-chain and cam-phaser conversation, documented in the checklist chapter.
