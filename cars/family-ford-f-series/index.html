The Ford F-Series family

17 generations and variants in the Index, 1948–present. Oldest first; every card opens the full page, with its own versions, figures and story.

  1. 1948–1952

    Ford F-1 (first generation F-Series)

    Ford's first purpose-built postwar truck, 1948: the Bonus-Built F-1 started the F-Series, with a flathead V8, a wider cab and a line that ran to the 3-ton F-8.

    100 hp21.0 sec 0-6078 mph top
    226 six · 239 V8 · 215 six
  2. 1953–1956

    Ford F-100 (second generation)

    The F-1 becomes the F-100, 1953: the prettiest early F-Series, the first with an automatic, and the truck hot-rodders have never stopped building.

    167 hp17.0 sec 0-6085 mph top
    223 six · 239 flathead V8 · 239 Y-block V8 · 272 Y-block V8
  3. 1957–1960

    Ford F-100 (third generation)

    Fenders go flush, 1957: the first modern-looking F-Series, the first Styleside bed, and from 1959 the first factory-built Ford 4x4.

    186 hp16.0 sec 0-6088 mph top
    223 six · 272 V8 · 292 V8
  4. 1961–1966

    Ford F-100 (fourth generation)

    Longer, lower, and briefly seamless: the 1961–66 trucks tried a unibody bed, invented Twin I-Beam suspension in 1965, and introduced the 300 six that would run for 31 years.

    208 hp14.0 sec 0-6090 mph top
    240 six · 300 six · 352 V8
  5. 1967–1972

    Ford F-100 (fifth generation)

    The “bumpside” trucks, 1967–72: cleaner, roomier, V8-heavy, and the generation that turned the pickup from pure tool into something people chose to drive.

    255 hp12.5 sec 0-6095 mph top
    300 six · 302 V8 · 360 V8 · 390 V8
  6. 1973–1979

    Ford F-100 / F-150 (sixth generation)

    The F-150 is born here, 1975, invented to dodge emissions rules, and by 1977 the F-Series was America's best-selling truck. It never gave the title back.

    245 hp10.5 sec 0-60100 mph top
    300 six · 302 V8 · 351M V8 · 400 V8 · 460 V8
  7. 1980–1986

    Ford F-150 (seventh generation)

    The 1980 “bullnose” redesign chased fuel economy, retired the F-100 in 1983, and in 1985 put the first fuel-injected engine in an American pickup.

    190 hp12.0 sec 0-6090 mph top
    4.9 (300) six · 5.0 V8, carbureted · 5.0 V8, fuel injection · 5.8 V8 High Output
  8. 1987–1991

    Ford F-150 (eighth generation)

    A smoothed nose, flush headlights and full fuel injection: the 1987–91 trucks modernized the F-150 without changing what anyone loved about it.

    185 hp10.5 sec 0-6095 mph top
    4.9 six · 5.0 V8 · 5.8 V8
  9. 1992–1996

    Ford F-150 (ninth generation)

    The “OBS” Ford, 1992–96: the last of the old-school F-150s, the first with an airbag, and the body style a generation of truck people now worships.

    205 hp10.0 sec 0-6096 mph top
    4.9 six · 5.0 V8 · 5.8 V8
  10. 1993–2004

    Ford SVT Lightning

    SVT's street truck, twice over: a 240-hp 5.8 in 1993, then a supercharged 380-hp 5.4 that made it the fastest production pickup in the world.

    380 hp5.2 sec 0-60147 mph top39,687 built
    Lightning, 1993–95 · Lightning, 1999–2000 · Lightning, 2001–04
  11. 1997–2004

    Ford F-150 (tenth generation)

    The 1997 truck went aero, went overhead-cam and went personal-use: the best-selling years in F-Series history, including 911,597 trucks in 2001 alone.

    260 hp8.5 sec 0-6098 mph top5,899,341 built
    4.2 V6 · 4.6 Triton V8 · 5.4 Triton V8
  12. 2004–2008

    Ford F-150 (eleventh generation)

    Bigger, stiffer, 300 hp: the 2004 truck doubled down on the boom years, then rode the F-Series through the crash that cut its sales nearly in half.

    300 hp8.2 sec 0-6098 mph top3,843,115 built
    4.2 V6 · 4.6 Triton V8 · 5.4 Triton 3V
  13. 2009–2014

    Ford F-150 (twelfth generation)

    Out of the crash came the big bets: the 2011 EcoBoost put twin turbos in America's best-seller, and the 2010 SVT Raptor invented the factory desert truck.

    365 hp6.2 sec 0-6096 mph top3,689,460 built
    5.4 Triton · 3.7 V6 · 5.0 Coyote V8 · 3.5 EcoBoost · 6.2 V8
  14. 2010–present

    Ford F-150 Raptor

    The factory Baja truck, 2010 on: long-travel suspension, desert pace, and since 2023 a 700-plus-hp supercharged V8 that runs 0–60 in 3.6 seconds.

    720 hp3.6 sec 0-60114 mph top
    SVT Raptor 6.2 · Raptor, 2nd generation · Raptor, 3rd generation · Raptor R
  15. 2015–2020

    Ford F-150 (thirteenth generation)

    Ford bet the best-selling vehicle in America on aluminum, cut up to 750 pounds, and won: the 2018 trucks nearly broke the all-time sales record.

    375 hp5.7 sec 0-60107 mph top5,091,145 built
    3.5 V6, then 3.3 V6 · 2.7 EcoBoost · 5.0 Coyote V8 · 3.5 EcoBoost · 3.0 Power Stroke diesel
  16. 2021–present

    Ford F-150 (fourteenth generation)

    The current truck: hybrid PowerBoost, an onboard power plant, a 2024 refresh, and still the best-selling vehicle in America, 828,832 strong in 2025.

    430 hp5.3 sec 0-60107 mph top3,725,231 built
    2.7 EcoBoost · 5.0 Coyote V8 · 3.5 EcoBoost · 3.5 PowerBoost hybrid · 3.0 Power Stroke diesel
  17. 2022–2025

    Ford F-150 Lightning

    The electric F-150, 2022–2025: 580 hp, 4.0 seconds to 60, a front trunk and a house-powering battery — killed after 4 model years and about 100,000 trucks.

    580 hp4.0 sec 0-60110 mph top100,659 built
    Standard range · Extended range · Platinum
