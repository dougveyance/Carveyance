This is the chapter to read before you buy any 2022–2024 gas Tundra. It isn't a reason to avoid them all. It's a reason to know exactly what's been done to the one in front of you.

## What went wrong

The twin-turbo V6 in the gas Tundra is known inside Toyota as the V35A. On some of these engines, machining debris wasn't fully cleared out during manufacturing. The latest recall report traces the most recent batch to engines built at Toyota's plant in Alabama. That debris can damage the main bearings that the crankshaft turns on. The first signs can be knocking, rough running or an engine that won't start. In the worst case the engine stalls while you're driving, and a loss of power at speed raises the risk of a crash.

## 3 recalls, one problem

[[chart:recall]]

- **24V-381, May 2024:** about 102,000 2022–2023 Tundras and Lexus LX 600s. The remedy was a replacement engine.
- **25V-767, November 2025:** an expansion to about 126,700 vehicles, including about 113,000 2022–2024 Tundras plus Lexus LX and GX models. At first, Toyota said the remedy was still being developed.
- **26V-320, May 2026:** about 43,600 more 2024 Tundras, built after the earlier cutoff but before Toyota improved the number 1 main bearing. Owner letters went out from July 2026.

All 3 list the gas Tundra. NHTSA files the hybrid as a separate model, and it isn't named in these campaigns. Check any Tundra's VIN anyway.

## The remedy

For the 2 later recalls, Toyota released its fix in phases through 2026. A dealer runs inspection software that checks the number 1 main bearing and collects the truck's drive data. If the software can't confirm the bearing is free of abnormal wear, the dealer replaces the engine. If it can, the truck goes back to the owner with its original engine. The repair is free either way. By late October 2026, Toyota had listed the remedy as available for about 49,000 trucks, and more phases are still to come.

That inspection-first approach has frustrated some owners, who wanted a guaranteed new engine. For a buyer, it means 2 trucks covered by the same recall can leave the dealer with different answers, so the paperwork matters as much as the recall number.

## What to ask before you buy

- **Which campaigns apply?** Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and at a Toyota dealer. A truck can be covered by more than one.
- **Is the remedy available yet for this VIN?** If not, you'll be driving a recalled truck until it is.
- **What was done?** Ask for the repair order. If the engine was replaced, ask for the documentation and any warranty on the new engine. If it passed the inspection, ask for the result.
- **Any symptoms?** Listen for knocking at idle and under load, and walk away from any truck that runs roughly or has a check-engine light the seller can't explain.
- **Is it in writing?** If you're buying from a dealer, put the recall status in the purchase agreement.

## How this changes the buying decision

A 2025 or 2026 gas Tundra sidesteps the issue, because the recalls so far stop at 2024 trucks. A 2022–2024 gas truck with its campaigns completed and documented can be a fair buy, and its price should already reflect the uncertainty. A truck with an open recall and no remedy yet available is a harder call. Unless the price makes up for the wait and the risk, it's one to pass on. The hybrid, the old 5.7-liter V8 and the newer gas trucks are the ways around it.
