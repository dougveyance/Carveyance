Honda built the Clarity as a single 5-seat midsize sedan designed to take 3 different powertrains. The Clarity Fuel Cell, running on hydrogen, came first, in December 2016. The Clarity Electric, a battery electric car, followed in August 2017, and the Clarity Plug-in Hybrid, with a battery you charge from the grid and a gasoline engine for longer trips, in December 2017. All 3 were built in Sayama, Japan.

They look almost the same, but they're very different used cars. The Plug-in Hybrid was sold everywhere in the US, and it's the one most buyers will find and the one most can live with. The Electric was leased only in California and Oregon, with an EPA range of 89 miles. The Fuel Cell was leased only in California, and it needs hydrogen stations near where you drive.

Honda dropped the Electric in early 2020 and ended production of the Plug-in Hybrid and Fuel Cell in August 2021, when it closed the Sayama plant. GoodCarBadCar counts about 40,800 Clarity sales in the US from 2017 to 2022.

This guide has 4 chapters: the Plug-in Hybrid, the Electric, the Fuel Cell, and the checklist to use before you buy.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder:** a Clarity Plug-in Hybrid, if you can charge at home. It covers 47 miles on electricity, then runs as a hybrid.
- **Most equipment:** a Plug-in Hybrid Touring, with leather and navigation.
- **Only if your driving is short and predictable:** a Clarity Electric, with 89 miles of EPA range when new and a title you've confirmed is clean.
- **Only if you live near reliable hydrogen stations:** a Clarity Fuel Cell. Confirm you can buy, register and fuel it before anything else.
