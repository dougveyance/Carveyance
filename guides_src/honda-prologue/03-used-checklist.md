A used Prologue is a fairly simple car to check, with no engine, gearbox or timing belt, but the things that matter most are invisible from the curb: the battery's health, how the car charges and whether its recalls and software updates are done. A clean car with a good battery report and a full charging test is worth more than a shiny one without them.

## First, know which one it is

Decode the VIN to confirm the year, the trim and the drivetrain. A 2025 or 2026 has the upgraded front motor and the longer range. An all-wheel-drive Prologue has a second motor at the rear; the Elite is always all-wheel drive and has 21-inch wheels.

## The years at a glance

| Model year | What it is | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 | First year. 212 hp FWD, 288 hp AWD, up to 296 miles | Front lower control arm (24V540). Instrument panel and camera software (26V112). Rearview camera (26V306) |
| 2025 | New front drive unit. 220 hp FWD, 300 hp AWD, up to 308 miles | Rearview camera (26V306) |
| 2026 | Carryover; $7,500 price cut from April 2026; last year | Check by VIN |

## The battery

The range shown on the dashboard isn't a battery health test: it changes with the weather, the last few drives and the car's learned efficiency. Ask a Honda dealer or a qualified shop for a state-of-health report on the high-voltage battery, and compare it with the warranty terms; Honda covers the battery for 8 years or 100,000 miles. Ask the seller how the car was charged, whether it was stored for long periods at a very high or very low charge, and whether it has had any repairs near the battery case under the floor.

## Prove that it charges

Plug it into a Level 2 charger and watch it charge, not just connect. If you can, do a DC fast-charge session as well, and check that the charge port latches, the session starts and stops normally and the car shows the right charging rate. Fast-charging speed depends on the battery's temperature and charge level, so a short session isn't a full test of peak power. Ask for the portable charging cable, and the NACS adapter if the seller has one.

## The ordinary car checks

An electric car still wears out tires and brakes, and a heavy one is hard on tires. Check all 4 for tread depth and even wear, the wheels for bends and the suspension for knocks. Look under the car for scrapes or dents near the battery case. Test the cameras, the climate control, the powered tailgate and the phone connections, and make sure the previous owner's app account and profiles have been removed.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2024:** 24V540, a right front lower control arm that may have a manufacturing defect and could fracture. 26V112, software faults that can blank the instrument panel or the rearview camera.
- **2024–2025:** 26V306, a rearview camera that can show a distorted or blank image. Honda replaces the camera.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls), ask a Honda dealer for the car's open campaigns and software updates, and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## What it costs to run

Electricity is the big saving, if you charge at home. At about 3 miles per kWh, 12,000 miles a year takes about 4,000 kWh, or roughly $800 a year at 20 cents per kWh, before charging losses. Fast charging on a road trip usually costs much more per mile than home charging, and if you can't charge at home at all, much of the saving disappears.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title, flood damage, a damaged battery case, a warning light the seller can't explain, a charging fault, or open safety recalls with no repair date. Walk away from any seller who won't let a dealer or independent shop inspect it.

## What is it worth?

[[chart:price]]

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and used electric car prices have moved fast. The simplest test is the new price: a 2026 EX started at $39,900 after the April 2026 cut, so a used 2024 or 2025 needs to be priced well below a new or leftover 2026 with the same drivetrain. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, trim and drivetrain, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Chevrolet Blazer EV, Hyundai Ioniq 5 or Tesla Model Y of the same age.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Prologue past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for electric drive parts, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2025 or 2026 EX or Touring with front-wheel drive.
- **Best value:** a 2026 EX at or below its April 2026 price, or a used one priced against it.
- **Best in snow:** a 2025 or 2026 with all-wheel drive.
- **Cheapest way in:** a 2024 with its recalls done, a good battery report and a charging test.
