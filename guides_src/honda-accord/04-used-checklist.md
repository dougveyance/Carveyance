The Accord's reputation is earned, but it can't tell you whether this particular car had its oil changed, its timing belt replaced or its recalls done. Shopping for a used Accord also means choosing between 3 generations, a V6, 2 turbo engines and 3 kinds of hybrid. Start by pinning down exactly which one you're looking at.

## First, know which one it is

Trim names and engines changed from year to year, so don't trust a listing's description. A "Sport" can have a 2.4-liter (2017), a 1.5-liter turbo or a 2.0-liter turbo (2018–2022), or a hybrid (2023 and later). Decode the VIN to confirm the year, engine and transmission, and check the window sticker if the seller has it. On a 2017, confirm whether the car has Honda Sensing, which was optional.

## The test drive

Start the car from cold and listen for rough running, rattles and belt noise as it warms up. Drive long enough for the engine and transmission to reach full temperature, then try gentle and hard acceleration. A CVT should feel smooth and free of shudders, and a 10-speed automatic shouldn't lurch or hunt between gears. On a manual, check where the clutch bites and listen for grinding. Run the air conditioning at full cold, both standing still and in traffic. Pair your phone, test the cameras and make sure no driver-assistance warnings come on.

## Engine checks

On a 2017 V6, ask for proof that the timing belt and water pump were replaced when the Maintenance Minder called for them; without it, budget for the job. On a 1.5-liter turbo, look for signs of coolant loss, misfires or an overheating history, and ask how often the oil was changed. On a 2.0T, ask whether the engine software has been tuned, look for aftermarket intake and exhaust parts, and check the tires for uneven wear. Modified cars aren't always bad cars, but they need a closer inspection and a lower price.

## Hybrid checks

Have a Honda dealer or a hybrid specialist scan the hybrid system for stored codes. The engine should start and stop smoothly, without shudders, and no hybrid warnings should appear on the dash. Check that the battery's cooling intake isn't blocked by objects or dirt. On a 2023 or later hybrid, confirm the control module software recall (25V785) and the tire repair kit recall (26V366) are done.

## The A/C warranty

Honda extended the warranty on the air conditioning condenser of 2018–2020 Accords and Accord Hybrids to 10 years with unlimited miles, for leaks caused by a manufacturing defect. It doesn't cover damage, such as a condenser punctured by road debris. If the air conditioning blows warm, ask a Honda dealer to check the VIN before you agree to a price.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2015–2017 V6:** 19V060. Particles in the fuel can reduce fuel pump performance and stall the engine. The fix is engine software and, if needed, a new pump.
- **2013–2023:** 23V858. The fuel pump in the tank may fail. Several other fuel pump recalls also cover 2018–2020 cars (20V314, 21V215).
- **2018–2020:** 20V771. Body control module software can disrupt the wipers, defroster, lights, windows and rear camera. 18V629 (2018): the rear camera image may not display.
- **2018–2019:** 23V158. The front seat belt buckles may not latch.
- **2016–2022:** 26V332, and 24V064 for 2020–2022. The front passenger seat weight sensor can crack, so the passenger air bag may not deploy or switch off correctly.
- **2021:** 21V900. The second-row center seat belt's locking mode can switch off, so a child seat may not be held securely.
- **2022:** 23V430. The stability control unit can leak brake fluid.
- **2023–2024:** 24V763, a high-pressure fuel pump that may crack and leak fuel; 24V859 and 26V054 (2023), a driver's seat cushion frame that may not be tightened; and 23V782, front seat belt pretensioners that may be missing a rivet.
- **2023–2025 Hybrid:** 25V785. The integrated control module can reset while driving and cut drive power. The fix is a software update. 23V588: 19 2023 hybrids got a faulty transmission, which Honda replaced.
- **2023–2026 Hybrid:** 26V366. The tire repair kit's sealant bottle can build pressure and its cap can fly off. Some cars repaired under it need the fix again (26V663).
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title, flood damage or an odometer that doesn't match its records. Walk away from hybrid warning lights the seller can't explain, from a 2.0T whose seller won't say what has been modified, and from any car whose seller won't allow an independent inspection.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and an average that mixes a 2017 LX with a 2022 Touring 2.0T tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same generation, engine, transmission, trim and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Toyota Camry of the same age, especially a hybrid, and take the cost of tires, brakes and overdue service off the price. On a 2017 V6 without records, take off the cost of a timing belt as well.

[[chart:fuel]]

Should you pay more for a hybrid? Work out your own fuel bill: divide the miles you drive in a year by each car's combined mpg, then multiply the difference by what you pay for fuel. At 15,000 miles a year, a 32-mpg 1.5-liter uses about 156 more gallons than a 48-mpg Hybrid EX-L. At 6,000 miles, the gap is about 63 gallons.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Accord past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for turbochargers and the hybrid system, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2023 or later Accord Hybrid EX-L with its recalls done.
- **Best value:** a 2021–2022 Sport or Sport SE with the 1.5-liter turbo and records.
- **Most fun:** a 2018–2022 Sport 2.0T or Touring 2.0T that hasn't been modified.
- **For long commutes:** a 2019–2022 Accord Hybrid with a clean hybrid system scan.
- **The last V6:** a 2017 V6 with its timing belt records.
