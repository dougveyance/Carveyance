The Tremor and the Lobo point in opposite directions. The Tremor is for owners who value off-road geometry, traction hardware and underbody protection. The Lobo is a street truck: lower, firmer and quicker on a back road. Both make the Maverick a more specialized choice, and neither should be bought just because the badge sounds adventurous.

## Tremor

Ford announced the Tremor on August 1, 2022, as an off-road package for 2023 EcoBoost Mavericks with all-wheel drive. It borrows the Bronco Sport Badlands' twin-clutch rear drive unit, which can lock the rear wheels together, and adds off-road suspension, more ground clearance, skid plates and all-terrain tires. For 2025 the Tremor became its own model rather than a package. Car and Driver ran a 2023 Tremor to 60 mph in 6.0 seconds. The EPA rated the 2023–2024 Tremor at 21 mpg combined and the 2025–2026 Tremor at 23.

It isn't a substitute for a body-on-frame Bronco or Ranger, and a used one deserves a close look for tire damage and underbody impacts after trail use. The 2025 Tremor started at $40,995 before delivery, or $42,800 in 2026 dollars.

## Lobo

The Lobo arrived for 2025 as a performance street truck. It has the 2.0-liter EcoBoost with a 7-speed automatic and paddle shifters, in place of the other EcoBoosts' 8-speed, all-wheel drive with a twin-clutch rear drive unit, a lower ride height, retuned springs, shocks and steering, bigger brakes and 19-inch wheels. It also has a Lobo drive mode, meant for closed courses, that sharpens its cornering. Car and Driver ran a 2025 Lobo to 60 mph in 5.8 seconds, the quickest Maverick it has tested, and the EPA rates it at 24 mpg combined.

## Which one?

Buy the Tremor if you'll really use the ground clearance on dirt roads and trails. Buy the Lobo if you like to drive and rarely leave the pavement. For everything else, a regular Maverick does the job for less money and better fuel economy.

## Used checks

On a Tremor, look at the skid plates, the control arms, the underbody shields and the tires. On a Lobo, check the wheels, the brakes, the tires and any suspension changes, and ask how it was driven: a closed-course drive mode invites track days. On both, make sure the rear drive unit works as the owner's manual describes.
