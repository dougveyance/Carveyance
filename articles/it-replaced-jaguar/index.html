Jaguar Didn't Replace the XJ. It Replaced Jaguar.

The 1,015-hp Type 01 isn't an electric successor to Jaguar's old sedans. It's an attempt to erase the company's old position and build a far more exclusive brand.

By Nathaniel Ruiz · 6 minute read

For years, Jaguar sold against BMW, Mercedes-Benz and Audi while insisting it was something more emotional, more daring and more British. Buyers increasingly stopped believing it.

Jaguar's sedans were often beautiful and rewarding to drive, but they lived in the shadow of better-selling German rivals. The F-Pace gave Jaguar an important SUV, the F-Type supplied the theater, and the electric I-Pace arrived in 2018, ahead of most established luxury EVs. None of them gave Jaguar the scale or the finances it needed. So Jaguar didn't replace those cars one by one. It stopped selling new cars in Britain at the end of 2024 and set out to replace the brand itself.

The Type 01 is the first physical proof that the reinvention goes beyond provocative advertising and the pink Type 00 concept shown in December 2024. It's a big four-door electric grand tourer with up to 1,015 hp and 1,365 Nm (1,007 lb-ft) of torque. US prices start at $130,500, and £130,000 in Britain. Orders open in early 2027, with deliveries in the second half of the year. This is no longer the Jaguar that tried to sell an XE next to a BMW 3 Series.

A long hood with no engine under it

The Type 01's proportions are deliberately irrational: a low roof, a very long hood, a long rear deck and 23-inch wheels. There's no gasoline engine under that hood that needs the space. The shape is there because Jaguar wants the car to look like a dramatic front-engine grand tourer, not an aerodynamic electric appliance. That choice will divide people. Some will say an EV should use its compact motors to maximize cabin space and efficiency. Others will understand that luxury cars are rarely bought because every inch was assigned with perfect logic. A Bentley Continental GT doesn't need its proportions, and a Rolls-Royce doesn't need its grille. Emotional excess is part of the product, and Jaguar is betting that presence is worth more than packaging. The rear is just as uncompromising. There's no rear window at all; a camera feeds the rearview mirror instead. Active aerodynamics, including moving vanes in the front vents and a 3-position rear spoiler, help Jaguar claim a drag coefficient of 0.23, low for a car this dramatic on wheels this big.

The numbers are serious

Underneath is Jaguar's new electrical architecture. 3 motors, one at the front and 2 at the rear, drive all 4 wheels and can shift torque from side to side. Jaguar quotes 0 to 60 mph in 3.1 seconds, very quick, though the Type 01 isn't chasing the most violent numbers from Tesla, Lucid or electric hypercars. Its priorities look like effortless speed and long-distance stability. The usable battery holds 118 kWh. Jaguar estimates up to 400 miles of range on the optional 21-inch wheels, and it also gives a more revealing number: about 320 miles at a steady 75 mph. For a grand tourer, that highway figure may matter more to owners than the bigger one, because a GT is supposed to cross countries, not post good numbers around town. An 850-volt system supports charging at up to 350 kW, which Jaguar says can add about 200 miles in 13 minutes. Every one of these figures is Jaguar's own, pending independent testing and certification.

Jaguar isn't only asking buyers to accept a new car. It's asking them to accept Jaguar as a different kind of company.

Jaguar is moving above its old rivals

The Type 01 isn't meant to bring back Jaguar's old sales volume. It's meant to make a much smaller volume pay, through much higher prices. That moves Jaguar out of BMW's and Mercedes's mainstream and toward the image, if not yet the prices, of Bentley and Aston Martin. It's a risky strategy, but staying where it was may have been riskier. Jaguar didn't have the scale to compete profitably with companies that share platforms, engines, electronics and software across millions of vehicles. An XE couldn't match the development budget behind a BMW 3 Series, and an electric XE would have faced the same problem. A smaller, more exclusive Jaguar needs fewer customers. It can spend more on each car, take unconventional design decisions and sell an experience instead of winning a spreadsheet comparison.

But higher prices bring higher expectations. At $130,500, beautiful materials and dramatic design are just the entry fee. The software has to work flawlessly, charging has to be repeatable, and the ride, the quiet and the way the dealer treats you have to feel clearly better than in an ordinary premium EV. Jaguar isn't only asking buyers to accept a new car. It's asking them to accept Jaguar as a different kind of company.

The real test isn't horsepower

More than 1,000 hp makes an excellent headline, but it doesn't guarantee anyone will want the car. Electric motors have made enormous outputs surprisingly easy to produce. The Type 01 will be judged on the things that don't fit in a headline: the steering, the brakes, the ride, the seats, the view out, the software, and how naturally it carries its considerable mass. The old Jaguar promise was never just speed. It was grace, space and pace, the idea that performance should arrive without brutality. The Type 01 has to carry that idea into an electric car, and convince buyers that Jaguar's disappearance was a beginning rather than an ending.

Jaguar didn't replace the XJ, the XF or the F-Type. It replaced Jaguar. Whether customers want the new one will decide whether this becomes one of the industry's bravest reinventions or one of its most expensive identity crises.

The short version

The Type 01 is a 1,015-hp electric grand tourer from $130,500, with Jaguar's estimate of up to 400 miles of range. It isn't a successor to the XJ or the XE. It's Jaguar's attempt to leave the BMW and Mercedes fight it couldn't win on scale and to sell fewer, far more expensive cars. The numbers are serious; the test is whether the car feels worth the price, and whether buyers accept a Jaguar that's a different company.
