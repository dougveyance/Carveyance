Every Super Duty conversation arrives here. The diesel premium on a new truck runs roughly $12,000 before the High Output's further step, and the used market preserves most of that gap, so the question deserves arithmetic instead of identity. The short version: the trailer decides.

## When the diesel wins

Above roughly 5,443 kg (12,000 lb) of regular trailer weight, or any serious fifth-wheel and gooseneck work, the 6.7 Power Stroke is the correct tool and nothing else is close. 1,050 lb-ft moves mountains at 1,600 rpm, the exhaust brake saves your service brakes on grades, and resale returns a real share of the premium when you sell. Fuel economy under load is the diesel's quiet win: owners report towing mileage the gas trucks cannot approach, and the bigger tank range to match. If the truck is the business — horses, equipment, a camper that lives attached — buy the diesel and stop reading.

## When the gas V8 wins

Below that threshold, the 7.3 Godzilla makes a devastating case. It costs 5 figures less up front, drinks cheaper fuel, needs no DEF, no particulate filter, no regens and no $2,000 CP4 insurance policy, and its service schedule reads like a 1995 pickup's. 430 hp and 485 lb-ft tow 4,500-kg (10,000-lb) class trailers without drama, just more revs and more frequent fuel stops. For plow trucks, service bodies, boats and the owner who tows 1 weekend a month, the gas truck is not the compromise — it is the right answer that diesel culture talks people out of.

## The ownership fine print

Diesel ownership is a relationship: DEF by the gallon, fuel filters on schedule, and the CP4 high-pressure pump's known intolerance of water or bad fuel, which is why disciplined owners buy from busy stations and keep receipts. Emissions-system repairs out of warranty are the big-ticket risk on any modern diesel, Ford's included. Gas ownership is a transaction: fuel, oil, plugs. Neither is wrong; they are different bills on different schedules. Price both over your real ownership horizon, then let the trailer cast the deciding vote.
