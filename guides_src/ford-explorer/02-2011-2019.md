After the truck-SUV market collapsed, the 2011 Explorer returned as something its own ancestors wouldn't recognize: a unibody, front-drive-based crossover out of Chicago Assembly. Purists mourned; 1.74 million buyers did not. This is now the default used 3-row in America, which means selection is endless and knowledge is leverage.

## Engines, best to skip

The **3.5 Duratec V6**, 290 hp, is the honest default — unstressed, familiar, 19 mpg combined with all-wheel drive. The **2.3 EcoBoost** (2016–19, 280 hp) is the pick of the 4-cylinders, quicker and thriftier at 21 combined, and available with AWD, which the earlier **2.0 EcoBoost** (2012–15, 240 hp, front-drive only) never was. The **Sport** (2013 on) is the one enthusiasts hunt: the 365-hp 3.5 EcoBoost from the F-150, 5.7 seconds to 60 in period tests, covered properly in the next chapter. If a V6 truck you're looking at has a water-pump history, read the checklist chapter before anything else — it is this generation's defining repair.

## The 2016 line

The 2016 refresh is this generation's version of a model change: new nose, far nicer cabin, the 2.3 EcoBoost, and the Platinum trim with the Sport's 365-hp engine in luxury dress. A 2016–2019 truck costs meaningfully more than a 2011–2015 and is worth the difference almost every time — the interiors age better and the options lists run deeper. Among early trucks, 2013 is the year the MyFord Touch complaints began to settle and the Sport arrived; 2011s and 2012s are price plays only.

## What they're like to live with

Roomy, quiet, a little wide-feeling on narrow roads, and heavier than they look — these are 2,085-kg (4,597-lb) vehicles that work their brakes and front tires. The third row fits actual humans, the Police Interceptor version proved the platform's durability in the hardest duty there is, and resale stays strong because demand never fades. Buy on maintenance records and the checklist, not on trim-badge glamour: an XLT with receipts beats a Limited without them, every single time.
