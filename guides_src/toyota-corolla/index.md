The Corolla has spent most of its life as the car you buy when you need a car. That's not an insult. Toyota had sold more than 46 million of them worldwide by the time the 12th generation arrived, and the reasons are the obvious ones: it's cheap to run, easy to live with and has a strong reputation for reliability.

What's changed is the range. Between 2017 and 2026 the Corolla name covered a plain sedan, a hatchback that started life as a Scion, the first Corolla Hybrid sold in the US, a hybrid with all-wheel drive and a 300-hp, turbocharged, all-wheel-drive hot hatch built for people who like rally stages. This guide has 6 chapters. Start with the one that matches what you're shopping for, or go straight to the checklist if you're about to look at one.

## Quick picks

- **Best value:** a documented 2023–2024 LE. It has the 169-hp 2.0-liter, Toyota Safety Sense 3.0 and the newer touchscreen.
- **Best for high mileage:** a Corolla Hybrid. A 2020–2022 is rated at 52 mpg, and a 2023 or later adds available all-wheel drive.
- **Most fun for the money:** a 2019–2022 Corolla Hatchback SE or XSE, especially with the 6-speed manual.
- **Most fun, full stop:** the GR Corolla. It's a very different car at a very different price.
- **Think twice if** you need rear-seat room or a big trunk. The hatchback's back seat is tight, and the Corolla Cross is the Corolla-based SUV.
