"A Corolla" used to be a single answer. Now it's a question with 4 good answers and a few wrong ones. The right Corolla depends less on the year than on how you drive, who rides with you and how much you care about the drive itself.

## The choices

| | Gas sedan | Hybrid | Hatchback | GR Corolla |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Years | 2017–2026 | 2020–2026 | 2017–2026 (iM to 2018) | 2023–2026 |
| Power | 132–169 hp | 121 hp, then 138 hp | 137 hp, then 168 hp | 300 hp |
| EPA combined, best version | 35 mpg | 52 mpg | 36 mpg | 24 mpg |
| All-wheel drive | No | 2023 on | No | Standard |
| Manual available | SE, to 2022 | No | To 2022 | Yes |
| Rear legroom | 34.8 in (2020 on) | 34.8 in | 29.9 in (2019 on) | Tight, and none in the Morizo |

[[chart:power]]

The GR Corolla is in a different world for power. Among the everyday Corollas, the 2.0-liter sedan and hatchback lead, and the hybrid trades power for efficiency.

## The cost of driving

[[chart:mpg]]

At 12,000 miles a year and $4 a gallon, a 35-mpg 2023–2026 LE uses about $1,371 of fuel a year. A 50-mpg 2023–2026 hybrid uses about $960, and a 52-mpg 2020–2022 hybrid about $923. A GR Corolla uses about $2,000, before the extra cost of premium fuel.

[[chart:fuelcost]]

These are examples, not forecasts. Your miles, your mix of city and highway driving and your local gas price will move them, and insurance, tires, financing and resale value will move the total cost even more.

## Choose the gas sedan if

You want the simplest, cheapest Corolla. A 2023–2026 LE has the 2.0-liter and modern safety tech, and a 2020–2022 car is a good, cheaper alternative if you accept the slower 1.8-liter in the L, LE and XLE. The sedan has the most rear-seat room of any Corolla in this guide.

## Choose the hybrid if

You drive a lot, especially in town, or you want all-wheel drive in a compact car. The hybrid costs a little more, but it uses about 30% less fuel than a gas LE and comes with a 10-year, 150,000-mile battery warranty. It's the Corolla most commuters should test-drive first.

## Choose the hatchback if

You want a Corolla that's more fun to drive and nicer to look at, and you rarely carry adults in the back. A 2019–2022 manual is the enthusiast's choice. Try the back seat and the cargo area with your own stuff before you buy.

## Choose the GR Corolla if

You want a performance car first and a Corolla second. It's fast, rewarding and usable every day, but it costs far more to buy and to fuel, and used ones need careful checking for hard use.

## Our pick depends on the driver

- **Choose the gas sedan** for the lowest price and the most rear-seat room.
- **Choose the hybrid** for the lowest fuel bills and available all-wheel drive.
- **Choose the hatchback** for style and a better drive, if you can live with the space.
- **Choose the GR Corolla** because you love driving.

Each choice can be the right one. None of them is right for everyone.

::: curiosity 60 Years, 50 Million and Counting
Toyota introduced the Corolla in Japan in 1966. It reached the US in spring 1968 as a 1969 model, starting at about $1,700, roughly $16,500 in 2026 money. In July 2021, Toyota sold the 50-millionth Corolla, and for 2027 it's marking the car's 60th anniversary with a special edition of the Corolla Hybrid.
:::
