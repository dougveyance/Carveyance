Honda sold the Prelude in America from 1979 to 2001, and for much of that time it was the company's showcase for clever engineering, including the first mechanical 4-wheel steering in a mass-produced car. For 2026 the name returned on a 2+2 liftback coupe that pairs the Civic Hybrid's 200-hp powertrain with the dual-axis front suspension, adaptive dampers and Brembo brakes of the Civic Type R. It went on sale on November 18, 2025, in a single, well-equipped trim, at $42,000 before delivery.

It's a car people argue about: a beautifully engineered chassis with a powertrain shared with a Civic that costs about $30,000. This guide has 3 chapters: our review of the car, a comparison with the Civics and rivals that cost less, and the checklist to use before you buy one, new or nearly new.

## Quick picks

- **Buy the Prelude if** you want a comfortable, efficient coupe that's genuinely good on a twisty road, and you'll use the hatchback and the 44-mpg rating every day.
- **Buy a Civic Sport Touring Hybrid instead if** you want the same powertrain for about $8,500 less. It was quicker to 60 mph in Car and Driver's tests.
- **Buy a Civic Si instead if** you want a manual gearbox and a limited-slip differential for about $11,000 less.
- **Buy a Civic Type R instead if** speed is the point. It has 315 hp and costs a few thousand dollars more.
- **Think twice if** you need a usable rear seat. The Prelude seats 4, but the back is for short trips.
