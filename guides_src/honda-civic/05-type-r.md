Honda had built Civic Type Rs since 1997, but Americans couldn't buy one new until 2017. When it finally arrived, it was worth the wait: a turbocharged, manual-only hatchback that Car and Driver called the quickest front-drive car it had ever tested. Both generations sold here, the FK8 and the FL5, are serious performance cars that also happen to have 5 doors and a big trunk.

## FK8: 2017–2021

The first US Type R has a turbocharged 2.0-liter with 306 hp and 295 lb-ft, a 6-speed manual and a limited-slip differential. It was built in Swindon, England, with an engine from Honda's plant in Anna, Ohio, and it introduced a dual-axis front suspension that keeps the steering from tugging under full power. Car and Driver ran one from 0 to 60 mph in 4.9 seconds, with 1.02 g of grip on its skidpad. It cost $33,900 for 2017 ($46,500 in 2026 dollars). Its styling, with the big wing, fake vents and triple exhaust, is as loud as its performance.

For 2020, Honda gave it a larger grille for better cooling, lighter 2-piece front brake rotors and Honda Sensing as standard. For 2021, it added the Type R Limited Edition: 600 cars for the US, all in Phoenix Yellow, with forged BBS wheels, Michelin Pilot Sport Cup 2 tires, less sound deadening and a numbered plaque, at $43,995 ($54,600 in 2026 dollars). There was no Type R for 2022.

## FL5: 2023–2026

The current Type R arrived for 2023 on the eleventh-generation Civic hatchback, built in Yorii, Japan, again with an Ohio-built engine. It has 315 hp and 310 lb-ft, the same manual and a calmer, less cluttered body. Car and Driver again ran it from 0 to 60 mph in 4.9 seconds. Inside, it shares the eleventh-generation Civic's cleaner dashboard, so it feels more grown-up than the FK8 from the driver's seat. Prices rose fast: $42,895 for 2023 ($47,400 in 2026 dollars), and $46,895 for 2026, before delivery.

## What to check before you buy

Many Type Rs are cherished by careful owners. Some have been tuned, tracked and modified, and a few have been all 3. Ask about track days, engine software, the intercooler, boost, the clutch and the tires, and ask for any original parts that were removed. Start the car from cold, then drive it until it's fully warm, watching the temperature and listening for boost leaks. Check that the clutch doesn't slip, that the gearbox doesn't grind going into second or third, and that the brake rotors aren't cracked or warped. Look closely for repaired front-end damage, because a Type R's nose is low and expensive. Check the recalls: several fuel pump campaigns cover FK8s (20V314, 21V215 and 23V858), and 2023–2025 FL5s are in the steering gearbox recall (24V744). Honda's A/C compressor shaft seal warranty extension also covers 2017–2021 Type Rs. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A stock 2020–2021 FK8 is the value pick: the updates, the brakes and Honda Sensing, for less than an FL5. The FL5 is the better car to live with every day and the one to buy if you'll use the cabin as much as the chassis. The Limited Edition is a collector's car, priced like one. Whatever you choose, buy the most original, best-documented car you can find, and walk away from any seller who can't tell you exactly what has been changed.

::: curiosity Swindon, Then Yorii
The FK8 Type R was one of the last cars built at Honda's factory in Swindon, England. When the FL5 replaced it, production moved to Honda's plant in Yorii, Japan, while the engine is still built in Anna, Ohio.
:::
