No vehicle did more to put America in SUVs than the Explorer. It arrived in 1990, sold 282,837 copies its first calendar year, peaked at an all-time SUV record of 445,157 in 2000, nearly died in 2009 at 52,190, and reinvented itself twice to sell 222,706 in 2025. For a used shopper that long arc matters practically: the modern Explorer era starts in 2011, when the truck became a unibody crossover, and the current rear-drive generation starts in 2020.

This guide covers both. The 2020–2026 trucks bring a 400-hp ST, a now-discontinued hybrid, and the 2025 refresh's simplified lineup; the 2011–2019 generation is the used-market volume play. The ST and its Sport ancestor get their own chapter, and the checklist closes with the specific things an Explorer must prove before you pay.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder, new:** a 2026 Active or ST-Line, 2.3 EcoBoost. 300 hp, up to 24 mpg combined, from $38,465.
- **Best used value:** a 2018–2019 XLT with the 2.3 EcoBoost — post-2016-refresh cabin, pre-2020 pricing.
- **The quick one:** the ST, 400 hp, Ford's claimed 5.5 to 60. Used 2020–2022 STs have shed a third of their sticker.
- **The efficient one:** a 2020–2024 hybrid, 25 mpg combined and 318 hp, quietly dropped from the retail lineup — which makes used ones a value.
- **Think twice if** you don't need the third row. An Edge or a RAV4-class crossover does the daily work for thousands less.
