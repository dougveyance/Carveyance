An old and a new Sequoia can both carry 7 or 8 people and tow a big trailer, and that's about where the similarity ends. The V8 is simple and familiar. The hybrid uses turbochargers, an electric motor and a 10-speed to do more with fewer cylinders. Neither is the wrong choice. The mistake is buying one without knowing what you gave up.

## How they feel

The 5.7-liter V8 makes 381 hp and 401 lb-ft, and its 6-speed delivers them in an easy, old-fashioned rhythm. The hybrid makes 437 hp and 583 lb-ft, 56 hp and 182 lb-ft more. Its electric motor fills in before the turbos build boost, so it feels much stronger pulling away and with a trailer. The V8 has the simpler engine, with no turbos or hybrid system to go wrong, and years of service history behind it.

## Fuel

[[chart:mpg]]

As 4x4s, the EPA rates the V8 at 14 mpg combined and the hybrid at 20. With 2-wheel drive, it's 15 against 22. That gap is real money.

[[chart:fuelcost]]

At 15,000 miles a year and $4 a gallon, the 4x4 hybrid saves about $1,286 a year. That's meaningful, but it takes years to recover the price difference between an old and a new Sequoia, so buy the hybrid for the whole package, not for the fuel alone. Towing, winter weather and short trips all lower both figures.

## Space

This is where the result flips. With both rear rows folded, the V8 has 120.1 cu ft, the hybrid 86.9. The V8's third row has more legroom and folds into a flat floor. The hybrid's slides, which helps, but its floor sits higher. The full numbers are in [the space chapter](../space/).

## Towing

The hybrid is rated at up to 4,318 kg (9,520 lb), against 3,357 kg (7,400 lb) for the V8. If your trailer is between those numbers, the choice is made for you. If it's lighter, both will do, and payload, not the trailer rating, is usually the limit. Check the door-jamb label of the vehicle you're buying, not the brochure.

## Running it

An old V8 needs attention to rust, coolant leaks, suspension wear and any maintenance the last owner skipped. A hybrid needs attention to warning lights, cooling, software updates, turbo plumbing and recall work. Toyota covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 150,000 miles from first use, which matters if you keep the Sequoia a long time. Neither is fragile, and neither is immune to neglect. The service records and an independent inspection should count for more than which engine it has.

## The decision

| If you need... | Buy |
|---|---|
| The most cargo space with every seat used | 2017–2022 V8 |
| A third row for adults | 2017–2022 V8, or test both |
| To tow more than 3,357 kg (7,400 lb) | 2023–2026 hybrid |
| Lower fuel bills | 2023–2026 hybrid |
| Modern safety equipment and screens | 2023–2026 hybrid |
| The lowest price | 2017–2019 V8 |

If you mostly drive in town and rarely tow, ask whether you need a full-size, body-on-frame SUV at all. A [Grand Highlander](/fitdrive/guides/toyota-grand-highlander/) seats 7 or 8, has a usable third row and uses far less fuel. That question can save more money than the choice between engines.

::: curiosity Fewer Cylinders, More Torque
The hybrid's twin-turbo V6 gives up 2 cylinders and about 2.2 liters to the V8 it replaced, yet makes 45% more torque. Toyota rates the hybrid's 583 lb-ft at just 2,400 rpm, where the V8 needed 3,600 rpm for its 401 lb-ft. Turbos and an electric motor, it turns out, are a bigger deal than displacement.
:::
