Honda redesigned the Accord for 2018 and gave up 2 things its buyers knew: the V6 and the coupe. What it built instead is lower, sleeker and up to 176 pounds lighter, with a wheelbase 2.16 inches longer and 1.9 inches more rear legroom. Every one has a 16.7-cubic-foot trunk, hybrid included. It's still a calm, roomy family sedan, but it steers and corners like a car that enjoys being driven.

## The engines

The standard engine is a turbocharged 1.5-liter 4-cylinder with 192 hp and 192 lb-ft of torque, through a CVT. It's smooth and economical, rated at 33 mpg combined, though it can sound strained when you work it hard. The 2.0T is the one people talk about: a turbocharged 2.0-liter with 252 hp and 273 lb-ft, which Honda says shares much of its design with the Civic Type R engine. It drives through a 10-speed automatic, which Honda called the first of its kind in a front-drive car. Car and Driver ran a 2.0T Touring from 0 to 60 mph in 5.5 seconds, quicker than the 2017 V6 it replaced, though the old V6 was still a little quicker from a rolling start.

The Accord Hybrid makes 212 hp from a 2.0-liter engine and 2 electric motors. Honda moved its battery beneath the rear floor, so it keeps the full 16.7-cubic-foot trunk. The EPA rated it at 47 mpg combined for 2018 and 48 from 2019. In town it's the smoothest Accord of all, because the engine often runs only as a generator while the motor drives the wheels.

## The manual

From 2018 to 2020, the Sport could be ordered with a 6-speed manual and either turbo engine, at no extra cost. Few people did: Car and Driver estimated that fewer than 5,000 manual Accords were sold in 2019, and Honda dropped it for 2021. A manual Sport 2.0T is now a collector's choice, and priced like one. Check the clutch, and ask what has been modified.

## The years

Honda Sensing was standard on every 2018 Accord, and the Touring had adaptive dampers. The 2021 refresh brought a new grille, standard Apple CarPlay and Android Auto on every trim, wireless versions of both on the EX-L, Sport 2.0T and Touring, a Sport SE in place of the 1.5-liter EX, and a rear seat reminder. The hybrid was updated at the same time; the 2021–2022 Hybrid Sport and Touring are rated at 43 mpg combined, against 47 to 48 for the other hybrid trims. 2022 was the last year for this generation and for the 2.0T.

## What to check before you buy

Most problems on this generation are recalls rather than patterns of failure. Several fuel pump recalls cover 2018–2020 cars (20V314, 21V215 and 23V858). Body control module software on 2018–2020 cars can disrupt the wipers, lights, windows and rear camera (20V771). The front seat belt buckles on some 2018–2019 cars may not latch (23V158). Honda also extended the warranty on the air conditioning condenser of 2018–2020 Accords and Accord Hybrids to 10 years with unlimited miles, for leaks caused by a manufacturing defect; if the air conditioning blows warm, ask a Honda dealer whether the VIN qualifies. On a 2.0T, ask whether the engine software has been tuned, and check the tires for wear that suggests hard driving. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A 2021–2022 Sport or Sport SE with the 1.5-liter turbo is the best value here: the refresh's smartphone features, Honda Sensing and 32 mpg, for less than a newer car. The 2.0T is the best-driving midsize family sedan of its time, and the reason to choose this generation over the next. The hybrid is the pick for long commutes. If efficiency and a modern cabin matter more than speed, read [the 2023–2026 chapter](../2023-2026/).

The 2020 model year ended a run that began with the very first Accord: every one until then could be bought with a manual transmission. The last manual Accords were built in December 2019.
