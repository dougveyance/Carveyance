The Clarity Electric arrived on August 1, 2017, at select dealers in California and Oregon. It was never sold outright when new: Honda offered it on a 36-month lease at $269 a month ($370 in 2026 dollars), with $1,730 ($2,370) due at signing plus the first payment, and an allowance of 20,000 miles a year. It was a closed-end lease with no option to buy the car at the end.

## What it can do

[[chart:mpge]]

It has the same body and cabin as the other Claritys, with a 161-hp electric motor making 221 lb-ft of torque, driving the front wheels, and a 25.5-kWh battery under the floor. Honda listed its curb weight at 1,825 kg (4,024 lb).

The EPA rated it at 89 miles of range and 114 MPGe combined: very efficient, but well short of the 238 miles of a 2017 Chevrolet Bolt. Honda quoted just over 3 hours for a full charge at 240 volts, and about 30 minutes to 80 percent at a DC fast charger using the CCS connector.

## The range problem

An 89-mile rating leaves little margin. A cold morning with the heater on, a long stretch of freeway or a detour can use up a large share of it, and a battery that's now several years old will have lost some of its capacity too. It can work as a second car for a short, predictable commute with charging at home. It can't be your only car unless your driving never strays far.

## Buying one now

Honda dropped the Clarity Electric in early 2020. Because it was leased with no purchase option, a car for sale today didn't come from its first driver in the usual way, and the paperwork matters more than usual. If you find one for sale, treat it as a specialist car and settle these questions before you talk about price:

- **Ownership:** check the title, and confirm that the seller owns the car outright and can transfer it.
- **History:** run the VIN for accidents, title brands and open recalls.
- **Battery:** ask a Honda dealer to report on the battery's health, and test the range on the kind of driving you do.
- **Charging:** check the charge port and the cable, and test both Level 2 and DC fast charging.
- **Service:** ask your nearest Honda dealer, in writing, whether it can service and repair this model.

## Should you buy one?

For most buyers, no: a used Clarity Plug-in Hybrid does nearly everything the Electric does, without the range limit or the doubts over ownership and service. The Electric is for someone who wants an interesting, efficient electric sedan for very short trips, who has checked all 5 points above, and who pays a price that reflects how hard it would be to sell on.
