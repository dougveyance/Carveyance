The GR Corolla shouldn't exist. A turbocharged 3-cylinder, an all-wheel-drive system with a rally pedigree and a 6-speed manual, in a Corolla, from Toyota, in the 2020s. It does exist, though, and it's one of the most entertaining cars you can buy for the money.

## What it is

Under the GR Corolla's widened, vented bodywork is a 1.6-liter turbocharged 3-cylinder making 300 hp. Torque was 273 lb-ft at first, and 295 lb-ft from 2025. Power goes to all 4 wheels through Toyota's GR-FOUR system, which lets the driver shift the torque split between a front-biased 60:40 and a rear-biased 30:70. The body is based on the regular Corolla's platform, with extra reinforcement, and the car is built at a dedicated GR Factory inside Toyota's Motomachi plant in Japan.

Toyota claims 0 to 60 mph in 4.99 seconds. Car and Driver did better, getting a 2023 Morizo Edition and a 2024 Premium to 60 mph in 4.4 seconds. The EPA rates the manual at 24 mpg combined. It wants premium fuel.

## Year by year

- **2023:** The launch year, manual only, with 3 versions. The Core started at $35,900 (about $39,700 in 2026 money), with open differentials and optional front and rear Torsen limited-slip differentials. The better-equipped Circuit Edition started at $42,900 (about $47,400) with the limited-slip differentials standard. The Morizo Edition, named for Akio Toyoda's racing alias, started at $49,900 (about $55,200). It drops the rear seat to save weight, gets 295 lb-ft, extra bracing and Michelin Pilot Sport Cup 2 tires, and was built in very small numbers.
- **2024:** A better-equipped Premium grade joined the Core.
- **2025:** An 8-speed automatic, which Toyota calls the GR-DAT, became a $2,000 option, with launch control and its own transmission cooler. Every GR Corolla got 295 lb-ft and standard front and rear Torsen limited-slip differentials, along with a new front bumper and suspension changes. A Premium Plus grade arrived, and the Circuit Edition was dropped. The Core started at $38,860.
- **2026:** The lineup narrows to the GR Corolla and the Premium Plus, from $39,920 with the manual. Toyota added more than 45 feet of structural adhesive to the body, and the Premium Plus gets a 9-speaker JBL system and a forged carbon-fiber roof.

## Living with it

The GR Corolla is a 5-door hatchback with a usable back seat (except in the Morizo) and a cargo area about the same as the regular hatchback's. The ride can feel busy, but the all-wheel drive makes it a year-round car if you fit winter tires. It's thirsty for a Corolla, though. At 12,000 miles a year and $4 a gallon, 24 mpg comes to about $2,000 a year, before you pay the premium-fuel surcharge.

## Buying a used one

GR Corollas attract enthusiasts, and enthusiasts use their cars. That's part of the appeal, and part of the risk.

- **Track and modification history:** Ask directly whether the car has been on a track or tuned. Look for aftermarket intakes, exhausts, engine tunes and signs that one was removed. Changes like these can complicate warranty claims.
- **Clutch and drivetrain:** On a manual, check that the clutch engages cleanly and doesn't slip under hard acceleration. Listen for whining or clunking from the differentials.
- **Tires and brakes:** Uneven or heavily worn tires and glazed or grooved brake rotors suggest hard use. The Morizo's Cup 2 tires are a poor match for cold or wet roads.
- **Check-engine light (2023):** Toyota service bulletin T-SB-0084-23 covers some 2023 cars that set a warning light with codes for a stuck-open turbo wastegate actuator or random misfires. The fix is an engine computer update.
- **Instrument cluster (2023–2025):** GR Corollas from these years were included in a recall because the digital cluster may not display warnings at startup. The fix is a software update (25V-595).

Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls).

## Which one to buy

- **2025–2026 manual:** the best GR Corolla, with more torque and standard limited-slip differentials.
- **2025–2026 automatic:** if you'd rather not shift. Reviewers found its shifts very quick, and it adds launch control.
- **2023–2024 Core with the limited-slip differentials, or a Circuit Edition:** good value if the price is right, but check the history carefully.
- **Morizo Edition:** a collector's piece. Buy one only if you know exactly what you're paying for.

## The verdict

The GR Corolla is a car for people who love driving, not one that will save money. It's quick, sharp, practical enough for every day and very unlike any other Corolla. Buy the newest, least-modified one you can afford.

::: curiosity A Factory Without a Conveyor
Toyota set up a dedicated GR Factory inside its Motomachi plant, the birthplace of the Lexus LFA and the A80 Supra. There's no conveyor belt: automated carts carry each car between stations, where experienced technicians do more of the work by hand, so every GR Corolla takes longer to build than a regular Corolla. Demand outgrew the factory, and Toyota added a second GR Corolla line at its plant in Burnaston, England, the first time a GR model has been built outside Japan.
:::
