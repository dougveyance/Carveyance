A used Highlander has usually been a family's main car, which means lots of miles, lots of short trips and a cabin that's seen some spills. That's not a reason to avoid one. Toyota built it to take that kind of use. But it's a reason to check the car in front of you carefully, rather than trusting the badge.

## Pick the job first

For a smooth, strong family SUV that tows, start with a 2020–2022 V6. If fuel costs matter more than towing, a 2020–2026 Hybrid is the one to find. If you want the newest gas Highlander and don't need the V6, look at a 2023–2026 turbo. For the lowest price, a 2017–2019 V6 or V6 hybrid still does the job well. If the third row has to carry adults, or you need more space behind it, look at the Grand Highlander instead.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2017–2019 | Refreshed third generation. 295-hp V6 with 8-speed, 185-hp 4-cylinder LE, 306-hp V6 hybrid | No Apple CarPlay or Android Auto. Hybrid oil cooler pipe campaign (19TC04). Older 8-year, 100,000-mile hybrid battery warranty |
| 2020–2022 | Fourth generation. V6 or 243-hp 4-cylinder hybrid, XSE added for 2021 | Front bumper cover recall. Passenger seat sensor recall (2020–2021). Second-row recliner recall (2021–2022). Hybrid cable program |
| 2023–2024 | 2.4-liter turbo replaces the V6, twin 12.3-inch screens on upper grades | Front bumper cover recall (2023). Second-row recliner recall. Camera and instrument panel software recalls. Hybrid cable campaign |
| 2025 | Hybrids get standard AWD, Limited 25th Edition Hybrid | Second-row recliner recall (expanded). Camera and instrument panel software recalls |
| 2026 | AWD standard on every model, LE dropped. The last gas and hybrid Highlander | Too new for long-term data |

## Body and history

Run a vehicle history report, but don't treat a clean one as proof. Look for uneven panel gaps, paint that doesn't match, overspray on rubber seals and replacement headlights on one side only. Check the lower front bumper cover on a 2020–2023 car for loose or broken tabs, which is the subject of a recall. Lift the cargo floor and look for water, rust or a dirt line that could point to flood damage, and check underneath for rust if the car came from a state that salts its roads.

If the car has a tow hitch, ask what it towed and how often. Look at the hitch and wiring for wear, and favor a car with records of transmission fluid changes.

## Engine, transmission and hybrid system

Start the car cold if you can. On a V6 or turbo, listen for ticking or knocking and watch for smoke, and make sure the 8-speed automatic shifts smoothly, without flares or harsh engagement. Check that the stop-start system restarts the engine cleanly. On a hybrid, make sure the car starts in Ready mode quietly, that the engine starts and stops smoothly and that no warning lights stay on. Ask a shop that knows Toyota hybrids to scan for stored codes and check the battery's health.

On a 2020–2024 all-wheel-drive hybrid, ask about the rear motor cable. Toyota covers corrosion at its connector under program 22TE09 and offers an inspection and protector seal under campaign 25TD01, after which the cable is covered for 10 years with no mileage limit. On a 2017–2019 hybrid, confirm the oil cooler pipe campaign was done.

## Cabin and safety tech

Test every row. Fold and raise the third row, and adjust each second-row seatback, then push on it to make sure it locks firmly, since that's the subject of a recall on 2021–2025 cars. Pair your phone, remembering that 2017–2019 cars have no CarPlay or Android Auto. Check the backup camera and, on a 2023–2025 car with the Panoramic View Monitor, that the image doesn't freeze. Make sure the instrument panel shows the speed and warning lights every time you start it. Test the radar cruise control, lane departure warning and blind spot monitor, if the car has one.

## Recalls and campaigns: check by VIN

- **2017–2019 Hybrid:** Engine oil cooler pipe replacement (campaign 19TC04).
- **2020–2023:** Lower front bumper cover tabs, with improved retention hardware (October 2023 recall).
- **2020–2021:** Front passenger seat sensor that can stop the passenger airbag from deploying (23V-865).
- **2020–2024 AWD Hybrid:** Rear motor cable corrosion (program 22TE09 and campaign 25TD01).
- **2021–2025:** Second-row seat recliners that may not lock, with new return springs (March 2026 recall, expanded in September 2026).
- **2023–2025:** Instrument panel software (25V-595) and, on cars with the Panoramic View Monitor, rear camera software (25V-744).
- **2024–2025:** Third-row center seat belt anchor bolt, on just 39 cars (25V-872).
- **Every year:** New recalls can appear at any time. Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask a Toyota dealer to check for open service campaigns too. Ask for proof that each repair was done.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly and hide the differences that matter. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, grade, engine, drive and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you, comparing a listing with similar ones nearby. Expect hybrids to carry a premium, and don't assume the premium is justified by the car's condition. A pre-purchase inspection is worth more than any seller's speech about Toyota reliability.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the car past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a documented 2020–2022 V6 XLE.
- **Lowest running costs:** a 2020–2026 Hybrid, with the cable campaign done.
- **Best for towing:** a 2020–2022 V6 or a 2023–2026 turbo, both rated for 2,268 kg (5,000 lb).
- **Budget pick:** a 2017–2019 V6 LE Plus or XLE with records.
- **Newest:** a 2026 Hybrid XLE or Limited, the last of its kind.
