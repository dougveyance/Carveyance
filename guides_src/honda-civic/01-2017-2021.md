The tenth-generation Civic arrived as a 2016 sedan and coupe, and the hatchback, Si and Type R followed for 2017, so 2017–2021 are the years when the whole family was on sale. It was longer, lower and wider than the car it replaced, with a dramatic, creased body that not everyone loved. Underneath, it was a genuinely good car: it steers precisely, rides well, and has a back seat that adults can sit in for more than a short trip.

## The engines

[[chart:power]]

The base engine is a 2.0-liter 4-cylinder with 158 hp, used in the LX and, from 2019, the Sport. It's not quick, but it's simple, smooth and rated at 33 to 34 mpg combined with the CVT. The turbocharged 1.5-liter makes 174 hp in the sedan and coupe and is rated at up to 36 mpg, the best of this generation. Every hatchback has the turbo: 174 hp in the LX and EX grades, and 180 hp in the Sport and Sport Touring, which want premium fuel. Edmunds ran a 2017 Hatchback Sport Touring from 0 to 60 mph in 7.3 seconds. A 6-speed manual was offered on several sedan, coupe and hatchback grades, but Honda narrowed the list as the years went on.

## Sedan, coupe or hatchback

The sedan is the easy choice, with the largest supply and the simplest ownership. The coupe, sold through 2020, has the same engines but a harder-to-reach back seat; buy it because you prefer the shape, not because it's rare. The hatchback was built in Swindon, England, and has a wide opening and a side-to-side cargo cover that rolls away. It's the most practical tenth-generation Civic, though its busy rear styling is the most divisive.

## The years

For 2019, the sedan and coupe got a lighter refresh with a new Sport grade, a volume knob on the touchscreen, and Honda Sensing standard on every grade except the Si. That suite brings collision mitigation braking, adaptive cruise control and lane keeping assist. The hatchback got its own refresh for 2020. The coupe ended after 2020, and 2021 was the last year of this generation.

## What to check before you buy

The 1.5-liter turbo had a known problem in its early years: in very cold climates and on short trips, fuel could dilute the engine oil. Honda updated the software on cars in 21 cold-weather states and extended the warranty on the camshafts, rocker arms and spark plugs of 2016–2018 Civics with this engine to 6 years with unlimited miles, though it says serious cases are extremely rare outside extreme cold. Check the oil level and ask how often it was changed. Honda also extended 2 air conditioning warranties to 10 years with unlimited miles: the condenser on 2016–2020 sedans and coupes and 2017–2021 hatchbacks, and the compressor shaft seal on 2016–2021 Civics. If the air conditioning blows warm, ask a Honda dealer whether the VIN qualifies. Recalls include several fuel pump campaigns (23V858, 21V215 and 20V314), the electric power steering on 2017–2018 cars (18V663), and the brake booster on 2020–2021 cars (23V458). The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A 2019–2021 sedan with the 2.0-liter engine is the safest buy here: Honda Sensing, the volume knob and no turbo to worry about. A turbo hatchback is the more useful and more fun car, if its records are good. The coupe is for people who want 2 doors and accept the compromise. If you want a newer cabin and a less fussy design, compare it with [the 2022–2026 Civic](../2022-2026/).

::: curiosity "Made in England"
For the first time, the tenth-generation Civic hatchback was sold in North America alongside the sedan, and every one came from Honda's factory in Swindon, England, which also built the Type R. Honda closed the plant in July 2021, and the last car it built was a Civic hatchback.
:::
