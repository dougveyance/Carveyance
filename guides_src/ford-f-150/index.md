Nothing else in the American market carries the weight of the F-150. It has been the best-selling truck in the country since 1977 and the best-selling vehicle of any kind since 1981, and in 2025 Ford moved 828,832 F-Series trucks. For a shopper, that dominance is good news twice over: the used market is an ocean, and Ford spreads the lineup so wide, a $40,085 steel-wheeled XL to a $113,725 supercharged Raptor R, that there is an F-150 shaped like almost any job or budget.

This guide covers the 2 generations you'll actually cross-shop: the aluminum-bodied 2015–2020 trucks, which made the used half-ton genuinely light and efficient, and the 2021–2026 trucks, which added the PowerBoost hybrid and its onboard generator. The Raptor gets its own chapter. Start with the generation you're shopping, or go straight to the checklist if you're about to look at one.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder, new or late-used:** a 2021 or later XLT or Lariat with the 2.7 EcoBoost. 325 hp, 21 mpg combined, and the simplest of the modern engines.
- **Best for towing and power needs:** the PowerBoost hybrid. 430 hp, 570 lb-ft, 24 mpg, and Pro Power Onboard can run a job site, a tailgate or a blacked-out house.
- **Best used value:** a documented 2018–2020 XLT, 2.7 or 3.5 EcoBoost, with its recalls done. The aluminum body means less rust worry than any older truck.
- **The V8 holdout's truck:** the 5.0, in either generation. Not the quickest and not the thriftiest, but naturally aspirated and the last of its kind in a half-ton.
- **Fastest, period:** the Raptor R. Car and Driver ran one to 60 mph in 3.6 seconds, the quickest gas truck it has ever tested.
- **Think twice if** you mostly drive alone in the city and never haul. A Maverick or Ranger does the truck things most people actually do, for much less money and fuel.
