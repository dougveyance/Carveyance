The fifth-generation CR-V arrived for 2017 with a longer wheelbase, 2.1 inches more rear legroom and a cargo area that grew to 39.2 cubic feet behind the rear seat and 75.8 cubic feet with it folded. That seat now folds completely flat with one motion, giving a load floor more than 5 feet long. It isn't exciting to drive, and it doesn't pretend to be. It's quiet, easy to see out of and very easy to live with, which is why so many families bought one.

## The engines

[[chart:power]]

For 2017–2019, the LX has a 2.4-liter 4-cylinder with 184 hp, rated at 28 mpg combined with front-wheel drive. Every other grade has a turbocharged 1.5-liter with 190 hp, rated at 30 mpg with front-wheel drive and 29 with all-wheel drive. For 2020, the 2.4-liter was dropped and the LX got the turbo too. Every version uses a CVT, and all-wheel drive was available on every grade. Car and Driver ran a 2017 CR-V with the turbo and all-wheel drive from 0 to 60 mph in 7.6 seconds, brisk for the class at the time.

## The years

In 2017, Honda Sensing, with collision mitigation braking, adaptive cruise control and lane keeping assist, was standard on the EX and above but not on the LX. The 2020 refresh brought a new front end, standard Honda Sensing on every grade, the turbo for the LX and, at the top of the range, the new [CR-V Hybrid](../hybrid/). 2022 was the last year of this generation.

## What to check before you buy

The 1.5-liter turbo had an oil dilution problem in its early years: in very cold weather and on short trips, fuel could get into the engine oil, raising the oil level and giving it a fuel smell. Honda updated the software on 2017–2018 CR-Vs in cold-weather states and extended the warranty on the camshafts, rocker arms and spark plugs to 6 years with unlimited miles, which has now run out on most of them. Check the oil level and ask how often it was changed. Recalls include several fuel system campaigns (17V442 for 2017, 21V215 for 2018–2019, 19V569 for 2019 and 23V858), the electric power steering on 2017–2018 cars (18V663), loose rear subframe bolts on 2019–2020 cars (19V865), and the front seat belt buckles on 2017–2020 cars (23V158). The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A 2020–2022 CR-V EX is the sweet spot: the turbo, standard Honda Sensing, the refreshed cabin and the full cargo area, for less than a newer CR-V. A 2017–2019 LX with the 2.4-liter avoids the oil dilution question entirely, at the cost of some power and safety equipment. If you'd like better fuel economy and standard all-wheel drive, read [the hybrid chapter](../hybrid/).

::: curiosity A Floor at 2 Heights
The gas CR-V's cargo floor panel can sit level with the folded rear seat, for a flat load space more than 5 feet long, or drop a few inches to make room for taller things. The hybrid's battery lives where that lower level would be.
:::
