The C-HR name covers 2 cars that share almost nothing but their sharp styling. The first, sold in the US for 2018–2022, is a small front-drive crossover with a 144-hp 2.0-liter engine and a CVT. It was meant to be a Scion, became a Toyota when Scion closed, and ended in the US after 2022. The second arrived in March 2026 as an electric crossover with 2 motors, all-wheel drive, 338 hp and up to 287 miles of EPA range.

For a used buyer, these are different decisions: the gas C-HR is an affordable, efficient city car with a dramatic look, and the electric one is a quick, well-equipped EV. This guide has 3 chapters: one for each, and the checklist to use before you buy.

## Quick picks

- **Best value:** a 2020–2022 gas C-HR XLE, with LED headlights, Apple CarPlay and Android Auto.
- **Cheapest way in:** a 2019 C-HR LE with its recalls done.
- **Best electric:** a 2026 or later C-HR SE, with 287 miles of EPA range on 18-inch wheels.
- **Quickest:** any electric C-HR. Edmunds ran one from 0 to 60 mph in 4.9 seconds.
- **Think twice if** you need all-wheel drive in a gas C-HR. It was never offered in the US.

## At a glance

|  | C-HR, gas (2018–2022) | C-HR, electric (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Powertrain | 2.0-liter 4-cylinder, 144 hp, CVT | 2 electric motors, 338 hp |
