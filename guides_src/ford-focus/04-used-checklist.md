Nearly 3.9 million Focuses mean infinite supply and no reason for charity. Run the checks, and let any failure send you to the next listing.

## The 5 checks

**1. The PowerShift shudder test (2012–18 automatics).** From a stop, accelerate gently to 20 mph, then repeat with moderate throttle; creep in stop-and-go for a block. Shudder like a worn clutch, hesitation or slipping sensations are the famous fault. Then check the paper: a replaced clutch pack and the latest software noted on dealer invoices is a car made right; no history plus symptoms is a walk. Manual cars: skip this entirely.

**2. RS head-gasket history (2016–17 builds).** Early RS engines suffered documented head-gasket failures, repaired by Ford under warranty. Demand the invoice showing the repair, or buy a later build. White exhaust on a warm engine, coolant loss or an unexplained sweet smell ends the conversation.

**3. Rust, underneath.** First-generation cars are 20-plus years old; arches, subframes and brake lines on northern cars tell the truth. This check alone sorts the 2000–2011 market.

**4. Recalls by VIN.** The early-2000s cars carried a famously long recall list and most were done decades ago — but confirm. On 2012-on cars, check for completed door-latch campaigns and software updates.

**5. Turbo and clutch health on the fast ones.** Any EcoBoost — 1.0, ST's 2.0, RS's 2.3 — demands oil-change receipts. On STs and RSs, a juddering or high-biting clutch is a wear item someone wants you to fund, and a tune without the hardware to match is a discount, not a feature.

## Reading the market

The arc above says it plainly: the Focus sold enormously in 3 separate waves, so ordinary cars will be cheap forever — buy on condition and gearbox, never on scarcity. The scarce ones are the fast ones, and they behave like a different market: SVTs are appreciating from nothing, STs sit at their value floor, and clean RSs already trade on desirability rather than age. In a nameplate Ford abandoned, the enthusiast cars are the only Focuses with a future that points up.
