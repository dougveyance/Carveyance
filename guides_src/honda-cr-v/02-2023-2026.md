The sixth-generation CR-V arrived for 2023 looking squarer and more expensive, and it's 2.7 inches longer than the car it replaced, with a wheelbase 1.6 inches longer. The cabin borrows the clean, horizontal dashboard of the 2022 Civic, with a honeycomb mesh over the air vents and physical controls for the climate. It's quieter, rides better and feels more grown-up than any CR-V before it.

## Turbo or hybrid

The gas versions, the EX, EX-L and, in some years, an entry-level LX, have the turbocharged 1.5-liter, now with 190 hp and a CVT, rated at 30 mpg combined with front-wheel drive and 29 with all-wheel drive. The Sport, Sport-L and Sport Touring are hybrids, with 204 hp and 247 lb-ft; for this generation, Honda made the hybrid available with front-wheel drive, except on the Sport Touring, which comes only with all-wheel drive. Car and Driver ran a Sport Touring from 0 to 60 mph in 7.9 seconds. A TrailSport Hybrid joined for 2026. The hybrid gets its own [chapter](../hybrid/).

## Space

Every sixth-generation CR-V has 36.3 cubic feet behind the rear seat and 76.5 cubic feet with it folded. On the EX and EX-L, the cargo floor drops to a lower level, giving 39.3 cubic feet behind the seat. The LX and the hybrids have a fixed floor, and the hybrids have no spare tire. Rear legroom is generous, and the rear seatbacks recline to 8 different angles.

## What to check before you buy

The most important recall is 24V744, which covers 2023–2025 CR-Vs and CR-V Hybrids: friction inside the steering gearbox can make the steering heavy or sticky, and the fix is a new worm gear spring and fresh grease. The front passenger seat back frame on some 2023 cars may be improperly welded (23V092), and some 2024 cars have tires missing a date code (24V508). On 2023–2025 Hybrids, the high-pressure fuel pump may crack and leak fuel (24V763), and on 2023–2026 Hybrids, the tire repair kit's sealant bottle can build pressure and blow its cap off if the nozzle isn't attached properly (26V366). The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

For most buyers, a front-drive Sport Hybrid is the CR-V to buy: quicker and more frugal than the turbo, for not much more money. Choose the turbo EX or EX-L if you want the lower cargo floor and a spare tire, or tow up to 1,500 pounds, against 1,000 for the hybrid. If you live where it snows, the all-wheel-drive versions of either are worth the small loss in fuel economy.
