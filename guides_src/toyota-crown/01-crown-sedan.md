When the Avalon ended in 2022, Toyota didn't replace it with another long, low sedan. It brought back the Crown name and gave it a shape nobody else was making: a 4-door sedan with a proper trunk that rides about 4 inches higher than the Avalon did. J.D. Power found that the raised stance gives it some of a crossover's easy entry and exit, with the privacy of a sealed trunk; Capital One's reviewer found it no easier to get into than an ordinary sedan. Either way, it looks like nothing else in a parking lot, and every one is a hybrid with all-wheel drive.

## How it drives

There are 2 powertrains, and they make 2 very different cars. The XLE and Limited use a 2.5-liter 4-cylinder with an electric motor at each axle, for 236 hp through a CVT. Toyota claims 0 to 60 mph in 7.6 seconds, and the EPA rates it at 41 mpg combined (42 city, 41 highway), remarkable for a car this size. It's calm, quiet and comfort-tuned, and it runs on regular fuel.

[[chart:power]]

The Platinum gets the Hybrid MAX: a 2.4-liter turbocharged 4-cylinder with electric motors, 340 hp and 400 lb-ft, through a 6-speed automatic rather than a CVT. Its all-wheel drive sends at least 30% of the power to the rear wheels at all times, and it adds Adaptive Variable Suspension. Toyota claims 5.7 seconds to 60 mph, and Edmunds' test car did it in 6.0. The price is fuel: the EPA rates the Hybrid MAX at 30 mpg combined.

[[chart:mpg]]

## Living with it

The trunk holds 15.2 cubic feet, a little more than a Camry's, and the rear seats fold for longer items. Every Crown has Toyota Safety Sense 3.0, a 12.3-inch touchscreen and Toyota's newer multimedia system, and the hybrid components are covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles. Reviewers split on the cabin: Kelley Blue Book found it edging toward Lexus quality, while CarGurus thought some materials felt cheap for the price. Sit in one before you decide.

## The years and grades

The Crown went on sale in early 2023 in XLE, Limited and Platinum grades, at $39,950 ($44,100 in 2026 dollars), $45,550 and $52,350 ($57,800 in 2026 dollars). For 2025, Toyota added a Nightshade grade with black trim and made heated and ventilated front seats, heated rear seats and a heated steering wheel standard on every grade. The Platinum gained a head-up display, rear-wheel steering, a power-adjustable steering wheel and a hands-free power trunk. With delivery, the 2025 range ran from $42,535 for the XLE to $56,085 for the Platinum.

## What to check before you buy

Run the VIN for 2 software recalls. On 2023–2025 cars, the 12.3-inch instrument display may be blank at startup, hiding warning lights (25V-595); 2023–2024 Crowns can take the fix over the air. And the rear camera image can freeze or go blank in reverse (25V-744). The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A Crown XLE or Limited is one of the most efficient big cars you can buy, with standard all-wheel drive and a comfortable, quiet ride. The Platinum is the enthusiast's choice: genuinely quick, with an adaptive suspension, at the cost of 11 mpg. If you can, choose a 2025 or later car for the extra standard equipment. If you need more cargo space than a sedan offers, read [the Crown Signia chapter](../crown-signia/).

::: curiosity The First Japanese Car in America
The original Toyopet Crown, launched in 1955, was the first Japanese car sold in the US when it arrived in 1958. It was a poor fit for American freeways, and Toyota sold just 287 that year. The name left the US market after 1972 and didn't return for 5 decades.
:::
