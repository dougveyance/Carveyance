The idea predates the badge. In 2013, Ford dropped the F-150's 365-hp 3.5 EcoBoost into the Explorer, called it Sport, blacked out the trim and created a 3-row that ran with hot hatches — 5.7 seconds to 60 in period instrumented tests. When the rear-drive platform arrived for 2020, the formula got the company's performance badge and a proper chassis: the Explorer ST, 400 hp and 415 lb-ft from a 3.0 EcoBoost V6, Ford's claimed 5.5 to 60 and a 143-mph top speed, numbers that beat the Focus ST hot hatch while hauling 6 passengers and a dog.

## Sport vs ST, honestly

The 2013–2019 Sport is the value entry: F-150 power, all-wheel drive standard, and used prices that now sit well under half of a new ST. Its front-drive-based platform understeers earlier and its 6-speed shifts lazier — it's a fast crossover. The 2020-on ST is the one with the complete argument: rear-drive balance, the 10-speed, bigger brakes, real steering, and since the 2025 refresh it is the only performance trim left standing. Platinum buyers quietly got most of the hardware too — the 2020–21 Platinum carries a 365-hp version of the same 3.0.

## Buying a used fast Explorer

Assume it was driven like its badge until proven otherwise. On Sports: the water-pump question from the checklist chapter applies double, turbos should spool without smoke, and the PTU (power transfer unit) wants fluid-change receipts nobody ever has — ask anyway and price accordingly. On STs: check the 21-inch tires (4-figure replacement sets), confirm recall work by VIN, and feel for brake judder from hard use. Both drink premium when pushed and return high teens at best when driven as intended, which the EPA, rating the ST at 20 mpg combined and the Sport at 18, politely assumes you won't.

The quiet truth: an ST costs SUV money and delivers the straight-line numbers of a muscle car that happens to do school runs. 35 years after the Eddie Bauer edition, this is what the Explorer grew up into.
