The third-generation Sienna went on sale for 2011, but the 2017–2020 cars got the engine it deserved. For 2017, Toyota replaced the old V6 and 6-speed with the 296-hp V6 and 8-speed automatic from the Highlander, and suddenly the family van was the most powerful in its class. It's a conventional minivan in the best sense: roomy, comfortable and easy to fix, with a powertrain any shop understands.

## How it drives

The 3.5-liter V6 makes 296 hp and 263 lb-ft and runs on regular fuel. Car and Driver ran a 2017 SE Premium from 0 to 60 mph in 6.8 seconds, the quickest Sienna it had tested, and a 2018 with all-wheel drive in 7.0. That's real passing power, even with a full load. Most grades are tuned for comfort; the SE adds a sport-tuned suspension and 19-inch wheels for buyers who want a little more control.

[[chart:power]]

All-wheel drive was offered on the LE, XLE and Limited from 2017, and on the SE from 2019. For these years, the Sienna was the only minivan you could buy with it; Chrysler added an all-wheel-drive Pacifica for 2021. The EPA rated the 2017 at 22 mpg combined with front-wheel drive and 20 with all-wheel drive, and Car and Driver reports the later front-drive vans at 21.

## Living with it

Kelley Blue Book lists up to 150 cubic feet of cargo space, and the second-row seats come out, so the Sienna can turn into a small van for a move or a trip to the hardware store. The L seats 7; most other grades seat 7 or 8, depending on whether the second row has a bench or captain's chairs.

The years differ mainly in safety and technology. For 2018, Toyota made its Toyota Safety Sense P suite standard, with automatic emergency braking, lane departure alert, adaptive cruise control and automatic high beams. It also refreshed the nose and added the Entune 3.0 multimedia system and 5 USB ports. Apple CarPlay and Amazon Alexa became standard for 2019, when the SE gained all-wheel drive, and a blacked-out Nightshade Edition arrived for 2020. Toyota also announced a dealer retrofit to add CarPlay to some 2018 Siennas, so ask a dealer whether a 2018 can get it.

## What to check before you buy

The sliding doors are the Sienna's known weak spot. A class-action settlement extended Toyota's coverage for the sliding door lock assemblies, cables and center hinges on 2011–2018 Siennas to 10 years from the date of first use, so a 2017 or 2018 may still have some time left. Check that both doors open, close and latch smoothly, by button, by key fob and by hand. Then run the VIN for the fuel pump recall on 2017–2020 vans. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A documented 2019–2020 LE or XLE is the sweet spot of the V6 Siennas, with CarPlay, the safety suite and the strong V6. A 2017 costs less and is mechanically the same, but it lacks the standard emergency braking. Choose all-wheel drive if you need it; the 2 mpg penalty is small for the traction. If you drive a lot of miles, compare the fuel cost of the hybrid in [the V6 or hybrid chapter](../v6-or-hybrid/) first.

::: curiosity The Only One With AWD
After General Motors dropped its all-wheel-drive minivans in 2006, the Sienna was the only minivan in its class sold with all-wheel drive, a run that lasted until the 2021 Chrysler Pacifica AWD arrived in 2020.
:::
