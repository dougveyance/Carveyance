The 2022 Tundra kept the name and the San Antonio factory, and almost nothing else. It sits on a new boxed ladder frame, shared with the Sequoia and later the Tacoma. The V8 is gone, replaced by a twin-turbo V6. The 6-speed became a 10-speed, the rear leaf springs became coil springs, with air springs available, and the cabin got screens of up to 14 inches. The bed is a composite with aluminum reinforcement, which shrugs off the dents and rust a steel bed collects.

It's a much more modern truck, and it rides, pulls and tows better than the one it replaced. It also has a recall history you need to understand before you buy a 2022–2024 gas truck. That has [its own chapter](../engine-recall/).

## The engine

Toyota calls it a 3.5-liter, but the twin-turbo V6 displaces 3,445 cc, so most testers call it a 3.4. In most grades it makes 389 hp and 479 lb-ft, 78 lb-ft more than the old 5.7-liter V8. The base SR got a milder version at launch, with 348 hp and 405 lb-ft.

[[chart:torque]]

The torque arrives early, and the 10-speed keeps the engine in its sweet spot, so the truck feels effortless with a trailer behind it. Car and Driver timed a 2022 Limited to 60 mph in 6.1 seconds. The EPA rates the gas V6 at 19 mpg combined as a 4x4 (17 city, 22 or 23 highway) and 20 mpg as a 4x2, a real gain over the V8's 14 or 15.

## Cabs, beds and towing

There are 2 cabs. The Double Cab has a 6.5- or 8.1-foot bed, and the CrewMax a 5.5- or 6.5-foot bed. Properly equipped, the new Tundra tows up to 5,443 kg (12,000 lb), and its maximum payload is 880 kg (1,940 lb). Both peaks belong to specific lightly equipped trucks. A loaded Platinum or 1794 Edition will be rated lower, so read the labels on the truck you're looking at.

Toyota Safety Sense 2.5 is standard, with automatic emergency braking and adaptive cruise control, and an 8-inch touchscreen is standard with a 14-inch screen available. The changes since launch are small. For 2025, a power tailgate became standard and the TRD Rally package arrived. For 2026, a 32.2-gallon fuel tank is standard on every grade, the TRD Pro can be ordered with the Tacoma's IsoDynamic seats and the TRD Rally package can add a 3-inch lift. The 2026 Tundra starts at $41,260, before delivery.

## What to check before you buy

The engine comes first. Machining debris left inside some twin-turbo V6s can wreck the main bearings and stall the engine, and Toyota has recalled certain 2022–2024 gas Tundras 3 times for it. Read [the engine recall chapter](../engine-recall/) before you go further on any 2022–2024 truck.

Then run the VIN for the rest:

- **2022:** Rear axle flange nuts that may need tightening (22V-445).
- **2022–2024:** Transmission software that can let the truck creep forward in Neutral (24V-125).
- **2022–2025:** Reverse lights that can fail when moisture gets in, about 443,000 trucks (25V-322), and rearview camera software that may not show an image, about 394,000 vehicles including the Sequoia (25V-657).
- **2024–2025 with the Panoramic View Monitor:** Parking assist software that may not show the rearview camera image, about 161,000 trucks (26V-038).

Test the truck on rough pavement and at freeway speed, listen for knocking from the engine at idle and under load, and make sure the 10-speed shifts smoothly in stop-and-go traffic. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A 2025 or 2026 gas Tundra is the cleanest buy, because the engine recalls so far end with 2024 trucks. A 2022–2024 can be a good truck and a good deal, but only with the engine campaigns checked by VIN and the paperwork in hand. The SR5 and Limited are the value picks. Pay for a Platinum, 1794 Edition or TRD Pro for what's in the cabin or under the truck, not for the badge.

Buy this Tundra if you tow, haul or simply want a modern full-size truck from Toyota. If you want the most torque, the hybrid is in [the next chapter](../hybrid/).
