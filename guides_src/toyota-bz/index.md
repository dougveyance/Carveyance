The bZ4X was Toyota's first mass-market electric SUV in the US. It arrived for 2023, about the size of a RAV4, comfortable and quiet, and it had a rough start: modest range, charging that could be slow, and a wheel recall weeks after launch. Toyota cut the price by up to $6,000 for 2025. For 2026 it renamed the car bZ and gave it bigger batteries, far more power with all-wheel drive, a Tesla-style NACS charging port and up to 314 miles of EPA range. A longer, more rugged sibling, the bZ Woodland, joined it.

For a used buyer, the split matters more than the name. A 2023–2025 bZ4X can be a bargain for someone who charges at home and mostly drives locally. A 2026 bZ is a much better car for road trips. This guide has 3 chapters: the 2023–2025 bZ4X, the 2026 bZ and bZ Woodland, and the checklist to use before you buy.

## Quick picks

- **Best for most people:** a 2026 bZ XLE FWD Plus, rated at 314 miles, the longest range of any version.
- **Cheapest way in:** a 2025 bZ4X XLE FWD, if you can charge at home. It cost $6,000 less new than a 2024.
- **Quickest:** a 2026 bZ with all-wheel drive, at 338 hp, or the 375-hp bZ Woodland.
- **Most space:** the bZ Woodland, with 74.3 cubic feet behind the front seats against the bZ's 67.1.
- **Think twice if** you can't charge at home, or often drive long distances in winter. A [RAV4 Hybrid](/fitdrive/guides/toyota-rav4/) needs no planning.
