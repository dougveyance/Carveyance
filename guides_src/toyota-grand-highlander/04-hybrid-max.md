The Hybrid MAX turns the Grand Highlander into a 3-row family crossover that's genuinely fast. It uses hybrid power for performance first and economy second, and the result is the quickest Grand Highlander by a wide margin, and the most expensive.

## What you get

The Hybrid MAX combines the 2.4-liter turbo 4-cylinder from the gas model with an electric motor at the front and a powerful electric rear axle. Together they make 362 hp and 400 lb-ft of torque. Unlike the regular Hybrid, it doesn't use a CVT. Power reaches the front wheels through a 6-speed automatic, and the rear motor drives the back wheels, so every Hybrid MAX has full-time all-wheel drive.

Toyota quoted 0 to 60 mph in 6.3 seconds. Car and Driver did better, running a Hybrid MAX Platinum to 60 mph in 5.6 seconds and through the quarter mile in 14.3 seconds, despite a test weight of 2,239 kg (4,936 lb). In a comparison test against other 3-row SUVs, it was the quickest in a straight line by a wide margin. It also tows 2,268 kg (5,000 lb), as much as the gas model.

The Hybrid MAX comes only in the 2 top grades, Limited and Platinum, and adds multi-terrain drive modes. For 2026, it starts at $55,190 for a Limited, before Toyota's destination charge.

## The numbers

[[chart:zero60]]

The EPA rates the Hybrid MAX at 27 mpg combined, with 26 in the city and 27 on the highway. That's better than the gas model's 22 to 24 mpg, but well short of the regular Hybrid's 34 to 36. In Car and Driver's 75-mph highway test, it managed 24 mpg. Think of it as a performance option that happens to use a little less fuel than the gas engine, not as the economy choice.

## How it drives

The Hybrid MAX feels far stronger than its siblings. The electric motors fill in below the turbo's boost, so it pulls hard from a standstill and keeps pulling on highway on-ramps and when passing. Because it uses a conventional 6-speed automatic rather than a CVT, it responds more like a gas car when you press the accelerator, and GearJunkie found it far sharper than Toyota's usual hybrids. Car and Driver measured 0.80 g on the skidpad and a 187-foot stop from 70 mph, numbers that show the chassis is willing, even if it's still a big, heavy family SUV.

## What to check

The Hybrid MAX shares its recalls with the rest of the range, from the 2024 curtain airbag recall to the 2024–2026 rear camera software update and, on cars built in spring and early summer 2026, the rear axle carrier and steering knuckle recalls. The checklist chapter has the details. Toyota covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 150,000 miles. Run the VIN on [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) before you buy.

## Which one to buy

- **Hybrid MAX Limited:** the better value of the 2, with the same powertrain.
- **Hybrid MAX Platinum:** every feature Toyota offers on the Grand Highlander.
- **Skip it if:** economy is your priority. An all-wheel-drive Hybrid saves about $360 a year in fuel over 12,000 miles, and the Hybrid range starts about $10,000 lower.

## The verdict

The Hybrid MAX is the Grand Highlander for the driver who doesn't want to give up performance for space. It's quick, it tows 2,268 kg (5,000 lb) and it uses a little less fuel than the gas model. It's also expensive, and if economy is what you want from a hybrid, the regular Hybrid is the smarter buy.

::: curiosity Nearly a Supra
The Hybrid MAX makes 362 hp and 400 lb-ft. The Drive pointed out that this puts it just 20 hp short of Toyota's own 6-cylinder GR Supra, and 32 lb-ft ahead of it on torque. One is a 2-seat sports car. The other seats 7 or 8 and has 13 cupholders.
:::
