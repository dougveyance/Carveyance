A used Corolla Cross is a young car. Even the oldest are 2022 models, and many are still under Toyota's factory warranty. That makes them easy to buy, but this model has had more recalls than its quiet reputation suggests, some of them important. Check every one.

## Pick the job first

For the best all-round car, look for a 2023–2026 hybrid, ideally an SE. For the lowest price, look at a gas LE, with all-wheel drive if you see snow. If you want the newest look and the bigger screen, look for a 2026. If you regularly carry adults in the back, sit there yourself before you buy.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | Launch year. Gas only, L, LE and XLE, FWD or AWD | Front passenger airbag panel recall |
| 2023 | Hybrid arrives, with standard AWD | Passenger airbag panel (some gas cars), driver airbag cable, hybrid brake software, hybrid reversing sound |
| 2024 | Hybrid Nightshade Edition, Toyota Safety Sense 3.0 on every model | Hybrid brake software, hybrid reversing sound |
| 2025 | New colors | Hybrid reversing sound |
| 2026 | Refresh, available 10.5-inch screen | Hybrid inverter bolt (some late-2025 builds). Too new for long-term data |

## Body and history

Run a vehicle history report, but don't treat a clean one as proof. Look for uneven panel gaps, mismatched paint and overspray on rubber seals, and check under the cargo floor for water or rust. Small SUVs often live hard lives as family cars, so look at the rear seats, cargo area and bumpers for wear and damage. If the car has a tow hitch, ask what it pulled. The limit is 680 kg (1,500 lb).

## Driving and drivetrain

On a gas model, the CVT should be smooth and quiet, with no shuddering, slipping or whining. On an all-wheel-drive gas model or any hybrid, drive slowly in tight circles in a parking lot and listen for binding or clunks.

On a hybrid, the car should start silently in Ready mode, and the engine should start and stop smoothly. There should be no warning lights. Ask a shop that knows hybrids to scan the system for stored codes. Every Corolla Cross Hybrid has Toyota's 10-year, 150,000-mile battery warranty, counted from the day it first went into service. In reverse, a hybrid should make an audible warning sound outside the car. If it's hard to hear, check whether the recall below has been done.

## Electronics and safety tech

Test the touchscreen, Apple CarPlay or Android Auto, the backup camera, radar cruise control, lane keeping and, where fitted, the blind spot monitor and parking sensors. A bumper or windshield repair done badly can upset the sensors these systems rely on.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2022–2023 gas models:** About 96,000 Corolla Crosses built between early September 2021 and early May 2023 were recalled because the instrument panel might not have the opening the front passenger airbag needs to deploy properly. Until the car was inspected and, if needed, fixed, Toyota recommended that nobody sit in the front passenger seat (23V-384). Some cars were later called back for a second inspection (23V-864). Make sure both are done.
- **2023:** About 18,000 gas models and 900 hybrids built between late February and late June 2023 needed the driver airbag's spiral cable inspected (23V-480). Some were called back again because the first inspection may have been done incorrectly (25V-040).
- **2023–2024 hybrids:** About 42,000 Corolla Cross Hybrids need a software update because the skid control computer could cause a loss of power brake assist when braking in a turn (24V-708).
- **2023–2025 hybrids:** About 73,500 Corolla Cross Hybrids may not make a loud enough pedestrian warning sound when reversing. Dealers update the software for free (26V-203).
- **2026 hybrids:** About 3,800 built between late August and late November 2025 may have a loose bolt inside the inverter, which can cause a loss of drive power. Dealers inspect the inverter and re-tighten the bolt or replace the inverter (25V-869).
- **Every year:** New recalls can appear at any time. Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly and hide the differences that matter. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, grade, powertrain, drivetrain, mileage and title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you, comparing a listing with similar ones nearby. Expect hybrids to carry a premium, and work out whether the fuel savings and extra performance are worth it to you.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the car past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best overall:** a 2023–2026 Hybrid SE with every recall done.
- **Best equipped:** a 2023–2026 Hybrid XSE.
- **Best value:** a 2022–2025 gas LE AWD, with proof of the airbag recall work.
- **Newest look:** a 2026 Hybrid SE or gas XLE with the 10.5-inch screen.
