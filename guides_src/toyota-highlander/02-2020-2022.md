For 2020, Toyota rebuilt the Highlander on its TNGA-K platform, the same basic architecture under the RAV4 and Camry. The new one is longer, more spacious in the cargo area and much better connected, with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto at last. It also kept the 3.5-liter V6, which makes the 2020–2022 cars the last V6 Highlanders. For many buyers, that's the reason to choose one.

## What changed

The fourth-generation Highlander is 194.9 inches long, about 2.4 inches longer than before, on a 112.2-inch wheelbase. Behind the third row there's 16 cu ft of cargo space, 48.4 cu ft with the third row folded and 84.3 cu ft with both rear rows down. The V6 carries over with 295 hp and 263 lb-ft of torque, and an 8-speed automatic. Front-wheel drive is standard. All-wheel drive is optional, and the upper grades use a Dynamic Torque Vectoring system that can disconnect the rear driveline to save fuel.

The 2020 Highlander came as an L, LE, XLE, Limited and Platinum, starting at $34,600, about $45,000 in 2026 dollars. The L and LE seat 8 on a second-row bench, and the XLE and Limited come with second-row captain's chairs and seats for 7. The Platinum got a 12.3-inch touchscreen, and the others an 8-inch screen. The new hybrid arrived alongside it; it has its own chapter.

For 2021, Toyota made Toyota Safety Sense 2.5+ standard and added the XSE, a sportier V6 model with a firmer suspension and its own styling, slotted between the XLE and Limited. For 2022, the XLE and up got an 8-way power passenger seat.

## The numbers

The EPA rated the front-drive V6 at 24 mpg combined and the all-wheel-drive V6 at 23, a small gain over the old car. Car and Driver ran a 2020 Platinum and a 2021 XSE, both with the V6 and all-wheel drive, from 0 to 60 mph in 6.8 seconds. Edmunds measured 7.5 seconds in its Platinum test car. Every V6 Highlander can tow 2,268 kg (5,000 lb).

## How it drives

The V6 is still the star. It's smooth, it sounds good when you ask for more, and it pulls the heavier new body with ease. The 8-speed automatic shifts smoothly and quickly enough. The ride is quiet and comfortable, and the handling is predictable rather than sporty, though the XSE's firmer suspension tightens things up a little.

The cabin is a step up from the old Highlander in quality and technology, and the cargo area behind the third row grows slightly. The third row is still tight for adults, which is exactly the gap the Grand Highlander was built to fill.

## What to check

- **Front bumper cover:** In October 2023, Toyota recalled about 751,000 2020–2023 Highlanders and Highlander Hybrids. A minor knock to the lower front bumper cover can make its mounting tabs come loose, and parts can fall off on the road. Dealers inspect the tabs and fit improved retention hardware, replacing the bumper covers if they're damaged.
- **Front passenger airbag sensor (23V-865):** 2020–2021 Highlanders were part of a December 2023 recall covering about 1 million Toyota and Lexus vehicles. A sensor in the front passenger seat can short-circuit, so the passenger airbag may not deploy. Dealers inspect and replace the sensor as needed.
- **Second-row seat recliners:** In March 2026, Toyota recalled about 550,000 2021–2024 Highlanders and Highlander Hybrids because the second-row seat recliners may not fully lock after the seatback is adjusted. Toyota later expanded the recall to include certain 2025 models, for a total of about 690,000. Dealers replace the recliner return springs. Until the work is done, check that each second-row seatback locks firmly in place.

Run the VIN on [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Which one to buy

- **LE or XLE:** the value picks, and the XLE adds the features most families want.
- **XSE (2021–2022):** the sportiest Highlander, V6 only.
- **Limited or Platinum:** the most comfortable, and the Platinum has the big screen.
- **AWD:** worth it if you see snow, and it costs only about 1 mpg.

## The verdict

The 2020–2022 V6 is the best gas Highlander of the decade. It's roomier and better equipped than the old car, it tows 2,268 kg (5,000 lb), and its V6 is smoother than the turbo 4-cylinder that replaced it. If you don't need a hybrid's fuel economy, this is the one to find.
