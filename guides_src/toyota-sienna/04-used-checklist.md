A minivan's life is hard on the parts you touch: sliding doors opened thousands of times, seats folded and slid, cabins full of crumbs and car seats. The Sienna's powertrains are durable, but judge the one in front of you by its records, an inspection and a careful look at its doors and seats, not by its reputation.

## First, know which one it is

The 2017–2020 Sienna is the third generation with the V6; the 2021–2026 is the fourth, and every one is a hybrid. Confirm whether it has front- or all-wheel drive, and whether it seats 7 or 8, because the seat recalls depend on the layout. If it's a wheelchair-accessible conversion, ask for the converter's paperwork too: conversions by companies such as BraunAbility have their own recalls.

## The test drive

Before you drive, work every door. Open and close both sliding doors several times with the button, the key fob and by hand, and the power liftgate too; listen for grinding and make sure each door latches fully. Fold, slide and stow every seat, including the third row. Then drive in town and on the freeway, brake hard from highway speed, and check that it tracks straight. Test the climate control in every zone, the rear entertainment system if it has one, the cameras and the phone connection.

## V6 checks

Ask for the oil-change history and cooling-system service, and look for leaks. The 8-speed should shift promptly and smoothly under load, with no flare or harshness. On a 2017–2018 van, ask a Toyota dealer whether the sliding door coverage from the class-action settlement still applies: it runs for 10 years from the date the van was first used, so it's ending around now for the earliest vans.

## Hybrid checks

Have a Toyota shop scan the hybrid system for stored codes. The engine will start and stop by itself, which is normal; warning lights and harsh clunks are not. Every 2021–2026 Sienna's hybrid battery is covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles from the date it was first sold, so check the in-service date and the mileage. On a 2025 or later van, confirm that the radar rear seat reminder and, on a Limited or Platinum, the vacuum and FridgeBox work.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2017–2020:** 20V-012 and 20V-682. The low-pressure fuel pump in the tank can fail and stall the engine. The fix is a new pump.
- **2017:** 18V-211. A badly machined brake booster vacuum pump rotor can cause a sudden loss of brake assist. 17V-295: some vans had a spare tire inflated to the wrong pressure. 17V-831: the load labels may be wrong.
- **2017–2019:** 19V-503. The load label on some vans may be wrong.
- **2018:** 18V-130. Certain alloy wheels can crack at the spokes and separate from the hub.
- **2019:** 19V-318. Incorrect welds in the front passenger door may affect the airbag sensor; the fix is a new door. 19V-244: the load label can become unreadable.
- **2021–2025:** 25V-086. The third-row seatback bolts may not have been tightened properly.
- **2022:** 21V-889. On some 8-seat vans, an incorrect seat belt guide on the outboard second-row seats can let the belt tear in a crash.
- **2022–2026 7-seaters:** 26V-690. When the second-row seat belt pretensioners fire in certain high-speed frontal crashes, the seatback recliners can unlock. The fix is an airbag computer software update, or new seatbacks on vans with a mobility package. Letters go out from December 7, 2026.
- **2025:** 25V-668. The second-row seat rails may be welded improperly. 25V-744: the rear camera image can freeze or go blank in reverse.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Walk away if

The van has a branded title or signs of flood damage, or a seat recall the seller can't show was done. Walk away from sliding doors that won't latch, a V6 that runs hot, hybrid warning lights the seller can't explain, and any seller who won't allow an inspection.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and an average that mixes a 2017 L with a 2025 Platinum tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, grade, drive, seating and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what vans actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Honda Odyssey, a Chrysler Pacifica and a Kia Carnival with similar miles, and take the cost of tires, brakes, door repairs and overdue service off the price.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Sienna past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for the hybrid system and the power sliding doors, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2021 or later XLE hybrid with its seat recalls done.
- **Best value:** a 2019–2020 LE or XLE V6 with records and its fuel pump replaced.
- **Most luxury:** a 2025 or later Limited or Platinum.
- **Best in snow:** any AWD hybrid.
