For 2026, Toyota gave its electric SUV a new name and changes that go well beyond the badge. The bZ answers most of the complaints about the bZ4X: it goes farther, charges more easily and, with all-wheel drive, is genuinely quick. Alongside it came the bZ Woodland, a longer, more rugged version with more space and even more power. Both are new enough that there's little long-term ownership history yet.

## What changed

The batteries grew. The base XLE FWD has a 57.7 kWh pack, and every other version has 74.7 kWh. Front-drive power rose to as much as 221 hp, up from 201, and all-wheel-drive output jumped to 338 hp, up from 214. Toyota claims 0 to 60 mph in 4.9 seconds for the AWD bZ, and MotorWeek ran a front-drive XLE FWD Plus to 60 in 6.3.

Charging improved most of all. The bZ has a NACS port, the Tesla-style plug, with Plug & Charge, so it can use compatible fast chargers without an adapter. Battery preconditioning is standard: it warms or cools the pack before a fast-charging stop, either when you switch it on or automatically when you set a fast charger as your navigation destination. Toyota quotes 10% to 80% in about 30 minutes under ideal conditions. The onboard charger for home and Level 2 charging went from 7 kW to 11 kW, which shortens a full overnight charge.

The range numbers finally compete. Edmunds drove an XLE FWD Plus 331 miles on a charge in its real-world test, 17 more than its 314-mile EPA rating.

## The bZ Woodland

The Woodland is nearly 6 inches longer than the bZ and a little taller at the back. It comes only with all-wheel drive, makes 375 hp and, by Toyota's estimate, reaches 60 mph in 4.4 seconds. It holds 74.3 cubic feet behind the front seats, against the bZ's 67.1, has 8.4 inches of ground clearance and can tow up to 1,588 kg (3,500 lb). X-MODE, with Grip Control for low-speed traction, is standard. There are 2 grades, the Woodland and the Woodland Premium, which adds JBL audio, ventilated front seats and a panoramic moonroof. Toyota expected it at dealers in March 2026, starting at $45,300.

Its own range figures make a useful point. The EPA rates the Woodland at up to 281 miles, but only 260 with the optional all-terrain tires. That 21-mile difference is a reminder that tires, roof racks and trailers all cost range, and that a tow rating tells you what the car can pull, not how far. If you plan to tow, map the chargers on your route and check whether you can reach the stalls without unhooking the trailer.

Ground clearance and X-MODE make the Woodland good on dirt roads and snowy driveways. It isn't a rock crawler. For remote trips far from chargers, a hybrid [4Runner](/fitdrive/guides/toyota-4runner/) or [Land Cruiser](/fitdrive/guides/toyota-land-cruiser/) is the easier tool.

## Which to buy

For most buyers, the bZ XLE FWD Plus is the sweet spot: the longest range, a reasonable price and enough power. Choose the AWD bZ if you want the speed or live where it snows, and accept 26 fewer miles of range than the FWD Plus. Choose the Woodland if you need the extra space, carry bikes or dogs, or drive unpaved roads. If you're weighing a used 2023–2025 bZ4X against a new or nearly new 2026, compare the range you'd actually use against the price gap. For anyone who takes regular road trips, the 2026 is worth a real premium.
