Every Highlander from 2017 to 2026 is the same kind of vehicle: a comfortable 3-row family SUV built on a car-like platform. What sets them apart is the engine. Choosing the right one matters more than choosing the right year, because it decides how much you spend on fuel, what you can tow and how the car feels every time you pull away.

## The 3 choices

- **3.5-liter V6 (2017–2022):** 295 hp and 263 lb-ft, with an 8-speed automatic. It's the smoothest and most refined, and it tows 2,268 kg (5,000 lb). The EPA rated it at 22 to 24 mpg combined, depending on year and drive.
- **2.4-liter turbo 4-cylinder (2023–2026):** 265 hp and 310 lb-ft, with an 8-speed automatic. It has the most low-speed pull and also tows 2,268 kg (5,000 lb). It's rated at 24 to 25 mpg combined.
- **2.5-liter hybrid (2020–2026):** 243 hp, with an electronically controlled CVT. It's the quietest in town and by far the most efficient, at 35 to 36 mpg combined, but it tows only 1,588 kg (3,500 lb).

The 2017–2019 Highlander Hybrid is a fourth option, pairing the V6 with electric motors for 306 hp and 28 to 29 mpg. It's quick, but it has the older tech and the older battery warranty. The base 2017–2019 4-cylinder is best avoided: it's slow and no more efficient than the V6.

Towing is where the choice is clearest. If you pull a boat, a camper or a loaded trailer heavier than 1,588 kg (3,500 lb), you need the V6 or the turbo. If you don't, the hybrid gives you the same space and comfort for about a third less fuel, as chart 4 in the hybrid chapter shows.

## How they feel

The V6 is the one enthusiasts miss. It's smooth, it revs freely and it sounds good. The turbo is stronger in everyday driving, with more pull at low revs, but it's less refined and gives up 30 hp at the top end. The hybrid is the calmest of the 3, gliding away on electric power in traffic, but it drones when pushed hard and has the least power for passing and merging.

## Which one to buy

- **Choose the V6 (2020–2022)** if you want the smoothest Highlander and tow up to 2,268 kg (5,000 lb), and you're happy with a used one.
- **Choose the turbo (2023–2026)** if you want a newer gas Highlander that tows 2,268 kg (5,000 lb), and you value low-speed pull over refinement.
- **Choose the hybrid (2020–2026)** if you tow less than 1,588 kg (3,500 lb), drive a lot or spend time in traffic. For most families, it's the best Highlander.
- **Choose the Grand Highlander** if you need a usable third row for adults or more cargo space, especially its Hybrid MAX if you want hybrid economy and 2,268 kg (5,000 lb) of towing.

## The verdict

For most buyers, the hybrid is the right Highlander. It costs less to run than either gas engine and does everything else just as well, unless you tow something heavy. If you do, the 2020–2022 V6 is the most satisfying gas engine, and the turbo is the newer choice.
