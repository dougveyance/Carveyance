The Corolla Cross has had a short, steady life so far: a launch, a hybrid, some trim shuffling and a light refresh. That makes it an easy used car to shop, as long as you know which details changed.

## Year by year

- **2022:** The launch year, gas only, in L, LE and XLE grades, each with front- or all-wheel drive. Toyota Safety Sense 2.0 was standard. The L started at $22,195 (about $25,600 in 2026 money), and all-wheel drive added $1,300 (about $1,500).
- **2023:** The hybrid arrives, with 196 hp, standard all-wheel drive, Toyota Safety Sense 3.0 and Toyota's newer touchscreen system with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto. It came in S, SE and XSE grades, starting at $27,970, $29,290 and $31,065 (about $30,900, $32,400 and $34,400 in 2026 money).
- **2024:** A Hybrid Nightshade Edition joins the lineup, based on the SE, with gloss-black 18-inch wheels and blacked-out badges. By now, Toyota Safety Sense 3.0 is standard on every Corolla Cross.
- **2025:** New colors. Every grade has an 8-inch touchscreen with Toyota's newer multimedia system.
- **2026:** A refresh. The hybrid gets a body-color grille, and the gas model a wider, more rugged one. Inside, there's a new center console, an available 10.5-inch touchscreen and a 7-inch or 12.3-inch digital cluster. Cavalry Blue paint and new 18-inch wheels arrive too.
- **2027:** Announced in October 2026, the 2027 drops the Hybrid S, leaving the SE and XSE, and otherwise carries over. Toyota lists the gas L at $25,535 and the Hybrid SE at $31,415.

## The grades

| Grade | Powertrain | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| L | Gas, FWD or AWD | The basics: 17-inch steel wheels, 7-inch screen (8-inch by 2025), Toyota Safety Sense. The 2022 L starts with a key |
| LE | Gas, FWD or AWD | 17-inch alloys, 8-inch screen, push-button start, blind spot monitor, wireless charging |
| XLE | Gas, FWD or AWD | 18-inch alloys, SofTex upholstery, power driver's seat, front and rear parking assist with automatic braking |
| S | Hybrid, AWD | 17-inch alloys, sport-tuned suspension, LED lights, smart key |
| SE | Hybrid, AWD | Adds roof rails, privacy glass and blind spot monitor |
| Nightshade | Hybrid, AWD (2024–2025) | SE-based, with black 18-inch wheels and black trim |
| XSE | Hybrid, AWD | 18-inch wheels, SofTex upholstery, heated front seats, power driver's seat |

## Which one to buy

- **Hybrid SE, any year:** the sweet spot. You get the hybrid's power and economy, roof rails and blind spot monitoring without paying for the XSE.
- **Hybrid XSE:** if you want heated SofTex seats and a power driver's seat.
- **2026 or 2027, any grade:** if you want the newest look and the bigger screen.
- **Gas LE:** the best-equipped value pick if you choose gas. It adds the safety and convenience features most people want over the L.
- **Gas L:** only if the price is clearly lower. It's basic, and a 2022 L starts with a key.

## The verdict

Don't overthink the year. A 2023 or later hybrid is the Corolla Cross to have, and the 2026 refresh is a nice bonus rather than a must. Focus on the condition and history of the car in front of you.

::: curiosity The Last Turn of the Key
When the Corolla Cross arrived for 2022, the base L was one of the few new cars left that you started with a turn of a key. Push-button start and a smart key came only on the LE and XLE.
:::
