By the 2017 model year the fifth-generation 4Runner had already been on sale for 7 years, and Toyota kept building it for 7 more. The recipe never changed: a 4.0-liter V6, a 5-speed automatic and a separate ladder frame under the body. While the rest of the SUV world went quieter, softer and unibody, the 4Runner stayed a truck with a family-sized cabin on top, and it never apologized for it.

That stubbornness is the appeal and the problem in one. If your weekends end at a campsite down a rutted fire road, or your winters involve chains and a ski-resort parking lot, there is real hardware under those square fenders. If your week is mostly freeway, a Highlander will be quieter at 75 mph, kinder over expansion joints and cheaper to fuel. The 4Runner earned its reputation. Make sure you need what it's good at before you pay for it.

## How it drives

The V6 makes 270 hp and 278 lb-ft, and it hands them over steadily rather than eagerly. With only 5 gears to choose from, the transmission holds each one a long time when you ask for a pass, and the engine lets the whole cabin know about it. Car and Driver timed a 2017 TRD Off-Road to 60 mph in 7.5 seconds and a 2019 TRD Off-Road Premium in 7.7, which is respectable for a truck weighing about 2,155 kg (4,750 lb).

The steering is slow, the body leans through corners and the high seat tells you more about the height of the truck than about the grip of the front tires. None of that is a fault. It's what a body-on-frame SUV designed for rocks feels like on a freeway on-ramp.

Two-wheel-drive versions are fine for commuting and towing. For trails you want a 4x4 with a low-range transfer case. Most grades use part-time four-wheel drive that you switch on when the surface gets loose. The Limited uses full-time four-wheel drive with a locking center differential, the better setup if you face snow and ice all winter rather than mud on the weekends. Properly equipped, any of them tows 2,268 kg (5,000 lb).

## Living with it

The cabin is the oldest part of the truck and, oddly, part of its charm: big knobs, chunky buttons you can work in gloves, hard-wearing plastics and a square cargo hold that swallows coolers whole.

[[photo:ch1-cabin]]

2020 is the year to know. That's when Toyota added an 8-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay, Android Auto and Amazon Alexa, put 2 USB ports in the back seat and made Toyota Safety Sense P standard on every grade. That package brought automatic emergency braking with pedestrian detection, lane departure alert, adaptive cruise control and automatic high beams. A 2019 is still a good truck. A 2020 is a much easier one to live with every day.

Fuel is the price of admission. The EPA rates a 4x4 at 17 mpg combined (16 city, 19 highway). At 15,000 miles a year that's about 882 gallons, or roughly $3,529 at $4 a gallon. People who love their 4Runners rarely bring that part up.

## What to check before you buy

The engine and transmission have a long, well-documented service record, but a good reputation is not an inspection. Get the truck on a lift and look hard at the frame and underbody, especially on trucks from states that salt their roads. Look for oil and coolant leaks, weeping differentials and transfer case seals, worn suspension bushings and tired brakes. Shift into four-high and four-low where it's safe to, and make sure both engage cleanly.

Be wary of modifications. Lift kits, oversized tires, winches and steel bumpers add weight and strain, and they're only as good as whoever installed them. A stock truck with records is worth more to you than a built one with stories.

Then run the VIN. Certain 2018 and 2019 4Runners were part of Toyota's recall for a low-pressure fuel pump that can fail and stall the engine (20V-012, later expanded as 20V-682). Ask for proof the repair was done. The full checklist is in [Look Under the Legend](../used-inspection/).

## The verdict

The sweet spot is a well-kept 2020–2024 SR5 Premium or TRD Off-Road: the newer screen and safety equipment on the same proven mechanicals. A 2017–2019 makes sense when the price reflects the older cabin and the inspection comes back clean. Pay TRD Pro money only if you'll use the Fox shocks, the skid plate and the extra clearance.

Buy this 4Runner if your life includes roads that aren't particularly interested in being roads. Look elsewhere if you want quiet, sharp handling and modest fuel bills. It's admirable. It isn't magic.
