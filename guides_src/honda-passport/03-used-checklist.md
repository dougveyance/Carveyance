A Passport is bought to carry people and gear somewhere interesting, and the badge on the tailgate tells you less about its life than the tires, the underside and the service records do. A TrailSport may never have left the pavement, and an EX-L may have spent its weekends on fire roads. Look underneath before you look at the roof rack.

## First, know which one it is

Decode the VIN to confirm the year, the trim and the drivetrain. A 2019–2022 Passport may have front-wheel drive; every 2023 and later Passport has all-wheel drive. A 2024 or later TrailSport has all-terrain tires on 18-inch wheels. A 2026 or later is the new generation, with the 10-speed, and a 2027 TrailSport sits higher than a 2026.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 | Third generation. 9-speed, FWD or AWD | Frame welds (19V784). Hood latch (21V932). Continental tires (21V165). Fuel pump (21V215, 23V858). Rear subframe in salt-belt states (26V365) |
| 2020 | Carryover | Continental tires (21V165). Instrument panel and camera software (20V439, 20V440). Mirror glass (23V174). Rear subframe (26V365) |

## The test drive

Start the engine from cold and listen for rattles or a ticking that doesn't fade. Drive in stop-and-go traffic and at highway speed: shifts should be smooth, with no flare, clunk or hesitation, and the push-button gear selector should respond without delay. On an all-wheel-drive car, make a few slow, tight turns in a parking lot and listen for groaning or binding. Test the rearview camera with the engine started from cold, and check the instrument panel shows every warning light at start-up.

## Engine and drivetrain checks

The V6 has a timing belt, due every 7 years or 105,000 miles, usually with the water pump. Ask for proof it was done, or price it in. Look for records of transmission fluid changes and, on all-wheel-drive cars, rear differential fluid. On a Passport that has towed, check the hitch, the wiring and the brakes. Look for oil leaks around the engine and the rear drive unit.

## Underneath, and the TrailSport

Get the car on a lift if you can. Look at the rear subframe, especially on a car from a salt-belt state, and at the skid plates, the recovery hooks and the lower edges of the bumpers for scrapes and dents. Check all 4 tires for even wear and cuts in the sidewalls, and the wheels for bends. A lift kit or oversized tires change how the car drives and wears, so ask who fitted them and when.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2019:** 19V784, front frame upper members that may not have been fully welded. 21V932, a hood latch striker that can separate, letting the hood open while driving. 21V215, a low-pressure fuel pump that can fail.
- **2019–2020:** 21V165, Continental tires that may have been overcured. 20V439 and 20V440, software faults that can stop the instrument panel or the rearview camera from displaying.
- **2019–2021:** 23V858, fuel pumps. 26V332, and 24V064 for 2020–2021, a passenger seat weight sensor that can fail, so the air bag may not work correctly.
- **2019–2023:** 23V431, a rearview camera connector. 26V365, rear subframe corrosion on cars sold in 22 salt-belt states and DC; Honda installs a reinforcement kit.
- **2020:** 20V067, a certification label whose ink can wipe off. **2020–2022:** 23V174, side mirror glass that can detach.
- **2021–2023:** 23V458, a brake master cylinder fastener that may not be secured.
- **2023:** 24V184, a steering gearbox ball joint that may loosen. **2023–2024:** 24V900, a fuel filler neck that can separate and leak.
- **2026:** 26V424, rear subframe bolts not tightened properly.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done. A recall for a model year doesn't mean every car is affected, and a car that has moved states may still need the subframe work.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title, flood damage or a musty smell you can't explain. Walk away from a transmission that slips or bangs into gear, an all-wheel-drive system that binds in tight turns, a rotted or freshly undercoated rear subframe the seller can't document, and any seller who won't allow an independent inspection.

## What is it worth?

[[chart:price]]

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and an average that mixes a 2019 Sport with a 2026 TrailSport Elite tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same generation, trim, drive and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Toyota 4Runner or a Subaru Outback of the same age, and take the cost of a due timing belt, tires and brakes off the price.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Passport past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for the transmission and the all-wheel-drive system, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2026 or later RTL with its recalls done.
- **Best value:** a 2023–2025 EX-L, with standard all-wheel drive.
- **Best for dirt roads:** a 2024 or later TrailSport, or a 2027 TrailSport for the most ground clearance.
- **Cheapest way in:** a 2019–2021 Sport or EX-L with front-wheel drive and every recall done.
