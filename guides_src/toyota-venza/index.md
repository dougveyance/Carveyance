The Venza came back to the US for 2021 as a 5-seat crossover sold only as a hybrid, and every one has all-wheel drive. Underneath, it's close kin to the RAV4 Hybrid, with the same 105.9-inch wheelbase and the same 219-hp system. On top, Toyota gave it a long, sloping body and a quieter, dressier cabin. The price of that shape is space: behind the rear seat it holds 28.8 cubic feet, against the RAV4 Hybrid's 37.6. It lasted 4 model years before the Crown Signia replaced it for 2025.

Every Venza has the same engine and the same fuel economy, so choosing one comes down to the year, the grade and the car's history. This guide has 3 chapters: the review, the trims and years, and the checklist to use before you buy.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2023–2024 XLE. It has the 12.3-inch screen and the newer Toyota Safety Sense 2.5 suite as standard.
- **Best value:** a documented 2021–2022 LE. Same hybrid, same 39 mpg, fewer features to go wrong.
- **Most luxury:** a Limited, ideally with the Star Gaze glass roof, tested in both modes before you buy.
- **Think twice if** you carry big strollers, crates or sports gear. A [RAV4 Hybrid](/fitdrive/guides/toyota-rav4/) holds about 30% more behind its rear seat.
