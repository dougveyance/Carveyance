With 4 model years and a single drivetrain, the Insight looks simple to shop for, and mostly it is. What separates a good one from a bad one is the history: which recalls have been done, how it was serviced, and whether the hybrid system has thrown any faults. A 2019 with complete records can be a better buy than a 2022 without them.

## First, know which one it is

Decode the VIN to confirm the year and trim. The LX was sold from 2019 to 2021 only, so a listing for a "2022 LX" is wrong. The EX and Touring have the 8-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, and the Touring has leather, navigation and a power driver's seat, and a lower EPA rating, 48 mpg instead of 52.

## The years at a glance

| Model year | What it is | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 | Launch year; LX, EX, Touring | Air bag control unit (18V664). Backup camera display (18V629). Front seat belt buckle (23V158). Fuel pump (20V314, 21V215, 23V858). Body control software (20V771). Passenger seat sensor (26V332) |
| 2020 | Carryover | Power supply that can cut drive power (20V798). Fuel pump (20V314, 21V215, 23V858). Body control software (20V771). Passenger seat sensor (26V332) |
| 2021 | Carryover | Power supply (20V798). Fuel pump (23V858). Passenger seat sensor (24V064, 26V332) |
| 2022 | Last year; EX and Touring only | Rear center seat belt retractor (21V900). Fuel pump (23V858). Passenger seat sensor (26V332) |

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2019:** 18V664, an air bag control unit that may not deploy the air bags or tighten the seat belts in a crash. 18V629, a backup camera image that may not appear. 23V158, a part in a front seat belt buckle.
- **2020–2021:** 20V798, a DC-DC converter, which supplies the car's 12-volt system from the hybrid battery, that can shut down and cause a loss of drive power. This is the most important one to confirm.
- **2019–2022:** fuel pump recalls, 20V314 and 21V215 for 2019–2020 and 23V858 for 2019–2022, for pumps that can fail and stall the engine. 26V332, and 24V064 for 2021, for a front passenger seat sensor that can affect the passenger air bag.
- **2019–2020:** 20V771, body control software.
- **2022:** 21V900, a rear center seat belt retractor.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls), ask a Honda dealer for any open campaigns and software updates, and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## The hybrid system

The battery's charge gauge rising and falling on a test drive is normal. What matters is how the car behaves. It should start and drive away on electric power, switch between electric and engine power without jolts, and show no hybrid system warnings. Ask a Honda dealer or a shop that works on Honda hybrids to scan for stored and pending fault codes and check the hybrid battery, because a fault can be stored without a light on the dashboard. Don't let anyone without hybrid training open the high-voltage system.

Ask the seller, or a Honda dealer with the VIN, what's left of the hybrid component warranty, and check the condition of the 12-volt battery, which still starts the car's systems.

## The rest of the car

Drive it over rough pavement and on the highway, listening for knocks, rattles and wheel-bearing hum. Check that the brakes feel consistent: the car uses the motor to slow down before the brake pads take over, and the change should be smooth. Run the air conditioning at idle, test the cameras and Honda Sensing, and if the windshield has been replaced, ask whether the camera behind it was recalibrated. Finally, load the trunk with what you usually carry; the opening is a sedan's, not a hatchback's.

## What it costs to run

At 12,000 miles a year, an EX at 52 mpg uses about 231 gallons, and a non-hybrid compact at 32 mpg about 375, about 144 gallons more. Multiply the difference by what you pay for fuel to see what the Insight saves you each year, and compare that with any extra you'd pay for it. Tires, brakes and the 12-volt battery are ordinary running costs, the same as on any car.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title, flood damage, damage near the hybrid battery under the rear seat, a hybrid system warning the seller can't explain, or an open 20V798 recall with no repair date. Walk away from any seller who won't allow an inspection by a shop that knows Honda hybrids.

## What is it worth?

[[chart:price]]

We don't publish average prices, because they go stale quickly. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year and trim, with similar mileage and the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Toyota Corolla Hybrid or Prius, a Hyundai Ioniq Hybrid and a Civic of the same age.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Insight past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for hybrid parts, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2019–2022 EX with its recalls done.
- **Most comfortable:** a Touring, if 48 mpg is enough.
- **Cheapest way in:** a 2019–2021 LX priced clearly below an EX.
