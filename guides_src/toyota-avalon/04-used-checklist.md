An Avalon feels so effortless that a seller can easily play down overdue maintenance. Ask whether it was ever a livery, rental or fleet car, and judge the one in front of you by its records and an inspection, not by the badge.

## First, know which one it is

The 2017–2018 Avalon is the fourth generation; the 2019–2022 is a different car, with a new platform, engines and technology. Don't compare asking prices as if they were the same. Confirm the powertrain too: V6, hybrid or, for 2021 only, the all-wheel-drive 4-cylinder.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 | Fourth generation. TSS-P newly standard | Airbag control unit recall (20V-024). Spare-tire recall on some cars (17V-295) |
| 2018 | Carryover | Airbag control unit recall (20V-024). Fuel pump recall on some cars (20V-012) |
| 2019 | Fifth generation. 301-hp V6, Apple CarPlay | Airbag control unit recall (18V-685). Fuel pump recalls (20V-012, 20V-682). Load-label recall (19V-244) |
| 2020 | Avalon TRD added | Fuel pump recall (20V-682). Steering column and passenger airbag sensor recalls (20V-734, 23V-865) |
| 2021 | AWD, XSE Nightshade, Android Auto, lithium-ion hybrid battery | Steering column and passenger airbag sensor recalls (20V-734, 23V-865) |
| 2022 | Final year. AWD and TRD dropped, TSS 2.5 standard | Run the VIN |

## The test drive

Spend most of the drive at freeway speed, because that's what an Avalon is for. Listen for wind and tire noise, feel for vibration through the steering wheel and the brake pedal, and check that it tracks straight. Brake hard from highway speed. Test the climate control on full heat and full cold, every seat adjustment, the heated and ventilated seats, the cameras, the phone connection and the navigation.

## V6 checks

Ask for the oil-change history and the cooling-system service. Look for coolant or oil leaks, and watch for overheating on a long drive. The automatic should shift smoothly and promptly under load, with no flare or harshness. Uneven tire wear points to alignment or suspension problems.

## Hybrid checks

Have a Toyota shop scan the hybrid system for stored codes. The engine will start and stop by itself, which is normal; warning lights and harsh clunks are not. Check the battery warranty: 2020–2022 hybrids are covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles from the date they were first sold, while 2017–2019 hybrids had 8 years or 100,000 miles in most states.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2017:** 17V-295. Some cars sold through Gulf States Toyota had a spare tire inflated below the pressure on the placard.
- **2017–2018:** 20V-024. The airbag control unit may malfunction in certain crashes, so the airbags and seatbelt pretensioners may not deploy.
- **2018–2020:** 20V-012 and 20V-682. The low-pressure fuel pump in the tank can fail, and the engine can stall while driving. The fix is a new pump.
- **2019:** 18V-685. The airbag control unit may wrongly detect a fault at startup, and the airbags may not deploy as intended. 19V-244: the load-capacity label can become unreadable.
- **2020–2021:** 20V-734. The steering column's breakaway pins may have been damaged in production, which could weaken the driver's airbag. 23V-865: a short in the front passenger's occupant sensor can stop that airbag from deploying.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title, signs of flood damage or a cooling system that runs hot. Walk away from a hybrid with warning lights the seller can't explain, and from any car whose seller won't allow an inspection.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and an average that mixes a 2017 V6 with a 2022 Hybrid Limited tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, grade, powertrain and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Camry XLE and a Lexus ES with similar miles, and take the cost of tires, brakes and overdue service off the price.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Avalon past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for the hybrid system, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2020–2022 Hybrid XLE with battery warranty left.
- **Best V6:** a 2019–2022 XLE or Limited with records.
- **Sportiest:** a 2020–2021 TRD or a Touring.
- **Cheapest way in:** a 2017–2018 XLE with records and its recalls done.
