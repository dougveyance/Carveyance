The Ford E-Series family

4 generations and variants in the Index, 1961–present. Oldest first; every card opens the full page, with its own versions, figures and story.

  1. 1961–1967

    Ford Econoline (first generation)

    Ford's answer to the VW Bus: a flat-nosed Falcon with the engine between the seats, $1,881 to start — and the first chapter of a van line that would pass 8.2 million.

    85 hp23.0 sec 0-6075 mph top552,004 built
    Cargo and panel van · Falcon Club Wagon and Station Bus · Econoline pickup (1961–67)
  2. 1969–1974

    Ford Econoline (second generation)

    The engine moved forward, the V8 moved in, and the custom-van decade found its canvas — plus the 1972 Hi-Cube, the cutaway that launched a million motorhomes.

    170 hp16.0 sec 0-6085 mph top
    Cargo van and Club Wagon · Hi-Cube cutaway (1972–)
  3. 1975–1991

    Ford Econoline (third generation)

    The first body-on-frame American van won Motor Trend's first Truck of the Year in 1978, took the sales crown in 1980 — and never gave it back while the vans lived.

    140 hp14.5 sec 0-6090 mph top
    E-150 (300 six and 302 V8) · 460 V8 and the diesels · Club Wagon (up to 15 seats)
  4. 1992–present

    Ford E-Series (fourth generation)

    Motor Trend's 1992 Truck of the Year became the longest-serving American van: best-seller every year the vans lived, retired in 2014 — and still rolling off the Ohio line as a cutaway.

    225 hp10.0 sec 0-6095 mph top3,154,948 built
    E-150 (4.6 Triton) · 4.9 inline-6 (1992–96) · 5.4 and 6.8 Triton (1997–2014) · Power Stroke diesels (1995–2010) · E-Series cutaway (2015–)
