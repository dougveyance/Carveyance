A Venza shopper can spend hours comparing listings without noticing the most useful fact: the powertrain never changed. Every Venza from 2021 to 2024 has the same 219-hp hybrid, the same all-wheel drive and the same 39 mpg combined. What changed is the equipment, and the year that matters is 2023, when Toyota updated the screens and the safety suite.

## The grades

| Grade | Years | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| LE | 2021–2024 | 18-inch two-tone wheels, an 8-inch touchscreen, Toyota Safety Sense and blind spot monitoring |
| XLE | 2021–2024 | 19-inch chrome wheels, front and rear parking assist with automatic braking and a 7-inch driver display instead of the LE's 4.2-inch. The 12.3-inch screen was optional for 2021–2022 and standard from 2023 |
| Nightshade | 2023–2024 | An XLE with gloss-black 19-inch wheels, black badges and darker trim |
| Limited | 2021–2024 | The 12.3-inch screen with JBL audio and the optional Star Gaze roof. By 2024 it also had a 12.3-inch digital cluster, a digital rearview mirror and a head-up display |

The LE is the value play. It has the same hybrid, the same fuel economy and the same standard safety equipment, and it skips the parts that are expensive to fix later. Don't dismiss a clean LE just because it lacks a big screen or a glass roof.

The XLE is the sweet spot for most buyers. It feels enough like a luxury car to justify choosing a Venza over a RAV4, without the Limited's price. A 2023 or 2024 XLE gets the 12.3-inch screen as standard.

The Nightshade is mostly about looks. Black wheels don't make the hybrid any healthier, so pay XLE money for it, check the wheels for curb damage and don't pay much extra for the color scheme.

The Limited is for buyers who'll use its extras. Test every one of them: the head-up display, the digital mirror, the parking cameras and, above all, the Star Gaze roof in both modes. Complex features cost more to repair once the warranty runs out, and the asking price should reflect that.

## The years

- **2021:** the launch year. Toyota Safety Sense 2.0, an 8-inch screen on the LE and XLE, and the 12.3-inch screen on the Limited. Certain early cars are in the rear turn-signal recall.
- **2022:** a carryover year. Certain 2021–2022 cars are in the stability-control software recall.
- **2023:** the update. A new Nightshade Edition, Toyota Safety Sense 2.5, Toyota's newer multimedia system with 8-inch and 12.3-inch screens, and a 12.3-inch digital cluster on the Limited. This is also where the 2025 display and camera recalls begin.
- **2024:** the last year, essentially unchanged, with Toyota Safety Sense 2.5+.

The prices tell an odd story. Once you account for inflation, a 2024 Venza cost less new than a 2021, because Toyota's increases didn't keep up with prices in general. Keep that in mind when a seller argues that a later car should cost much more.

## Our picks

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2023–2024 XLE, for the 12.3-inch screen and the newer safety suite.
- **Best value:** a 2021–2022 LE with records and its recalls done.
- **Most luxury:** a Limited whose features all work, with the Star Gaze roof if you want it.
- **Nightshade:** only at an XLE price.

A documented 2021 can be a better buy than a neglected 2024, so let the history and an inspection decide between them. [The used checklist](../used-checklist/) has what to look for.
