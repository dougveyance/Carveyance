For 2023, the Highlander lost its V6. In its place went a 2.4-liter turbocharged 4-cylinder shared with Lexus, and the refreshed Highlander picked up bigger screens and a few styling changes to go with it. It's the Highlander most new-car shoppers have seen in showrooms for the past 4 years, and with the 2027 model going electric, it's the last gas Highlander.

## What changed

The turbo engine makes 265 hp, 30 fewer than the V6, but 310 lb-ft of torque, 17% more than the V6's 263 lb-ft, and that torque arrives at low revs. It drives through an 8-speed automatic with stop-start, and it's rated to tow 2,268 kg (5,000 lb), the same as the V6. Toyota said the new engine cut NOx and non-methane organic gas emissions by more than 50%. The 2023 Highlander started at $36,420 for an L with front-wheel drive, about $40,300 in 2026 dollars.

From 2023, the upper grades have twin 12.3-inch screens, one for the touchscreen and one for the instruments. For 2025, Toyota marked the Highlander's 25th model year with a Limited 25th Edition Hybrid, limited to 2,500 cars, and made all-wheel drive standard on every hybrid.

For 2026, the gas Highlander got all-wheel drive as standard too, and Toyota trimmed the lineup. The LE was dropped, leaving 4 gas grades, XLE, XSE, Limited and Platinum, and 3 hybrid grades, XLE, Limited and Platinum. The gas XLE uses a mechanical all-wheel-drive system, and the XSE, Limited and Platinum get Dynamic Torque Vectoring all-wheel drive. With the cheaper versions gone, the 2026 Highlander starts at $45,270 before destination, $4,950 more than the 2025.

The EPA rated the front-drive turbo at 25 mpg combined and the all-wheel-drive version at 24. That's only 1 mpg better than the V6 it replaced, so the gain is mostly in low-speed pull and cleaner exhaust, not in fuel bills. For 2026, with all-wheel drive standard, every gas Highlander is rated at 24 mpg.

## How it drives

The turbo feels stronger than the V6 in everyday driving, because its torque comes in so low. Pulling away from a light or merging onto a highway takes less effort. The trade is refinement. Reviewers found it less refined than the V6. The 8-speed automatic is well matched to it, and the ride and handling are unchanged from the 2020–2022 car, comfortable and predictable.

## What happens next

Toyota unveiled an all-electric 2027 Highlander in February 2026, its first 3-row battery-electric vehicle for the US and the first battery-electric vehicle it assembles in America. It has no gas or hybrid version. In July 2026, Toyota delayed the electric model's launch, and kept the 2026 gas and hybrid Highlander in production through the end of the year. If you want a new Highlander with an engine, the 2026 is the last chance. After that, the Grand Highlander becomes Toyota's gas and hybrid 3-row crossover.

## What to check

- **Front bumper cover:** 2023 Highlanders are part of Toyota's October 2023 recall of about 751,000 2020–2023 Highlanders. The lower front bumper cover's mounting tabs can come loose after a minor knock. Dealers fit improved retention hardware.
- **Second-row seat recliners:** 2023–2025 Highlanders are part of the March 2026 recall, expanded later in the year to about 690,000 vehicles. The second-row recliners may not fully lock after adjustment. Dealers replace the return springs.
- **Instrument panel software (25V-595):** Certain 2023–2025 Highlanders may start up without the speedometer and some warning lights showing. The fix is a free software update.
- **Rearview camera software (25V-744):** About 45,600 2023–2025 Highlanders and 38,000 Highlander Hybrids with the Panoramic View Monitor can show a frozen or blank rear camera image in reverse. The fix is a free software update.
- **Third-row center seat belt (25V-872):** Just 39 2024–2025 Highlanders and Highlander Hybrids were recalled because the bolt for the third-row center seat belt's roof anchor may have been installed incorrectly. Dealers reinstall it with a new bolt.

Run the VIN on [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Which one to buy

- **XLE:** the value pick, especially as a 2023–2025 with front-wheel drive if you don't need all-wheel drive.
- **XSE:** the sportier look and feel.
- **Limited:** twin 12.3-inch screens and the most comfort for the money.
- **2026:** the newest and the last, with all-wheel drive on every car.

## The verdict

The turbo Highlander is a good family SUV with a strong, torquey engine, but it doesn't save much fuel over the V6 and isn't as smooth. If you want the newest Highlander and don't need the hybrid's economy, it's a sound choice. If you can stretch to the hybrid, it's the better buy.

::: curiosity The Engine Came From Lexus
The Highlander's 2.4-liter turbo didn't start in a Toyota. It came from the Lexus NX and RX, and the same engine powers the gas Grand Highlander. Paired with electric motors, it also drives the Toyota Crown and the Grand Highlander Hybrid MAX, which makes 362 hp.
:::
