In 2010, Ford's Special Vehicle Team sold a warranty on a truck built to cross deserts at highway speed, and invented a segment. The SVT Raptor was 7 inches wider than a standard F-150, rode on Fox internal-bypass shocks and 35-inch tires, and from 2011 carried a 411-hp 6.2 V8. Nothing else like it existed; everything like it since, from the Ram TRX to the TRD Pros and ZR2s, exists because of it.

## 3 generations, 3 characters

The 2010–2014 original is the analog one: leaf springs out back, the big V8 up front, period tests in the mid-6s to 60. It is also the cheapest entry into the badge, and clean ones are already appreciating. The 2017–2020 second generation went aluminum and turbocharged, a 450-hp 3.5 EcoBoost High Output and the 10-speed; MotorTrend ran a SuperCab to 60 in 5.2 seconds, and purists who mourned the V8 sound went quiet somewhere around the second whoop section. The 2021-on third generation re-engineered the rear end with coil springs and a 5-link axle, 13 inches of rear travel, available 37-inch tires, and a speed limiter raised from 107 to 120 mph. It lands jumps the way the marketing photos promise.

## The R

When Ram's 702-hp TRX arrived, Ford answered in 2023 with the Raptor R: the Mustang Shelby GT500's supercharged 5.2 V8, detuned to a mere 700 hp, 720 from 2024. Car and Driver ran one to 60 in 3.6 seconds and through the quarter mile in 12.0, the quickest gas-powered truck it had ever tested. The TRX has since died; the R starts at $113,725 for 2026 and has the segment's summit to itself. The standard 450-hp Raptor, $81,800, remains the rational pick, a phrase doing heavy lifting in this chapter.

## Buying one

Raptors live hard lives in exactly the way the brochure suggests. Underbody photos are not optional: look at the skid plates, the shock bodies for rock rash, and the bead-lock-capable wheels for impact marks. On 2017-on trucks, confirm the cam-phaser recall work; on any truck, stock suspension matters, since a Raptor on aftermarket coilovers has usually been used as intended, twice. The EPA rates the EcoBoost trucks at 15–16 mpg combined and the R at 12, numbers you will not achieve. Insurance quotes before purchase are strongly advised; so is the purchase.
