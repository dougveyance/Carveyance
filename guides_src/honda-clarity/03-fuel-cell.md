The Clarity Fuel Cell was the first Clarity to reach customers, on December 19, 2016, in Southern California. It was Honda's third fuel cell car, after the FCX and the FCX Clarity, and like them it was leased, not sold. The lease was $369 a month ($510 in 2026 dollars) for 36 months, with $2,868 ($3,930) due at signing, 20,000 miles a year and up to $15,000 ($20,600) of hydrogen fuel included. There was no option to buy the car at the end.

## What it cost to lease

[[chart:lease]]

Over 3 years, the Fuel Cell's lease came to $15,783 ($21,700 in 2026 dollars) before taxes and fees, with up to $15,000 of hydrogen included. That was much less than the FCX Clarity before it, which leased for $600 a month ($940 in 2026 dollars) from 2008, and more than the battery-powered Clarity Electric.

## How it works

The Clarity Fuel Cell is an electric car that carries its own power station. Hydrogen, 5.46 kg of it stored at 10,000 psi, flows into a fuel cell stack, where it combines with oxygen from the air to make electricity, with water as the only exhaust. The electricity drives a 174-hp motor making 221 lb-ft of torque, with a small battery to help under hard acceleration. Honda shrank the whole fuel cell powertrain to about the size of its 3.5-liter V6, so it fits under the hood and leaves room for 5 in the cabin.

The EPA rated it at 366 miles and 68 MPGe for 2017 and 2018, and 360 miles from 2019. Car and Driver ran a 2017 to 60 mph in 8.1 seconds, and found it limited to 105 mph.

## Refueling

Honda quoted 3 to 5 minutes to fill the tanks at a 70 MPa (10,000 psi) hydrogen station, about the same as filling a gas tank. That's the Clarity's great advantage over a battery electric car, and its great weakness: it can only refuel at hydrogen stations, and there have never been many. Honda only leased it in California, where most of the stations in the US are.

A station on a map isn't the same as a station you can rely on. Stations can be closed for maintenance, out of hydrogen or unable to fill to full pressure, and when one closes, the next may be far away. Before you consider a Clarity Fuel Cell, check which stations near your home and work are open and working, how often they're down, and what hydrogen costs there now. The fuel allowance came with the original lease, so don't count on it.

## Recalls

The 2017 and 2018 Fuel Cell had recalls of their own: 18V068 and 18V921 for fuel cell control software that could cut power or stop the car, and 19V315 for a water pump that could corrode. The 2019 had 20V326 for the rear seat belts. Check every one by VIN.

## Should you get one?

Honda ended production in August 2021 and kept leasing the remaining cars through 2022. Because every one was leased with no purchase option, a Clarity Fuel Cell for sale needs the same paperwork checks as the Electric: a clean, transferable title, the seller's right to sell it, and a Honda dealer willing to service it in writing.

For most people it isn't a practical car. It makes sense only if all of these are true: you can buy and register it legally, you live and work near hydrogen stations that are reliably open, a Honda dealer near you will service the fuel cell system, and the price reflects all of that.
