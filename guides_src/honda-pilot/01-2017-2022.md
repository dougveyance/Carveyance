The third-generation Pilot arrived for 2016, and it's the most common used Pilot you'll find. It traded the boxy look of the old one for a rounder, lower-drag shape, lost nearly 136 kg (300 lb) on the upper trims, and gained a direct-injected V6 with 280 hp. Its job is to move a family and its luggage quietly, and it does that well. The steering is light, the ride is soft, and the third row is usable by teenagers on short trips and by children on long ones.

## The engine and the 2 gearboxes

[[chart:power]]

Every version has the same 3.5-liter V6. What changes is the transmission. From 2016 to 2020, the LX, EX and EX-L have a 6-speed automatic, while the Touring and Elite have a 9-speed automatic with paddle shifters. For 2021, Honda made the 9-speed standard on every Pilot. Car and Driver ran a 2017 Elite with the 9-speed and all-wheel drive to 60 mph in 6 seconds, and a front-drive 2016 EX with the 6-speed in 6.2. All-wheel-drive models use Honda's i-VTM4 system, which can vary the torque sent to each rear wheel. Properly equipped, a front-drive Pilot can tow 1,588 kg (3,500 lb) and an all-wheel-drive one 2,268 kg (5,000 lb).

## The years

The 2016 launch brought standard LED taillights and a backup camera, and Honda Sensing was standard on the Touring and Elite; on the 2017 EX and EX-L it was a $1,000 option ($1,400 in 2026 dollars). For 2017, every trim but the LX got Apple CarPlay and Android Auto. The 2019 refresh added a new front end, standard LED headlights, a revised 9-speed and start-stop system, and made Honda Sensing standard on every trim, along with CabinTalk, which plays the driver's voice through the rear speakers. A Black Edition arrived for 2020 and a Special Edition for 2021. For 2022, a Sport replaced the LX and EX, and a TrailSport joined the range with all-wheel drive, a slightly raised ride and black trim.

## Space

There's 16.5 cubic feet behind the third row, 46.8 behind the second and 83.9 with both folded, plus a storage well under the cargo floor. Most seat 8 on a second-row bench; the Elite has heated second-row captain's chairs and seats 7.

## What to check before you buy

The early transmissions got attention from Honda's service department. Dealers updated the 9-speed's software on 2016 Pilots for shift quality, and 2016–2017 6-speeds had a torque converter judder fix that included new software and a fluid flush. Ask for the records. The biggest recalls are 21V932, a hood latch striker that can let the hood fly open on 2016–2019 Pilots; 23V751, connecting rod bearings that may wear and seize on some 2016 and 2018–2019 engines; and 26V365, rear subframe corrosion on 2016–2022 Pilots sold in salt-belt states, where Honda is installing a reinforcement kit. The V6 uses a timing belt, due every 7 years or 105,000 miles. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A 2019–2022 EX-L is the sweet spot: the refreshed cabin, standard Honda Sensing and leather, without paying for the Touring's extras. If you're shopping 2016–2018, favor a Touring or Elite with the 9-speed and its software updates done, or a 6-speed car with the torque converter fix. Whatever the year, confirm the recalls by VIN before you talk price.

::: curiosity Can You Hear Me Back There?
CabinTalk, added for 2019, works like an in-car public address system, putting the driver's voice through the rear speakers so nobody in the third row can claim they didn't hear. Honda first put it in the 2018 Odyssey.
:::
