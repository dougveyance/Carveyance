Both Avalon powertrains are proven and pleasant, so the choice is about how you drive. The V6 gives you effortless speed and the smoothness of 6 cylinders. The hybrid gives you quiet, relaxed progress and far lower fuel bills. In the 2019–2022 car, the hybrid cost $1,000 more new than the same grade with the V6.

## How they feel

The 2019–2022 V6 makes 301 hp and reached 60 mph in 6.1 seconds in Car and Driver's test of a Touring. The hybrid makes 215 hp and took 7.8 seconds in the same magazine's test of an XSE. On the highway, the V6 passes with a gentle squeeze of the pedal, while the hybrid has to work harder and you'll hear the engine. Around town, the hybrid is the quieter and smoother choice, gliding on electric power at low speeds.

The 2017–2018 versions follow the same pattern with less power: 268 hp for the V6 and 200 hp for the hybrid.

## Fuel

[[chart:mpg]]

The EPA rates the 2019–2022 Hybrid XLE at 44 mpg combined and the V6 XLE at 26. The other hybrid grades are rated at 43 and the other V6 grades at 25. The 2021 all-wheel-drive 4-cylinder sits in between at 28. For 2017–2018, it's 40 for the hybrid against 24 for the V6.

[[chart:fuelcost]]

At 12,000 miles a year and $4 a gallon, a 2019–2022 V6 XLE uses about $1,846 of fuel and a Hybrid XLE about $1,091, a difference of roughly $755 a year. Drive 20,000 miles a year, mostly in town, and the gap grows past $1,250. Drive mostly on the highway and it shrinks, because the hybrid's advantage is largest in stop-and-go traffic.

## Running it

The V6 is conventional and familiar to any shop. The hybrid has a battery and electronics that are best diagnosed by a Toyota-trained technician, though Toyota has been selling hybrids in the US for more than 20 years. The warranty is the biggest difference between years: a 2020–2022 hybrid's battery is covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles, while 2017–2019 hybrids had 8 years or 100,000 miles in most states, so many are now out of coverage. Factor in the cost of a replacement battery when you price an older, high-mileage hybrid.

## The decision

- **Choose the hybrid** if you drive more than about 12,000 miles a year, mostly in town, or if you simply like quiet. A 2020–2022 hybrid with battery warranty left is the best Avalon for most people.
- **Choose the V6** if you do lots of highway miles, like strong passing power, or are buying an older car where the hybrid battery's warranty has run out.
- **Choose the 2021 AWD** only if you need the traction. It's slower than both and uses more fuel than the hybrid.

::: curiosity The Battery Got Lighter
For 2021, Toyota swapped the Avalon Hybrid's nickel-metal hydride battery for a lighter lithium-ion pack. The 2017–2018 hybrids, and the 2019–2020 cars, use the older type.
:::
