The 200 Series has the proportions of a small office building and the subtlety of a bank vault. It doesn't shout about adventure. It just assumes the road might end and that you'll want to keep going. In America, the 2017–2021 Land Cruiser was a quiet, expensive alternative to flashier luxury SUVs, built on the old-fashioned idea that durability matters more than the screen.

That reputation is why used ones hold their value so stubbornly. It's also a trap. A Land Cruiser can cost enough to convince you it must be trouble-free, and it isn't. It's an aging, heavy, complex truck with real running costs, and it deserves a real inspection.

## How it drives

Every 2017–2021 Land Cruiser has the same 5.7-liter V8, with 381 hp and 401 lb-ft, an 8-speed automatic and full-time 4-wheel drive with a 2-speed transfer case and a locking center differential. Toyota says 90% of the peak torque is there from 2,200 rpm, and it shows: the V8 pulls smoothly and without fuss, loaded or not. Car and Driver timed a 2018 model to 60 mph in 6.7 seconds, and it weighed 2,692 kg (5,934 lb).

[[chart:power]]

The ride is soft and calm, and the driving position is high. Part of that comes from the Kinetic Dynamic Suspension System (KDSS), which hydraulically adjusts how hard the anti-roll bars resist lean. That keeps the truck flatter on the road and lets the wheels move more freely off it. It's still a heavy truck, with plenty of body roll on winding roads and slow steering, and parking takes patience. With its standard tow package, it pulls up to 3,674 kg (8,100 lb).

## Living with it

The cabin is solid and conservative, with leather, a cooler box in the center console on most versions and seats for up to 8. The tech is old. The 9-inch touchscreen never got Apple CarPlay or Android Auto from the factory, though aftermarket kits can add them. Every 2017–2021 model does have Toyota Safety Sense P, with automatic emergency braking and adaptive cruise control.

Fuel is the price of all that weight. The EPA rated the 2017 at 15 mpg combined and the 2021 at 14. At 15,000 miles a year and $4 a gallon, 14 mpg is about $4,286 of fuel. Tires, brakes and suspension parts aren't cheap either.

For 2020, Toyota added the Heritage Edition, with bronze BBS forged wheels, a Yakima roof rack and only 2 rows of seats. For 2021, it could also be ordered with a third row. It's desirable, but the badge doesn't exempt it from an inspection.

## What to check before you buy

Get it on a lift. Look for rust on the frame, body mounts, brake lines and skid plates, and be suspicious of fresh undercoating. Look for leaks at the differentials and transfer case, and for any fluid around the KDSS cylinders and lines. Check the coolant level with the engine cold and look for seepage, then start it and listen for ticking or exhaust leaks. Ask for proof of oil, coolant, transmission and differential service.

Then run the VIN. Certain 2008–2019 Land Cruisers are part of a recall for a passenger seat belt sensor wire that can break and switch off the passenger-side airbags (18V-887), and certain 2018–2019 models are part of the fuel pump recall (20V-012 and 20V-682). The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

The best buy is a documented 2019–2021 example with stock suspension, clean records and a clean underside. A 2017–2018 makes sense when its condition or price is clearly better. Be careful with heavily modified trucks: lift kits, big tires, winches and extra wiring can all hide problems.

Buy a 200 Series if you need its mix of 8 seats, heavy towing, comfort and serious 4x4 hardware, and can afford to feed it. If you mostly want the badge, the fuel bills may change your mind.

::: curiosity Cruise Control for Rocks
The 200 Series has CRAWL Control, which Toyota describes as working in low range to manage engine output and braking, with 5 speed settings. In effect, it's cruise control for rocks: the truck holds a slow, steady pace over rough ground while the driver only steers. That sounds lazy, but keeping a steady crawl over boulders is one of the hardest skills in off-roading.
:::
