A used Supra's biggest variable isn't its model year. It's what the last owner did with it. These are enthusiast cars. Plenty have been modified, some have been tracked, and a few have been quietly put back to stock before going up for sale. Use this list to find out which kind you're looking at.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | 3.0 only, 335 hp, automatic only | Brake-assist recall (21V-598) and starter-relay fire recall (25V-636) |
| 2021–2022 | 3.0 raised to 382 hp, chassis retuned; new 2.0 with 255 hp; automatic only | 21V-598 on 2021 cars; starter recalls 25V-636 and 26V-056 |
| 2023–2024 | 6-speed manual arrives on the 3.0; last years of the 2.0 | Starter recalls 26V-056 and 26V-438 |
| 2025–2026 | 3.0 only; the MkV Final Edition for 2026, 1,300 cars | Too new for long-term data. Confirm a Final Edition by VIN and window sticker |

## Start with the right version

2020 3.0 cars carry the original 335-hp calibration; 2021-and-later 3.0 cars make 382. The 6-speed manual arrived for 2023, on the 3.0 only. The 2.0, sold from 2021 through 2024, is a different car, not just a cheaper trim of the same one, and chapter 2 covers it. The 2026 Final Edition is a distinctive last-year car, not a substitute for a pre-purchase inspection.

## Inspect before you negotiate

Ask for the full service records, and check for modifications, engine tuning, exhaust and suspension changes, non-standard tire sizes and the wheel alignment. Look for uneven tire wear and damage to the plastic panels underneath. On a car that may have done track days, inspect the brake rotors and ask when the brake fluid was last changed. Ask about accident repairs around the nose and the rear corners. Drive it cold and fully warm, scan it for stored fault codes, and make sure the electronics, cameras and driver-assistance systems all work. On a manual, pay attention to how the clutch engages, how the gears go in, and any sign of abusive launches.

## Where the records matter most

Ask which engine software is installed and whether the car has ever been tuned. Have a specialist look at the cooling system, the turbo plumbing, the differential and, where fitted, the adaptive suspension. Compare the car against its original build sheet. Enthusiast cars are often modified and later returned to stock appearance, and a dealer listing that says "unmodified" is not the same as an actual history. If track use is documented, judge the fluid and brake service against that use, not against the ordinary commuting schedule in the owner's manual.

## 1 long test drive is worth 10 photos

Drive it over bad pavement, in stop-and-go traffic and at a steady highway speed. Lower the windows partway and check the buffeting. Make sure the tight cabin fits your body and that the limited visibility won't wear on you every day. Once warm, the engine and gearbox should behave normally with no warning messages. On a manual, check that every gear engages cleanly. On an automatic, check for smooth shifts in every drive mode. Be especially wary of uneven tire wear on a car with a history of suspension changes.

## Recalls: check by VIN

BMW builds the Supra, so BMW files its recalls with US regulators, but Toyota dealers do the work. These are the ones that matter most.

- **2020–2021:** about 13,000 Supras are covered by 21V-598. Engine software could damage the vacuum pump that powers the brake assist, which can lengthen stopping distances. The fix is a software update, plus a new pump if the old one is damaged.
- **2020–2022:** 25V-636. The engine starter relay can corrode and short-circuit, a fire risk whether the car is being driven or not. About 1,500 Supras are included, and until the repair is done, the owner is told to park outside and away from buildings.
- **2021–2024:** 26V-056, expanded by 26V-438 in July 2026. The starter itself can overheat and, in rare cases, catch fire. Dealers replace the starter.
- **Every year:** newer recalls can appear at any time. Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done. Recalls and service bulletins aren't the same thing, so ask about both.

## Ownership math

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly and hide the differences that matter. Compare listings for the same year, engine, gearbox, mileage and title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Before judging a listing, get quotes for insurance, tires and service from a dealer or an independent specialist, plus any catch-up maintenance it needs. A bargain-priced modified 3.0 can cost more over a few years than a carefully kept original one.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the car past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## The alternatives worth considering

Compare the smaller, lighter GR86 if involvement at legal speeds matters most. Compare a Nissan Z if you'd rather have more room and a more relaxed feel than the Supra's precise chassis. And don't let a small difference in asking price hide bigger differences in insurance, tires, repair know-how or remaining warranty. The right Supra should feel like a deliberate sports-car purchase, not an accidental financial experiment.

## Our shortlist

- **Best value:** a stock, documented 2021–2022 3.0 automatic with every recall closed.
- **Best to drive:** a 2023–2026 3.0 manual with a healthy clutch and no track history it can't document.
- **Budget pick:** a 2.0, but only if it's clearly cheaper than a comparable 3.0.
- **Collector:** the 2026 MkV Final Edition, bought to keep, not as a bargain.
