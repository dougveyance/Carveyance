A used Venza sounds like an easy decision: Toyota hybrid economy, standard all-wheel drive and a nicer cabin than a RAV4. Most of the risk isn't mechanical. It's buying a car that doesn't fit your life, or one whose electronics and recall work were left undone. Check the fit first, then the car.

## Start with the space test

Before anything else, load the things you actually carry: the stroller, the dog crate, the golf bag, the suitcases. The Venza has 28.8 cubic feet behind the rear seat and 55.1 with it folded, but the sloping tailgate cuts into tall items more than those numbers suggest. If it's tight on the test drive, it'll be tight every week. Drive a RAV4 Hybrid the same day if space matters.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | Launch year. TSS 2.0, 8-inch screen on the LE and XLE | Rear turn-signal recall (22V-033). Stability-control software recall (22V-239) |
| 2022 | Carryover | Stability-control software recall (22V-239) |
| 2023 | New multimedia, TSS 2.5, Nightshade Edition, digital cluster on the Limited | Instrument display recall (25V-595). Camera recall on cars with the Panoramic View Monitor (25V-744). Label recall on cars with dealer accessories (24V-548) |
| 2024 | Final year, TSS 2.5+ | The same display, camera and label recalls |

## The hybrid inspection

Start the car cold and let it idle. The engine will switch on and off by itself, which is normal. Warning lights, harsh clunks when the engine joins in, or a hybrid system message are not. On the drive, brake gently from moderate speed and feel for a smooth blend between regenerative and friction braking. Watch the energy display to confirm the battery charges and discharges as you'd expect.

Ask for the service records, and have a shop that knows Toyota hybrids scan the system for stored codes. Every Venza is a 2020-or-later Toyota hybrid, so its hybrid battery is covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles from the date it was first sold, whichever comes first, and the coverage passes to later owners. The other hybrid parts, including the inverter and control modules, have an 8-year, 100,000-mile warranty. Work out how much is left from the in-service date, not the model year. A warranty isn't a substitute for an inspection.

## Screens, cameras and the glass roof

Test every display, every camera view and every parking sensor, including the 360-degree view if the car has it. On a Limited, check the head-up display and the digital rearview mirror. If the car has the Star Gaze roof, switch it between clear and frosted several times, and check the headliner around it for water stains. Look at all 4 tires for matching brand, size and wear, since uneven tires are hard on an all-wheel-drive system. Check underneath for scrapes, and around the body for paint that doesn't match.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2021:** 22V-033. Water can get into the rear turn-signal lamps and short the circuit board, so the turn signals and hazard lights may dim or stop working. About 41,500 cars.
- **2021–2022:** 22V-239. If the driver turned the stability control off, it may not switch back on by itself after a restart. A software update fixes it.
- **2023–2024:** 25V-595. On cars with the 12.3-inch instrument display, the display may be blank at startup, hiding the speedometer and warning lights. A software update fixes it.
- **2023–2024 with accessories fitted before sale:** 24V-548. A load-capacity label that may list the wrong accessory weight. The fix is a new label.
- **2023–2024 with the Panoramic View Monitor:** 25V-744. The rearview camera image may freeze or not appear. A software update fixes it.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Walk away if

The hybrid system shows a warning that the seller can't explain, the title is branded, the car has been in a flood, or the seller won't let a shop inspect it. Walk away, too, if your own luggage doesn't fit. That one won't get better.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and an average that mixes a 2021 LE with a 2024 Limited tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, grade and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Price a RAV4 Hybrid and a Lexus NX hybrid with the same mileage, too, so you know what you're paying for the Venza's cabin. Take the cost of tires, overdue service and any undone recall work off the price.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Venza past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2023–2024 XLE with its display and camera recalls done.
- **Best value:** a 2021–2022 LE with records.
- **Most luxury:** a Limited whose features all work.
- **Walk away from:** any Venza that can't swallow what you carry.
