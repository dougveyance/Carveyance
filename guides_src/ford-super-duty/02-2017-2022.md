2 years after the F-150's aluminum gamble paid off, the Super Duty got the same body treatment and finally shared a cab with the half-ton again. The weight saved — up to 159 kg (350 lb) — went straight back into frame and axle, and the cab stopped rusting, which in the salt states matters more on a 20-year truck than any spec. The 2017–2022 generation is now the heart of the used Super Duty market, and it splits cleanly in the middle.

## Before and after 2020

The 2017–2019 trucks carry the 6-speed automatic, the 385-hp 6.2 gas V8 and a 6.7 Power Stroke rated 440 then 450 hp with up to 935 lb-ft — thoroughly capable, meaningfully cheaper. The 2020 update is the line worth paying to cross: the 10-speed automatic, the diesel strengthened to 475 hp and 1,050 lb-ft, and the arrival of the 7.3-liter "Godzilla" gas V8, 430 hp of iron-block pushrod simplicity that became a cult object the week it shipped. A 2020–2022 truck is this guide's default used recommendation in the segment.

## What they're like to own

The 6.7 of this era has a solid reputation — strong bottom end, reliable emissions hardware by diesel standards, and the same CP4 fuel-pump caution as every modern high-pressure diesel: it wants clean fuel and dies expensively without it, so fuel-history and filter receipts are part of any purchase. The Godzilla asks almost nothing: gas, oil changes and spark plugs, no DEF tank, no regens, at the cost of a few mpg and less towing margin at max weights. The 6.2 is the honest budget engine for lighter duty. Tremor packages from 2020 on add genuine off-road ability without wrecking payload.

## What to pay attention to

Most of these trucks worked for a living. Assume max-weight towing until the seller proves otherwise: ask for the trailer, not just the mileage. Check the hitch and gooseneck puck wear, look for gauge-cluster hour counts that dwarf the odometer (idle time ages a diesel invisibly), and confirm the front end tracks straight — solid-axle trucks that have towed hard eat ball joints and track-bar bushings, the first stop on the road to the death-wobble conversation in the checklist chapter.
