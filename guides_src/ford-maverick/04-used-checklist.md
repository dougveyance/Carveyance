Start with 3 questions. Do you want the best fuel economy? Do you need all-wheel drive? Will you tow regularly? For 2022–2024, the hybrid means front-wheel drive, and the EcoBoost is the way to get all-wheel drive. From 2025, the hybrid can have all-wheel drive and, with it, the 4K Tow Package. Don't assume a truck advertised as all-wheel drive has the 4K Tow Package: read its original equipment list, look at the hitch and check the owner's manual limits. Payload and towing ratings vary with the engine, drive, options and passengers.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | Launch. Hybrid front-drive standard | Hybrid engine failure and fire risk (22V484, 23V380). Side curtain air bags (22V594). Fuel tank drilled during bedliner installation (21V976). Battery monitoring and 12-volt battery (24V267, 25V019) |
| 2023 | Tremor package | Hybrid engine (23V380). 12-volt battery (25V019). Trailer tail lights (23V160). Tires (24V223) |
| 2024 | EcoBoost standard, hybrid optional | Fuel tank seam (24V185). Child seat tethers (24V778). Tail lights (24V293, 25V095). Turn signals (23V848, 25V094) |
| 2025 | Update. Hybrid AWD, Lobo, Tremor model | Instrument panel cover with the air bag (25V884). Park module (25V863). Moonroof glass (26V157). Cylinder head (25V828) |
| 2026 | Front-drive EcoBoost back | Instrument panel cover (25V884). Moonroof glass (26V157). Joints that may be loose (26V550) |

## Recall history is a checklist, not a verdict

- **2022–2023 hybrids:** 23V380, and 22V484 for 2022, because if the hybrid's engine fails, oil and fuel vapor can collect near hot parts and start a fire. Owners are told to pull over and shut the engine off if they hear unexpected engine noises or lose power. 25V345 covers trucks repaired incorrectly. 24V140 and 25V123, an instrument cluster that may not show warning lights.
- **2022–2024 hybrids:** 24V330 and its follow-up 25V133, hybrid control software that can shift into neutral unexpectedly.
- **2022–2023:** 25V019, a 12-volt battery that can degrade and fail suddenly. 24V267 and its follow-ups, 24V590, 25V127 and 25V158, modules that may not detect a weak 12-volt battery.
- **2022–2024:** 24V684, a rearview camera image that can freeze. 24V293 and 25V095, tail lights the body control module can switch off. 23V848 and 25V094, turn-signal faults. Many of these follow-ups exist because an earlier repair was done incorrectly.
- **2025–2026:** 25V884, an instrument panel cover that can separate when the air bag deploys. 26V157, moonroof glass that may not be bonded properly. 25V863, an integrated park module that may fail.
- **2022–2026:** 26V104, a trailer module that can lose communication, cutting a trailer's brake and turn-signal lights. 26V340, front lower control arm ball joints that may have been installed or repaired incorrectly at the plant; Ford tells owners of affected trucks not to drive them until it's fixed.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and with Ford, confirm each remedy was completed, and check for new recalls just before you buy. This isn't a complete list.

## Test-drive the whole truck

Before driving, look for coolant leaks, oil seepage, signs of underbody impacts, mismatched tires on an all-wheel-drive truck and amateur wiring spliced into the factory harness. Start it cold and make sure the warning lights come on and go out. In a hybrid, watch for warning messages, check that it switches smoothly between electric and gas power, and that the brakes feel consistent. In an EcoBoost, check how the boost builds, how it shifts and whether it runs hot under load. A clean drive is encouraging, but it doesn't prove the battery's condition, the recall work or an accident-free history.

## Inside, outside, underneath

Open and close every door, check both rear seat latches and every seatbelt, try the backup camera several times and test the climate control, USB ports, phone pairing and driver aids. Drop the tailgate and look for damage to the bed rails, water getting in, trailer wiring, a corroded hitch and signs of overloading. Look at the rear suspension, the alignment and the tire wear: a small pickup can be worked very hard by someone who bought it because it looked cheap to fix. On a Tremor, inspect the skid plates, control arms and underbody shields. On a Lobo, examine the wheels, brakes, tires and any suspension changes.

## Records matter more than internet folklore

Ask for the original window sticker or Ford build information, service invoices, an open-recall printout, a title report and proof of regular oil and fluid service. If an early hybrid has had battery or powertrain repairs, ask for the repair order, not an assurance that Ford took care of it. Only a qualified technician with the right equipment should check the high-voltage battery; never open it yourself. Check the hybrid warranty against the VIN and the original in-service date.

## Walk away if

The truck has a branded title, flood damage, an open recall the seller won't address, a hybrid with warning lights or a noisy engine, an all-wheel-drive system that binds in tight turns, or wiring and accessories nobody can explain. Walk away from any seller who won't allow an independent inspection.

Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same powertrain, drive, trim and package, with the same mileage and title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what trucks actually sold for. ## Our shortlist

- **Best value:** a 2022–2024 hybrid with documented recall repairs and a healthy 12-volt battery.
- **Best all-rounder:** a 2025–2026 hybrid with all-wheel drive.
- **Best for towing:** an EcoBoost with all-wheel drive and the 4K Tow Package, with the trailer-module recall done.
- **Most fun:** a stock, well-kept Lobo.
