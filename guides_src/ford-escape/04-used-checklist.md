5 million Escapes mean the used market has every condition and price imaginable. The checks below sort them fast, and most take a VIN report and a cold start rather than a lift.

## The 5 checks

**1. Recalls, by VIN, before anything else.** The 1.6 EcoBoost (2013–16) drew repeated campaigns for overheating and fire risk; 2014 cars added door latches; a 2022 campaign covered cracked fuel injectors on 2020-generation 1.5s, shared with the Bronco Sport. All are free dealer fixes — an open one on the report is either a condition of sale or a reason to leave.

**2. The first generation's gearbox.** The CD4E 4-speed behind the 2001–07 V6 is the era's known weak point. Insist on evidence of fluid service, feel for flare between 2nd and 3rd on a warm test drive, and price any shudder as a transmission, not a fluid change.

**3. Rust, underneath and in the arches.** The 2001–2012 trucks are old enough that body condition now matters more than miles, especially rear wheel arches and subframes on salt-state cars. A clean southern example is worth a premium over a cheaper rusty one every time.

**4. Turbo oil history.** Every EcoBoost — 1.6, 1.5 or 2.0 — forgives little oil neglect. Receipts showing regular changes are the difference between a bargain and a gamble; a dark dipstick and no paperwork on a small turbo is the classic trap.

**5. Hybrid pack health.** On 2005–2012 hybrids, watch the battery gauge behave through a full warm-up drive and confirm the cooling fan area behind the rear seat is clean and unblocked. The packs are taxi-proven, but age is age. 2020-onward hybrids: just confirm recalls and drive it — they have been the most trouble-free Escapes of all.

## Reading the market

The arc above is the whole supply story. The 300,000-a-year peak era (2014–2017) keeps third-generation cars abundant and cheap — be patient and picky. The 2020s slide means fourth-generation cars are scarcer used but discounted new, and the 2026 finale puts a hard date on the nameplate: parts will be around for decades, but resale on late cars will behave like a discontinued model's. Buy accordingly — pay less going in, keep it longer, and let the hybrid's running costs do the math for you.
