Toyota put the Mirai on sale in California for 2016. It runs on compressed hydrogen: a fuel cell stack turns the hydrogen into electricity for an electric motor, and water is the main thing that comes out of the tailpipe. Toyota has sold it almost entirely in California, because that's where almost all the stations are.

For a shopper, the story splits in 2. The first generation, through 2020, is a front-drive 4-seater with 151 hp and a 312-mile EPA range. The second, from 2021, is a longer, lower rear-drive sedan with 182 hp, 5 seats and up to 402 miles of range. The car is the easy part. The fuel is the hard part, and it has changed a lot since most of these cars were sold. This guide has 2 chapters: the review, and the checklist to use before you buy.

## Quick picks

- **Best bet:** a Toyota Certified Used 2021-or-newer Mirai XLE that comes with a fuel card. Toyota has offered $15,000 cards on certified used Mirais, but the offers change, so get the details in writing.
- **Most range:** the XLE, rated at 402 miles. The 2021–2024 Limited is rated at 357.
- **Cheapest way in:** a 2017–2020 car, but only if you have reliable stations close to home and work.
- **Think twice if** you don't live and work near at least 2 working hydrogen stations, or might move out of California. A hybrid or a battery EV you can charge at home will be far easier to live with.
