The Prelude's toughest competition is parked in the same showroom. The Civic Sport Touring Hybrid has the same 200-hp powertrain and more space for less money. The Civic Si has a manual gearbox. The Civic Type R has 315 hp. So the question isn't whether the Prelude is good. It's whether it's better than a Civic for the way you drive.

## Price

[[chart:price]]

When the Prelude went on sale, Motor1 lined it up against its family and rivals, with delivery included. At $43,195, it sat about $11,000 above the Civic Si and a few thousand below the Type R, and it cost more than a Mazda MX-5 Miata, a Toyota GR86, a Subaru BRZ or an Acura Integra. Only the Nissan Z and the Type R cost more. A 2026 Civic Sport Touring Hybrid hatchback, with the same powertrain, was about $8,500 less than the Prelude.

## Performance and fuel economy

[[chart:mpg]]

In a straight line, the Prelude isn't the quickest of its family. Car and Driver's Civic Sport Touring Hybrid reached 60 mph in 6.2 seconds, the Prelude in 6.5, and the last Civic Si it tested in 6.8, while both Type R generations it tested did it in 4.9. The Prelude's advantages are elsewhere: Type R suspension and brakes, a stiffer, more settled feel on a fast road, and S+ Shift for drivers who miss gearchanges. The Civic Hybrid hatchback is rated at 48 mpg combined, the Prelude at 44, the Si at 31 and the Type R at 24.

## Which to buy

**The Civic Sport Touring Hybrid** is the rational choice: the same powertrain, a usable rear seat, 24.5 cubic feet of cargo space in the hatchback and better fuel economy, for much less money. Buy it if you want a quick, frugal daily car.

**The Civic Si** is the enthusiast's bargain: a 6-speed manual, a limited-slip differential and a sharp chassis, for about $11,000 less than the Prelude. Buy it if you want to shift for yourself.

**The Civic Type R** is the performance car. Buy it if speed and track days matter more than comfort and fuel economy.

**The Prelude** is for drivers who want a coupe, enjoy a good road more than a drag strip, and will use its comfort, its hatchback and its 44 mpg every day. It's the most refined way to buy Honda's best chassis hardware without the Type R's firm ride and thirst.

## The rivals

A Toyota GR86 or Subaru BRZ offers rear-wheel drive and a manual for much less, with a smaller trunk. A Mazda MX-5 Miata is cheaper still, with 2 seats and an open roof. The Nissan Z has far more power and costs more.

::: curiosity "Same Price, Different Ideas"
In Japan, Honda priced the Prelude the same as the Civic Type R Racing Black Package, at ¥6,179,800 (about $41,700 at the time, or $43,500 in 2026 dollars), and nearly a quarter above the regular Type R.
:::
