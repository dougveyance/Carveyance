The fourth-generation RAV4 arrived for 2013 and got its big refresh, and its first hybrid, for 2016. By 2017 and 2018 it was a mature, well-sorted compact SUV, and those last 2 years are the ones worth shopping. They brought the most standard safety equipment this generation ever had, and they're now the least expensive way into a RAV4 that will still feel familiar to anyone who drives a newer one.

## What you get

The gas RAV4 uses a 2.5-liter 4-cylinder with 176 hp and 172 lb-ft of torque, driving the front wheels or all 4 through a 6-speed automatic. It's an honest, unexciting engine. The RAV4 Hybrid pairs a 2.5-liter engine with electric motors for 194 hp, and an electric motor drives the rear wheels, so every hybrid has all-wheel drive. Toyota said the hybrid reached 60 mph in 8.1 seconds, and Consumer Guide timed a Hybrid Limited at 8.2. It's the quicker of the 2.

For 2017, Toyota made Toyota Safety Sense P standard on every grade. That means a pre-collision system with pedestrian detection, lane departure alert with steering assist, automatic high beams and radar cruise control, even on the base LE. The gas RAV4 came as an LE, XLE, SE, Limited and, new for 2017, Platinum. The hybrid came as an XLE, SE or Limited. Prices started at $24,910 for an LE with front-wheel drive, about $34,200 in 2026 dollars, and $29,030 for a Hybrid XLE, about $39,900 today.

For 2018, Toyota added the Adventure grade, with a more rugged look, and a cheaper Hybrid LE. That made 6 gas grades and 4 hybrid grades.

The EPA rated a front-drive LE or XLE at 26 mpg combined and an all-wheel-drive LE or XLE at 25. The SE, Limited and Platinum were rated 1 mpg lower. The hybrid was rated at 32 mpg combined, a bigger gain than the numbers suggest, since most of it comes in town, where the hybrid can run on electricity at low speeds.

## How it drives

The 2017–2018 RAV4 is quiet at a cruise, rides comfortably and steers with little feel. The gas engine gets loud when you ask it to work, and the 6-speed automatic is smooth but not quick to kick down. The hybrid feels stronger off the line, because its electric motors deliver their pull instantly, and its continuously variable transmission avoids gear changes altogether. Neither will entertain you on a twisty road. Both will do what most owners want, which is everything, quietly, for a long time.

The cabin is roomy for the class, with a flat floor in back and plenty of headroom. The tech is the weak spot. The touchscreen is small, and there's no Apple CarPlay or Android Auto. CarPlay didn't arrive until the redesigned 2019 model.

## What to check

These RAV4s are now 8 to 10 years old, so condition and maintenance history matter more than any model-year quirk. There's 1 recall to check on every car, and it's a big one.

- **12-volt battery hold-down (23V-734):** In November 2023, Toyota recalled 1,853,568 RAV4s from the 2013–2018 model years. Some replacement 12-volt batteries have a smaller top than the original. If one of those is fitted and the hold-down clamp isn't tightened properly, the battery can shift in hard turns, and the positive terminal can touch the clamp and short-circuit, which creates a fire risk. Dealers replace the hold-down clamp, battery tray and positive terminal cover free of charge. Because most cars this age have had at least 1 replacement battery, this matters on almost every example.
- **The hybrid:** Ask how the car was maintained and have a shop that knows Toyota hybrids scan the system for stored codes. Make sure the battery cooling intake isn't blocked by pet hair or debris.
- **Everything else:** Look for oil leaks, uneven tire wear, rust underneath if the car spent winters on salted roads, and signs of water in the cargo area.

Run the VIN on [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask the seller for proof the recall work was done.

## Which one to buy

- **LE or XLE with AWD:** the sensible, inexpensive choice if you need traction in winter.
- **Hybrid XLE or LE:** the best of this generation. It's quicker, every one has all-wheel drive, and it saves fuel in town.
- **Adventure (2018):** the most rugged-looking version of this generation.
- **Limited or Platinum:** the most comfortable and best equipped, if you find one with low miles.

## The verdict

A 2017–2018 RAV4 is a sound used buy, and the hybrid is the one to find. You give up the newer car's screens, power and economy, but you keep the reputation, the space and a full suite of driver aids. Just make sure the battery hold-down recall is done.

::: curiosity What RAV4 Stands For
The name began as an abbreviation of Recreational Active Vehicle with 4-wheel drive. Toyota first showed the idea as the RAV-FOUR concept at the 1989 Tokyo Motor Show, and the production RAV4 arrived in 1994, built like a car but riding high like a small SUV. Plenty of RAV4s since then have had front-wheel drive only, which is why the name has also been explained as Robust Accurate Vehicle with 4-wheel drive.
:::
