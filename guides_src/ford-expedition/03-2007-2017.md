The 2007 Expedition landed at the worst possible moment. Gas crossed $4 a gallon in 2008, the economy followed it down, and sales collapsed from 90,287 in 2007 to 31,655 in 2009 — the year production left Wayne, Michigan for the Kentucky Truck Plant. Ford's response was to simply keep building it: 11 model years on one body, the longest run of any Expedition. For a used shopper that longevity is the gift, because the newest trucks of the generation carry hardware from a different decade than their styling suggests.

## 5.4 or EcoBoost

Through 2014 the only engine was the 5.4 Triton — 300 hp in 2007–08, 320 from 2009 with the 6-speed. It's a slow, steady thing: high-8s to 60, 14–15 mpg combined, and happiest towing below its ratings. The 2015–17 trucks swapped in the 3.5 EcoBoost twin-turbo V6 — 365 hp, 420 lb-ft, low-to-mid-6s acceleration and 17 mpg combined — plus a refreshed cabin with Sync. Same body, transformed truck: the 2015–17 EcoBoost cars are the sleeper buy of the whole Expedition story, the newest-feeling big Ford on the used lot for used-Tahoe money.

## The EL, and what to pay attention to

The Expedition EL — Max in Canada — stretched the wheelbase to 131 inches and the body past 221, replacing the Excursion with something that actually fit in a garage. At about 2,754 kg (6,071 lb) it's the heaviest Expedition, and the only one of its era with real cargo room behind a raised 3rd row; big families should hold out for one. Whichever body, the generation's known issues are age-driven now: the 5.4 3-valve's spark plugs and cam phasers, rear load-leveling suspension on loaded trims, and rust-belt frame checks — all in the checklist chapter. Prices run from genuinely cheap for early 5.4s to low-$20,000s for the best 2017 EcoBoosts, which is the least money that buys this much verified truck.
