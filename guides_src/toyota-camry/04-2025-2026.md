For 2025, Toyota made the biggest change in the Camry's history without making a fuss about it. The 4-cylinder and the V6 are gone. Every Camry now pairs a 2.5-liter engine with Toyota's fifth-generation hybrid system, and the cheapest one is also the most efficient.

## What changed

The front-wheel-drive Camry makes 225 hp, more than any previous base Camry. Add all-wheel drive and a separate electric motor drives the rear wheels when needed, bringing the total to 232 hp. Unlike the 2020–2024 all-wheel-drive Camry, which was 4-cylinder only and limited to certain grades, this system is available on every grade.

The cabin moved on too. The LE and SE have a 7-inch digital instrument cluster, and the XLE and XSE get a 12.3-inch screen. An 8-inch touchscreen is standard, a 12.3-inch one is available, and the XLE and XSE offer a 10-inch head-up display. Wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto are standard.

For 2026, Toyota added the Nightshade Edition, a blacked-out version that sits above the SE, with 19-inch black wheels and black badging. It also added a hazy blue paint called Dark Cosmos on the SE, XLE and XSE. Otherwise, the 2026 is the same car. The 2027 Camry, now arriving at dealers, is also unchanged apart from a $700 price increase on every grade.

The Camry is still built in Georgetown, Kentucky.

## The numbers

[[chart:mpg25]]

The LE is the economy pick by a clear margin. Its FWD version is rated at 51 mpg combined, and even with all-wheel drive it's rated at 50. The SE, XLE and XSE use bigger wheels and are rated at 47 with FWD. The sporty XSE with all-wheel drive is the thirstiest, at 44 mpg, which is still better than any previous non-hybrid Camry.

Car and Driver ran a 2025 XSE AWD from 0 to 60 mph in 6.8 seconds. Edmunds needed 7.8 seconds in its XSE test car. Either way, it's quicker than the old 4-cylinder but well short of the old V6.

## How it drives

The new Camry feels like a quieter, smoother version of the 2018–2024 hybrid. It moves away on electric power, blends in the engine without drama and stays calm at highway speeds. Press hard and the engine revs loudly, though Toyota now leans harder on the battery to keep the revs from flaring the way older hybrids did.

The all-wheel-drive version doesn't turn it into a sports sedan. It's there for traction on wet, snowy or icy roads, and the efficiency cost is small. If you live where winters are rough, it's an easy choice.

## What to check

The 2025–2026 Camry is new enough that most examples are still under warranty: 3 years or 36,000 miles basic, 8 years or 100,000 miles for hybrid parts and 10 years or 150,000 miles for the hybrid battery. The used-car work is mostly about recalls, and there are 3 to check.

- **Instrument cluster software:** In August 2026, Toyota recalled about 508,000 2025–2026 Camrys in the US because the 7-inch combination meter can go blank at startup. When it does, the turn signals, hazard lights and some warning chimes, including the seat belt reminder, may not work. Only the LE, SE and Nightshade use the 7-inch meter. The fix is a free software update, and Toyota planned to notify all owners by early October 2026.
- **12.3-inch cluster software (25V-595):** Certain 2025 Camrys with the larger 12.3-inch digital cluster, used on the XLE and XSE, were part of a September 2025 Toyota and Lexus recall. At startup, the display may fail to show the speed and the brake and tire-pressure warning lights. The fix is a free software update.
- **Hybrid inverter bolt (25V-869):** About 51,600 2025–2026 Camry Hybrids built between late August and mid-November 2025 may have a bolt inside the inverter that wasn't tightened properly. It can cause a loss of drive power and could also create a fire risk. The dealer inspects the inverter and either re-tightens the bolt or replaces the inverter, free of charge.

On any car you're considering, run the VIN on [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask the seller for proof that the work was done.

## Which one to buy

- **LE FWD:** the best value and the best economy. It has everything most drivers need.
- **LE AWD:** the efficient choice for snowy places. It gives up only 1 mpg.
- **SE or Nightshade:** sportier looks and a firmer feel, for a few mpg less.
- **XLE:** the comfortable, well-equipped pick, with the larger screens and the available head-up display.
- **XSE AWD:** the quickest and most expensive, and the least efficient.

## The verdict

The 2025–2026 Camry asks nothing of its owner that the old gas Camry didn't. It drives the same way, it's maintained the same way and it costs far less to fuel. If your budget reaches this far, it's the best Camry so far. Just make sure the recall work is done.

::: curiosity AllTrac's Long Shadow
Toyota first sold an all-wheel-drive Camry, the AllTrac, from 1988 to 1991. Then it disappeared for nearly 30 years. When all-wheel drive came back for 2020, it used a mechanical system borrowed from the RAV4. The 2025 Camry took a different route: there's no driveshaft to the rear axle at all, just an electric motor that pushes the rear wheels when the front ones need help.
:::
