If you hear "hybrid" and think of a Prius, the i-FORCE MAX will surprise you. Toyota built it to add muscle, not to stretch a tank of gas. It takes the same 2.4-liter turbo as the gas Tacoma and adds a 48-hp electric motor inside the 8-speed automatic, fed by a 1.87-kWh nickel-metal hydride battery. Together they make 326 hp and 465 lb-ft, 148 lb-ft more than the gas turbo and 200 more than the old V6.

## Where you'll find it

The hybrid comes only as a 4x4 Double Cab with the automatic. It's optional on the TRD Sport, TRD Off-Road and Limited, and standard on the 2 top off-road grades. The TRD Pro adds Fox QS3 shocks with 3-way adjustment, 33-inch tires and Toyota's IsoDynamic Performance Seats, which use their own small shock absorbers to keep the front occupants steadier on rough ground. The Trailhunter, developed with Australian off-road brand ARB, gets Old Man Emu shocks, 33-inch Goodyear tires, rock rails, a steel rear bumper, an onboard air compressor and a 2,400-watt power inverter.

## How it drives

The electric motor fills in the gap before the turbo builds boost, so the hybrid pulls hard from a standstill and when climbing at low speed, which is exactly where off-road drivers want it. The extra hardware adds weight, though, and the hybrid's own grades carry heavy off-road equipment. Edmunds timed a hybrid TRD Pro to 60 mph in 7.4 seconds, only a little quicker than the 7.6 of a gas TRD Sport.

The fuel savings are real but small. The EPA rates most hybrid grades at 23 mpg combined (22 city, 24 highway), against 20 or 21 for a gas 4x4. At 15,000 miles a year and $4 a gallon, the difference between 20 and 23 mpg is about $390. That won't pay back the hybrid's price premium on fuel alone. Buy it for the way it drives.

Towing goes down, not up: the hybrid is rated for 2,699 to 2,722 kg (5,950 to 6,000 lb), against up to 2,948 kg (6,500 lb) for the gas turbo. Maximum payload is about 776 kg (1,710 lb).

## Living with it

The hybrid gets the same cabin and the same 8- or 14-inch screens as the gas truck. Toyota covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 150,000 miles and the other hybrid components for 8 years or 100,000 miles. On a used one, that coverage is worth confirming in writing.

## What to check before you buy

The hybrid shares the gas Tacoma's recalls, and 2 of them hit it directly. About 2,400 2025 hybrids are in the front driveshaft recall (25V-656), and about 4,450 2024 hybrid TRD Off-Roads are in the August 2026 shock reservoir recall. The 16-inch-brake hose recall (25V-058) covers 2024–2025 trucks too. Run the VIN and ask for proof of every repair.

Then check the hybrid system itself. Make sure the truck starts quietly and that no hybrid warnings come on, and ask a shop that knows Toyota hybrids to scan for stored codes. On a TRD Pro or Trailhunter, look closely underneath, around the skid plates, sliders and suspension mounts, for signs of hard trail use. Ask whether it was used to run power tools or campsite equipment from the inverter, and how often.

## The verdict

Choose the hybrid if you'll use the torque: towing in the hills, crawling on trails or simply wanting the strongest Tacoma. A TRD Off-Road i-FORCE MAX is the sensible way in. The TRD Pro and Trailhunter are the best factory off-road Tacomas Toyota has built, and they're priced like it. If you mostly commute, the 278-hp gas truck is the better buy.

::: curiosity A Seat With Its Own Suspension
The TRD Pro's IsoDynamic Performance Seat uses an air-over-oil shock absorber that lets the seat move up, down and side to side, separately from the truck. The idea is to keep the driver's head and eyes steadier at speed over rough ground. Toyota calls the design patent-pending, and a lever on the seatback locks it out for normal driving. For 2026, it became an option on the Tundra TRD Pro too.
:::
