If the word hybrid makes you picture a silent commuter creeping through the school pickup line, the 4Runner i-FORCE MAX has news for you. It pairs the turbocharged 2.4-liter with an electric motor built into the 8-speed transmission, for 326 hp and 465 lb-ft. That's 148 lb-ft more than the regular turbo. This is a hybrid in muddy boots, tuned to pull rather than to sip.

The battery is small: 1.87 kWh of nickel-metal hydride. You can't plug it in, and it isn't meant to carry you any distance on electricity alone. Its job is to fill in torque, especially at low speeds, which is exactly where crawling over rocks and dragging a trailer up a grade happen.

## Pull versus sip

The extra torque shows up when the truck is loaded or climbing, and on the stopwatch. Car and Driver got a Trailhunter to 60 mph in 6.7 seconds, and Motor Trend timed one at 7.4. Either way, it's the quickest 4Runner in this guide.

It's also the heaviest. Toyota lists the Trailhunter at 2,495 kg (5,500 lb), against 2,254 kg (4,970 lb) for a turbo Limited 4x4, and that weight eats into the fuel savings.

[[chart:mpg]]

The savings are real but small. The EPA rates the hybrid 4x4 at 23 mpg combined against 21 for the turbo 4x4. At 15,000 miles a year that's 652 gallons against 714, a difference of about 62 gallons, or roughly $250 at $4 a gallon. Buy the hybrid for the torque. If fuel savings are the reason, do the math on the price difference first.

## Where you'll find it

The hybrid is standard on the Platinum, TRD Pro and Trailhunter, and optional on the TRD Off-Road and Limited. That means it usually arrives bundled with expensive suspension, armor or leather, so there's no single "hybrid premium" to compare. Line up 2 trucks of the same grade before you decide what the hybrid really costs.

[[photo:ch3-badge]]

It brings a couple of things of its own. A 2,400-watt power outlet comes standard, enough for a camp kettle or power tools. A third row doesn't, because the battery sits where the extra seats would go.

## Owning one

There's no decade of data on this powertrain yet. Toyota's hybrid record is encouraging, but it isn't a promise. Since the 2020 model year, Toyota has covered hybrid batteries for 10 years or 150,000 miles from first use, and other hybrid parts for 8 years or 100,000 miles. Confirm what's left for the exact VIN before you buy.

On a test drive, start it cold, let it warm up fully and drive it slowly as well as briskly. Listen for odd drivetrain noises, watch for warning lights and make sure the brakes feel consistent: the hand-off between regenerative braking and the friction brakes should be seamless. Ask for records of software updates and recall work.

## The verdict

The hybrid is for buyers who want the strongest 4Runner, need a hybrid-only grade, or tow and climb often. It isn't automatically the value pick or the economy pick. The regular turbo is the sensible alternative, and the old V6 is a different purchase entirely.

::: curiosity The Speaker That Leaves With You
Tucked behind the touchscreen on many 2025–2026 4Runners is a JBL Flex, a real portable Bluetooth speaker docked in the dash. Press a button and it pops out, ready for the campsite, the tailgate or the beach, with up to 6 hours of battery, and you can charge it away from the truck. It comes on the Limited, Platinum, TRD Pro and Trailhunter, and with the Premium package on the TRD Sport and TRD Off-Road, so every hybrid-only grade has one. Just remember to put it back before you drive off.
:::
