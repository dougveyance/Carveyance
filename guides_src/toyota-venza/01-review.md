The Venza is what happens when Toyota takes a sensible formula and dresses it for dinner. It shares its platform, its wheelbase and its hybrid system with the RAV4 Hybrid, then hides them under a long, sloping body and a cabin with softer surfaces and fewer hard edges. Toyota sold it only as a hybrid, every one has all-wheel drive, and it lasted just 4 model years. The Crown Signia took its place for 2025.

## How it drives

Every Venza uses a 2.5-liter 4-cylinder and 3 electric motors, one of which drives the rear wheels, for 219 hp combined. That's the same output as the RAV4 Hybrid. It's adequately quick rather than fast: Edmunds measured 0 to 60 mph in 7.6 seconds. Push hard and the engine drones the way Toyota hybrids do. Drive it gently and it's hushed and smooth, slipping between electric and gas power without fuss.

The payoff is fuel. The EPA rates every Venza at 40 mpg city, 37 highway and 39 combined, and there's nothing to plug in. At 12,000 miles a year and $4 a gallon, 39 mpg works out to about 308 gallons, or roughly $1,230 a year.

The ride is comfortable and the handling is secure, but nobody buys a Venza for the corners. Car and Driver's test car reached only 0.79 g on the skidpad on its all-season tires, and it needed 179 feet to stop from 70 mph, which is long for the class. It weighs 1,745 to 1,775 kg (3,847 to 3,913 lb) depending on the grade, about 215 kg (475 lb) more than a base 2021 RAV4.

## Living with it

Inside is where the Venza earns its price. The dashboard is more ambitious than a RAV4's, the materials are nicer and the cabin is quiet at highway speed. The Limited adds a 12.3-inch touchscreen with JBL audio and, by 2024, a 12.3-inch digital instrument cluster, a digital rearview mirror and a 10-inch head-up display. Toyota Safety Sense came standard on every grade from the start, along with blind spot monitoring.

The catch is space.

[[chart:cargo]]

Behind the rear seat there's 28.8 cubic feet, against the RAV4 Hybrid's 37.6, and 55.1 with the seat folded. The sloping tailgate takes more than the number suggests, because tall, square things like crates and stroller frames hit the glass first. The rear seat is comfortable for 2 adults, and there's no third row. Bring the bulky things you carry to the test drive, and fold the seat yourself.

The 2021–2022 cars used touch-sensitive buttons on the center stack, which not every tester liked. The 2023 update brought Toyota's newer multimedia system, with an 8-inch screen on the LE and a 12.3-inch screen on everything else.

## What to check before you buy

The hybrid system is proven Toyota hardware, so focus on the things that are unique to this car. Test every screen, camera and parking sensor, and run the Star Gaze roof through both of its modes. Check that recall work was done: rear turn signals on 2021 cars, stability-control software on 2021–2022 cars, and the 12.3-inch instrument display and camera software on 2023–2024 cars. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

The Venza makes sense for a buyer who likes the RAV4 Hybrid's running costs but wants a nicer cabin and a calmer drive. It's a poor fit for a family that fills the cargo area every weekend. Don't buy it as a bigger RAV4, because it isn't one. Buy it as a more refined one, and pick it on condition and records rather than on the year alone. If you want the newer tech, [the trims chapter](../trims-and-years/) explains what changed for 2023.

::: curiosity A Roof That Frosts on Command
The Star Gaze roof was a first for Toyota: a fixed glass panel that switches from clear to frosted at the press of a button, so you can let the light in without the glare. On a 2021 Limited it was a $1,400 option, about $1,700 in 2026 dollars.
:::
