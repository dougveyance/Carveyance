Toyota made hybrids normal, then took its time with an electric SUV. The bZ4X arrived for 2023 looking like the future but driving like a familiar Toyota: soft, quiet and easy. It's about the size of a RAV4 and rides on a platform Toyota developed with Subaru, which sells a twin called the Solterra. It was also criticized from the start for its range, for charging that could be slow, and for a name that reads like a password.

## How it drives

Front-drive versions have a single 201-hp motor and a 71.4 kWh battery. All-wheel-drive versions have a motor on each axle, 214 hp combined, a 72.8 kWh battery and Subaru's X-MODE traction system for snow and mud. Car and Driver ran an AWD Limited from 0 to 60 mph in 6.3 seconds, quick enough for any commute, with the instant response that makes electric cars easy to drive in traffic.

[[chart:power]]

The ride is soft and the cabin is quiet. On the test drive, set the seat and wheel where you like them and check that the rim doesn't hide the gauges. There's no one-pedal driving mode, but you can choose stronger regenerative braking, which slows the car when you lift off the accelerator.

## Range and charging

[[chart:range]]

The EPA rates the bZ4X at 252 miles for the XLE FWD, 242 for the Limited FWD, 228 for the XLE AWD and 222 for the Limited AWD. Real-world tests came in lower. Edmunds drove a Limited FWD 227 miles on a charge, and Car and Driver's AWD Limited managed only 160 miles in its highway range test.

Toyota's own charging figure is from the low-battery light to 80% in about 30 minutes with front drive, or about 60 minutes with all-wheel drive, under ideal conditions. Cold weather makes every EV charge more slowly, and reviewers criticized the bZ4X for slow charging even into 2025. For a used car, the number on the fast charger's screen matters less than what the car actually accepts on a cold morning, so test it if you can.

For a household with a home charger and a predictable commute, none of this may matter. For one that drives across the state every other weekend, it will.

## The wheel recall

In June 2022, weeks after deliveries began, Toyota told bZ4X owners to stop driving their cars. Recall 22V-444 found that the hub bolts on a wheel could loosen after little use, to the point where the wheel could come off. About 260 US cars, built from late March to late April 2022, were affected, and Toyota offered owners a buyback among other options. The fix, which began that fall, replaced the hub bolts with redesigned bolts and washers and fitted improved wheels. On any 2023 bZ4X, ask for proof that this recall was done.

## The 2025 changes

For 2025, Toyota cut the price by up to $6,000, bringing the XLE FWD to $37,070. It added a Nightshade Edition, based on the XLE AWD, and gave the Limited Traffic Jam Assist, Lane Change Assist and Front Cross Traffic Alert. New buyers got a year of free charging on the EVgo network. The best EPA range stayed at 252 miles.

## What to check before you buy

Confirm the battery size, the drive and the grade from the window sticker or the build data. Test Level 2 charging and, if you can, a DC fast-charging session. Check that the hub-bolt, defroster and camera recalls were done. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A 2023–2025 bZ4X is a comfortable, well-built commuter that works best for a household that charges overnight and rarely drives far. The 2025 price cut and the much better 2026 model both weigh on what earlier cars are worth, which helps a used buyer. If you take long trips, or charge mostly in public, read [the 2026 chapter](../2026-bz-and-woodland/) first.

::: curiosity What bZ4X Means
"bZ" stands for beyond Zero, Toyota's name for its zero-emission models. The 4 says it's about the size of a RAV4, and the X marks it as a crossover. For 2026, the North American car dropped the last 2 characters and became simply the bZ.
:::
