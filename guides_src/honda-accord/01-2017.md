The ninth-generation Accord arrived for 2013 and was refreshed for 2016, so the 2017 car is its last and most complete year. It looks like a sensible midsize sedan because it is one: roomy, quiet and easy to see out of. What makes it interesting now is what came next. For 2018, Honda dropped the V6, the coupe and the naturally aspirated 4-cylinder in one go, so a 2017 is the last chance to buy a particular kind of Accord.

## 3 different cars

[[chart:power]]

Most 2017 Accords have a 2.4-liter 4-cylinder with 185 hp, or 189 in the Sport, with a CVT or a 6-speed manual. It's smooth, it's frugal, and the EPA rates the CVT sedan at 30 mpg combined. The 3.5-liter V6 makes 278 hp and changes the character completely: it pulls hard from low speeds, sounds good doing it and turns freeway merges into a non-event. The sedan pairs it with a 6-speed automatic, rated at 25 mpg, and the coupe could also be ordered with a 6-speed manual. The Accord Hybrid returned for 2017 after a year off, with Honda's 2-motor system, 212 hp and an EPA rating of 48 mpg combined. Car and Driver ran one from 0 to 60 mph in 7.2 seconds. The trade-off is space, because its battery sits behind the rear seat and cuts the trunk to 13.7 cubic feet.

## Sedan or coupe

The coupe is a different purchase, not just a sedan with 2 fewer doors. The rear seat is harder to reach, and the people who want one usually want the V6, ideally with the manual. That makes it worth more to an enthusiast, and also more likely to have been driven hard or modified. A stock 4-cylinder sedan and a modified V6 coupe can't be judged by the same rules, so price each one against its own kind.

## The equipment to check

Honda Sensing, with collision mitigation braking, adaptive cruise control and lane keeping assist, was an option on most 2017 Accords rather than standard, so a listing that says "with Honda Sensing" is telling you something useful. Confirm it on the window sticker or with a VIN decoder. Apple CarPlay and Android Auto arrived with the 2016 refresh, but not on every grade, so connect your own phone on the test drive rather than trusting the listing.

## What to check before you buy

On a V6, ask for proof that the timing belt was replaced when the Maintenance Minder called for it. Unlike the 4-cylinder's timing chain, the belt is a scheduled part, and a V6 without records needs one budgeted for. The 2015–2017 V6 is also in a fuel pump recall (19V060), because particles in the fuel can reduce pump performance. On a hybrid, have a Honda shop scan the hybrid system and check for warning lights. On every car, run the VIN for open recalls, including the front passenger seat weight sensor recall that covers 2016–2022 Accords (26V332) and the fuel pump recall for 2013–2023 cars (23V858). The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A documented 4-cylinder sedan is the rational buy: cheap to run, roomy and still pleasant to drive. A V6 with its timing belt records is the one to find if you want effortless performance and the sound of 6 cylinders, and a V6 coupe with the manual is a genuine enthusiast car with a family-car badge. The hybrid suits high-mileage drivers who can live with the smaller trunk. If you'd rather have the newer chassis and the 252-hp turbo, compare it with [the 2018–2022 Accord](../2018-2022/) before you decide.
