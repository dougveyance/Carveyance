A CR-V is often a family's main car, which means school runs, road trips, dogs, sports gear and plenty of short trips. None of that is a reason to avoid one, but it does mean the condition of the car in front of you matters far more than the badge. Check the records, the recalls and the cabin, as well as the engine.

## First, know which one it is

Decode the VIN to confirm the year, the engine and whether it has all-wheel drive. A 2017–2019 LX has the 2.4-liter; every other gas CR-V has the 1.5-liter turbo. A 2020–2022 Hybrid always has all-wheel drive, but a 2023 or later hybrid may not. And a hybrid has a smaller cargo area and no spare tire, so check the listing photos against the car.

## The test drive

Start the car from cold and listen for rough running and rattles. Accelerate gently and then hard; the CVT should feel smooth, with no shudder or slipping. On a 2023–2025 car, turn the wheel slowly from lock to lock in a parking lot and note any heavy or sticky spots, a symptom of the steering gearbox recall. Run the air conditioning on full cold, test every window and door, and check the rear seat's fold-flat levers in the cargo area. On an all-wheel-drive car, make a few tight, slow turns and listen for groaning or binding.

## Engine and hybrid checks

On a 2017–2018 turbo, check the oil level and smell the dipstick for fuel, especially on a car from a cold state that has done mostly short trips. On any CR-V, look for oil leaks and ask for the service records. On a hybrid, have a Honda dealer scan the hybrid system and confirm how much of the hybrid component warranty is left for the VIN. Check the 12-volt battery, and make sure the tire repair kit is in the car and its recall is done.

## Cabin and body checks

Family cars collect wear in predictable places. Look at the rear seat and cargo area for stains, damage and pet hair, check the power tailgate if it has one, and look under the cargo floor for water. Check the bumpers and lower doors for parking damage, and the tires for uneven wear.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2017:** 17V442. The fuel supply pipe may disconnect and leak. 17V256: a certification label whose ink can wipe off.
- **2017–2018:** 18V663. A magnet in the electric power steering's torque sensor can come loose, and the steering assist can briefly push the wrong way at full lock.
- **2017–2020:** 23V158. The front seat belt buckles may not latch.
- **2018–2019:** 21V215. The low-pressure fuel pump may fail. 23V858 also covers 2013–2023 CR-Vs, including the 2020 Hybrid.
- **2019:** 19V569, a fuel tank weld that may fail; 19V383, burrs on the steering wheel that can damage the driver's air bag wiring.
- **2019–2020:** 19V865. The rear subframe bolts may loosen.
- **2020:** 22V380. A clip inside the fuel tank may come loose and make the fuel gauge read wrong.
- **2017–2022:** 26V332, and 24V064 for 2020–2021. The front passenger seat weight sensor can crack, so the passenger air bag may not work correctly.
- **2021:** 21V900, and the 2022 Hybrid. The second-row center seat belt's locking mode can switch off.
- **2023:** 23V092. The front passenger seat back frame may be improperly welded and fail in a crash.
- **2023–2025, including the 2025 e:FCEV:** 24V744. Friction inside the steering gearbox can make steering difficult. 24V763 (2023–2025 Hybrids): the high-pressure fuel pump may crack and leak fuel.
- **2023–2026 Hybrids and the e:FCEV:** 26V366. The tire repair kit's sealant bottle can build pressure and blow off its cap. Cars repaired with faulty replacement parts need the fix again (26V663).
- **2025 e:FCEV:** 25V858. Coolant can leak into the fuel cell stack case and cut drive power. The fix is a new stack.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title, flood damage or a musty smell you can't explain. Walk away from hybrid warning lights the seller can't explain, from an all-wheel-drive system that binds in tight turns, and from any car whose seller won't allow an independent inspection.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and an average that mixes a 2017 LX with a 2023 Sport Touring Hybrid tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same generation, engine, drive, trim and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Toyota RAV4 or RAV4 Hybrid of the same age, and take the cost of tires, brakes and overdue service off the price.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the CR-V past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for the turbo, the all-wheel-drive system and the hybrid system, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2023 or later Sport Hybrid with its recalls done.
- **Best value:** a 2020–2022 EX with the turbo and records.
- **Best for snow:** a 2020–2022 Hybrid, or any all-wheel-drive CR-V with good tires.
- **Most cargo space:** a 2023 or later EX or EX-L.
- **Simplest engine:** a 2017–2019 LX with the 2.4-liter.
