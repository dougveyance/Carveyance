The 2013 Fusion was the car that made the rental row glamorous: a Ford of Europe design on the global CD4 platform, sold abroad as the Mondeo, with a grille the press spent years comparing to Aston Martin. America responded with 4 straight years near or above 300,000 sales. Then Ford announced in 2018 it was leaving the US sedan business, built the last car in July 2020, and handed the segment back to Toyota and Honda — which is exactly why these are now the value play of the mid-size used market.

## The engine ladder

The **2.5 Duratec** base engine (175 hp) is slow, simple fleet fodder — fine at the right price. The **1.5 EcoBoost** (181 hp, 27–29 mpg) is the volume pick. The **2.0 EcoBoost** (240 hp, 245 from 2017) is the one that makes the car feel like its styling promised, especially with the available all-wheel drive. The early **1.6 EcoBoost** (2013–14) is the only one with a recall cloud — verify completion by VIN. And then there's the **Sport**: the F-150's 2.7 twin-turbo V6, 325 hp and 380 lb-ft through standard AWD, 5.1 seconds to 60 in Car and Driver's hands. It was a budget Audi S4 wearing an Uber costume, and the used market still hasn't noticed.

## Years, trims, money

Buy 2017-on if the budget allows: the refresh brought Sync 3, the rotary shifter, the 245-hp 2.0 and the Sport, and late cars added standard Co-Pilot360 safety equipment. Titanium is the trim sweet spot — the Platinum's quilted leather rarely justifies its used premium. A 2017 S listed at $22,120, about $30,300 in 2026 dollars; clean mid-trim cars now change hands in the low teens, Sports only a little above that. The sedan exodus that killed the Fusion is your negotiating partner: supply is enormous, demand is quiet, and patience gets rewarded.
