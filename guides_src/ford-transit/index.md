In 2015 Ford did something almost unthinkable: it killed the Econoline, America's best-selling van for 35 straight years, and replaced it with a tall European unibody. Nobody mourned for long. The Transit out-hauled the old van, drove like something half its size, and came in so many configurations — 2 wheelbases, 3 roofs, cargo, 15-seat wagon, cutaway, chassis cab — that it swallowed every job the E-Series ever did and a few it didn't, from plumbing fleets to #vanlife conversions. About 1.47 million have sold since, with 2025 the best year yet, and since 2022 the electric E-Transit has quietly become America's best-selling electric van.

For a used buyer the Transit is a rare thing: a modern vehicle whose spec sheet genuinely matters more than its year. Roof height, length, series and engine define what the van can do, and the wrong combination can't be fixed later. Sort that out first and the rest is a condition game played against fleet mileage.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-around used buy:** a 2020-or-newer 250 medium roof with the base 3.5 V6 and the 10-speed — the refresh brought the better gearbox, available AWD and modern driver aids.
- **The strong one:** any 3.5 EcoBoost — 310 hp and 400 lb-ft tows 7,500 lb and never feels loaded. Worth seeking for camper conversions and mountain states.
- **The electric one:** a 2024-or-newer E-Transit with the 89-kWh battery and 159-mile range — if your days are local and you can charge at home or depot, nothing is cheaper to run.
- **The people-mover:** a 350 low-roof XLT wagon. Up to 15 seats, car-like to drive, and the used market is full of lightly-used church and hotel examples.
- **Approach with eyes open:** the 2015–19 3.2 diesel — torquey and durable but orphaned, with pricey parts and no fuel-cost case anymore.
