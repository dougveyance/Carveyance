The Crown is Toyota's longest-running car name. It first went on sale in Japan in 1955, left the US after 1972, and came back for 2023 as something new: a tall, all-wheel-drive hybrid sedan that replaced the Avalon. For 2025, Toyota added the Crown Signia, a wagon-shaped SUV that replaced the Venza. Both are built in Japan, both are hybrids, and both have all-wheel drive as standard.

For a used buyer, the choices are body style and powertrain. The Crown offers a 236-hp hybrid rated at 41 mpg or a 340-hp turbocharged Hybrid MAX rated at 30; the Signia has a 240-hp hybrid rated at 38 mpg and much more cargo space. This guide has 3 chapters: one for each model, and the checklist to use before you buy.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder:** a Crown XLE or Limited, rated at 41 mpg combined.
- **Quickest:** a Crown Platinum with the Hybrid MAX, which Toyota rates at 5.7 seconds to 60 mph.
- **Most practical:** a Crown Signia XLE, with 25.8 cubic feet behind the rear seat and 68.8 with it folded.
- **Most equipment for the money:** a 2025 or later Crown, with heated and ventilated front seats standard.
- **Think twice if** you need a big trunk. The Crown sedan has 15.2 cubic feet, about the same as a Camry.
