The Toyota Crown family

17 generations and variants in the Index, 1955–present. Oldest first; every card opens the full page, with its own versions, figures and story.

  1. 1955–1962

    Toyota Crown (first generation)

    The first Crown, 1955–1962: the Toyopet Crown, Toyota's first big postwar sedan and the first Toyota exported to the United States.

    48 hp25.9 sec 0-60
  2. 1962–1967

    Toyota Crown (second generation)

    The second Crown, 1962–1967: bigger, with an American-style grille, the first crown badge, a 6-cylinder from 1965, and the first Toyota sold in Europe.

    89 hp18.5 sec 0-60250,000 built
  3. 1967–1971

    Toyota Crown (third generation)

    The third Crown, 1967–1971: a safer perimeter frame, a luxurious Super Saloon, and the last Crown offered as a full line of commercial vehicles.

    108 hp16.5 sec 0-60277,641 built
  4. 1971–1974

    Toyota Crown (fourth generation)

    The fourth Crown, 1971–1974: the first marketed worldwide as a Toyota rather than a Toyopet, and the last Crown sold in America until 2023.

    122 hp14.1 sec 0-60287,970 built
  5. 1974–1979

    Toyota Crown (fifth generation)

    The fifth Crown, 1974–1979: sedans, 2-door and 4-door hardtops, wagons and vans, with a pillared-hardtop Royal Saloon at the top.

    138 hp14.5 sec 0-60404,699 built
  6. 1979–1983

    Toyota Crown (sixth generation)

    The sixth Crown, 1979–1983: the first Crown sold in Germany, and in October 1980 the first Crown Turbo.

    143 hp10.5 sec 0-60326,891 built
  7. 1983–1987

    Toyota Crown (seventh generation)

    The seventh Crown, 1983–1987: a twin-cam inline-6, a supercharged 2.0-liter from 1985 and independent rear suspension on most models.

    173 hp9.2 sec 0-60539,485 built
  8. 1987–1991

    Toyota Crown (eighth generation)

    The eighth Crown, 1987–1991: Toyota's first car with an airbag, the world's first CD-ROM navigation system, and a 4.0-liter V8 from the Lexus LS 400.

    187 hp8.9 sec 0-60873,760 built
  9. 1991–1995

    Toyota Crown (ninth generation)

    The ninth Crown, 1991–1995: a hardtop with new styling and a new Royal Touring trim, built only in right-hand drive.

    227 hp9.3 sec 0-60370,921 built
  10. 1995–1999

    Toyota Crown (tenth generation)

    The tenth Crown, 1995–1999: boxier, and the first Crown built as a unibody instead of body-on-frame.

    217 hp8.7 sec 0-60503,430 built
  11. 1999–2003

    Toyota Crown (eleventh generation)

    The eleventh Crown, 1999–2003: Royal and sporty Athlete sedans, a turbocharged Athlete V, and a mild-hybrid 3.0-liter from 2001.

    217 hp7.7 sec 0-60351,888 built
  12. 2003–2008

    Toyota Crown (twelfth generation)

    The twelfth Crown, 2003–2008: based on the Zero Crown concept, and offered in left-hand drive again through Chinese production.

    252 hp7.4 sec 0-60
  13. 2008–2012

    Toyota Crown (thirteenth generation)

    The thirteenth Crown, 2008–2012: Royal, Athlete and Hybrid models, with a V6 hybrid system shared with the Lexus GS 450h.

    252 hp7.4 sec 0-60210,567 built
  14. 2012–2018

    Toyota Crown (fourteenth generation)

    The fourteenth Crown, 2012–2018: a bold new grille, touch-panel controls, and a Crown Hybrid from January 2013.

    200 hp8.0 sec 0-60149,638 built
  15. 2018–2022

    Toyota Crown (fifteenth generation)

    The fifteenth Crown, 2018–2022: built on Toyota's TNGA-L luxury platform, with a 2.0-liter turbo and 2.5-liter and 3.5-liter hybrids.

    242 hp7.3 sec 0-60
  16. 2023–present

    Toyota Crown (2023–)

    The Crown name's return to the US after 1972: a tall, all-wheel-drive hybrid sedan that replaced the Avalon for 2023, rated at 41 mpg, with a 340-hp Hybrid MAX on the Platinum.

    236 hp7.2 sec 0-6058,356 built
    Hybrid · Hybrid MAX
  17. 2025–present

    Toyota Crown Signia

    The Crown as a long, low hybrid SUV: 240 hp, all-wheel drive, 38 mpg combined and up to 68.8 cubic feet of cargo space. It replaced the Venza for 2025 and is sold in Japan as the Crown Estate.

    240 hp7.0 sec 0-6050,923 built
