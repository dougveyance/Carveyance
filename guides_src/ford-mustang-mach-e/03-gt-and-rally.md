The GT is the Mach-E that earns the Mustang badge in a straight line. Every one has all-wheel drive, the extended-range battery and 480 hp. The GT Performance Edition, sold for 2021–2023, raised torque from 600 to 634 lb-ft. From 2024 the Rally joined the range: a GT tuned for gravel and rough roads, with 700 lb-ft. On the 2026 GT, the GT Performance Upgrade brings it to 700 lb-ft as well.

## How quick they are

[[chart:accelGT]]

Car and Driver ran a 2021 GT to 60 mph in 4.3 seconds and a 2021 GT Performance Edition in 3.7. The 2024 Rally took 3.3 seconds, the quickest time for a Mach-E in Car and Driver's tests. All 3 weigh about 2,220 to 2,260 kg (4,890 to 4,990 lb), so the speed comes from the motors' instant torque rather than from lightness.

## How far they go

[[chart:rangeGT]]

Pace costs range. The EPA rated the 2021 GT at 270 miles and the Performance Edition at 260. On the 2026 cars, the GT is rated at 280 miles and the Rally at 255, against 300 miles for an extended-range all-wheel-drive Premium. Big wheels and grippy tires are part of the reason, and they also make tires the biggest running cost after electricity.

| 2026 starting price | $53,395 | — | $57,690 |
| Best for | Pace on paved roads | The quickest early Mach-E | Rough roads and bad weather |

The 2021 GT started at $59,900 before delivery, or $74,300 in 2026 dollars, so a 2026 GT costs much less new than the first one did.

## Who should buy one

A GT or a Rally makes sense if you want a quick, practical family car and accept the range penalty and the tire bills. If you'll mostly drive on highways, an extended-range Premium will go further on a charge for less money and still feels quick. Test-drive on rough roads before you buy; the GT's firmer setup and big wheels aren't to everyone's taste.
