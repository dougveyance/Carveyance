The F-150 Lightning is finished. Ford ended production at the Rouge Electric Vehicle Center in December 2025 after 4 model years and about 100,000 trucks, taking a $19.5 billion write-down on its EV program and promising a range-extended successor. None of that makes the truck itself worse. It makes it cheaper, and the Lightning was always a better truck than it was a business: 775 lb-ft in every version, 60 mph in 4.0 seconds in Car and Driver's test, a 400-liter front trunk, and a battery that can run your house through a blackout.

The used market is where the Lightning story gets good. Early buyers paid pandemic markups on trucks that now trade at deep discounts, and a discontinued model with an 8-year, 100,000-mile federal battery warranty is a calculated bargain rather than a gamble. This guide covers the lineup, the batteries that define it, and the checks a used electric truck demands.

## Quick picks

- **Best value:** a 2023–2024 XLT with the extended-range battery. 580 hp, 320 miles of EPA range, and the depreciation already happened to someone else.
- **Best work truck:** a Pro, either battery. Vinyl floors, the full 775 lb-ft, and Pro Power Onboard for the job site.
- **Best all-out:** a Lariat extended range. Platinum money buys 22-inch wheels and 20 fewer miles of range.
- **Skip if** you tow long distances often. Towing can halve the range, and that is the one job this truck does worse than a gas F-150.
