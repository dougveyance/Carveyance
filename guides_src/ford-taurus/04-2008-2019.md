When Alan Mulally arrived at Ford and learned the Taurus was dead, he revived the name within a year — the Five Hundred became the 2008 Taurus with a 263-hp 3.5 and a real 6-speed, a 2-year placeholder with Volvo-derived bones and 5-star crash ratings. The real revival landed for 2010: a genuinely imposing full-size flagship from $25,995 (about $40,100 in 2026 dollars), refreshed in 2013 with a 288-hp V6, a 25-mpg 2.0 EcoBoost, and the SHO on top. The Police Interceptor Sedan succeeded the Crown Victoria in 2013, and the last civilian car left Chicago on March 1, 2019 at around $28,000 — $36,800 in 2026 dollars.

## Why these are the smart used buy

Full-size, safe, quiet, and unfashionable — which is the whole opportunity. A 2013–2019 Limited costs thousands less than the equivalent Avalon while giving more car, and the 2.0 EcoBoost version returns compact-car fuel bills in a limousine footprint. The 2008–09 cars are the budget version of the same idea and genuinely rare. Avoid nothing on reputation; these powertrains are shared with Explorers and F-150s and are well understood.

## The checklist

**Water pump on the 3.5 V6:** it's driven internally by the timing chain, and replacement means major labor — a car with coolant-service records and no overheating history is worth paying up for; an unexplained coolant loss is a walk. **PTU fluid on AWD cars:** the little transfer unit cooks its fluid; evidence of changes is gold. **2.0 EcoBoost:** standard turbo rules — oil history or no deal. **Ex-police cars:** cheap and mechanically fine, but priced for a reason; idle hours matter more than miles. **MyFord Touch (2013–15):** confirm the screen behaves or budget for the updated unit. Then buy the biggest, quietest bargain in the full-size used market.
