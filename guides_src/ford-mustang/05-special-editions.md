Between the GT and the Shelbys, Ford has kept a run of special Mustangs going since 2019, each with a name from the car's past. Their prices and their purposes vary enormously: a Bullitt is a GT tuned for the road and dressed to recall a film, while a GTD is a hand-finished track car that costs more than most houses. What they share is a V8 and a reason to exist beyond the spec sheet.

## The lineup

| Model | Years | Engine | Power | Transmission | Starting price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bullitt | 2019–2020 | 5.0-liter V8 | 480 hp | 6-speed manual only | $46,595 for 2019, or $61,300 in 2026 dollars |
| Mach 1 | 2021–2023 | 5.0-liter V8 | 480 hp | Tremec 6-speed manual or 10-speed automatic | $51,720 for 2021, or $64,200 in 2026 dollars |
| Dark Horse | 2024–2026 | 5.0-liter V8 | 500 hp | Tremec 6-speed manual or 10-speed automatic | $57,970 for 2024, or $62,100 in 2026 dollars; $65,080 for 2026 |
| Dark Horse SC | 2026 | 5.2-liter supercharged V8 | 795 hp | 7-speed dual-clutch automatic | $106,090 |
| GTD | 2025–2026 | 5.2-liter supercharged V8 | 815 hp | 8-speed dual-clutch automatic | About $325,000 for 2025, or about $338,800 in 2026 dollars |

Prices are before delivery. Ford says the GTD "starts at approximately $325,000."

Car and Driver ran a 2019 Bullitt to 60 mph in 4.4 seconds and a 2021 Mach 1 with the Handling Package in 4.5. The Dark Horse took 4.1 seconds with the manual. The GTD's 2.8 seconds is the quickest time Car and Driver has recorded for a Mustang.

## Bullitt, 2019–2020

The Bullitt was a GT with 480 hp, a manual gearbox only, painted Dark Highland Green, like the 1968 car in the film, or Shadow Black. It's the road-going choice of the group: quick, understated and less extreme than the cars that followed. The EPA rated the 2019 car at 18 mpg combined.

## Mach 1, 2021–2023

The Mach 1 replaced the Bullitt for 2021. It kept the 480-hp V8 but borrowed parts from the Shelby GT350 and GT500, including a Tremec 6-speed manual, to sit between the GT and the Shelbys as a car meant for track days. A 10-speed automatic was also available, and an optional Handling Package was aimed squarely at the track. It's the best-value track Mustang of the sixth generation.

## Dark Horse, 2024–2026

The Dark Horse took the Mach 1's place on the seventh-generation car, with 500 hp from a modified 5.0-liter V8 and the Tremec manual. For 2026 the 10-speed automatic became standard and the manual optional. It's the most capable Mustang you can buy for less than $100,000.

## Dark Horse SC and GTD

At the top of the range, both use a 5.2-liter supercharged cross-plane V8. In the 2026 Dark Horse SC it makes 795 hp, at $106,090, and in August 2026 Ford added a Dark Horse SC convertible, tuned more for fast touring than for lap times. The GTD makes 815 hp and 664 lb-ft. Its body starts at the Flat Rock plant, and Multimatic finishes the car by hand in Markham, Ontario. Ford took applications rather than ordinary orders. In December 2024, a GTD lapped the Nürburgring in 6:57.685, the first time a Mustang had gone under 7 minutes there. Ford quotes a top speed of 202 mph.
