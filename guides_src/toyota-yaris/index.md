The Yaris was Toyota's cheapest car in the US, and its last 4 years are confusing, because the name covered 2 unrelated cars. The 2017–2018 Yaris hatchback was Toyota's own design, built in France, with a 5-speed manual or a 4-speed automatic. Alongside it, from 2017, Toyota sold the Yaris iA, a sedan designed and built by Mazda in Mexico and closely related to the Mazda2. For 2019 the iA became simply the Yaris Sedan, the Toyota-built hatchback was dropped, and for 2020 a Mazda-built Yaris Hatchback replaced it. Toyota ended US sales of the Yaris after 2020.

Every one of them has a 106-hp 1.5-liter 4-cylinder, but that's where the similarity ends. Parts, service and the way they drive all depend on which car it really is. This guide has 3 chapters: the Toyota-built hatchback, the Mazda-built cars, and the checklist to use before you buy.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2019–2020 Yaris Sedan or a 2020 Yaris Hatchback, with 6 gears, up to 40 mpg on the highway and low-speed automatic emergency braking.
- **Most fun:** a 2017–2018 Yaris iA or a 2019–2020 sedan with the 6-speed manual.
- **Most practical:** a 5-door hatchback, either the 2017–2018 Toyota-built one or the 2020 Mazda-built one.
- **Cheapest way in:** a documented 2017–2018 Yaris iA.
- **Think twice if** adults ride in the back often, or you drive long distances on the freeway. A [Corolla](/fitdrive/guides/toyota-corolla/) is roomier and quieter.

| Designed and built by | Toyota, in France | Mazda, in Mexico | Mazda, in Mexico |
| Body | 3-door or 5-door hatchback | 4-door sedan | 5-door hatchback |
| Engine | 1.5-liter 4-cylinder, 106 hp | 1.5-liter 4-cylinder, 106 hp | 1.5-liter 4-cylinder, 106 hp |
