The RAV4 is the default answer to "what should I buy?" for millions of American households, and Toyota has made it the best-selling vehicle in the country that isn't a pickup. That popularity cuts both ways for a shopper. There are plenty to choose from, but there are also a lot of versions to sort through: a 4-cylinder gas model, 2 generations of hybrid, the 302-hp RAV4 Prime and, from 2026, a RAV4 that comes only as a hybrid or a plug-in.

Between 2017 and 2026 the story splits 3 ways. The 2017–2018 RAV4 is the last of the fourth generation, the one with the softer nose and the 6-speed automatic. The 2019–2025 RAV4 is the boxy fifth generation, sold as a gas model, a hybrid and, from 2021, a plug-in hybrid called the Prime. And the 2026 RAV4 is the sixth generation, which drops the gas-only version altogether. This guide has 6 chapters. Start with the one you're shopping, or go straight to the checklist if you're about to look at a car.

## Quick picks

- **Best value:** a documented 2019–2022 RAV4 Hybrid. It's quicker than the gas model, uses far less fuel and has all-wheel drive as standard.
- **Cheapest way in:** a 2017–2018 RAV4 with service records. Toyota Safety Sense P is standard, so even the base LE has automatic emergency braking.
- **Fastest and thriftiest:** a 2021–2025 RAV4 Prime or Plug-in Hybrid, if you can charge at home. It runs up to 42 miles on electricity before the engine starts.
- **Light off-roading:** a 2020–2025 TRD Off-Road or a 2023–2025 Hybrid Woodland Edition.
- **Newest:** a 2026 Hybrid LE or XLE Premium. It's rated at up to 43 mpg combined with front-wheel drive.
- **Think twice if** you tow anything heavy. Most RAV4s are rated to tow 794 kg (1,750 lb) or less, and even the strongest versions stop at 1,588 kg (3,500 lb).
