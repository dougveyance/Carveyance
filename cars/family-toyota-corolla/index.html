The Toyota Corolla family

14 generations and variants in the Index, 1968–present. Oldest first; every card opens the full page, with its own versions, figures and story.

  1. 1968–1970

    Toyota Corolla (E10)

    The first Corolla, sold in America from 1968 to 1970: a small, simple rear-wheel-drive car, launched in Japan in 1966, that started the best-selling nameplate in history.

    60 hp17.0 sec 0-601,000,000 built
  2. 1971–1974

    Toyota Corolla (E20)

    The second Corolla, 1971–1974: bigger and curvier, with an optional 1.6-liter and, from 1972, 5-speed SR-5 coupes.

    102 hp11.4 sec 0-602,410,000 built
  3. 1975–1979

    Toyota Corolla (E30)

    The third Corolla, 1975–1979: the generation behind Toyota's biggest growth in America after the fuel crisis, with SR5 coupes and liftbacks.

    75 hp13.9 sec 0-601,067,695 built
  4. 1980–1983

    Toyota Corolla (E70)

    The fourth Corolla, 1980–1983: the last all-rear-wheel-drive Corolla, with a 1.8-liter engine and some of the last true pillarless hardtops sold in America.

    75 hp13.4 sec 0-603,330,000 built
  5. 1984–1987

    Toyota Corolla (E80)

    The fifth Corolla, 1984–1987: front-wheel drive at last for the sedans, with a rear-drive GT-S coupe whose twin-cam AE86 later became a drifting legend.

    112 hp9.8 sec 0-602,810,000 built
    Twin-cam 1.6-liter · 1.6-liter
  6. 1988–1992

    Toyota Corolla (E90)

    The sixth Corolla, 1988–1992: rounder and sleeker, built in California and Ontario, with an All-Trac 4-wheel-drive wagon and a sporty GT-S coupe.

    102 hp11.3 sec 0-601,040,064 built
  7. 1993–1997

    Toyota Corolla (E100)

    The seventh Corolla, 1993–1997: a bigger, more refined sedan and wagon with a 1.8-liter, and no coupe.

    115 hp10.3 sec 0-601,045,824 built
  8. 1998–2002

    Toyota Corolla (E110)

    The eighth Corolla, 1998–2002: sedan only in America, built in North America, with a new all-aluminum 1.8-liter.

    120 hp10.3 sec 0-601,229,168 built
  9. 2003–2008

    Toyota Corolla (E120)

    The ninth Corolla, 2003–2008: bigger than the Japanese version, with a 170-hp XRS, and the first Corolla to sell more than 300,000 a year in America.

    130 hp8.2 sec 0-602,109,713 built
    1.8-liter · XRS
  10. 2009–2013

    Toyota Corolla (E140)

    The tenth Corolla, 2009–2013: a sedan styled like a small Camry, with electric power steering and a 158-hp 2.4-liter XRS.

    132 hp8.6 sec 0-601,396,342 built
    1.8-liter · XRS 2.4-liter
  11. 2014–2019

    Toyota Corolla (E170)

    The 11th-generation Corolla sedan: roomy, frugal and famously slow, with a 132-hp 1.8-liter. From 2017, every one came with automatic emergency braking and radar cruise control as standard.

    132 hp9.5 sec 0-60111 mph top1,701,887 built
  12. 2016–2018

    Toyota Corolla iM

    A well-equipped, well-made hatchback that started life as the Scion iM. It had a more sophisticated independent rear suspension than the Corolla sedan, but its 137-hp engine kept it slow.

    137 hp9.1 sec 0-60112 mph top62,796 built
  13. 2019–present

    Toyota Corolla (E210)

    The 12th-generation Corolla: a sharper, better-made car in sedan, hatchback and hybrid form. The 2.0-liter is the engine to have, standard on every gas sedan from 2023, the year the hybrid added all-wheel drive.

    168 hp8.3 sec 0-601,944,508 built
    2.0-liter · 1.8-liter · Hybrid
  14. 2023–present

    Toyota GR Corolla

    A 300-hp, all-wheel-drive, turbocharged 3-cylinder hot hatch, built in a dedicated GR Factory and launched with a manual only. The Corolla for people who'd rather be on a rally stage.

    300 hp5.0 sec 0-60142.9 mph top5,567 built
