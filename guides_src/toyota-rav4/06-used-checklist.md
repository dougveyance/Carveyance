The RAV4 is so popular that used ones sell quickly and rarely sell cheap. That makes it tempting to skip the homework. Don't. A RAV4 can be crashed, flooded, neglected or driven hard like any other car, and the hybrids and plug-ins have a few specific things to ask about. Reliability is a reason to buy one, not a reason to skip the inspection.

## Pick the job first

For cheap, simple transportation, start with a 2017–2018 RAV4 or a 2019–2025 gas LE or XLE. If you drive a lot, especially in town, a 2019–2025 RAV4 Hybrid is the best value in this guide. If you can charge at home, a 2021–2025 Prime or Plug-in Hybrid will run most of your errands on electricity. If you tow a small trailer or use dirt roads, look at a 2019–2025 Adventure or TRD Off-Road, or a 2026 with all-wheel drive. If your budget reaches a new one, the 2026 Hybrid LE is the efficiency champion.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2017–2018 | End of the fourth generation. 176-hp gas engine or 194-hp hybrid, standard Toyota Safety Sense P | No Apple CarPlay or Android Auto. 12-volt battery hold-down recall (23V-734) |
| 2019 | Fifth generation. 203-hp engine and 8-speed automatic, 219-hp hybrid with standard AWD | Roof rail leak program (22TE05). Rear motor cable program on hybrids (22TE09, 25TD01). Older 8-year, 100,000-mile hybrid battery warranty |
| 2020–2022 | Adds the TRD Off-Road and Android Auto (2020) and the Prime (2021) | Roof rail leaks (through 2021). Passenger seat sensor recall (2020–2021). Rear motor cable program on hybrids and Primes. Software recall on 2021 Primes |
| 2023–2025 | New multimedia and digital gauges, Hybrid Woodland Edition. The Prime becomes the Plug-in Hybrid for 2025 | Instrument panel and rear camera software recalls (25V-595, 25V-744). Cable seal campaign on 2023–2024 hybrids and Primes |
| 2026 | Sixth generation, hybrid or plug-in only, front-wheel drive hybrids, GR Sport | Plug-in Hybrid software recall (26V-659). Too new for long-term data |

## Body and history

Run a vehicle history report, but don't treat a clean one as proof. Look for uneven panel gaps, paint that doesn't quite match, overspray on rubber seals and replacement headlights on one side only. Lift the cargo floor and check the spare tire well for water, rust or a dirt line, which can point to flood damage. If the car came from a state that salts its roads, look underneath at the subframes, brake lines and rocker panels.

On a 2019–2021 RAV4, check the headliner near the roof rails and the carpet in the front footwells for water stains, which can be a sign of the roof rail clip leak. Plenty of RAV4s spend their lives in rental and rideshare service. That isn't automatically bad, but it means hard miles. Compare the wear on the driver's seat, steering wheel and rear seats with the odometer.

## Engine, transmission and hybrid system

Start the car cold if you can. On a gas RAV4, listen for ticking or knocking, watch for smoke and make sure the automatic shifts smoothly, without flares or harsh engagement. On a hybrid or plug-in, make sure the car starts in Ready mode quietly, that the engine starts and stops smoothly as you drive and that no warning lights stay on. Ask a shop that knows Toyota hybrids to scan for stored codes and check the battery's health, and check that the battery cooling intake isn't blocked.

On every 2019–2024 hybrid and Prime with all-wheel drive, ask about the rear motor cable. Corrosion at its connector can cause AM radio static or a no-start. Toyota covers the repair under program 22TE09 and offers a free inspection and protector seal under campaign 25TD01, which extends coverage on the cable to 10 years with no mileage limit once the seal is fitted. Get the dealer to confirm what's been done, by VIN.

On a Prime or Plug-in Hybrid, charge it before the test drive and check the electric range shown on the dash. Make sure the charging cable is included and works.

## Electronics and safety tech

Test everything with a screen or a button. Pair your phone and try Apple CarPlay or Android Auto, remembering that 2017–2018 cars have neither and 2019 cars have CarPlay only. Check the backup camera, the radar cruise control, the lane departure warning and the blind spot monitor, if the car has one. On a 2023–2025 car, make sure the instrument panel shows the speed and warning lights every time you start it, and that the rear camera image doesn't freeze, since both are subjects of software recalls.

## Recalls and programs: check by VIN

- **2013–2018:** 12-volt battery hold-down clamp, battery tray and terminal cover replacement, because a smaller replacement battery can move and short-circuit (23V-734).
- **2019–2021:** Roof rail clip seal leaks, covered by customer support program 22TE05.
- **2019–2024 AWD hybrids and Primes:** Rear motor cable corrosion, covered by program 22TE09 and campaign 25TD01, which runs until September 30, 2028.
- **2020–2021:** Front passenger seat sensor that can stop the passenger airbag from deploying (23V-865).
- **2021 Prime:** Hybrid system software that could shut the system down after cold-weather EV driving.
- **2023–2025:** Instrument panel software (25V-595) and, on cars with the Panoramic View Monitor, rear camera software (25V-744).
- **2026 Plug-in Hybrid:** Motor generator control software that can cut power at higher speeds (26V-659).
- **Every year:** New recalls can appear at any time. Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask a Toyota dealer to check for open service campaigns too, since those don't always show up as recalls. Ask for proof that each repair was done.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly and hide the differences that matter. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, grade, powertrain, mileage and title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you, comparing a listing with similar ones nearby. Expect hybrids and plug-ins to carry a premium, and don't assume the premium is justified by the car's condition. A pre-purchase inspection is worth more than any seller's speech about Toyota reliability.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the car past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best value:** a documented 2020–2022 RAV4 Hybrid LE or XLE with the cable campaign done.
- **Cheapest good one:** a 2017–2018 RAV4 or RAV4 Hybrid with records and the battery hold-down recall done.
- **Electric commuter:** a 2021–2025 Prime or Plug-in Hybrid, if you can charge at home.
- **Light trail and towing duty:** a 2020–2025 TRD Off-Road or Adventure, or a 2026 Woodland.
- **Newest:** a 2026 Hybrid LE or XLE Premium.
