Between 2017 and 2026, Toyota sold 2 Land Cruisers in America, with a gap in the middle. The 200 Series, sold through 2021, is a big luxury 4x4 with a 5.7-liter V8, seats for 8 and a starting price of about $85,000, roughly $106,000 to $116,000 in 2026 dollars. Toyota then stopped selling the Land Cruiser here for 2022 and 2023. When the name came back for 2024, it was on the 250 Series: a smaller, boxier 5-seater with a turbocharged 4-cylinder hybrid and a starting price of $55,950, about $60,000 in 2026 dollars.

They aren't consecutive versions of the same truck, so the first question isn't which year but which Land Cruiser. This guide has 5 chapters. Start with the one you're shopping, read the grades chapter if you're looking at a 2024 or newer, or go straight to the checklist if you're about to see one.

## Quick picks

- **Most space and towing:** a documented 2019–2021 200 Series. It seats 8 and tows 3,674 kg (8,100 lb).
- **Best value:** a 2024–2026 Land Cruiser 1958 with its recalls done.
- **Best all-rounder:** a 2024–2026 Land Cruiser grade, which adds the disconnecting front anti-roll bar and a much nicer cabin.
- **Lowest running costs:** any 250 Series, at 23 mpg combined against the V8's 14 or 15.
- **Think twice if** you need a third row. The 250 Series doesn't have one in the US. Look at a [Sequoia](/fitdrive/guides/toyota-sequoia/) or a 200 Series instead.
