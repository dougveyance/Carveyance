Begin with the window sticker or Ford's build record, not the badge. Confirm whether the car has the standard-range or extended-range battery, rear-wheel or all-wheel drive, its wheel size and its packages. The difference between a standard-range rear-drive Select and an extended-range all-wheel-drive Premium matters far more than the difference between adjacent years. Compare each car with its original EPA rating, not the range shown on the dashboard; that number is a forecast based on recent driving and temperatures, not a measure of the battery's health.

## Battery and charging

Ask a Ford dealer or an EV specialist for a battery health report, and compare it with the car's age, mileage and climate. Ask how it was charged; frequent fast charging and high annual mileage aren't disqualifying, but they deserve context. Ask the seller to show the car charging on a 240-volt charger, and if possible start a DC fast-charging session too. A connector that plugs in isn't proof that a session will start and keep going.

If you'll rely on public fast charging, check which networks the car can use on your regular routes, and whether it has the NACS adapter for Tesla Superchargers. On a 2026, the adapter is an option, not standard.

## Underneath and on the road

On a lift, look at the battery's protective underbody and the cooling lines for impact damage. Damage there needs a specialist's assessment, not a casual discount. On the drive, test full steering lock in a parking lot, listen for suspension knocks and any whine or clunk from the rear drive unit, and use rough pavement to judge the ride on the wheels fitted. Check the climate system's output, the cameras, the driver-assistance warnings, the phone connection, both keys and the front-trunk release.

If BlueCruise matters to you, confirm the car has the hardware and check whether the subscription is active; the hardware alone doesn't guarantee you can use it.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2021–2022:** 22V412, battery main contactors that can overheat; 48,924 cars, fixed with a software update. 23V687, Ford's 23S56, the same problem on 34,762 extended-range cars, fixed by replacing the high-voltage battery junction box; 25V441 added 239 more 2022s. 25V130 covers cars repaired incorrectly under 22V412.
- **2021–2025:** 25V404, electronic door latches that may stay locked after the front occupants get out when the 12-volt battery is low, which could trap rear passengers; 197,432 cars, fixed with software.
- **2021–2023:** 26V417, a rear differential pinion shaft that can fracture; 42,784 cars. The remedy isn't available yet; Ford expects it in late December 2026.
- **2024–2026:** 25V863, an integrated park module that may not lock in Park; 272,645 vehicles including F-150 Lightnings and Mavericks, fixed with software over the air or at a dealer.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and with Ford, and ask for proof each repair was done. A recall for a model year doesn't mean every car is affected, and an empty list online is never proof a VIN is clear.

## Documents to ask for

Ask for the window sticker or build record, the service history, recall and software-update records, the battery health report, both keys, the mobile charging cord and the NACS adapter if it came with one. Check the title for salvage, flood and theft history, and ask Ford whether any remaining battery warranty transfers to you.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title or flood damage, a battery underbody that's been hit, repeated charging faults nobody can explain, an open junction-box or contactor recall on a 2021–2022 extended-range car, or a seller who won't let you see it charge.

## What is it worth?

[[chart:price]]

We don't publish average prices. Used EV prices move quickly, and an average that mixes a 2021 standard-range Select with a 2025 Rally tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, battery, drive and trim with similar mileage, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Include the cost of a home charger, tires and insurance; a $2,000 saving can disappear quickly on a car that needs 4 performance tires.
