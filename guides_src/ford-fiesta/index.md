The Fiesta's American story is 2 short visits separated by 30 years. The German-built original of 1978–1980 weighed 832 kg (1,835 lb), handled like a toy and was killed by fuel-economy accounting rules. The Mexican-built Mk6 of 2011–2019 brought the badge back as the best-driving subcompact in America, peaked at 71,073 sales in 2013, gave the world the beloved Fiesta ST — and left when Ford quit cars entirely.

For a buyer the market splits 3 ways: ordinary Mk6 Fiestas are some of the cheapest running cars in the country, with one transmission rule deciding which to touch; the Fiesta ST is a modern classic you can still use daily; and the Mk1 is a genuine collectible for almost no money, if you can find one.

## Quick picks

- **The rule first:** on 2011–2019 cars, buy the manual. The PowerShift dual-clutch generated class actions covering about 1.5 million cars and a $30 million settlement — about $39 million in 2026 dollars. Stick-shift cars were never affected.
- **Best cheap car, full stop:** a 2014–2019 SE hatch, 5-speed manual — the class-best chassis for $5,000-ish money.
- **The legend:** a Fiesta ST. 197 hp on overboost, manual only, and steering that embarrassed cars at 3 times the price. Clean ones have stopped getting cheaper.
- **The economy sleeper:** a 1.0 EcoBoost SFE manual — 35 mpg combined, the thriftiest gas Ford of its day.
- **The little classic:** a 1978–1980 Mk1 survivor. Rare, slow-ish, joyful, and still priced like a curiosity rather than a collectible.
