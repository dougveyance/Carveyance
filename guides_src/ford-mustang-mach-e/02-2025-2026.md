For 2025 Ford gave the Mach-E its most useful set of changes so far, most of them under the skin. Every 2025 has a vapor-injection heat pump, which helps efficiency in cold weather. The rotary gear selector on the console gave way to a stalk behind the steering wheel. Premium models got ventilated front seats, new low-rolling-resistance tires and retuned suspension, as did the Select. Ford also redesigned the front trunk, though the heat pump left it smaller than before.

## What changed for 2025

- **Heat pump:** a vapor-injection heat pump on every model.
- **Shifter:** a stalk-mounted shifter in place of the rotary dial.
- **Comfort:** ventilated front seats on the Premium.
- **Chassis:** revised suspension tuning on the Select and Premium, and low-rolling-resistance tires on the Premium.
- **Driver assistance:** automatic emergency braking gains oncoming-vehicle detection, lane keeping gains road-edge detection, and BlueCruise adds automatic lane changes.
- **Charging:** the mobile power cord comes with an adapter for NACS fast chargers, including Tesla Superchargers.
- **Options:** a Sport Appearance Package for the Premium, with the GT's front fascia.

## What changed for 2026

The 2026 lineup starts at $37,795 for the Select, and some features that used to be standard moved into options. The front trunk is now an option. The 360-degree camera, front parking sensors and reverse brake assist on the Select and Premium, and the power liftgate, wireless charging pad and garage-door opener on the higher trims, moved into the Technology Package. The NACS adapter became an option rather than part of the mobile cord. On the GT there's a new GT Performance and Handling Package and a California Special Package.

When you shop a 2026, check the window sticker for the Technology Package; 2 cars with the same trim name can be equipped quite differently.

## How far they go

[[chart:range26]]

The EPA rates the 2026 extended-range rear-drive car at 320 miles, the best of any Mach-E, and the extended-range all-wheel-drive car at 300. Standard-range cars are rated at 260 miles with rear-wheel drive and 240 with all-wheel drive.

## How efficient they are

[[chart:eff26]]

Efficiency explains part of the range difference. The EPA rates the extended-range rear-drive car at 110 MPGe combined, against 90 for the GT and 84 for the Rally. Car and Driver ran a 2026 standard-range rear-drive Select, rated at 264 hp, to 60 mph in 5.6 seconds.

## What's coming for 2027

For 2027 Ford is adding the Mach-E Trail, a package for the Select with all-wheel drive. It rides 10 mm (0.4 inch) higher at the front and 15 mm (0.6 inch) higher at the rear, and it borrows the Rally's steering rack, 19-inch wheels and Michelin CrossClimate 2 tires. A 400-watt Pro Power Onboard outlet is standard.

## Which one to buy

A 2025 or 2026 is the Mach-E to buy if you live somewhere cold, because of the heat pump. For range, choose the extended-range battery with rear-wheel drive. For the lowest price, look at a 2026 Select, but price the options you want before assuming it's the bargain.
