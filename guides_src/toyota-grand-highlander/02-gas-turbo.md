Toyota expected the gas model to make up about 70% of Grand Highlander sales, and it's easy to see why. It's the cheapest way into the biggest Toyota crossover, it tows 2,268 kg (5,000 lb), and it's quick enough. It also uses the same engine as the 2023–2026 Highlander, so it's a well-known quantity.

## What you get

The gas Grand Highlander uses a 2.4-liter turbocharged 4-cylinder with 265 hp at 6,000 rpm and 310 lb-ft of torque from 1,700 to 3,600 rpm. It drives through an 8-speed automatic, and you can have front-wheel drive or all-wheel drive. Toyota quoted 0 to 60 mph in 7.5 seconds for a front-drive model. Car and Driver ran an XLE with all-wheel drive to 60 mph in 7.0 seconds and through the quarter mile in 15.3 seconds.

It's offered in every grade, from the LE added for 2025 through the XLE and Limited to the Platinum. For 2026, the gas Grand Highlander starts at $41,360 for an LE with front-wheel drive, before Toyota's destination charge. The fuel tank holds 17.8 gallons.

The EPA rates the front-drive gas model at 24 mpg combined. With all-wheel drive, the LE and XLE are rated at 23, and the heavier Limited and Platinum at 22. That's well behind the Hybrid's 34 to 36 mpg, and the gap in fuel costs adds up quickly, as chart 4 in the next chapter shows. But the gas model tows 2,268 kg (5,000 lb), against 1,588 kg (3,500 lb) for the Hybrid.

## How it drives

The turbo has plenty of low-speed pull, so the Grand Highlander feels stronger in town than its horsepower suggests. It gets noisier when you work it hard, though Car and Driver noted it settles down to a quiet 67 decibels at a steady 70 mph. The 8-speed automatic shifts smoothly and keeps the revs low on the highway. In its test, Car and Driver's XLE stopped from 70 mph in 184 feet, which it called longish, and it blamed the all-season tires more than the chassis.

## What to check

The gas Grand Highlander shares its recalls with the hybrids, and they're listed in full in the checklist chapter. The most important are the 2024 curtain airbag recall, which covers most 2024 models, and the 2026 rear axle carrier and steering knuckle recalls on cars built in spring and early summer 2026. Run the VIN on [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) before you buy.

## Which one to buy

- **LE (2025–2026):** the cheapest Grand Highlander, with the same space as every other version.
- **XLE:** the sweet spot, with more equipment for a modest step up.
- **Limited:** a longer list of features and the most comfort short of the Platinum.
- **Platinum:** fully loaded, but at that price, look hard at the Hybrid MAX too.
- **AWD:** worth it in snow, at a cost of about 1 mpg.

## The verdict

The gas Grand Highlander is the practical choice: plenty of space, plenty of towing and the lowest price. If you tow more than 1,588 kg (3,500 lb), it's the obvious pick. If you don't, the Hybrid will save you a lot of fuel for a modest premium.
