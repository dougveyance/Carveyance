After a year off, the Ridgeline returned in June 2016 as a 2017 model, rebuilt on the same platform as the Pilot, with 73% of its parts shared. That gives it a unibody instead of a separate frame, a transverse V6 and independent rear suspension, which is why it rides more smoothly and steers more accurately than other midsize pickups. It won North American Truck of the Year for 2017. It also looks more like a conventional pickup than the first Ridgeline did, though not much more.

## The engine and drivetrain

The 3.5-liter V6 makes 280 hp and 262 lb-ft. From 2017 to 2019 it uses a 6-speed automatic; for 2020, a 9-speed replaced it. Front-wheel drive was standard on the lower trims, and all-wheel drive with i-VTM4, which can vary the torque to each rear wheel, was available on every trim and standard on the RTL-E and Black Edition. Car and Driver ran a 2017 RTL-E to 60 mph in 6.4 seconds, quick for a pickup. The EPA rated it at 22 mpg combined with front-wheel drive and 21 with all-wheel drive.

## The bed

The bed is 64 inches long, about 5 feet wide and dent- and scratch-resistant without a liner. Under the rear of the floor is the In-Bed Trunk, a lockable, weather-sealed 7.3-cubic-foot compartment with a drain plug, so it doubles as a cooler. The Dual-Action Tailgate folds down like any other, or swings open from the side to reach the trunk without leaning over. Higher trims added the world's first truck-bed audio system, which plays music in the bed, and a power outlet there too. Maximum payload is 718 kg (1,584 lb), and an all-wheel-drive Ridgeline can tow 2,268 kg (5,000 lb).

## The trims

For 2017, there were 7: RT, RTS, Sport, RTL, RTL-T, RTL-E and Black Edition. Prices started at $29,475 for a front-drive RT ($40,400 in 2026 dollars) and ran to $42,970 for the Black Edition ($59,000). Apple CarPlay and Android Auto came with the RTL-T and above. Honda trimmed the range for 2020, when the 9-speed arrived.

## What to check before you buy

The recall unique to this truck is 19V053, which covers 2017–2019 Ridgelines: acidic car-wash soaps can crack the fuel pump's feed port and let fuel leak. Dealers replace the pump if needed and fit a cover. Tailgate use can break the rearview camera wiring on 2017–2019 trucks (22V867), and on 2020 trucks the harness was recalled again (24V321). The hood latch striker on 2017–2020 trucks can separate (21V932), some 2017 and 2019 engines are covered by the connecting rod bearing recall (23V751), and 2017–2023 trucks sold in salt-belt states are getting rear subframe reinforcements (26V365). The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A 2019 or 2020 RTL with all-wheel drive is the sweet spot, if its recalls are done. Front-wheel drive saves money and a little fuel, and it's fine for most driving in dry climates, but all-wheel drive is the one to have for snow, boat ramps or wet grass, and it's required for the full 2,268-kg (5,000-lb) rating. The RTL-E and Black Edition add the truck-bed audio and more luxury, but not more capability.

::: curiosity The Tailgate Speakers
Honda called the 2017 Ridgeline's truck-bed audio system the world's first. Paired with the In-Bed Trunk as a cooler and a power outlet in the bed, it made the Ridgeline the rare pickup designed as much for tailgate parties as for work.
:::
