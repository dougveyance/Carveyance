No vehicle in this library has a longer American service record. The Econoline arrived in 1961 as a flat-nosed Falcon with the engine between the seats, grew a truck frame in 1975, took the full-size-van sales crown in 1980 and kept it every single year until Ford retired the van in June 2014 — then refused to die, soldiering on as the cutaway chassis under the nation's box trucks, ambulances, shuttle buses and RVs. More than 8.2 million in total, making it the third-best-selling van line in history.

For buyers it is really 4 different machines: a genuine 1960s collectible, a 1970s–80s nostalgia wave that's just starting, the cheapest serious work van in America, and a brand-new chassis you can still order with a 7.3-liter V8. All of them share one virtue — there is nothing on them a local mechanic hasn't fixed a hundred times.

## Quick picks

- **Best used work van:** a 2009–2014 E-250 or E-350 with the 5.4 Triton — the final, most sorted years, built like anvils and priced like furniture.
- **The legend for fleets:** a 7.3 Power Stroke diesel (1995–2003). Ambulance companies still fight over them; expect to pay for the privilege.
- **The collectible:** a 1961–1967 flat-nose, pickup above all — the cab-over truck is the family's blue-chip piece.
- **The nostalgia play:** a clean 1975–1991 Club Wagon Chateau, two-tone if possible. Nothing is more 1980s America, and prices are still kind.
- **The people-mover caution:** 15-passenger wagons are cheap and useful — load them evenly, mind the tires, and skip roof cargo; their rollover record earned the warnings.
