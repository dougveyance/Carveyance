The Si has always been the Civic for people who like to drive but don't want, or can't afford, a Type R. It doesn't promise to be the quickest car at the light. It promises good steering, a slick manual gearbox and a chassis that's more fun on a back road than its power suggests, in a car that still commutes like a Civic.

## 2017–2020: sedan or coupe

The tenth-generation Si arrived for 2017 as a sedan or a coupe, at $23,900 for either ($32,800 in 2026 dollars). It uses a turbocharged 1.5-liter with 205 hp and 192 lb-ft, a 6-speed manual, which is the only transmission, a limited-slip differential and adaptive dampers. Summer tires were a $200 option ($270 in 2026 dollars). The EPA rated it at 32 mpg combined. Edmunds ran a 2017 Si sedan from 0 to 60 mph in 6.8 seconds. Honda Sensing wasn't offered at first; by 2020 it was standard on every Civic grade, the Si included. The coupe ended with the 2020 model year, and there was no Si at all for 2021.

## 2022–2026: sedan only

The Si returned for 2022 as a sedan, on the new eleventh-generation body. Its 1.5-liter turbo makes 200 hp and the same 192 lb-ft, and it keeps the manual and the limited-slip differential. It adds rev-matching, which blips the throttle for you on downshifts and can be switched off. It loses the adaptive dampers. Car and Driver ran a 2022 Si from 0 to 60 mph in 6.8 seconds, 0.2 second slower than the car before it, though the 2 matched each other from a rolling start. It started at $27,300 for 2022 ($31,400 in 2026 dollars), and Honda asked $31,495 for a 2026 Si.

The awkward fact is that the 2025 Civic Hybrid is quicker in a straight line: Car and Driver's hybrid reached 60 mph 0.6 second sooner than the last Si it tested. The Si still has the manual, the sharper steering and the limited-slip differential, which is why its fans buy it.

## What to check before you buy

An Si is more likely than a regular Civic to have been modified or driven hard. Ask whether the engine software has been tuned and whether the intake, exhaust, turbo plumbing or suspension have been changed, and ask for the parts that came off. Check the clutch: it should engage smoothly and not slip in a high gear at low revs. Shift quickly from second to third and listen for grinding. Look for uneven tire wear, curb-damaged wheels and signs of track use, such as worn brake pads and blued rotors. On a 2017–2018 car, also check the oil level, and ask a Honda dealer whether the 1.5-liter turbo's oil dilution warranty extension covers the VIN. The 2022 and later Si is in the steering gearbox recall (24V744). The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A stock, documented Si is one of the best-value enthusiast cars of its time. The 2017–2020 car, sedan or coupe, is the slightly quicker one and the only Si with adaptive dampers. The 2022 and later sedan has the nicer cabin and rev-matching. Either way, pay for condition and records, not for modifications. If you want more speed than this, [the Type R](../type-r/) is a different class of car.

::: curiosity A Year Off for Each
Neither of the Civic's performance models was sold in every year of this guide. There was no Si for 2021, as Honda switched generations, and no Type R for 2022.
:::
