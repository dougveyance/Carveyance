Unveiled at the 2012 Los Angeles show and on sale in early 2013 as a 2014 model, the US Fiesta ST was the car the press couldn't put down: a 1.6 EcoBoost making 197 hp on overboost, a 6-speed manual as the only transmission, torque vectoring, 3 stability-control modes and a chassis tuned by people who plainly commuted on back roads. Ford quoted under 7 seconds to 60 and a limited 137 mph, and every comparison test of the decade ended the same way — the cheapest car won.

## What to pay and what to check

STs ran roughly $21,000–$26,000 new and bottomed out years ago; clean, stock, adult-owned cars now hold their money and the best ones inch upward. The checks are few and specific. **The 2014–15 recall** for engine-fire risk from coolant circulation must show complete on the VIN report. **Oil history** is non-negotiable on the little turbo. **Clutch and mounts** take the abuse first — judder on takeup is your negotiating lever. And the optional **Recaro seats** are brilliant or unbearable depending on your build; sit in them for 10 minutes before paying the package premium. Tunes are everywhere; stock is worth more.

## Why it holds up

The ST formula — light car, honest gearbox, fast steering — stopped being offered in America when this car left in 2019. Nothing replaced it at the price, which is why a used economy car has collectors. Buy the best unmodified one you can find, service the turbo on time, and enjoy owning the answer to "what's the most fun per dollar on 4 wheels?" for as long as the question exists.
