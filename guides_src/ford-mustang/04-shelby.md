The sixth-generation Mustang had 2 Shelbys, and they're less alike than their badges suggest. The Shelby GT350, sold from 2015 to 2020, was built around a naturally aspirated 5.2-liter V8 with a flat-plane crankshaft, Ford's Voodoo engine. It makes 526 hp and came only with a 6-speed manual. The Shelby GT500, sold from 2020 to 2022, went the other way: a supercharged 5.2-liter V8 with 760 hp and 625 lb-ft, sent through a 7-speed dual-clutch automatic. There was no manual.

## How they compare

| Years | 2015–2020 | 2015–2020 | 2020–2022 |
| Engine | 5.2-liter flat-plane-crank V8 | 5.2-liter flat-plane-crank V8 | 5.2-liter supercharged V8 |

Prices are before delivery and, on the GT500, before the gas-guzzler tax. For 2020, Ford's sticker for the GT500 was $73,995 including a $1,095 destination charge and a $2,600 gas-guzzler tax.

## How quick they are

[[chart:accel-shelby]]

Car and Driver ran a 2016 GT350 to 60 mph in 4.3 seconds and a GT350R in 3.9. The 2020 GT500 with the Carbon Fiber Track Pack took 3.6 seconds, and 3.4 on Michelin Pilot Sport 4S tires. On a road course the gap narrows, because the GT350's appeal was always its steering, its brakes and an engine that loves to rev, not its straight-line pace.

## Which one is for you

The GT350 is the purist's car: a manual gearbox, an engine with a howl unlike any other Mustang, and a chassis that rewards a committed driver. It's also the one that needs the most careful history check, because a high-revving engine that's been tracked hard and serviced lazily is an expensive gamble. The GT500 is the fastest Mustang of its generation by a wide margin and easier to drive quickly, thanks to the dual-clutch gearbox, but it's heavier, thirstier and dearer to maintain.

## What to check

- **Engine history first.** Ask for every oil change with dates and mileage, and whether the car has been tracked. On a GT350, a specialist's compression or leak-down test is worth paying for.
- **Cooling and brakes.** Both cars were built to run hot laps. Look for signs of overheating, cooked brake fluid, glazed rotors and worn pads.
- **Tires and wheels.** Performance tires wear quickly and cost a lot. On a GT350R or a GT500 with the Carbon Fiber Track Pack, inspect the carbon-fiber wheels for chips and cracks.
- **Modifications.** Tunes, aftermarket exhausts and pulley changes affect value and warranty claims. Factory-original cars command the strongest prices.
- **Recalls.** Run the VIN. Shelbys can be covered by the same recalls as other Mustangs of their year, such as 23V727 on 2020–2023 cars, a brake fluid warning that may not light when the fluid is low.
