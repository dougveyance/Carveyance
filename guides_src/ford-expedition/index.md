The Expedition is what Ford built when America outgrew the 2-door Bronco: an F-150 with 4 doors, 3 rows and, at the 1999 peak, 233,125 buyers a year. The arc since is the whole big-SUV story in one model — down to 31,655 sales in 2009 when gas hit $4, back to 86,422 by 2019 on the strength of an aluminum-bodied reinvention, and now a 5th generation whose party trick is a tailgate that opens like a pickup's.

For a shopper the Expedition splits cleanly into 3 eras. The 2025–2026 trucks are the new ones, 400 or 440 hp with the Split Gate. The 2018–2024 generation is the used sweet spot — aluminum body, 10-speed, and the only full-size SUV of its day that could run 4.9 to 60. The 2007–2017 trucks are the budget 3-row play, especially the 2015–17 EcoBoost cars. The checklist at the end covers the specific things — spark plugs, cam phasers, recalls — an Expedition must prove before you pay.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder, new:** a 2026 Active, 400 hp, from $62,700. Same frame and engine family as the $84,360 King Ranch.
- **Best used value:** a 2018–2021 XLT — the aluminum body and 10-speed at half the new price, 19 mpg combined.
- **The quick one:** a 2022–2024 Limited with the Stealth Performance package — 440 hp, 4.9 seconds to 60 in Car and Driver's test.
- **The budget 3-row:** a 2015–2017 with the 3.5 EcoBoost — 365 hp and the newest-feeling truck of the 11-year 3rd generation.
- **Think twice if** 3 rows is the whole mission. An Explorer does the school run for $20,000 less, and the Expedition's size is real money in tires, fuel and parking.
