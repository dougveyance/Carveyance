In a Prius, the hybrid system exists to save fuel. In the Tundra, it exists to add torque. The i-FORCE MAX puts an electric motor inside the bell housing, between the twin-turbo V6 and the 10-speed automatic, and powers it from a nickel-metal hydride battery under the rear seat. Together they make 437 hp and 583 lb-ft, the most of any Tundra.

## Where you'll find it

The hybrid is standard on the TRD Pro and the Capstone, the luxury flagship Toyota added for 2022, and optional on the Limited, Platinum and 1794 Edition. It isn't offered on the SR or SR5.

## How it drives

The electric motor fills the gap before the turbos build boost, so the hybrid pulls hard from a standstill and keeps pulling with a trailer behind it. Car and Driver ran a 2022 TRD Pro to 60 mph in 5.7 seconds, 0.4 second quicker than a gas Limited it tested. The engine shuts off at a stop and restarts as you pull away, which takes a little getting used to in a truck this size.

The extra hardware adds weight. A 2022 TRD Pro weighs about 2,800 kg (6,172 lb), and the hybrid's grades are the heaviest Tundras in the range, so they usually carry lower payload and towing ratings than a lighter gas truck. A 2022 Capstone hybrid was rated to tow 4,690 kg (10,340 lb), against up to 5,443 kg (12,000 lb) for the best-equipped gas truck.

## The fuel math

[[chart:mpg]]

The EPA rates the hybrid at 20 mpg combined as a 4x4 and 22 as a 4x2, about 1 or 2 mpg better than the gas V6. The TRD Pro, with its off-road tires, is rated lower.

[[chart:fuelcost]]

At 15,000 miles a year and $4 a gallon, the hybrid 4x4 saves about $160 a year over the gas 4x4. That won't repay its price premium. The real gains are against the old V8, which uses about $1,100 to $1,300 more fuel a year than the new engines. Buy the hybrid for the way it pulls, or because you want a TRD Pro or Capstone.

## The engine recall and the hybrid

Toyota's 3 recalls for machining debris in the twin-turbo V6 list the gas Tundra, which NHTSA files as a separate model from the Tundra Hybrid. That doesn't make every hybrid immune to every problem, and it doesn't mean every gas truck is affected. Check any Tundra's VIN. The hybrid does share the camera, reverse light and transmission software recalls in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## What to check before you buy

Make sure the truck starts quietly in Ready mode, that the engine stops and restarts smoothly and that no hybrid warnings come on. Ask a shop that knows Toyota hybrids to scan for stored codes. Toyota covers the hybrid battery on its recent hybrid trucks for 10 years or 150,000 miles, so confirm in writing how much is left. On a TRD Pro, look underneath for trail damage around the skid plates and suspension.

## The verdict

The hybrid is the best Tundra to drive and the strongest truck Toyota sells. It isn't the best value. Choose it if you'll use the torque, or if the TRD Pro or Capstone is the truck you want. If you tow at the limit or need maximum payload, a lighter gas V6 may suit you better.
