For 2021, Toyota dropped the V6 and made every Sienna a hybrid. The new van rides on the TNGA-K platform, looks sleeker than the old one, and is rated at 36 mpg combined with front-wheel drive, about 70% better than the last front-drive V6. It went on sale in November 2020 and has been built, like the old one, in Princeton, Indiana.

## How it drives

A 2.5-liter 4-cylinder and 2 electric motors make 245 hp combined, through an electronically controlled CVT. All-wheel drive, available on every grade, adds a third motor for the rear wheels and is rated at 35 mpg. Car and Driver ran the hybrid from 0 to 60 mph in 7.7 seconds, nearly a second slower than the V6 it replaced, but noted that it feels strong at low speeds, where the electric motors do much of the work. Push harder, on a long climb or a fast merge, and the CVT sends the engine to higher, noisier revs.

The tow rating stays at up to 1,588 kg (3,500 lb), and the hybrid battery is covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles from the date the van was first sold.

## Living with it

The seating is the big change, and it matters. In the 7-seat versions, the second-row captain's chairs can slide a long way, about 25 inches, so third-row passengers and second-row passengers can trade legroom, and the Limited and Platinum add ottomans. But those chairs have side airbags built in, so you can't take them out; the old van's removable second row is gone. On the 8-seat LE and XLE, the small middle seat can come out, which helps people reach the third row. Edmunds ran a Sienna for a year and found that the fixed second row bothered its staff less than it expected. If you regularly haul big loads, measure your stuff before you buy.

[[chart:price]]

The 2021 range ran from the LE at $34,460 ($42,800 in 2026 dollars) to the Platinum at $49,900, with all-wheel drive adding $2,000 on the LE and XLE. Between them are the XLE, the sporty XSE and the Limited. For 2025, Toyota gave the cabin its biggest update: a newer multimedia system with an 8-inch screen on the LE and 12.3 inches on every other grade, a digital instrument cluster on the XSE and above, USB-C ports, wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, and a radar-based rear seat reminder that can detect a child or pet moving in the back after the van is locked. The Platinum gained a built-in vacuum and a small refrigerator called the FridgeBox, both available on the Limited. The 2025 LE started at $39,185.

## What to check before you buy

The fourth generation has had several seat recalls, so run the VIN and ask for proof of each repair. The third-row seatback bolts may not have been tightened properly on 2021–2025 vans (25V-086). The second-row seat rails may be welded improperly on 2025 vans (25V-668). And on 7-seat 2022–2026 vans, the second-row seatbacks can unlock in certain high-speed frontal crashes when the seat belt pretensioners fire (26V-690), with owner letters due from December 7, 2026. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

For most families, a 2021 or later Sienna is the better van: it uses about 40% less fuel than the V6, offers all-wheel drive on every grade and has newer safety technology. The XLE is the sweet spot, and a 2025 or later car is worth the extra if you want the bigger screen and the radar-based rear seat reminder. Make sure the seat recalls have been done, and test the fixed second row with your own car seats and cargo before you commit.
