Every other choice on a Lightning is trim. The battery is destiny. The standard-range pack holds 98 kWh and rates 240 miles; the extended-range pack holds 131 kWh and rates 320 miles in XLT and Lariat trim, 300 in the heavier, 22-inch-wheeled Platinum. The bigger battery also unlocks the bigger numbers everywhere else: 580 hp against 452, 4,536 kg (10,000 lb) of towing against 3,493 kg (7,700 lb), and the 9.6-kW version of Pro Power Onboard. On the used market the extended-range truck holds value better for exactly these reasons, and it is worth the stretch.

## Real range, honestly

EPA numbers assume no trailer, moderate speeds and mild weather. Owners report the extended-range truck delivering close to its rating in town, Edmunds beat the Platinum's 300-mile rating by 32 miles in its real-world loop, and meaningfully less at an 80-mph winter cruise. Towing is the hard truth: a heavy trailer can halve the range, turning a 320-mile truck into a 160-mile one with a charging stop that needs a pull-through stall. For commuting, job sites and regional hauls, the Lightning is effortless; for cross-country towing, a PowerBoost hybrid is the better F-150.

## Charging and the plug question

On a 150-kW DC fast charger, the extended-range pack goes from 15 to 80 percent in about 41 minutes. At home, the 80-amp Charge Station Pro that came with extended-range trucks charges overnight and unlocks the party trick: **Intelligent Backup Power**, which can run a typical house for 3 days in an outage, longer with rationing. Ask whether the seller's home hardware is included, it is a 4-figure value. All Lightnings shipped with the CCS port; Ford opened Tesla's Supercharger network to them via a NACS adapter in 2024, so confirm the adapter is in the glovebox, and budget for one if not.

## The efficiency math

The EPA rates the extended-range truck at 70 MPGe combined, the standard at 68 and the Platinum at 66. In dollars, home charging runs a fraction of gas money for the same miles, which is the quiet reason high-mile Lightnings exist: the people who drove them most saved the most. That history is readable in the truck itself, which is what the checklist chapter is for.
