Some Priuses have worked for a living as fleet or rideshare cars, and others have been long-distance commuters. That isn't a reason to avoid a high-mileage one, but it is a reason to judge each car by its records, its hybrid system and an inspection rather than by its odometer alone.

## First, know which one it is

Confirm the model and generation before you compare prices: a 2017–2022 Prius, a 2023 or later Prius, a Prius Prime or Prius Plug-in Hybrid, a Prius c or a Prius v. Each is a different car. Then confirm the drive: front-wheel drive, AWD-e (2019–2022) or AWD (2023 and later). The VIN and the badge on the tail will tell you, and the window sticker or a VIN decoder will confirm the grade.

## The test drive

Start the car from cold and watch the dashboard: the "Ready" light should come on with no warning messages. Drive in town and on the freeway. The engine will start and stop by itself, which is normal, and it should do so smoothly, without shudders or clunks. Brake gently from 30 mph to a stop several times; the change from regenerative braking to the friction brakes should feel seamless. Test the climate control on full heat and full cold, every window and lock, the cameras, the phone connection and, on a 2023 or later car, all 4 doors, opening each from inside and out.

## Hybrid checks

Have a Toyota shop, or a specialist who knows hybrids, scan the hybrid system for stored codes and check the battery's health. Check the battery warranty: 2020 and later Priuses are covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles from the date they were first sold, while 2017–2019 cars had 8 years or 100,000 miles in most states. Look at the hybrid battery's cooling intake by the rear seat. It can clog with lint and pet hair, and a car that has shown a message asking for maintenance of the hybrid battery cooling parts needs it cleaned. A weak 12-volt battery can also stop a Prius from starting, even with a healthy hybrid battery, so ask when it was last replaced.

## Plug-in checks

On a Prime or Plug-in Hybrid, make sure the charging cable comes with the car, and charge it during the inspection to confirm that it accepts a charge. Then drive it on electricity and compare the range with the EPA figure: 25 miles for a 2017–2022 car, and 39 to 44 miles for a 2023 or later car, less in cold weather.

## Recalls: check by VIN

- **2016–2017:** 16V-741. The parking brake cable can come loose, so the parked car can roll. The fix is a clip on the cable end.
- **2017:** 17V-295. Some cars, including the Prius c, had a spare tire inflated to the wrong pressure.
- **2016–2018:** 18V-579. The engine wire harness can wear against its cover and short, creating a fire risk.
- **2014–2017 Prius v:** 20V-369. Excessive voltage in the inverter can shut down the hybrid system and stall the car. The fix is a software update.
- **2019:** 19V-544. The brake booster pump can fail, switching off stability control and brake assist; it also covers 2019–2020 Primes. 19V-876: a short can blank the speedometer, odometer and fuel gauge. 19V-244: the load label can become unreadable.
- **2022:** 21V-734. ECU software can falsely detect an error and shut down the hybrid system, with a loss of drive power, on the Prius and Prime.
- **2023–2026:** 26V-049. Water can short a rear door switch, so an unlocked rear door may open unexpectedly. It expands and replaces 24V-274, and cars already repaired under 24V-274 need the new remedy.
- **2023–2025:** 25V-744. The rear camera image can freeze or go blank in reverse; the fix is a software update. 24V-548 (2023–2024): the load label can show the wrong figures.
- **Every year:** run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof that each repair was done.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title or signs of flood damage, which is especially serious on a hybrid. Walk away from hybrid system warning lights the seller can't explain, from a plug-in that won't charge, and from any car whose seller won't allow an inspection.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly, and an average that mixes a 2017 Prius Two with a 2023 Limited tells you very little. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same model, year, grade, drive and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Corolla Hybrid and other compact hybrids with similar miles, and take the cost of tires, brakes, a 12-volt battery and overdue service off the price. On an older car, also price a replacement hybrid battery.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the Prius past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for the hybrid system, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2023 or later Prius LE with its recalls done.
- **Best value:** a 2020–2022 Prius LE or XLE with records.
- **Best plug-in:** a 2023 or later Prime SE or Plug-in Hybrid SE, if you can charge at home.
- **Best in snow:** a 2020–2022 AWD-e, or a 2023 or later Prius AWD.
- **Most space:** a 2017 Prius v with its inverter recall done.
