Start with the one check that outranks all others on V6 cars: cooling-system history. The 3.5 and 3.7 Duratecs (and the related 2.7's coolant care) put the water pump inside the engine, driven by the timing chain — a failure that can take the engine with it if coolant mixes with oil. A seller with coolant-service receipts has told you the car was loved; a sweet smell, a low reservoir, or milky oil ends the visit. On EcoBoost cars the religion is oil: turbos forgive nothing, so stamped intervals beat low miles, every time.

Then the family-crossover standards. AWD cars want PTU fluid-change evidence (the little transfer unit runs hot and small); panoramic-roof drains should run clear water out behind the front wheels; wheel bearings drone their confession on the test drive; and the touchscreen era needs every function poked — MyFord Touch modules (2011–15) and early Sync screens cost more than their refinement deserves. On 2019-plus cars, verify recalls closed and the 8-speed's parking-lot manners. None of this is exotic; the Edge's mechanical population is huge and every shop knows it.

## The deal

Price bands run honest: $4,000–$8,000 buys a running early car, $10,000–$16,000 a tidy facelift or early gen-2, high teens to mid-$20,000s the 2019-plus sweet spot, and the ST holds the top of the range on enthusiasm. The discontinuation is your leverage — no dealer has a new Edge to anchor pricing, supply is deep, and the nameplate carries zero collector froth outside the ST. Buy the service file, favor the 8-speed, and let a canceled badge furnish the driveway for thousands less than the segment's survivors.
