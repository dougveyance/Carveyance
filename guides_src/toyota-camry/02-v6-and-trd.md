While its rivals were swapping V6s for small turbocharged fours, Toyota kept a naturally aspirated 3.5-liter V6 in the Camry right up to 2024. In the eighth generation it made 301 hp and 267 lb-ft, drove the front wheels through an 8-speed automatic, and turned the most sensible car in America into one that could embarrass people at traffic lights.

It was never the default Camry. It's the one you buy because you like engines. And since the 2025 Camry is hybrid-only, the 2018–2024 V6 is the last of its kind.

## The engine

Car and Driver timed a 2018 XSE V6 from 0 to 60 mph in 5.8 seconds, 2.1 seconds quicker than the 4-cylinder SE it tested. That's quick by any family-car standard, and the way it gets there matters as much as the number: instant response, a smooth, rising engine note and effortless passing at highway speed, with none of the waiting a turbo can bring.

The V6 came in the XLE, the comfort-focused grade, and the XSE, the sporty one with 19-inch wheels and a firmer suspension. It was always front-wheel drive. The 2018 XSE V6 started at $34,950 (about $46,900 in 2026 money), and the EPA rated both grades at 26 mpg combined.

## The TRD: real work beneath the wing

The Camry TRD arrived for 2020 and ran through 2024. It's easy to dismiss as a body kit, and that would be a mistake. Toyota Racing Development lowered it 15 mm (0.6 in) on its own springs, shocks and thicker anti-roll bars, added underbody braces to stiffen the structure, and fitted larger front brakes: 2-piston calipers on bigger rotors, up from 12.0 inches to 12.9. Then it added the aero kit and the wing, so nobody could miss it.

What it didn't change is the engine. The TRD has the same 301-hp V6 as an XLE or XSE, rated at 25 mpg combined. It also launched at $31,170 (about $40,600 in 2026 money), less than an XSE V6, because Toyota built it on the simpler SE and left out some comfort features.

The result is the best-handling Camry ever sold, with a firmer ride to match. On smooth roads it's a genuine pleasure. On broken city streets, the stiffer suspension and low-profile tires make themselves known. Drive one over your worst local pavement before you buy.

## How it feels day to day

On a straight road the V6 Camry feels like a near-luxury car, quiet and relaxed with power in reserve. The 8-speed automatic is tuned for smoothness rather than the snap of a sports transmission, and the front-drive chassis keeps things honest on a twisting road: it's fast for a family sedan, not a BMW 3 Series rival. That's fine. It was never trying to be.

## Buying one used

V6 Camrys attract enthusiasts, so look harder than you would at an LE.

- **Tires and alignment.** Check for matched tires, even wear and no pull. Cheap mismatched tires on a 301-hp front-drive car are a warning sign.
- **Brakes.** Look for warped rotors or uneven pad wear, especially on a TRD.
- **Modifications.** Aftermarket exhausts, lowering springs and engine tunes are common. Ask, and look underneath.
- **A knock at idle.** Toyota service bulletin T-SB-0115-22 covers a knocking noise from the vacuum pump at idle on some 2018–2023 Camrys with this V6. It's a repair procedure for dealers, not a safety recall, and not every car has the noise. Listen with the hood open.
- **Recalls.** The 2018–2019 brake vacuum pump recall and the 2018–2020 fuel pump recall covered certain Camrys. Check the VIN.
- **The transmission.** Make sure it shifts cleanly cold and warm.

## The case against it

The hybrid uses about half as much fuel, and it's the smarter choice for most people who simply need a car. A V6 Camry costs more to run, in gas and in tires. And if you want a sports sedan, a rear-drive car like a BMW 330i drives with more involvement. The V6 Camry is a charmingly unnecessary luxury. That's exactly why it matters.

## Which one to buy

- **XLE V6** if you want quiet power and comfort.
- **XSE V6** if you want the sharper look and handling.
- **TRD** only if you want its firmer ride, its brakes and its wing, and you've driven one on bad roads first.

Don't pay a big premium for the badge on a poorly documented car. The V6's end after 2024 may support prices, but it doesn't guarantee they'll rise.

## The verdict

The V6 is the most interesting footnote in a decade of Camrys: the dependable family car with an unexpectedly serious engine. The TRD is the showman, but the regular XLE or XSE V6 is the sleeper. If you want a car that behaves responsibly on Monday and surprises you on Saturday, Toyota built one, briefly, and then moved on.

::: curiosity The Trunk That Lost Its Pass-Through
To stiffen the Camry TRD's structure, Toyota added cross braces behind the rear seat. They do their job, and they also block the opening that lets long items slide from the trunk into the cabin, so the TRD lost the pass-through other Camrys have. Skis go on the roof. The price of handling is sometimes paid in lumber.
:::
