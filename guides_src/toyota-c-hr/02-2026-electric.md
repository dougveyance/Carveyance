The gas C-HR left the US after 2022, and for 2026 Toyota brought the name back on an electric crossover. The 2026 C-HR shares its platform and its 74.7 kWh battery with the bZ and bZ Woodland, but it's Toyota's smallest electric car, and every one has 2 motors and all-wheel drive. It went on sale in March 2026.

## How it drives

The front and rear motors make 338 hp combined. Toyota claims 0 to 60 mph in 4.9 seconds, and Edmunds' test car matched that exactly, while Car and Driver reported an even quicker run. That's far quicker than the 11-second gas C-HR. Paddles on the steering wheel let you choose from 4 levels of regenerative braking, but there's no one-pedal driving mode: you still use the brake pedal to stop.

## Range and charging

The EPA rates the SE, on 18-inch wheels, at 287 miles, and the XSE, on 20-inch wheels, at 273 miles. It charges through a NACS port, the same plug Tesla uses, so it can use Tesla Superchargers without an adapter. Toyota says a DC fast charger can take it from 10% to 80% in about 30 minutes under ideal conditions, and an 11-kW onboard charger handles Level 2 home charging.

## Living with it

Even the SE comes with a 14-inch touchscreen, a digital instrument cluster, 2 wireless phone chargers, a power liftgate and Toyota Safety Sense 3.0. The XSE adds 20-inch wheels and more comfort features, and options included a panoramic roof and two-tone paint. The SE started at $37,000 and the XSE at $39,000, before delivery.

## What to check before you buy

Ask whether recall 26V-570 has been done. On 8,521 C-HRs built between February and July 2026, a software error can let regenerative braking overcharge the battery when it's already full or nearly full, for example on a long downhill after an overnight charge. The system then shuts down the electric drive, and the car loses power. The fix is a software update at a Toyota dealer, and owner letters were due from October 18, 2026. Test DC fast charging if you can, and confirm the charging cable and any adapters come with the car. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

The electric C-HR is a much better car than the gas one: quick, roomy enough for most people, well equipped and easy to charge on the Supercharger network. The SE is the one to buy for its longer range and softer-riding tires; the XSE is for buyers who want the bigger wheels and extra features. Make sure 26V-570 is done, and ask whether a nearly new one costs much less than a 2027, which Toyota priced from $37,080.

::: curiosity Shared With the Woodland
The C-HR uses the same 74.7 kWh battery as the bigger bZ Woodland, with both cars rated at 338 hp or more from 2 motors. The C-HR wraps that hardware in a smaller body.
:::
