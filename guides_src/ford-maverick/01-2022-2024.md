The Maverick arrived for 2022 asking what a truck would look like if it were only as large as the job. It was compact enough to park without a strategy meeting, it had 4 real doors, and it offered an efficient hybrid in a vehicle with an open bed. There was a hint of rebellion in its refusal to pretend that every trip to the hardware store involves towing a skid steer.

## A truck, but not a small F-150

The Maverick uses a unibody structure shared with Ford's crossovers rather than a separate ladder frame, which gives it a more carlike ride and easy maneuverability. The compromise is real: it isn't meant to replace the towing, off-road articulation or range of cabs and beds of a Ranger or an F-150. It's useful precisely because it doesn't insist on being those trucks. The 4.5-foot FLEXBED takes bikes, bags of mulch, dirty gear and do-it-yourself dividers, and with the tailgate partly lowered it carries 4-by-8-foot sheets. Its short length is an advantage in parking lots and a limitation with long material.

## 2 very different powertrains

The hybrid pairs a 2.5-liter 4-cylinder with an electric motor for 191 hp combined, through an electronically controlled continuously variable transmission. For 2022–2024, the hybrid came only with front-wheel drive. The EPA rates it at 37 mpg combined. It's the compelling Maverick for commuters with errands and weekend projects, especially anyone used to paying pickup-truck fuel bills.

The 2.0-liter EcoBoost turbo makes 250 hp and 277 lb-ft with an 8-speed automatic, and could be ordered with front- or all-wheel drive. It's the stronger choice when acceleration, traction and towing matter more than fuel: with all-wheel drive and the 4K Tow Package it's rated to tow 1,814 kg (4,000 lb), against 907 kg (2,000 lb) for the others. The EPA rates it at 25 or 26 mpg.

Car and Driver ran a 2022 hybrid to 60 mph in 7.7 seconds and an EcoBoost with all-wheel drive in 5.9.

## What it's like, and what it isn't

Expect easy low-speed driving, a commanding enough view and straightforward utility, but not luxury-car quiet. Hard plastics are everywhere, especially in the XL and XLT. They're sometimes cheerfully textured and colored, and they're unmistakably inexpensive. The rear seat works for adults on shorter trips, but it isn't a full-size crew cab. The ride can get busy over broken pavement with the bed empty, which is why the Maverick still feels like a small working truck rather than a raised compact sedan.

## Choosing a 2022, 2023 or 2024

| Model year | What changed |
|---|---|
| 2022 | Launch. Hybrid standard at $19,995 before delivery. FX4 Off-Road Package for EcoBoost all-wheel-drive XLT and Lariat models |
| 2023 | Tremor Off-Road Package for EcoBoost all-wheel-drive XLT and Lariat |
| 2024 | EcoBoost becomes the standard engine. The hybrid is a $1,500 option |

The earliest Maverick has one lasting advantage: it introduced the formula at a remarkably low price, $19,995, or $23,000 in 2026 dollars. Today's asking prices shouldn't be judged by those old ads. For 2024, Ford made the EcoBoost the standard engine and charged $1,500 more for the hybrid, which makes a used window sticker worth more than assumptions about what a trim included.

## The Carveyance verdict

The Maverick is most persuasive as a clever everyday vehicle that happens to have a bed. It brings a pickup's grimy convenience to people who neither need nor want a 5-figure towing rating. Its drawbacks are a small bed, a modest cabin, front-drive-only hybrids in these years and a long list of recalls, and they aren't footnotes. They define the right buyer. For someone whose real cargo is bicycles, potting soil, furniture and the occasional home-improvement triumph, this little Ford may answer the better question.
