Every Fit from 2017 to 2020 came with a choice of gearbox on its cheaper trims: a 6-speed manual or a CVT, a continuously variable automatic. The engine is the same 1.5-liter 4-cylinder either way, but the 2 cars feel different, use different amounts of fuel, and wear out in different ways.

## Who got which

- **2017:** the manual on the LX and EX; the CVT on all 3 trims, and the only choice on the EX-L.
- **2018 and 2019:** the manual on the LX, Sport and EX; the CVT on every trim. Honda Sensing was standard on the EX and EX-L, so a 2018 or 2019 EX manual is the only manual Fit with adaptive cruise control and automatic emergency braking. On the LX and Sport, Honda Sensing came only with the CVT.
- **2020:** the manual on the LX and Sport only. The EX and EX-L were CVT only, and they were the only trims with Honda Sensing.

## Speed

[[chart:accel]]

Neither is fast, but the manual is quicker. Car and Driver ran a 2015 EX manual to 60 mph in 7.7 seconds and a 2018 Sport manual in 8.2, while a 2016 EX-L with the CVT took 8.5. From 2018 Honda rated the engine at 130 hp with the manual and 128 hp with the CVT.

With the manual you pick the gear and hold it. The CVT has no gears to shift, so it's smoother in traffic, but under hard acceleration it holds the engine at high revs. WardsAuto found the engine of a 2015 EX-L with the CVT droned when accelerating hard, such as on a freeway on-ramp.

## Fuel economy

The CVT is the economical choice. From 2018, the EPA rates an LX with the CVT at 36 mpg combined, a Sport, EX or EX-L with the CVT at 33, and every manual at 31. For 2017 the figures were 36 for the LX CVT, 34 for the EX and EX-L CVT, and 32 for the manual.

At 12,000 miles a year, 36 mpg uses about 333 gallons and 31 mpg about 387, a difference of about 54 gallons a year. Multiply it by what you pay for a gallon to see what it's worth to you.

## Checking a manual

Drive it from cold. The clutch should take up smoothly, not near the very top of the pedal travel, and it shouldn't shudder pulling away in first. Accelerate hard in a high gear at low speed: if the revs rise without the car speeding up, the clutch is slipping. Shifts should be clean, without grinding, and there shouldn't be any noise that changes when you press or release the clutch pedal. A city car driven in heavy traffic can wear out a clutch early, so ask whether it has been replaced and when.

## Checking a CVT

Ask for records of CVT fluid changes, done with Honda's own CVT fluid when the Maintenance Minder called for them. On the drive, it should pull away smoothly from a stop and up a slight hill in reverse, with no shudder, slipping or delay when you select Drive or Reverse. Try it cold and again after a long drive, because some faults only show up when the fluid is hot. Any transmission warning light, or a seller who can't show fluid changes on a high-mileage car, is a reason for a specialist to look before you buy.

## Which to buy

If you like to shift for yourself and most of your miles are on open roads, a Sport or EX manual is the one to find, and a 2018 or 2019 EX manual adds Honda Sensing. If you sit in traffic every day or want the best fuel economy, an LX with the CVT is the smarter buy. Either way, a well-maintained car with the less-wanted gearbox is a better buy than a neglected one with the gearbox you prefer.
