Because Toyota changed the powertrain and the generation at the same time, choosing between the V6 and the hybrid Sienna also means choosing between an older van and a newer one. The V6 gives you more power, a removable second row and a lower price. The hybrid gives you far lower fuel bills, newer safety technology and a longer battery warranty.

## How they feel

The V6 makes 296 hp, and Car and Driver ran a 2017 from 0 to 60 mph in 6.8 seconds. The hybrid makes 245 hp and took 7.7 seconds, but it feels stronger than that number around town, because its electric motors pull hard from low speeds. The V6 is the better choice for frequent long climbs or fast freeway merges with a full load. Both are rated to tow up to 1,588 kg (3,500 lb).

## Fuel

[[chart:mpg]]

The EPA rates the 2021–2026 hybrid at 36 mpg combined with front-wheel drive and 35 with all-wheel drive. The V6 is rated at 21 or 22 with front-wheel drive, depending on the year, and 20 with all-wheel drive. The gap is widest in town, where the hybrid can run on electricity at low speeds.

[[chart:fuelcost]]

At 12,000 miles a year and $4 a gallon, a 2018–2020 front-drive V6 uses about $2,286 of fuel and a front-drive hybrid about $1,333, a difference of roughly $953 a year. With all-wheel drive, the gap is about $1,029. Drive 20,000 miles a year and it grows past $1,500. That saving helps cover the hybrid's higher purchase price, especially if you keep the van for years.

## Seats, traction and running costs

The seating may decide it for you. The V6 van's second-row seats come out, which turns it into a small cargo van. On the hybrid, the second-row captain's chairs have built-in airbags and stay in place; only the middle seat of the 8-seater comes out.

Both offer all-wheel drive. The V6 uses a conventional mechanical system, on most grades; the hybrid adds an electric motor at the rear, on every grade, and gives up only 1 mpg for it. The V6 is familiar to any shop. The hybrid's battery and electronics are best diagnosed by a Toyota-trained technician, but every 2021–2026 hybrid is covered for 10 years or 150,000 miles from the date it was first sold, so most used ones still have years of coverage left.

## The decision

- **Choose the hybrid** if you drive more than about 10,000 miles a year, mostly in town, or want the newest safety features. It's the better van for most families.
- **Choose the V6** if you need a removable second row, tow or climb often with a full load, or want the lowest purchase price.
- **Choose all-wheel drive** on either if you see snow. On the hybrid it costs very little fuel.

::: curiosity Both From the Highlander
Each Sienna in this guide borrows its powertrain from the Highlander SUV. The 2017 V6 and 8-speed came from the Highlander, and the 2021 Sienna's hybrid system is shared with the Highlander Hybrid.
:::
