Before you compare prices or mileage, find out which Clarity you're looking at. A Plug-in Hybrid is a normal used car that happens to plug in. An Electric or a Fuel Cell started life on a lease with no purchase option, and needs its ownership, its range and its service support confirmed before anything else.

## First, know which one it is

Decode the VIN and check the badges, the fuel door and the charge port. The Plug-in Hybrid has both a fuel filler and a charge port; the Electric has a charge port only; the Fuel Cell has a hydrogen filler and no charge port. The Plug-in Hybrid came in base and Touring trims; the Electric and Fuel Cell came in a single trim.

## The years at a glance

| Model | Years | What it is | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plug-in Hybrid | 2018–2021 | 47 miles on electricity, 340 in all; sold nationwide | Fuel pump on 2018–2019 (23V858). Rear seat belts on 2019 (20V326) |
| Electric | 2017–2019 | 89-mile range; leased in California and Oregon | Rear seat belts on 2019 (20V326). Title and transfer |
| Fuel Cell | 2017–2021 | 366-mile range (360 from 2019); hydrogen; leased in California | Fuel cell software (18V068, 18V921). Water pump (19V315). Rear seat belts on 2019 (20V326). Title and transfer |

## Every Clarity

Run the VIN for title brands, accidents and open recalls at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls), and ask a Honda dealer for open campaigns and software updates. Ask a Honda dealer to scan the car for stored fault codes, and look underneath for scrapes or damage near the battery or the hydrogen tanks. Test the cameras, Honda Sensing, the climate control and the phone connections, and check the tires and brakes as on any car.

## The Plug-in Hybrid

Charge it fully and drive it on electricity until the engine starts, then keep going: the switch to hybrid running should be smooth, with no warnings. Watch how far it goes on a charge in the kind of driving you do, remembering that the 47-mile EPA figure was for a new car, and that the dashboard's range estimate isn't a battery health test. Plug it into a Level 2 charger and a household outlet and confirm both work, and that the charging cable that came with the car is there. Ask for service records, and confirm the fuel pump recall on 2018–2019 cars.

## The Electric

Start with the paperwork: the title, the seller's right to sell, and whether a Honda dealer near you will service it, in writing. Then ask for a battery health report and test the range on a full charge. With 89 miles when new, a loss of even a few miles matters. Test both Level 2 and DC fast charging.

## The Fuel Cell

Check the paperwork in the same way, then check the hydrogen stations you'd actually use, as chapter 3 explains. Confirm the fuel cell software recalls and the water pump recall, and ask a dealer about any inspections the hydrogen system needs. If any of this can't be confirmed, stop there.

## What it costs to run

On electricity, the EPA rates the Plug-in Hybrid at 31 kWh per 100 miles. At 12,000 miles a year on electricity, that's about 3,700 kWh, or roughly $740 a year at 20 cents per kWh. On gasoline it's rated at 42 mpg. An Electric's running cost is all electricity. A Fuel Cell's depends on the price of hydrogen at your station, which you should check before anything else.

## Walk away if

The car has a branded title, flood or crash damage near the battery or tanks, a warning light the seller can't explain, a charging fault, unclear ownership papers, or open safety recalls with no repair date. For an Electric or Fuel Cell, walk away if no Honda dealer near you will service it.

## What is it worth?

[[chart:price]]

We don't publish average prices, because they go stale quickly. For a Plug-in Hybrid, compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year and trim with similar mileage and the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what cars actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you. Cross-shop a Toyota Prius Prime, a Chevrolet Volt or a Hyundai Ioniq Plug-in of the same age, and a regular hybrid if you can't charge at home.

An Electric or Fuel Cell has so few sales that there's little to compare it with. Price it on what it costs you to make it work: the range you'll actually get, the hydrogen or charging you'll use, and how hard it would be to sell on.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping a Clarity past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, especially for battery and hybrid parts, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a Clarity Plug-in Hybrid, base or Touring, with a home charger.
- **Only for short, predictable trips:** a Clarity Electric with clean paperwork and a good battery report.
- **Only near reliable hydrogen stations:** a Clarity Fuel Cell with clean paperwork and dealer support in writing.
