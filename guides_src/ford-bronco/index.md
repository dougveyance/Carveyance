Ford built the Bronco from 1966 to 1996 and brought the name back for 2021 as a body-on-frame 4x4 with removable doors and a removable roof, built in Wayne, Michigan, like the 5 generations before it. It shares its basic platform with the Ranger pickup, but it's a different vehicle: shorter, more upright and built first for trails, with 4-wheel drive on every version. The modern Bronco has sold strongly from the start, and 2025 was its best year yet.

Every 2021–2026 Bronco belongs to the same sixth generation, so choosing one is less about the year than about the body, the engine, the gearbox and the off-road hardware. This guide has 4 chapters. Start with the Bronco itself, or go straight to the checklist if you're about to look at one.

## Quick picks

- **Best value:** a documented 2021–2024 Big Bend 4-door with the 2.3-liter engine and the automatic, with its recalls done.
- **Best for driving it yourself:** a 2.3-liter with the 7-speed manual. Car and Driver ran a manual Black Diamond 2-door to 60 mph in 7.0 seconds.
- **Best for trails:** a Badlands, with its front stabilizer-bar disconnect, or any version with the Sasquatch Package and 35-inch tires.
- **Quickest:** the Bronco Raptor, with 418 hp. Car and Driver timed one at 5.6 seconds to 60 mph.
- **Think twice if** you mostly drive on the freeway, tow more than 1,588 kg (3,500 lb) or want a quiet cabin. A Ford Explorer or a Toyota 4Runner will suit you better.

## At a glance

|  | 2.3-liter | 2.7-liter V6 | Raptor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engine | 2.3-liter turbo 4-cylinder | 2.7-liter twin-turbo V6 | 3.0-liter twin-turbo V6 |
| Transmission | 10-speed automatic or 7-speed manual | 10-speed automatic | 10-speed automatic |
| Power | 300 hp | 330 hp | 418 hp |
| Torque | 325 lb-ft | 415 lb-ft | 440 lb-ft |
| EPA combined, 2026 | 20 mpg automatic, 19 mpg manual | 17 to 20 mpg | 15 mpg |
| Bodies | 2-door or 4-door | 2-door or 4-door | 4-door |
| Tows up to | 1,588 kg (3,500 lb) | 1,588 kg (3,500 lb) | 2,041 kg (4,500 lb) |

Ford rates the power with premium fuel. For 2027, Ford's order guide drops the 2.7-liter V6 and offers a 380-hp version of the Raptor's 3.0-liter on some trims instead.
