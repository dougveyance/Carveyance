For years, the Corolla's sporty side was mostly a matter of badges. The hatchback changed that. It's the Corolla you might buy because you like it, not just because it makes sense.

## 2017–2018: the Corolla iM

The first hatchback in this guide wasn't born a Corolla. It was the Scion iM, a version of the Auris sold in other markets, and it became the Corolla iM when Toyota closed the Scion brand for 2017. It came in one well-equipped grade, with a 137-hp 1.8-liter and a choice of a 6-speed manual or a CVT. Toyota Safety Sense C was standard, with automatic emergency braking, lane departure alert and automatic high beams, though not the radar cruise control of the sedan's TSS-P.

The iM had something the Corolla sedan didn't: an independent rear suspension, a more sophisticated setup than the sedan's. It was built in Japan and priced at $18,750 with the manual or $19,490 with the CVT (about $25,800 and $26,800 in 2026 money). The EPA rated it at 31 mpg combined with the CVT and 30 with the manual. It was pleasant and well made, but slow.

## 2019–2026: the real thing

The 12th-generation Corolla arrived as a hatchback first, for 2019, a year ahead of the sedan. It brought a new 2.0-liter engine with 168 hp, 31 more than the iM, and 2 interesting transmissions.

The 6-speed manual, which Toyota called an Intelligent Manual Transmission, could blip the throttle for smooth downshifts and help prevent stalls. The CVT added a real first gear for quicker, more natural starts before handing over to the belt. With the CVT, the 2019 SE was rated at 36 mpg combined. The manual was rated at 31.

[[chart:zero60]]

There were 2 grades at launch, the SE and the better-equipped XSE. The SE started at $19,990 with the manual (about $26,300 in 2026 money), and the XSE with the CVT at $24,090 (about $31,700).

For 2023, Toyota dropped the manual, added Toyota Safety Sense 3.0 and a new 8-inch touchscreen. For 2026, the hatchback comes as a Nightshade, an FX Edition or an XSE, rated at up to 35 mpg combined. It continues for 2027 with updated digital displays.

## The catch

The hatchback trades space for style. Its wheelbase is shorter than the sedan's, and rear legroom shrinks to 29.9 inches, against 34.8 in the sedan. Cargo space behind the rear seat is about 17.8 cubic feet, more than the sedan's 13.1-cubic-foot trunk but less than you'd guess from the shape. If you regularly carry adults in the back, try the back seat before you buy.

## What to check

Certain 2019 hatchbacks built between mid-June and early November 2018 were part of Toyota's fuel pump recall, in which a failing pump can stall the engine (20V-012, expanded as 20V-682). On a manual, check the clutch: it should engage smoothly and not slip in a high gear when you press the accelerator hard. Look at the tires and wheels for signs of hard driving or curb damage, since some of these cars were bought by enthusiasts.

## Which one to buy

- **2019–2022 SE or XSE manual:** the most fun. The rev-matching manual is a gem, and it won't be made again.
- **2023–2026 SE, Nightshade or XSE:** the newer safety tech and screen, with the CVT only.
- **2017–2018 iM:** a cheap, well-made hatchback, if you don't mind the modest pace.

## The verdict

The Corolla Hatchback is the best-driving regular Corolla in this guide, and a manual from 2019 to 2022 is something of a modern classic for people who like shifting their own gears. Just make sure the back seat is big enough for your life.

::: curiosity The Stick-Shift Paradox
When the hatchback launched, Toyota said 15% of its buyers chose the manual. Across the whole Corolla lineup, though, the share was under 1% in 2018, and only 1.7% by the time Toyota dropped the manual for 2023. Then came the GR Corolla, which launched for 2023 with only a manual. When Toyota added an automatic for 2025, it still expected about 80% of buyers to choose the stick.
:::
