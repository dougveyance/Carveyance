Every Expedition era has one famous weakness, and each is checkable in a driveway. Work the list against the specific years in front of you, and make the seller's paperwork do half the inspection.

## The 5 checks

**1. Spark plugs on the 5.4 3-valve (2005–2008).** The 24-valve 5.4's original 2-piece plugs are notorious for snapping off in the head at change time. It's a solved problem — 1-piece replacement plugs exist and good shops extract broken ones routinely — but ask when the plugs were last done and by whom. A receipt showing the job completed is worth real money; no history on a high-mile truck means budgeting for a careful extraction job.

**2. Cold-start rattle on the 3.5 EcoBoost (2018–2021 especially).** Cam phaser rattle — a second or 2 of diesel-like clatter on a cold start — is the known flag on these engines, addressed by Ford service bulletins. Insist on a stone-cold start. A documented phaser repair under warranty is fine; an undocumented rattle is a negotiating lever measured in thousands.

**3. Gearboxes and transfer cases.** The 10-speed (2018-on) should shift without clunks on light throttle; software campaigns cover much of the harshness, so check the VIN for completed updates. The 6-speed (2009–17) is durable but needs fluid history. On any 4WD truck, run the transfer case through its modes in a parking lot and listen.

**4. Suspension and tires.** The independent rear that made the 2003 truck famous has wear items — toe links and bushings — that announce themselves as clunks over speed bumps. Loaded trims with rear load-leveling should sit level overnight. And price the tires: a set for a 22-inch Platinum is a 4-figure line item, and uneven wear usually means neglected alignment, not bad luck.

**5. The paper trail.** Recall completion by VIN — including the 2020 Kentucky Truck Plant campaign on 4th-generation trucks — plus fluid history on anything that tows. An Expedition that's towed its whole life with documentation beats a lighter-use truck with none.

## Reading the market

The sales arc above is the used market's supply map. The late-90s flood means 1st-generation trucks are cheap but mostly used up — buy on condition, and remember the 9-seat capacity if you find a front-bench truck. The 2009 trough makes clean 2008–2010 examples genuinely scarce. The 2019-era recovery is why 2018–2021 trucks are plentiful and competitively priced — the era this guide recommends — and the 2025 changeover is already pushing lightly-used 4th-generation trucks down to make room.
