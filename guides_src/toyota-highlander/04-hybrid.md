The Highlander Hybrid has been around since 2006, but the version you can buy today is a very different animal from the one sold before 2020. The 2017–2019 hybrid used a V6 and was built for power as much as economy. The 2020–2026 hybrid uses a 4-cylinder, gives up some power and delivers fuel economy few 3-row SUVs can match. Both have the same towing limit, 1,588 kg (3,500 lb), and both are among the most sensible used family SUVs you can buy.

## 2017–2019: the V6 hybrid

The third-generation Highlander Hybrid pairs a 3.5-liter V6 with electric motors for 306 hp. A rear electric motor drives the back wheels, so every one has all-wheel drive, and the transmission is an electronically controlled CVT. For 2017, Toyota added LE and XLE grades below the Limited and Limited Platinum, and Car and Driver worked out that the hybrid premium had shrunk to as little as $1,350 over an equivalent gas model, about $1,860 in 2026 dollars.

The EPA rated it at 29 mpg combined as an LE and 28 in other grades, against 22 to 23 for the gas V6. Car and Driver measured a hybrid from 0 to 60 mph in 7.0 seconds, as quick as the gas V6, though its test car averaged only 23 mpg in mixed driving. It's heavy, at 2,219 kg (4,891 lb) in Limited form.

## 2020–2026: the 4-cylinder hybrid

The fourth-generation hybrid swaps the V6 for a 2.5-liter 4-cylinder and electric motors, for 243 hp. It's slower than the old V6 hybrid, but it's far thriftier. The EPA rated it at 36 mpg combined with front-wheel drive and 35 with all-wheel drive. For the first time, the Highlander Hybrid was offered with front-wheel drive, from 2020 through 2024. From 2025, all-wheel drive is standard again.

Toyota added a hybrid-only Bronze Edition for 2022, based on the XLE, and for 2025 an XLE Nightshade and a Limited 25th Edition, limited to 2,500 cars. By 2023, the hybrid accounted for more than 25% of Highlander sales. For 2026, it comes as an XLE, Limited or Platinum.

## The numbers

[[chart:fuelcost]]

Over 12,000 miles a year at $4 a gallon, the 2020–2026 hybrid saves about $590 a year against the turbo and about $670 against a 2020–2022 V6. The older V6 hybrid saves less, but still around $430 a year against its gas twin.

## How it drives

The 2020–2026 hybrid is calm and quiet, and it moves away on electric power in traffic. Under hard acceleration the engine drones, as most Toyota hybrids do, and it doesn't have the easy shove of the V6 or the turbo. For most family driving, it's more than enough. The 2017–2019 V6 hybrid feels stronger, with real punch when you press the accelerator, but you pay for it at the pump.

## What to check

- **Rear motor cable corrosion (programs 22TE09 and 25TD01):** On all-wheel-drive Highlander Hybrids from 2020 on, the high-voltage cable connection at the rear motor can corrode, which can cause AM radio static or a no-start. Toyota's customer support program 22TE09 covers 2020–2022 Highlander Hybrids, and limited service campaign 25TD01 covers about 166,600 2020–2024 Highlander Hybrids with all-wheel drive, inspecting the connector and fitting a protector seal. With the seal fitted, Toyota covers the cable for 10 years with no mileage limit. Ask whether it's been done.
- **Oil cooler pipe (campaign 19TC04):** About 30,000 2017–2019 Highlander Hybrids can develop a small oil leak from the engine oil cooler pipe. Dealers replace the pipe free of charge.
- **Front passenger airbag sensor (23V-865):** About 58,100 2020–2021 Highlander Hybrids are part of this December 2023 recall. Dealers inspect and replace the sensor as needed.
- **Recalls shared with the gas Highlander:** The front bumper cover recall (2020–2023), the second-row seat recliner recall (2021–2025) and the 2023–2025 camera and instrument panel software recalls apply to hybrids too. See the turbo chapter for details.
- **Hybrid battery warranty:** From the 2020 model year, Toyota covers the hybrid battery for 10 years or 150,000 miles. A 2017–2019 hybrid had 8 years or 100,000 miles, so most have run out. Have a hybrid specialist check the battery on an older car.

Run the VIN on [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls), and ask a Toyota dealer to check for open service campaigns as well.

## Which one to buy

- **2020–2024 Hybrid LE or XLE with FWD:** the most efficient Highlander ever, if you don't need all-wheel drive.
- **2020–2026 Hybrid with AWD:** the all-weather family hauler, at 35 mpg.
- **Bronze Edition or 25th Edition:** for the look, at a premium.
- **2017–2019 V6 Hybrid:** if you want power and all-wheel drive, and find one with the oil cooler campaign done and a healthy battery.

## The verdict

The 2020–2026 Highlander Hybrid is the Highlander to buy if you don't tow more than 1,588 kg (3,500 lb). It uses about a third less fuel than the gas models, it's quiet and smooth, and it carries the same 3 rows. The older V6 hybrid is a more specialized choice, quicker but thirstier, and now old enough that its battery needs a careful look.

::: curiosity From Muscle to Mileage
Before 2020, the Highlander Hybrid used a V6 and made more power than the gas model, 306 hp against 295 in 2017. The 2020 redesign flipped that idea. Toyota gave the hybrid a 4-cylinder and made efficiency the whole point, and buyers followed. By 2023, more than 25% of all Highlanders sold in the US were hybrids.
