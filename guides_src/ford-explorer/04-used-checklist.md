Start with the question that sorts Explorers into 2 piles: **"Has the water pump been done?"**

## The big one: 3.5 V6 water pump

On the 3.5-liter V6s — Duratec and EcoBoost alike, 2011 through 2019 — the water pump lives inside the engine, driven by the timing chain, and its weep hole drains... into the oil pan. When the pump seal fails, coolant contaminates the oil, and an unnoticed failure can take the engine with it. The repair is a timing-cover-off job that commonly quotes in the $2,000–$3,000 range even caught early. So: service records showing the pump done is a selling point worth paying for; a high-mileage 3.5 with no pump history prices the job into your offer; and on any test drive, check the oil for a milky tint and the coolant level against the marks. The 2.3 and 2.0 EcoBoost fours and the 2020-on 3.0 use conventional external pumps — one more quiet argument for the 4-cylinder trucks.

## Driveline and recalls

- **6-speed (2011–19):** firm 1–2 shifts when warm are common; flaring, slipping or hard clunks are not. Budget a fluid service if there's no record of one.
- **10-speed (2020 on):** smoother story, but confirm the early-build recalls — including the 2020–21 **rear-axle-bolt campaign** — are closed by VIN at the dealer or NHTSA.
- **PTU on AWD trucks:** the power transfer unit runs hot and holds little fluid; a service receipt is gold, a whine on throttle is a quote waiting to happen.
- **2011–17 trucks:** ask whether the **exhaust-odor TSBs** (the Police Interceptor carbon-monoxide complaints made national news in 2017) were addressed; Ford issued repairs for affected vehicles.

## The rest of the hour

- **Cold start:** listen 2 minutes for chain rattle on 3.5s and turbo whistle-to-smoke on EcoBoosts.
- **Cooling history on EcoBoosts:** these run hot in traffic with a trailer; a radiator and thermostat receipt on a tow-package truck is a good sign, not a red flag.
- **Tires and brakes:** 2-ton-plus crossovers on 18–21-inch wheels eat both; uneven front wear also flags alignment or worn end links.
- **Interior electronics:** every camera view, the liftgate, 3rd-row folding, and on 2016-on trucks the Sync screen's touch response — cabin electronics are this family's most common annoyance.
- **Paperwork:** recalls closed, oil intervals proven, and for AWD and ST trucks, the fluid services nobody does. The Explorer that's had them is the Explorer to buy.

35 years of volume means there is always another one for sale. Let the pump question and the folder do the choosing.
