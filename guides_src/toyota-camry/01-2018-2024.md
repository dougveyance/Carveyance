For 2018 Toyota did something it rarely does with its most important car: it took a risk. The eighth-generation Camry moved to the company's TNGA platform, sat lower, wore a far more aggressive face, and got a new 2.5-liter engine and an 8-speed automatic. It is still a Camry, which is to say a comfortable, roomy, easy car that asks very little of its owner. But for the first time in a long time, it also seemed interested in the road ahead.

This chapter covers the 4-cylinder car, which is most of the eighth generation. The V6 and the hybrid have chapters of their own. And because a 2017 sits at the start of this guide's decade, it gets a section of its own near the end.

## How it drives

The 2.5-liter makes 203 hp in most grades and 206 hp in the XSE, which has dual exhausts. Car and Driver timed a 2018 SE from 0 to 60 mph in 7.9 seconds, only a tenth quicker than the 2017 car it replaced, so the extra power shows up more as relaxed merging than as speed. The 8-speed automatic is tuned for economy and can feel busy, hunting between gears on hills or at low speed. Drive the exact car you're thinking of buying, warm and cold, before you decide whether that bothers you.

The chassis is the real improvement. The lower center of gravity and stiffer new platform make the Camry calmer through corners and more settled at highway speed. The SE and XSE are firmer, and the XSE's 19-inch wheels sharpen the steering a little at the cost of some ride comfort and pricier tires. The LE and XLE ride better. None of them is a sports sedan. All of them are better to drive than any Camry before.

[[chart:mpg]]

Fuel economy is excellent for a car this size. The 2018 L was rated at 34 mpg combined and 41 on the highway, and the other 4-cylinder grades at 32 combined. Later XLE and XSE models, with more equipment, were rated at 31, and the AWD versions at 28 or 29.

## Living with it

The cabin is roomy front and back, the trunk is big enough for a family's luggage, and most controls are where you'd expect them. Some plastics are hard, and road noise rises on coarse pavement, especially with the larger wheels. The low seating position feels sporty but takes more effort to climb out of than a crossover.

## Year by year

- **2018:** The redesign. No Apple CarPlay or Android Auto at launch. Toyota later offered dealers a way to add CarPlay and Amazon Alexa to 2018 cars.
- **2019:** Apple CarPlay and Amazon Alexa compatibility arrive.
- **2020:** Android Auto joins them. All-wheel drive becomes a stand-alone option on the 4-cylinder LE, SE, XLE and XSE, the first AWD Camry since 1991. The TRD model arrives (see the V6 chapter).
- **2021:** A refresh brings new front styling, a 7-inch or 9-inch touchscreen, Toyota Safety Sense 2.5+ on every car and a new XSE Hybrid. The base L grade is dropped.
- **2022–2024:** Small changes in equipment and colors. 2024 is the last year for the gas-only Camry and the V6.

## The all-wheel-drive Camry

From 2020 to 2024, any 4-cylinder LE, SE, XLE or XSE could be ordered with all-wheel drive. The system came from the RAV4 and can disconnect the rear axle when it isn't needed, to save fuel. It adds about 75 kg (165 lb), and the engine makes 202 hp, or 205 hp in the XSE. The V6 and the hybrids of this generation were front-drive only. If you want a hybrid with AWD, you need a 2025 or later.

## What to check before you buy

Certain 2018–2019 Camrys were recalled because a part inside the brake vacuum pump could break, causing a sudden loss of brake assist (NHTSA 21V-890, about 227,000 cars). Certain 2018–2020 Camrys were also included in Toyota's large low-pressure fuel pump recall, in which the pump could fail and the engine could stall (NHTSA 20V-012, expanded as 20V-682). Neither covers every car of those years, so check the exact VIN at [NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask for proof the repair was done.

Beyond recalls, judge it like any used car. Check the shifting from cold, look for oil and coolant leaks, listen for suspension knocks, read the tire wear and pay for an independent inspection. The used checklist chapter has the full list.

## And the 2017?

The 2017 Camry is the last year of the seventh generation, and it's a different, older car. Its 2.5-liter makes 178 hp through a 6-speed automatic, and it's rated at 27 mpg combined. A 268-hp V6 and a 200-hp hybrid were also offered. The 2017 LE started at $23,070 (about $31,700 in 2026 money), unchanged from 2016.

It's an honest, comfortable car, and a well-kept one can be a sensible buy. But its platform, cabin and safety technology are a generation behind, and a 2018–2019 Camry is often not much more money. Buy a 2017 only if it's clearly cheaper and its records are clearly better.

## The verdict

The eighth-generation Camry lets you buy the sensible car without feeling sentenced to sensible driving. For most people, a well-kept 2021–2023 LE or SE is the sweet spot: the refreshed cabin, the full safety suite and smartphone integration, without the premium the last year tends to carry. A 2019–2020 car is the value play, if the price reflects its older screen. Choose by condition and records, not trim. A well-bought LE is a better car than a neglected XSE.

::: curiosity CarPlay, Installed Later
The 2018 Camry launched without Apple CarPlay or Android Auto, when many rivals already had both. Toyota added CarPlay for 2019, and in 2019 it began offering owners of 2018 Camrys a dealer-installed upgrade that added CarPlay and Amazon Alexa to the car they already had. Android Auto had to wait for the 2020 model. If a 2018 seller says the car "has CarPlay," it's worth checking whether the update was ever done.
:::
