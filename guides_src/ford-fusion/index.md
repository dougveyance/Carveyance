The Fusion is a used-market gift built on a corporate retreat. Ford sold about 3.2 million of them in 15 years, won Motor Trend's 2010 Car of the Year with the first generation, peaked at 306,860 sales in 2014 with the second — and then announced in 2018 that it was done with American sedans entirely. The last car left Hermosillo in July 2020, and because nameplates without successors don't hold value, every Fusion now trades below its merits.

That makes this guide a value hunt. The 2013–2020 cars are handsome, global (Europe knew them as the Mondeo) and cheap; the 2006–2012 cars are simple Mazda-boned transportation for almost nothing; the hybrids are the running-cost champions; and the 2017–2019 Fusion Sport — the F-150's 325-hp twin-turbo V6 with all-wheel drive — is one of the great overlooked performance bargains of the decade.

## Quick picks

- **Best all-rounder:** a 2017–2020 Titanium with the 2.0 EcoBoost — 245 hp, the refreshed cabin, grown-up equipment, low-teens money.
- **The sleeper:** a 2017–2019 Fusion Sport. 325 hp, AWD, 5.1 to 60 in Car and Driver's test, and the market still prices it like an SE.
- **Best running costs:** a 2014–2020 Hybrid at 41–42 mpg combined — or an Energi plug-in (97 MPGe) if you can charge at home.
- **Cheapest honest car:** a 2010–2012 SEL 2.5 — Car of the Year hardware at beater prices.
- **Think twice if** resale matters to you. The Fusion's discount works in your favor buying, against you selling — buy to keep, not to flip.
