The Highlander was Toyota's first midsize crossover when it arrived in 2000, a Camry-based SUV for families who wanted a third row without driving something built like a truck. 25 years later it's still the default 3-row Toyota for a lot of households, even with the bigger Grand Highlander now parked beside it. Between 2017 and 2026, though, the Highlander changed engines more often than most buyers realize.

For a shopper, the story splits 3 ways. The 2017–2019 Highlander is the refreshed third generation, with a 295-hp V6, a base 4-cylinder and a V6 hybrid. The 2020–2022 Highlander is the fourth generation with the same V6 and a new, far more efficient 4-cylinder hybrid. And the 2023–2026 Highlander swaps the V6 for a 2.4-liter turbo 4-cylinder. The 2026 is also the last gas and hybrid Highlander: Toyota has turned the 2027 model into an electric SUV. This guide has 6 chapters. Start with the years you're shopping, or go straight to the checklist if you're about to look at one.

## Quick picks

- **Best value:** a documented 2020–2022 V6 LE or XLE. It's smooth, it tows 2,268 kg (5,000 lb) and it has Apple CarPlay and Android Auto.
- **Lowest running costs:** a 2020–2026 Highlander Hybrid, rated at up to 36 mpg combined.
- **Best for towing:** any V6 or turbo model, rated for 2,268 kg (5,000 lb). The hybrids stop at 1,588 kg (3,500 lb).
- **Newest:** a 2025–2026 Hybrid, now with all-wheel drive as standard, or a 2026 turbo.
- **Think twice if** you need an adult-size third row or lots of cargo space with every seat up. Behind the third row there's only about 16 cu ft. The Grand Highlander is the better fit.
