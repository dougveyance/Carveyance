For 13 years the Transit Connect was the answer to a question American van buyers had never been asked: what if you don't need the whole Econoline? The 2010 original — tall, narrow, Turkish-built, 136 hp — created the US compact-van segment by itself and won North American Truck of the Year doing it. The 2014 second generation grew onto Focus bones, added a 7-seat wagon and New York taxi duty, and peaked at 52,221 sales in 2015. Then the segment it invented dissolved: rivals left, fleets went full-size, and Ford pulled the plug after 2023. Total: 495,932 sold.

For a used buyer that arc is good news. Nobody collects Transit Connects yet, fleets retire them constantly, and a tidy one is the cheapest way into a genuinely useful enclosed work vehicle — or an oddball family hauler with sliding doors and a liftgate tall enough to stand under.

## Quick picks

- **Best used buy:** a 2019–2023 cargo van with the 2.0 GDI and 8-speed — the most refined drivetrain, the newest safety kit, and fleet depreciation has done its work.
- **The quick one:** a 2014–2018 with the 178-hp 1.6 EcoBoost — 8.7 seconds to 60 in Motor Trend's test, which is hot-hatch pace by van standards.
- **The family oddball:** a 7-seat Wagon, Titanium if you can find one. Minivan function, parkable footprint, invisible price.
- **The history piece:** a clean 2010–2013 — the chicken-tax original, charming, slow, and increasingly rare in unrusted form.
- **Approach with eyes open:** ex-taxi and ex-utility Wagons — honest machines, but priced for the miles they've lived.
