The fourth-generation Avalon arrived for 2013, so by 2017 it was a mature car, and that's its appeal. It's a big, quiet, front-drive sedan with a soft ride, a roomy back seat and the kind of easy, unstressed V6 that has almost disappeared from mainstream showrooms. It looks more conservative than the car that replaced it, and plenty of buyers prefer it that way.

## How it drives

The V6 is a 3.5-liter with 268 hp and 248 lb-ft, paired with a 6-speed automatic that has paddle shifters and blips the throttle on downshifts. It pulls smoothly and quietly, and it never sounds strained. The hybrid pairs a 2.5-liter 4-cylinder with an electric motor and a nickel-metal hydride battery for 200 hp, with a CVT in place of the 6-speed. It's slower but feels relaxed in traffic, and it's rated at 40 mpg combined against the V6's 24.

[[chart:power]]

Neither is a sports sedan. The Avalon is tuned for comfort first, and its best place is a long stretch of highway, where it's calm and settled. That's what most Avalon buyers want.

## Living with it

For 2017, Toyota made its Toyota Safety Sense P suite standard on every grade. That means automatic emergency braking with pedestrian detection, lane departure alert with steering assist, adaptive cruise control and automatic high beams. Before 2017, the suite was optional, and only on the Limited, which is a real reason to choose a 2017 over a 2016. The cabin uses touch-sensitive controls on the center stack, and the trunk holds 16.0 cubic feet.

The weak spot is the technology. The infotainment system predates Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, which only arrived on the redesigned car. If you live in your phone's maps and music, budget for an aftermarket head unit or choose a 2019 or later.

## What to check before you buy

Ask for oil-change and cooling-system records, and drive it long enough to feel the transmission shift under load. On a hybrid, have a Toyota shop check the hybrid system for stored codes. The battery warranty on these cars is 8 years or 100,000 miles in most states, so many have little or none left. Run the VIN for the airbag control unit recall and the spare-tire recall. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A documented 2017–2018 Avalon is one of the most comfortable used sedans for the money, with standard emergency braking and a reputation for lasting. Pick the hybrid if you drive a lot of miles, especially in town, and the V6 if you want the smoothest highway car. Pay less than you would for a 2019 or later, to account for the older technology and the missing phone connection. If those matter to you, read [the 2019–2022 chapter](../2019-2022/).

::: curiosity Named for an Island
The Avalon takes its name from the legendary island of the Arthurian tales. When Toyota retired it after 2022, the big sedan's replacement took a name with even deeper roots at the company: the Crown, first sold in the 1950s.
:::
