The third-generation Fit went on sale on April 14, 2014, as a 2015 model, at $15,525 ($22,000 in 2026 dollars). Earlier US Fits had been shipped in from Japan and China; this one was built in Celaya, Mexico. It had a new 1.5-liter engine with direct injection, rated at 130 hp and 114 lb-ft, and a CVT automatic in place of the old 5-speed automatic.

This guide covers the last 4 model years, 2017 to 2020, but a 2015 or 2016 is the same generation, with the same engine and the same seats, so the advice applies to them too.

## The Magic Seat

Most small cars put the fuel tank under the rear seat. The Fit's sits under the front seats, which leaves the space under the rear seat empty. The rear seat cushions flip up against the seat backs, leaving a floor-to-roof space behind the front seats for something tall, like a plant or a bicycle with the front wheel off. Or the seat backs fold down to make a long, flat load floor. The cargo area holds 16.6 cubic feet behind the rear seat and 52.7 cubic feet with it folded, and the rear seat has room for adults.

## The drivetrain

[[chart:mpg]]

Every Fit in this guide has the 1.5-liter 4-cylinder and front-wheel drive. From 2018, Honda rated it at 130 hp with the 6-speed manual and 128 hp with the CVT. It's light: Honda listed curb weights of 1,140 to 1,198 kg (2,513 to 2,642 lb) for the 2015. Car and Driver ran a 2018 Sport with the manual to 60 mph in 8.2 seconds.

The LX with the CVT is the most economical, at 36 mpg combined. From 2018, the Sport, EX and EX-L with the CVT, which ride on 16-inch wheels, are rated at 33 mpg, and every manual is rated at 31. The next chapter compares the 2 gearboxes.

## 2017

The 2017 was a carryover in LX, EX and EX-L trims, with Lunar Silver replacing Alabaster Silver as the only change. The LX with the manual started at $15,990 ($21,900 in 2026 dollars), and the EX-L with navigation, CVT only, at $21,265 ($29,200). A multi-angle rearview camera was standard, and the EX and EX-L had Honda's LaneWatch camera, which shows the right-hand blind spot on the dashboard screen. There was no Honda Sensing.

## 2018: the update

The updated 2018 Fit went on sale on July 17, 2017, from $16,190 ($21,700 in 2026 dollars). It had a new nose and tail, a new Sport trim with a front spoiler, a rear diffuser, orange pinstripes and black 16-inch wheels, and 2 new colors, Helios Yellow and Orange Fury.

The bigger changes were under the skin. Honda retuned the dampers, stiffened the steering and the body, and added acoustic glass and more insulation to make it quieter inside. Honda Sensing, with adaptive cruise control, automatic emergency braking and lane keeping assist, became available for the first time: standard on the EX and EX-L, and an option on the LX and Sport with the CVT. A 7-inch touchscreen with Apple CarPlay and Android Auto came on the Sport, EX and EX-L.

## 2019 and 2020

The 2019 went on sale in April 2018 with the same trims and the same $16,190 starting price. For 2020, the manual was limited to the LX and Sport, the EX and EX-L were CVT only, and Honda Sensing came only on the EX and EX-L. The 2020 started at $16,190 ($21,000 in 2026 dollars) for an LX manual and $19,060 ($24,800) for an EX.

In July 2020, Honda said the Fit would end in North America after the 2020 model year, so a 2020 is the last new Fit sold in the US.

## Which one to buy

A 2018–2020 is the better car: quieter, with phone mirroring on most trims and Honda Sensing on the EX and EX-L. An EX with the CVT has the most useful equipment for the money. If you want the manual and Honda Sensing together, only a 2018 or 2019 EX has both.

A 2017 is still worth buying if it's priced well below a 2018, especially an LX with the CVT, which is rated at 36 mpg. Whatever the year, condition and service records matter more than the trim, and the checklist in chapter 3 covers what to look for.
