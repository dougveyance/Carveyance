Ford showed the Mustang to the public at the New York World's Fair on April 17, 1964, at $2,368, or $25,700 in 2026 dollars, and it has been on sale ever since. For a shopper looking at 2017 to 2026 models, there are 2 generations. The sixth generation, sold from 2015 to 2023, has independent rear suspension and was offered with a turbo 4-cylinder, a V8 and, until 2017, a V6. The seventh generation arrived for 2024 with a new cabin built around screens, more power and the Dark Horse.

In between sit some of the most specialized Mustangs Ford has built: the high-revving Shelby GT350, the 760-hp GT500, the Bullitt and Mach 1, and, at the very top, the Dark Horse SC and the $325,000 GTD. This guide has 6 chapters. Start with the generation you're shopping, read the engine chapter if you're deciding between 4 and 8 cylinders, or go straight to the checklist if you're about to look at one.

## Quick picks

- **Best value:** a documented 2018–2023 GT. The 2018 update brought 460 hp and a 10-speed automatic.
- **Best daily driver:** a 2024 or later EcoBoost or GT. The cabin is a decade newer, and the 2.3-liter EcoBoost is rated at 26 mpg combined.
- **Best for the track on a budget:** a 2021–2023 Mach 1, with Shelby GT350 parts and a choice of manual or automatic.
- **Best collector's car:** a 2016–2020 Shelby GT350, if its engine history is documented.
- **Think twice if** you need 4 real seats, all-wheel drive or a big trunk. A Mustang is a 2+2, and every one of them is rear-wheel drive.
