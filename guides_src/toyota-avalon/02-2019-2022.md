For 2019, Toyota rebuilt the Avalon on its newer TNGA platform, shared with the Camry and the Lexus ES. It's lower, longer-looking and far more modern inside, with a huge grille that split opinion from the day it was shown. Toyota was openly chasing younger buyers, and the lineup showed it: alongside the comfortable XLE and Limited came the sportier XSE and Touring, and later a TRD. It was the last Avalon. Sales fell to about 18,400 in 2020, and Toyota ended production in Kentucky in August 2022.

## How it drives

The V6 is a new 3.5-liter with 301 hp and 267 lb-ft, 33 hp more than before, and an 8-speed automatic. Car and Driver ran a 2019 Touring from 0 to 60 mph in 6.1 seconds. It's genuinely quick and still silky, and it's one of the last big naturally aspirated V6s in a mainstream sedan.

The hybrid pairs a 2.5-liter 4-cylinder with electric motors for 215 hp. It's slower, at 7.8 seconds to 60 in Car and Driver's test of an XSE, but it's quiet in traffic and rated at up to 44 mpg combined. For 2021 it switched to a lighter lithium-ion battery.

The chassis is tighter than the old car's, so it feels more controlled on a winding road without giving up much of the Avalon's calm. The Touring adds Adaptive Variable Suspension and 19-inch wheels.

## The TRD, the AWD and the Nightshade

The Avalon TRD arrived for 2020, the first TRD version of the car, with an aero kit, a TRD cat-back exhaust, matte black wheels and the same 301-hp V6. Car and Driver ran one to 60 in 6.0 seconds. It lasted 2 model years.

For 2021, Toyota added all-wheel drive to the XLE and Limited, but only with the Camry's 2.5-liter 4-cylinder, rated at 205 hp and 185 lb-ft with the 8-speed automatic. It's the economy choice for snowy climates, at 28 mpg combined, but it gives up nearly 100 hp to the V6. All-wheel drive was offered for 2021 only. The 2021 car also brought an XSE Nightshade Edition with black trim and wheels.

For 2022, the final year, Toyota dropped the TRD, the all-wheel-drive models and the gas XSE Nightshade, added a Nightshade version of the XSE Hybrid and made Toyota Safety Sense 2.5 standard.

## Living with it

The cabin is roomy, the front seats are comfortable on long drives and the back seat is generous. The trunk holds 16.1 cubic feet. The 2019 Avalon was the first Toyota with Apple CarPlay; Android Auto followed for 2021, along with USB-C ports. Toyota Safety Sense P is standard through 2021, and Toyota Safety Sense 2.5 for 2022.

Prices for 2019 started at $35,650 for the XLE, about $46,900 in 2026 dollars, and rose to $42,350 for the Touring, about $55,700. The hybrid cost $1,000 more than the same grade with the V6. By 2022, the XLE started at $36,375.

## What to check before you buy

On a V6, ask for oil-change and cooling-system records and feel for smooth shifts under load. On a hybrid, have a Toyota shop scan the hybrid system, and work out how much of the hybrid battery warranty is left: 2019 cars have 8 years or 100,000 miles in most states, and 2020–2022 cars have 10 years or 150,000 miles. Run the VIN for the fuel pump recalls on 2018–2020 cars and the airbag recalls on 2019–2021 cars. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

This is the Avalon to buy if your budget stretches. The XLE and Limited are the comfortable choices, the Touring and TRD the sporty ones. A 2021 AWD makes sense only if you need the traction. Read [the V6 or hybrid chapter](../v6-or-hybrid/) before you choose a powertrain.

::: curiosity CarPlay Came Here First
Toyota resisted Apple CarPlay for years. When it finally gave in, the first car to get it wasn't a Camry or a RAV4 but the Avalon, the model with the oldest buyers in the lineup.
:::
