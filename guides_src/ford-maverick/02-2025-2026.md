The 2025 update, unveiled on July 31, 2024, didn't try to solve the small-truck question by making the truck bigger. Instead it fixed the complaint that made early hybrid shoppers hesitate: the fuel-saving version couldn't have all-wheel drive. Suddenly the most interesting Maverick was no longer a compromise between efficiency and traction. Ford also gave it a new face, a much larger screen and 2 ways out of the polite-commuter story: the off-road Tremor and the street-oriented Lobo.

## Hybrid AWD is the headline

The 2.5-liter hybrid is still rated at 191 hp combined, but from 2025 it can have all-wheel drive, and with all-wheel drive it can have the 4K Tow Package for 1,814 kg (4,000 lb) of towing. The EPA rates the hybrid at 37 mpg combined with all-wheel drive and 38 with front-wheel drive. Its CVT isn't a conventional belt-driven CVT: it's a power-split hybrid transmission with different parts and behavior. Car and Driver ran a 2025 hybrid with all-wheel drive to 60 mph in 6.7 seconds, a full second quicker than the 2022 front-drive hybrid it tested. For anyone who meets wet mountain roads, light snow or dirt parking lots, this is the combination the original Maverick should have offered.

## New screens and the EcoBoost's output

Ford replaced the earlier 8-inch screen with a 13.2-inch touchscreen running SYNC 4, added an 8-inch digital instrument cluster and wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, and made a 360-degree camera available.

[[photo:ch2-cabin]]

For 2025, the EcoBoost came only with all-wheel drive. Ford's first 2025 specification sheet listed it at 238 hp, down from 250, but Ford later told reporters the 250-hp rating carried over, and its 2026 specifications list 250 hp and 280 lb-ft. Don't borrow an output figure from a different year or listing; check the specific truck.

## 2026: the cheaper EcoBoost returns

For 2026, the front-drive EcoBoost came back, at $1,000 less than the standard front-drive hybrid on the XL and XLT, and Ford made the EcoBoost with all-wheel drive a no-cost alternative to the hybrid. Ford quoted a starting price of $28,145 for the 2026 Maverick. The EPA rates the 2026 front-drive EcoBoost at 26 mpg combined.

## The money question

The update adds equipment and capability, but later stickers and used prices can erase some of the budget magic that made the original famous. The 2022 Maverick started at $19,995, or $23,000 in 2026 dollars, so a 2026 costs about $5,000 more after inflation. Compare the real out-the-door price of a hybrid XLT with all-wheel drive against an EcoBoost Maverick, a Hyundai Santa Cruz and, if towing or rough trails matter, a Ranger. A 2025 or 2026 may justify its premium with hybrid all-wheel drive and the better cabin. It shouldn't get an automatic recommendation just because it's newer.

## The Carveyance verdict

This update makes the Maverick more complete without changing its personality. The hybrid with all-wheel drive is the sensible star, the Lobo is the entertaining oddball and the Tremor is for owners with realistic trail plans. The winning choice is still the simplest Maverick that can do the work you actually do.
