For 2025, Ford gave the Bronco Sport a new face and a new dashboard. The engines carried on with small changes to their ratings, and the shape and the packaging that made it worth buying stayed the same. What changed most is what you look at and touch.

## What changed for 2025

The front and rear were restyled, with new grilles and wheels. Inside, a 12.3-inch digital instrument cluster and a 13.2-inch center touchscreen replaced the old displays, running SYNC 4. SiriusXM with 360L, a wireless charging pad and Ford's Co-Pilot360 Assist+ driver aids became standard, and new packages arrived: a Black Diamond Package for the Big Bend and Sasquatch Packages for the Outer Banks and the Badlands, with more off-road hardware.

[[photo:ch2-cabin]]

Ford now rates the 1.5-liter at 180 hp and 200 lb-ft and the Badlands' 2.0-liter at 250 hp and 280 lb-ft. The EPA rates the 1.5-liter at 27 mpg combined, 1 mpg better than before, and a 1.5-liter with the Sasquatch Package at 24. With the Class II tow package, a 1.5-liter can tow 998 kg (2,200 lb) and a Badlands 1,225 kg (2,700 lb).

## And what 2026 took away

A familiar trim name doesn't promise the same equipment from one year to the next. For 2026, Ford put an 8-inch instrument cluster in the Big Bend, Heritage and Outer Banks instead of the 12.3-inch one, and deleted the wireless charging pad and the garage door opener from every trim. The Outer Banks lost its rain-sensing wipers, the Free Wheeling edition was dropped, and some items moved out of packages into separate options. The 13.2-inch center screen stayed. Compare window stickers, not trim names.

## Is the update worth it?

The bigger screen and SYNC 4 make the cabin feel much newer, and the Sasquatch Packages bring real off-road hardware within reach of an Outer Banks. But more technology isn't better for every driver. Spend 10 minutes using the menus and the physical controls before you decide you prefer them. A well-kept 2023 or 2024 with documented service and its recalls done can still be the better buy than a 2025 with accident repairs or neglected tires.

## What they cost

The 2026 Bronco Sport started at $31,845 before delivery for a Big Bend, $33,395 for a Heritage, $35,145 for an Outer Banks and $37,265 for a Badlands. A 2021 Base had started at $26,660, or $33,100 in 2026 dollars, so the cheapest Bronco Sport costs about what it did at launch, after inflation, with more equipment. For 2027, Ford trimmed the Big Bend's price slightly, to $31,695.
