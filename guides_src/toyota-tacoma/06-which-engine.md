Between 2017 and 2026, the Tacoma offered 4 engines: a 2.7-liter 4-cylinder and a 3.5-liter V6 in the old truck, and a 2.4-liter turbo and a turbo hybrid in the new one. Choosing between them matters more than choosing a year, because it decides how the truck pulls, what it can tow and what it costs to run.

## 4-cylinder or V6, 2017–2023

The 2.7-liter makes 159 hp and is rated to tow 1,588 kg (3,500 lb). It suits a light-duty work truck driven gently around town. On the freeway, up long grades or with a loaded bed, it struggles. It doesn't save much fuel either: as a 4x4, the EPA rates it at the same 20 mpg combined as the V6.

The V6 makes 278 hp, tows up to 3,084 kg (6,800 lb) and is the one most buyers want. At the same price and condition, buy the V6. Choose the 4-cylinder only for local, light work at a real discount.

## Old V6 or new turbo

On paper, they make the same 278 hp. In practice, the turbo's 317 lb-ft arrives far lower in the rev range than the V6's 265 lb-ft, and the 8-speed keeps it there. Car and Driver found a 2024 TRD Off-Road about 0.6 second quicker to 60 mph than the V6 version it tested.

The turbo also brings the newer frame, the coil-spring rear suspension on most grades and a modern cabin. The V6 brings a simpler, naturally aspirated engine with years of service history behind it, a higher tow rating and a lower price. Neither is the wrong answer. The turbo is the better truck to drive. The V6 is the known quantity.

## Turbo or hybrid, 2024–2026

The hybrid adds 148 lb-ft and about 2 to 3 mpg. It's the stronger truck off-road and on steep grades, but it tows a little less, costs more and has more hardware to maintain. For most buyers, the 278-hp turbo is enough. Choose the hybrid if you want a TRD Pro or Trailhunter, which come only as hybrids, or if you'll really use the extra torque.

## Towing

[[chart:tow]]

The old V6 has the highest rating, but only with the Tow Package on certain configurations. Among the new trucks, the gas turbo tows more than the hybrid. Whatever you buy, check the label on the door jamb and the owner's manual for that exact truck. Passengers, gear in the bed and the trailer's tongue weight all come out of the payload, and a midsize truck runs out of payload long before it runs out of torque.

## Our picks

- **Light work on a budget:** a 2017–2023 4-cylinder, if the price is right.
- **The proven choice:** a 2020–2023 V6.
- **The best all-rounder:** a 2024–2026 turbo with the automatic.
- **The most capable:** a 2024–2026 i-FORCE MAX.
- **For towing:** a 2017–2023 V6 with the Tow Package, or a 2024–2026 gas turbo XtraCab.
