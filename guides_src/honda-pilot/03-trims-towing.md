Most Pilot shoppers are choosing a trim as much as a car, and on this SUV the trim decides more than leather and screens. On a 2016–2020 Pilot it decides the gearbox, and on a 2023 or later Pilot it decides how many people you can carry and how the second row works. Check the actual car, not the listing.

## The 2017–2022 trims

The LX, EX and EX-L were the volume models, with the 6-speed automatic until 2021, front-wheel drive as standard and all-wheel drive available. The EX added more comfort and convenience equipment, and the EX-L added leather. Honda Sensing, the bundle of automatic emergency braking, adaptive cruise and lane keeping, was optional on the EX and EX-L until 2019 and standard on the Touring and Elite, which also had the 9-speed automatic. The Elite came only with all-wheel drive and second-row captain's chairs. For 2019, Honda Sensing became standard on every trim; for 2021, the 9-speed did. In 2022, a Sport became the entry model and the TrailSport added all-wheel drive, black trim, 18-inch wheels and a small suspension lift.

## The 2023–2026 trims

[[chart:price]]

The 2023 range starts with the Sport, then the EX-L with leather and the larger screen, the Touring with the removable second-row middle seat, and the Elite, which adds the most luxury and comes only with all-wheel drive. All-wheel drive cost $2,100 on the Sport, EX-L and Touring in 2023 ($2,300 in 2026 dollars). The TrailSport is the real off-roader: 1 inch more ride height, 8.3 inches of ground clearance, steel skid plates, recovery points rated at twice its gross weight, a trailer hitch and the first all-terrain tires Honda fitted to an SUV. A Black Edition returned for 2025.

## All-wheel drive

Every all-wheel-drive Pilot since 2016 uses i-VTM4, which can vary the torque sent to each rear wheel, helping it turn and find grip. The 2023 version's rear differential takes 40% more torque. It's a capable system for snow and dirt roads, but the Pilot is still a unibody SUV with car-like clearance on every trim except the TrailSport.

## Towing

Properly equipped, a 2016–2022 Pilot can tow 1,588 kg (3,500 lb) with front-wheel drive and 2,268 kg (5,000 lb) with all-wheel drive. Honda rates the 2023 and later Pilot at up to 2,268 kg (5,000 lb), with a standard tow mode. The rating assumes the right hitch and wiring, and the trailer's tongue weight comes out of the Pilot's payload, along with everyone inside and their luggage. Before you tow, check the rating for the exact year and drivetrain in the owner's manual, and on a used Pilot, look at the hitch, the transmission fluid records and the brakes.

## The verdict

On a 2016–2020 Pilot, the trim matters most for the gearbox, so decide whether you want the 6-speed's simplicity or the 9-speed's extra ratios, and check the software updates either way. On a 2023 or later Pilot, decide on the seating first: the Touring's removable middle seat is the most flexible layout, and the EX-L is the value choice. Buy a TrailSport for its hardware, not its badge.
