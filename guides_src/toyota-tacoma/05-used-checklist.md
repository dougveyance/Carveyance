Used Tacomas sell on reputation, which is exactly why you shouldn't. A truck that towed heavy for years, spent winters on salted roads or came back from the trails on a winch line can be expensive whatever the tailgate says. Start with the job you need it to do, then let records and an inspection decide.

## Pick the job first

For towing up to 3,084 kg (6,800 lb), look at a 2017–2023 V6 with the Tow Package. For the best daily driver, look at a 2024–2026 turbo with coil springs. For the most torque and the best off-road hardware, look at a 2024–2026 hybrid TRD Pro or Trailhunter. For the lowest price, a 2017–2023 4-cylinder does light work, slowly. And if adults ride in the back every day, look at a Tundra instead.

## The years at a glance

| Model years | What they are | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 2017–2019 | Third generation. 159-hp 4-cylinder or 278-hp V6, 6-speed automatic or manual | No Apple CarPlay or Android Auto. Fuel pump recall. Rear whine on 2019 Double Cabs |
| 2020–2021 | Same truck with Apple CarPlay, Android Auto and a power seat on V6 grades | Fuel pump recall (2020). Rear axle whine bulletin |
| 2022–2023 | The last third-generation trucks | Rear axle nut recall (24V-152) |
| 2024 | All-new fourth generation. Turbo or i-FORCE MAX hybrid | Brake hose, TRD Off-Road shock and instrument panel recalls |
| 2025 | Small updates | Brake hose, driveshaft and instrument panel recalls |
| 2026 | Black TRD badges, new wheel option | Too new for long-term data |

## Body, frame and history

Run a vehicle history report, but don't treat a clean one as proof. Look for uneven panel gaps, mismatched paint and fresh undercoating that could hide rust. On a lift, check the frame rails, crossmembers, skid plates, rock sliders and differential housings for dents and scrapes. A scraped skid plate shows the truck was used. A bent crossmember or cracked weld shows it was used hard.

Check the bed for heavy damage, the hitch for wear and the trailer wiring for splices. Ask what the truck towed and how often, and favor one with records of transmission and differential fluid changes.

## Engine, transmission and 4WD

Start it cold if you can. On a V6, listen for ticking and watch for smoke, and expect the 6-speed to downshift often on hills. On a turbo, make sure it pulls smoothly with no hesitation and that the 8-speed shifts cleanly. On a hybrid, make sure it starts quietly with no warning lights, and ask a shop that knows Toyota hybrids to scan for codes. On a manual, check the clutch for slipping.

Engage 4-high and 4-low where it's safe to, and test the rear locker, Crawl Control and Multi-Terrain Select if the truck has them. Uneven tire wear, a crooked steering wheel or a speedometer that reads wrong all point to suspension changes or the wrong tire size.

## Recalls and campaigns: check by VIN

- **2017–2020:** Low-pressure fuel pump that can fail and stall the engine (20V-012 and 20V-682).
- **2019–2023 Double Cab:** Rear whine, covered by service bulletin T-SB-0045-23.
- **2022–2023:** Rear axle retaining nuts that can loosen because of welding debris, about 381,000 trucks (24V-152).
- **2024–2025 with 16-inch brakes:** Rear brake hoses that mud and dirt can damage, about 222,000 trucks (25V-058).
- **2024–2025:** Instrument panel software that may not show the speedometer and warning lights (25V-595).
- **2024–2025 TRD Off-Road:** Bilstein shock reservoirs that can corrode and break off, about 48,000 trucks (August 2026 recall).
- **2025 4x4:** Front driveshaft CV joints that can deform and break, about 6,000 trucks (25V-656).
- **2024–2025 with accessories fitted before sale:** A load-capacity label that may list the wrong accessory weight (24V-548 and 26V-179). The fix is a new label.
- **Every year:** New recalls can appear at any time. Run the VIN at [NHTSA's recall lookup](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) and ask a Toyota dealer to check for open service campaigns too. Ask for proof that each repair was done.

## What is it worth?

We don't publish average prices. They go stale quickly and hide the differences that matter, and Tacoma prices swing more than most with grade, cab, drive and modifications. Compare 20 to 30 listings for the same year, grade, engine, cab, bed, drive and mileage, with the same title history, and keep dealer asking prices separate from what trucks actually sold for. [FitDrive's deal check](/fitdrive/) does the first part for you, comparing a listing with similar ones nearby. Treat lifts, wheels and accessories as worth little or nothing, and a pre-purchase inspection as worth a lot.

Buying from a private seller, or keeping the truck past its factory warranty? Service plan providers to compare include [Chaiz](https://www.chaiz.com), [Endurance](https://www.endurancewarranty.com) and [Olive](https://www.olive.com). Carveyance doesn't rank or recommend them. Read what each one covers and excludes, and see the [FTC's guidance on auto service contracts](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/what-know-about-auto-service-contracts-extended-warranty-scams) first.

## Our shortlist

- **Best all-rounder:** a stock, documented 2020–2023 SR5 V6 or TRD Off-Road.
- **Best daily driver:** a 2024–2026 SR5 or TRD Off-Road with the 278-hp turbo, recalls done.
- **Strongest:** a 2024–2026 i-FORCE MAX TRD Off-Road.
- **Best for towing:** a 2017–2023 V6 with the Tow Package.
- **Budget pick:** a 2017–2019 SR5 V6 with records.
