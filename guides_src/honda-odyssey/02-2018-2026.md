The fifth-generation Odyssey went on sale in May 2017 as a 2018 model, built in Lincoln, Alabama. It kept the old van's wheelbase but brought a stiffer body, a new rear suspension, a 280-hp direct-injected V6 and a much more modern cabin. It's quick for a minivan, quiet on the highway and still easy to drive in a parking garage, and it's the Odyssey most used buyers will be looking at.

## The engine and the 2 gearboxes

[[chart:accel]]

Every version has the same V6, but for 2018 and 2019 the gearbox depends on the trim. The LX, EX and EX-L have a 9-speed automatic, and the Touring and Elite have Honda's own 10-speed, described at launch as the first 10-speed in a front-wheel-drive car. From 2020, every Odyssey has the 10-speed. Both are rated at 22 mpg combined. Car and Driver ran a 2018 Elite to 60 mph in 6.6 seconds and a 9-speed EX-L in 6.7; a 2025 Elite managed 6.4. Like the old engine, this V6 uses a timing belt.

## The seats and the gadgets

On the EX and above, the second row has Magic Slide seats: take out the center seat, and the 2 outboard seats slide sideways as well as fore and aft, so you can push them together, spread them apart or open a path to the third row. The LX has a conventional second row. The Touring and Elite add CabinWatch, a camera view of the rear seats on the dashboard screen, CabinTalk, which puts the driver's voice through the rear speakers, in-car Wi-Fi and a rear entertainment system. There's 32.8 cubic feet behind the third row, which folds into the floor.

## The years

Honda Sensing was standard on the EX and above from the start. The 2021 refresh brought a new grille, made Honda Sensing standard on every trim and added a rear seat reminder. For 2023, the LX was dropped and a Sport trim appeared. The 2025 refresh dropped the EX, renamed the Sport the Sport-L, and added a 7-inch digital instrument display, a 9-inch touchscreen with wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto, and a larger 12.8-inch rear screen.

## What to check before you buy

Test both power sliding doors repeatedly. Some 2018–2019 vans have rear latches that may stick and not latch (18V795), and on 2018–2020 vans, water can freeze the outer handle cables in cold weather so the doors don't latch (20V437). Side and side curtain air bags can deploy unexpectedly on 2018–2022 vans until their software is updated (26V227). Some 2018–2019 engines are covered by the connecting rod bearing recall (23V751), and 2022 vans by a second-row seat rail recall (21V432). In the updated IIHS moderate overlap test, which focuses on rear passengers, the 2023 Odyssey was rated Poor. The full list is in [the used checklist](../used-checklist/).

## The verdict

A 2021 or later EX-L is the best buy: standard Honda Sensing, the 10-speed, Magic Slide seats and leather, without the Touring's price. For a 2018–2019, prefer a Touring or Elite with the 10-speed, or a 9-speed EX or EX-L with its software up to date. Whatever you buy, make sure the door, air bag and engine recalls are done.
