The 2015 redesign kept the formula and modernized everything under it: stiffer structure, quieter cabin, and an engine lineup led by the 245-hp 2.0 EcoBoost, with the old 3.5 demoted to option and the Sport packing the F-150's 315-hp 2.7 twin-turbo. The 2019 refresh mattered more than most: an 8-speed automatic, standard Co-Pilot360 — and the Edge ST, the first SUV Ford ever let wear the badge, its 2.7 tuned to 335 hp and 380 lb-ft and good for high-5-second runs to 60 in period tests. Peak year 2017 saw 142,603 sales; 2 generations together cleared 2 million.

Then Ford simply ended it. The last North American Edge left Oakville on April 26, 2024 with no successor — the name survives only on China's Changan-built Edge L — making this the rare healthy nameplate retired for plant strategy rather than failure. The segment it pioneered now belongs to the RAV4s and CR-Vs a size down and the Grand Highlanders a size up.

## Which one

The 2.0 EcoBoost is the right answer for most: 24 EPA combined front-drive, proven in millions of Fords, happiest with documented oil changes. The 2015–18 3.5 suits the turbo-skeptical and tows the same; the 2015–18 Sport is the forgotten bargain — ST pace before the badge existed, priced like an SEL. The ST itself is the enthusiast's pick and holds its value accordingly: all-wheel drive standard, real brakes available, and a soundtrack the school run never expects.

On any 2019-plus car, confirm the 8-speed's low-speed manners and completed recalls; on 2.7s, look for clean oil and coolant history (it's a wet-belt-free, iron-block sweetheart, but turbos forgive nothing); on everything, check the panoramic roof drains and that the big Sync 3 screen behaves. Supply is enormous — be fussy for free.
